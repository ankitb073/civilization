# 0011: An age of contact, colonization and virgin-soil epidemics

**Context.** With two continents and generous coastal sailing, worlds met in the Bronze Age, so nothing like an age of discovery happened.

**Decision.**
- Sea range is short until ocean navigation is known: 85 / 115 / 150 map units by era, then 2,600.
- When a trade route first joins two landmasses, a dated `first-contact` event is recorded.
- The less-connected side then suffers a virgin-soil epidemic: high mortality, spreading inland along routes.
- Navigating states plant overseas colonies and make war on weaker overseas states.
- Overseas regions weigh heavily in revolt odds, so independence movements emerge on their own.

**Why.** These are among the most consequential dynamics of real history. They produce some of the archive's best documents: ships' logs, settler letters, native complaints, chronicles of the newcomers.
