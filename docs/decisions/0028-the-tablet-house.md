# 0028: The Tablet House, a game about who writes history

**Context.** The archive's central idea is that the record and the documents disagree, because scribes bend what they write for the people who pay them. The lying-chronicle game (0017) lets you catch a liar; nothing yet lets you be one. The Tablet House (`#/tablet`, design in `docs/lld/tablet-house.md`) puts you in the palace scribe's seat for the five days a city falls or holds, and then sets your tablet beside the record and the chronicle that really survived.

**Decision.**
- **Thirty playable moments from the record** (`scenarios.json`, `engine/src/export/scenarios.ts`) with one agent-written script each (`scenario-scripts/<id>.json`, checked by `engine/src/debug/scenario-validate.ts`). Scripts never type a figure; the game fills `{value}`, `{tablet.*}` and `{find.*}` at play time.
- **Scripts reach the browser bundled with the atlas**, one lazy chunk per file, found at build time by `import.meta.glob` over `scenario-scripts/`. No engine change and no copy step; a new script ships with the next atlas build. A scenario plays only when its script exists and is whole (`normalizeScript`); until then it is left out of the daily draw and "Another city", and `#/tablet/<id>` says the scribe has not finished yet. Without `scenarios.json` the game plays a fixture city with a generic script.
- **The five days are a small state machine** (`atlas/src/game/tablet/engine.ts`, pure): day 1 the muster, day 2 our dead, theirs and who held the field, day 3 a card, day 4 a card and the end, day 5 the king reads the tablet. Each slot offers the record's figure, a rounded one and the king's (more men, fewer of ours, more of theirs; one significant figure, so the boast looks like a royal number). Meters start at 0 and move by the scripts' small whole numbers; the first ending whose `when` holds wins.
- **Day five is read by what the card does**, since writers invented their own flags: `stand` leaves the tablet as written; `amend` has the king's version pressed over every line that does not already flatter him (each line is smoothed out and pressed again on screen); `edge` amends and presses the true dead along the tablet's lower edge; `hidden` amends and keeps the truth elsewhere (an envelope, a slip, a draft, a binding). Known flags are in a table; others are read from their effects (favour up and conscience down amends; both up amends and hides the truth).
- **How the tablet survives depends on the story.** A burned archive bakes clay hard (and chars paper); otherwise the king has the tablet kiln-fired only if he is pleased with the scribe, and an unbaked tablet loses a line to damp. The dead the archaeologist quotes never crumble, so the scripts' paragraphs always hold.
- **The score is how much of the truth reached AY 49xx:** twenty points per line in the record's reading, ten if rounded, none if the king's; a lost line counts for nothing unless a hidden copy (the jar, the edge, an envelope) carries it. The share line is the biggest lie you told ("I wrote 3,000 enemy dead. The record says 194."), five coloured squares and your fate, linking to `s/tablet.html?c=<id>`, which opens the same city. "Save the card" draws a 1200×630 image of your fired tablet and the line in the browser.
- **The tablet is drawn, not photographed** (`clay.ts`): a canvas of lit clay with a pillow bevel, grain, a thumbprint and rulings; a condensed wedge alphabet (`strokes.ts`) pressed stroke by stroke as cuneiform wedges, each head three facets and each shaft a V-groove shaded by its angle to the light; ink on papyrus, parchment and paper for later scenarios. Painted scene art (`atlas/public/game/art/art.json`) sits behind each day when present; the 3D city (`backdrop.ts`) frames the opening and ending, and any day without art.

**Why.**
- **The idea made playable:** the player feels the king's pressure line by line, sees the boast pressed into clay, and then sees it caught. The game's own rule that the king fires only the tablets he likes means honest tablets crumble more often: across all thirty scripts an honest tablet let stand scores 80–100 (82 on average), the same tablet rewritten for the king scores 0, and rewriting it while hiding the truth scores 40.
- **Credibility:** every figure comes from the record or is bent from it by a stated rule; `engine/test/tablet.test.ts` plays every script through all 243 tablets and all 27 card paths (196,830 games) and checks every line, ending and paragraph fills, and every ending in every script is reachable.
- **Habit and reach:** a city of the day, thirty cities to come back to, and a share line and card made to be posted.
- **Speed:** each script is its own small chunk, the game itself one lazy chunk; the 3D backdrop and the soundscape load behind the first screen and the game never waits for them.
