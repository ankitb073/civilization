# 0018: Lives II and III, a hundred lives

**Context.** The first 40 Lives (fl-601–640) were all rulers, chosen by how much the record holds about them. A reader of the archive met kings and queens, and everyone else only as names in their wars: the generals who fought the battles, the inventors, the founders of faiths, the merchants whose houses outlived empires, the rebels, regents and chroniclers, and the envoys who drafted the treaties. These people are already in the record, often with their own documents.

**Decision.** Sixty more Lives, written and checked the same way as the first forty (decision 0010):
- **Lives II (fl-641–688, dossier wave 6):** 48 subjects by kind, with a quota per kind: generals 11, inventors 6, founders of faiths or schools 6 (government reformers share this quota and are labelled as such), merchants 5, rebels 7, regents 4, chroniclers 5, archaeologists 2 and elected heads of state 4. At most one of a kind per century, so each kind spans the five millennia.
- **Lives III (fl-689–699 and fl-600, wave 7):** the twelve ids left in the Lives range go to the kinds still missing: envoys 4, priests 3, usurpers 3 and philosophers 2. That makes a hundred Lives.
- **Who is eligible:** dead by AY 4950, with a page in the atlas, and not the subject of any earlier dossier or flagship.
- **Thin records:** where the record of a life is thin (a priest with two tablets), the fact sheet asks for 650–1,000 words instead of 900–1,400, and for the silences to be stated as silences.
- **Pipeline:** one writer agent per Life, then a fact-checker per group of two to four, then an independent audit of a random third of Lives II, which measured the error rate left after one check (docs/accuracy.md). The first check fixed about one error per 90 claims (58 in 60 files); the audit found 2 minor errors in about 1,195 claims, one of them introduced by a checker.
- **In the atlas:** the Lives collection lists every Life, not the first 40, in order of the subject's birth, noted with what they were and when they lived ("General, AY 3580–3646").

**Why.**
- **Coverage drives interest.** Each kind of life is a different way into the same world: a merchant's ledger prices, a general's battles from both sides, an envoy's treaty against both chronicles. A hundred is also a number readers remember.
- **The record was ready.** These people already have events, roles and documents; the Lives only read them closely.
- **The writers found the record's faults.** Sixty close readings turned up dozens of inconsistencies in the generated archive, and the worst are now fixed at the source: translators who died before the texts they translated (#38), republics' chronicles that crowned their presidents (#40), one treaty dated three different days (#41), editorial notes that said "ground truth" (#42), victors' assemblies debating in the losers' words (#43), coins that "ended" while states still used them, and coin events naming deposed rulers. Seven flagships that quoted the old texts were revised. The rest are filed (#39, #44, and comments on #18, #32, #34, #35).
