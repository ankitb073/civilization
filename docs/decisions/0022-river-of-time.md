# 0022: The River of Time (`#/river`), a 3D Histomap

**Context.** The Histomap (`#/histomap`) shows five thousand years of relative power as streams, but only one number per state (its share of the world's people). The decade surveys also record each state's districts and soldiers, and the record holds the great wars, plagues and eruptions. We wanted one cinematic view that carries all of it, readable on a phone and postable as a still.

**Decision.**
- **A lazy view, `#/river`, in `atlas/src/three/river/`** (three.js shared with `#/3d`; the view's own chunk is 17 KB gzip plus 3 KB CSS). Linked once from the 2D Histomap.
- **Geometry.** Time runs up a valley (AY 0 at its head, 0.1 units a year). Each state is a ridge: its width is its share of the world's people (or, with the People/Land switch, of the world's land districts), stacked west to east as on the Histomap; its height is its share of the world's soldiers, with a contour every 2.5%. Ridges are resampled every 5 years (Catmull-Rom between the 20-year surveys), meshed in 250-year chunks so the camera culls what it can't see, and clipped at the present year, where a cut face shows the living states' cross-sections.
- **Height is soldiers, not treasury.** The army is already published on every state's page (the decade survey series), so the river and the pages agree; treasury isn't exported. It is a share of the world's soldiers, not a head count, so every age reads at the same scale. Head counts are on hover.
- **Its own export, `river.json` (46 KB gzip).** The streams are the Histomap's 140, plus every state that ever held 2.5% of the world's people (338 in all). The Histomap ranks states by absolute peak population, which leaves the first two millennia almost empty (one stream at AY 500). Each stream carries its people share, districts and soldiers per survey, its arms (`ArmsSpec`), and its colour. The file also carries 28 standing stones: the deadliest war of every five centuries plus the three deadliest overall, the worst plagues, the VEI 7 eruptions, first contact, the first script, the great trade collapse and the worst famines, each with its page and its figure. It is built in `engine/src/export/river.ts` and is a new file, so no existing consumer changes.
- **Reading aids.** Each state's arms float over its highest point so far, era bands and names lie on the valley floor, centuries are ruled across it, and stones stand on the banks (wars on the west, nature and discovery on the east). Labels are a decluttered DOM layer that keeps clear of the HUD. Picking is analytic (a ray marched over the same heightfield), so it costs nothing per frame. "Whole river" frames all the years so far from the west bank, with a 2.8× vertical exaggeration.
- **Motion.** A fresh visit plays from AY 0 at 90 years a second. Dragging along the valley or scrolling travels in time, dragging across swings the camera, and pinching or ctrl+scroll zooms. Under reduced motion it doesn't autoplay or sway.

**Why.** Width, height and the stones each answer a different question (how many, how armed, what befell them), and the deterministic, record-read data keeps the view honest. Phones hold 60 fps (frame p95 16.7 ms with a 4× CPU slowdown in Playwright) because only the near chunks are drawn and the fog ends the far plane.
