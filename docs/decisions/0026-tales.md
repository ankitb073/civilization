# 0026: Tales of Yüvinzhi, short stories checked against the record

**Context.** The archive's sources are documents: chronicles, steles, ledgers, Lives, a History. A reader learns *what* happened at the sack of a city or in a plague year, but rarely what it was like to be there, and fiction is how most readers first enter a past. A story set in an invented world usually can't be checked. Here every public fact a story touches is already in the record.

**Decision.** A new genre of flagship, the Tale (`form: short story`, genre `story`, catalogue code `ST`, ids fl-1001 and up):
- **The moments (dossier wave 8):** events chosen by kind, each with a quota and spread across the five millennia: sacks, sieges, large battles, plagues, famines, eruptions, droughts, revolutions, usurpations, civil wars, revolts, conversions, persecutions, collapses, inventions, marriages, debasements, successions, treaties and more. A moment is skipped if it is the subject of an earlier dossier or flagship. The builder made 100 dossiers, and 62 were written (fl-1001–1062): 14 sacks, 10 sieges, 10 battles, 10 usurpations, 5 civil wars, 4 famines, 3 plagues, 3 eruptions, 2 droughts and a revolution. The rest are left for later.
- **The fact sheet:** what happened, who ruled and commanded, the faiths, the money and prices, and the technology of that date. The ruler, the place names and the people's names all come from the district's culture *at that date*, so the names are the period's own, not the city's later ones.
- **The rules:**
  - Public facts are the record's. Private lives are the writer's.
  - Invented people get period names of the right sex.
  - Nothing anachronistic.
  - Period documents are quoted only verbatim.
  - Each Tale ends with an author's note citing the documents it drew on by catalogue number.
  - The author is "a novelist of the modern age", writing in the AY 4990s.
- **The pipeline:** as for the Lives (decision 0010). There is one writer per Tale, then a fact-checker per group of four.
  - The checkers verified 2,820 claims and fixed 42 errors (about one per 67 claims): names, places, figures, languages and dates. 32 Tales needed no fix.
  - The writers' brief gained a variety section after the first batches, because Tales were starting to share devices.
- **In the archive:**
  - A "Tales" collection, the "Tales of …" parts of the EPUB, and a share series. All three are ordered by the year in which each Tale is set.
  - Tales are kept out of the History's sources.

**Why.**
- **Reader interest.** A story is the easiest way into a world, and the most shareable piece of it. These stories lead back into the documents, because the author's notes cite them.
- **Credibility.** Each Tale can be checked against the same record as everything else, so the fiction never contradicts the archive.
- **The record was ready.** The events, persons, prices and faiths already existed; the Tales only put a person inside them.
