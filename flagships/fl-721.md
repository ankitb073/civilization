*Sung over a cradle in [[set:45|Nonen]], in the 7th year of [[per:34093|Yidiiji]].*

Hush now, my lamb, the lamp is low,  
the boats are tied where the black waters go,  
the market is shut and the bell won't ring,  
and the cough goes walking. Hush, I sing.  

They call it the [[evt:34536|Shaking Cough]] in the square;  
it shook the weaver and left him there,  
it shook the baker, it shook his wife;  
it shall not shake you. Hush, my life.  

They say it was born in our own town,  
by the harbour stairs where the boats come down;  
the boatmen spit when they hear our name.  
Sleep, little one. You're not to blame.  

I've tied a cloth across my face,  
as the preacher told us to, in every place;  
I'll kiss you through it, I'll hold you tight,  
and the cough shall pass our door tonight.  

*Hush, hush, the lamp is low,*  
*the Light is in it, and the Light won't go.*  

The preacher of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] came with his lamp,  
he blessed the door and he blessed the damp,  
he said: "There is One, and only One;  
the old gods are shadows; the One is the sun.  
Seek it within by the inner light,  
and do what is just, and keep the measure right."  
He gave us his blessing, he gave us bread.  
The One keep the preacher. Lay down your head.  

Your father went out with the king's own men  
to fight the rebels and come home again;  
since the [[evt:34620|secession of Nuvon]] they go and go,  
like geese in autumn, row on row.  
They won, they say, at the [[evt:34629|battle of Ninshinuesnun]];  
he'll come back walking when the war is done.  

The king is in [[set:27|Tushi]] in a golden bed,  
King Yidiiji with the crown on his head;  
the One keep the king, the One keep the land,  
the One hold [[pol:1397|Ayshi]] in his hand.  

A measure of grain for an [[cur:675|Isa]] and more,  
and who will carry it to our door?  
The miller is sick and the baker's gone;  
I'll grind it myself when the night is done.  

*Hush, hush, the lamp is low,*  
*the Light is in it, and the Light won't go.*  

Your sister sleeps in the burying ground,  
under the cypress, where the bells go round;  
she'd sing you this if she were here.  
I sing it for both of you, my dear.  

Don't shake, my lamb, don't cough, don't cry,  
the One is near and the night goes by;  
the moon is up on the harbour wall,  
and the One keeps watch over us all.  

Hush now, my lamb, the lamp is low,  
the boats are tied where the black waters go,  
the market is shut and the bell won't ring.  
Sleep, my life. Hush, I sing.
