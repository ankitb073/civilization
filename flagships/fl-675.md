[[per:40499|Enginumalin]]'s name means *victory lion*. He was born in AY 4239, a Kunelmu man of the [[pol:1630|Kawnunuy]] Empire. The victories of his age were claimed on steles by kings and queens; his name appears on none. The record keeps very little of him: in 4280, aged forty-one, he founded a merchant house at [[set:79|Taalinnuy]], the imperial capital, and in 4292 he died a natural death at fifty-three. It names no parents, no wife, no children. No letter, seal or account in his hand survives. Yet the [[hou:1975|House of Bitsi]] ran for 254 years. Of the more than 2,500 houses, banks and companies in the record, only two lasted longer.

## A map that halved

Of his childhood and training the record is silent; only the state around him can be described. In the survey of the 4240s the empire counted 7,854,171 people in 116 districts. [[per:40137|Avun]] took the throne in 4246, when Enginumalin was seven, and reigned thirty years. The [[evt:40738|Kawnunuy–Viririnu War]] of 4262–4266 won him eight districts and a vassal, and by the end of the 4260s the empire held 124 districts and 8,293,404 people, the most it reached in Enginumalin's lifetime.

Then it broke. In the [[evt:40858|Yidhe–Kawnunuy War]] Avun's 19,003 men met 153,712 under Pimpavbukhin II at Nidhinge in 4275 and lost 5,596 to the enemy's 1,726. Yidhe sacked Taalinnuy that year, and the city's archives burned. The Treaty of Taalinnuy, a bronze tablet that survives complete (PAL·TR·02562), gave up Tunnge, "38 districts in all", and bound the empire to pay 8,486,596 [[cur:914|Niva]]: about 79,774 kg of silver, nearly a quarter of what the treasury had held at the end of the 4260s. Its last clause speaks to men like him: "Let it be read aloud in the markets of both lands." By the end of the 4270s the empire had 62 districts and 4,453,388 people. Enginumalin was thirty-six when his city was sacked. What he lost in it, the record does not say.

## A house in a year of war

Avun died in 4276, and his daughter Kumugurath, aged twenty-one, succeeded him; she would be remembered as Kumugurath the Old. In 4278 she built a granary in the capital. Its limestone slab, excavated at Taalinnuy in AY 4908, reads: "Kumugurath, queen of Kawnunuy, built this granary in Kawnunuy" (PAL·IN·06217). The slab calls the city by the empire's name, and so, a century later, does the house's own ledger.

In 4279 Birnathu seceded and the [[evt:40914|Second Kawnunuy Civil War]] began. The [[evt:40930|Founding of the House of Bitsi]] fell in its second year. Why a man of forty-one chose that moment, the record does not say. It does say what he dealt in: timber, horses, grain, woollen cloth and spices, the goods a sacked city rebuilds with, eats and fights with. The Niva held its weight of 9.4 grams of silver all his life, but goods did not hold their price. At Taalinnuy a measure of grain cost about 0.85 Niva in the 4260s and 1.2 in the 4280s; a horse about 19.5 Niva in the 4270s and 23 in the 4280s.

The record names five partner towns for the house: Turamtu, Diviminnge, Nidhinge, Jerathnu and Pinnga. The list belongs to the house's long life: Pinnga was not founded until 4288. The rest make an uneasy map. Turamtu lay inside Yidhe, the empire that had sacked his city. Nidhinge was where Avun was beaten. At the port of Jerathnu, Kumugurath defeated the rebels in 4283 and 4284. Her stele for the second battle claims "She killed 19,600 soldiers" (PAL·IN·03080); the record gives the rebels' losses in it as 3,738.

## Caravans held back

The [[evt:40995|Third Kawnunuy Civil War]] (4285–4290) and the [[evt:41006|Fourth Kawnunuy Civil War]] (4286–4290) ran side by side. The Bimathshi rebels came three times against Taalinnuy itself, with 308, 478 and 287 men against defenders numbering between 10,834 and 19,568. The Bimathshi chronicler, writing in 4323, says that in the first attack in 4287 "26 of ours were lost, but 104 of the enemy" (PAL·CH·07233); the record says the attackers lost 81 and the defenders 32. These were skirmishes, not sieges, but they were fought at Enginumalin's door.

No paper of the House of Bitsi from these years survives. The nearest witness is a rival's: the day-book of the House of Rure at Nuwkennga for 4288 (PAL·LE·02819), whose entries price iron and copper at Taalinnuy. In its margin: "Caravans are held back on account of the Third Kawnunuy Civil War", and "The caravan guards ask double". If his caravans travelled those roads, they paid those guards too; the record cannot say.

Both rebellions were crushed in 4290. Enginumalin died two years later, after twelve years at the head of his house. By the end of the 4290s the empire had climbed back to 86 districts.

## What the house kept

The house passed to Neshin, aged twenty-three; the record names no tie of blood between them. Ten heads held it in all, and it was dissolved in 4534, 104 years after the Kawnunuy Empire itself collapsed. No other house founded in that empire lasted more than 91 years.

Its later papers keep his choices. A ledger of 4381 by the fifth head, Mubuthnilin, missing several leaves (PAL·LE·02882), still prices horses, spices, timber and grain. Mubuthnilin's testament of 4415 leaves "50 beams of timber from the warehouse" to his "faithful agent", woollen cloth to his sister's son, and 111 Niva to the house of the Gyevigu, as the documents of that age call the [[mov:69|Kievi]] faith, "for the feeding of the poor on the feast day" (PAL·WL·00947). That faith asked for justice in dealings and moderation in all things. Whether Enginumalin kept it, the record does not say.

All five of his goods appear in those papers. The rulers of his age set up steles that multiplied the dead. Enginumalin left no monument and no words; he left a counting-house that outlasted the empire which sacked his city and the one that failed to protect it.
