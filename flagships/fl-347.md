*[Column I]*

[[dei:0|Atlie]], father of the high sky, why did you turn away your face?  
[[dei:1|Uelti]], lord of the right word, why was your word not spoken?  
[[dei:2|Muevas'e]], mother of the furrow, why did you open your lap to the stranger?  
[[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river, why did you carry the boats of the enemy?  
O city, O [[set:2|Mieshlothie]], your gate is a mouth that cries.  
Woe for the city. Woe for the house. Woe for the city and the house.

The boats of [[pol:6|Ana]] came up the river for the road of trade.  
They wanted the road of the river; they wanted the silver of the river.  
They did not ask with bread. They asked with the spear.  
So began the [[evt:668|First Iiyyi Trade War]], the war of the river road.  
Woe for the city. Woe for the house.

In the [[evt:686|Battle of Toiyoe]] the men of [[pol:2|Mieshlothie]] went down.  
They went down in the morning, they did not come up in the evening.  
The ground drank them; Muevas'e drank them.  
Then the host of Ana sat down before the walls,  
sat down before the broad gate, sat down by the water-gate.  
That was the [[evt:687|First Siege of Myishlithie]]; the bread grew thin.  
Woe for the city. Woe for the house.

*[Column II]*

At Sase, in the [[evt:689|Battle of Sase]], the last spears went out.  
Three thousand and seven hundred went out against the strangers,  
and the strangers broke them, and a thousand lay down by Sase.  
[[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, where was your spear?  
Your feet were not red with their blood; your feet were red with ours.

Then the gate was given. Then the house was given.  
That was the [[evt:690|First Sack of Myishlithie]], the day of weeping.  
The silver of the temple went down the river in the boats of Ana.  
The bronze of the doors went down the river.  
The cloth of the weavers, the oil of the presses, went down the river.  
The young women went down the river, and did not look back.  
The old men sat in the ashes of the gate and did not rise.  
[[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon looked down and saw it,  
and the moon was dark three nights for shame.  
Woe for the city. Woe for the house.

They came a second time. Who can bear it? They came a second time.  
At Sase again, in the [[evt:696|Battle of Sase]], the field was theirs.  
That was the [[evt:697|Second Sack of Myishlithie]]; what was left, they took.  
Then the king of the city bowed and set his seal.  
That was the [[evt:698|Treaty of Myishlithie]].  
Fields and a town of ours were given to Ana.  
The silver goes down the river each year, thirty years it goes down.  
The road of the river belongs to the boats of Ana.  
Woe for the city. Woe for the house.

*[Column III]*

Now it is the eighth year of [[per:953|Assatlinieme]], lady of the land.  
She has set the brick of the gate upon the brick.  
She has washed the threshold of Atlie with water and with oil.  
She has called the priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] back to the altars.  
All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again.  
Let the city that was given be taken back. Let the house that was emptied be filled.

Atlie, look down again. Uelti, speak the word again.  
Muevas'e, close your lap to the stranger; give the barley to your own.  
Niele'i, carry our boats, not theirs.  
Nieshashta, let your feet be red, but not with ours.  
O Mieshlothie, O city of the broad gate, rise.  
Joy for the city. Joy for the house. Let it be so. Let it be so.
