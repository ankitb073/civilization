*Sermon preached by [[per:27615|Khieshewiluesha]] at [[set:228|Nizon]], on the day the first elders were ordained, in the 23rd year of [[per:27397|Yoni Yueve]], king of [[pol:1193|Bene]]; taken down by one who heard it.*

Friends, sit. The ground is dry and the sermon is not long; or it will not be long if you do not interrupt me, which the people of Nizon have never yet managed.

You have been taught, as I was taught, that all things turn on a great wheel; that what ends will begin again; that the harvest returns, and the flood returns, and the king returns in his son, and so it shall be forever. The priests of the [[mov:2|Tsenzo]] taught it to me with the best will in the world, and I do not mock them. A wheel is a comforting thing. If all things come round again, then nothing is ever truly lost, and nothing is ever truly owed.

But look about you. Twenty-two years ago the old kingdom fell to pieces, in the [[evt:27758|Collapse of the Diebegovebenga State]]; you remember it, the older of you, the roads full of carts and the granaries broken open. Out of it came the [[evt:27761|Rise of Bene]], and this house that shelters us. Did the old kingdom come round again? It did not. It is gone; its scribes are dead; its coins are melted. Some years after, our young men marched against Shetsi in the [[evt:27852|Bene–Shetsi War]], and marched back again with nothing gained and nothing settled, and some of them did not march back at all. Did those young men come round again? Ask their mothers.

No. Time is not a wheel. Time is a river. It rises in one place and runs to one sea, and it does not run back. And at the sea there is a judgment, where every deed that was done upon the river will be weighed.

Now, if that is so, then everything changes. If nothing comes round again, then every cruelty is final, and every kindness is final also. The beggar you did not feed today will not be fed by you in another turning; there is no other turning. The suffering you could have eased and did not ease will stand against you at the sea. That is why I say to you: compassion is not one virtue among many. It is the only one that the river remembers.

You will ask me about the gods. I do not throw down [[dei:14|Wolime]] of the spear, or [[dei:15|Wenba]] of the earth, or [[dei:16|Tomamnol]] of the sky. I say only that they are servants, or shadows, of a single Power that stands at the mouth of the river. When you poured to Wenba for the harvest, it was that Power that heard you, through her, as a king hears through his steward.

You will ask whether a rich harvest is still a blessing. It is. Prosperity is still a sign of favour; I do not say otherwise. But I say this also: the favour is lent, and it will be counted. The [[cur:551|Niesh]] in your purse is a question the Power is asking you. Answer it well.

You will ask why I ordain elders today, when I have spoken against the old priests. I have not spoken against priests. I have spoken against the wheel. The truth must be kept, and passed down in order, or in two generations it will be a rumour; therefore I lay hands on these seven, and they will lay hands on others, and so the truth will run down the years as the river runs, in one bed, toward one sea.

And you will ask whether this teaching is for Bene alone. It is not. The Tsenzo belong to one people and seek no one; but the river runs past every door. Tell the Shetsi. Tell the traders at [[set:105|Buey]]. Tell your enemies first, because they need it most.

Here then is the [[mov:107|Yeish]]. Let this day at Nizon be called the [[evt:27987|Founding of the Yeish Faith]], if anyone must call it anything. I would rather you called it the day you started counting your deeds.

Go in peace. Do something today that the river will remember.
