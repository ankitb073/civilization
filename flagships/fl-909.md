In the eighteenth year of Ololomen VI, in the month of the barley harvest, a priest of the sky-god Domamnol at Taulin heard a sound from the hills "like the bellowing of a bull as large as the world." The sound came three times, and the temple doors shook in their sockets. By evening ash lay a finger deep on the roofs. Next morning the sun rose "red, like a coal taken from the hearth," and at noon it was the colour of copper. That year the barley did not ripen and frost came in the month of the figs. It came again the next year, and the year after. The priest wrote it all down sixteen years later, in the Account of the Veiled Sun (PAL·CH·08449), when the sky had at last cleared, "clean as washed linen."

He was describing the [[evt:17383|Eruption of Mount Shenlamuokhner (AY 2162)]], a blast of the seventh magnitude that cooled the world by about a degree. The 2160s were the coldest decade in the whole record of Yüvinzhi, 1.56 °C below the long-run mean. It is tempting to blame that ash for the fall of the great empires, but the record does not allow it. Between AY 2000 and 2250 nearly every great power broke apart, and five of the great empires had fallen before the mountain spoke. Most of them died fat, at or near their greatest size and with almost no order left inside them. Cold, drought and plague came afterwards, and they decided how hard the fall would be.

A few numbers frame the story. In AY 2000 some 66.3 million people lived under 41 states. By 2120 there were 70.9 million under only 31 states, because the empires had swallowed their neighbours. By 2240 the total had slipped to 68.8 million, divided among 45 states, most of them heirs of the old empires. Along the way about 3.4 million soldiers died in war, about 4.7 million people died in four great plagues, and some two million died or fled in famines.

## Eight empires and a ford (AY 2000–2075)

In AY 2000 the map belonged to a ring of old empires. [[pol:580|Yennga]], ruled from [[set:155|Yinnge]] by [[per:15623|Tathreltafvafen]] the Victorious until 2011, held about 6.9 million people. [[pol:664|Yeda]] ruled from [[set:0|Taulin]], [[pol:526|Los]] from [[set:127|Lus]], [[pol:649|Eshueethie]] from [[set:3|Ishwyithie]], [[pol:637|Kazhe]] from [[set:84|Azhi]], [[pol:598|Luthe]] from [[set:9|Dagafu]], [[pol:612|Pibetie]] from [[set:91|Pivithie]] and [[pol:629|Puvu]] from Kabaf. The younger kingdom of [[pol:686|Nilu]] was growing at their edges. Reigns were long. [[per:15657|Ishfama]] ruled Eshueethie until 2005, and from 2018 [[per:16110|Natlatl I]] the Great held its throne for sixty-four years. [[per:15961|Ololomen IV]], called the Old, ruled Yeda from 2003 to 2055, and [[per:16279|Nakathash]] ruled Nilu from 2037 to 2094. Money was still weighed rather than minted: Yeda's Kerel was 8.5 grams of silver and Nilu's Fiyi 10.3. In the 2000s a measure of grain cost 16.5 grams on average, though it cost about 11 grams at Azhi and 42 at Yinnge.

Yennga grew as it long had, by sending huge armies against tiny ones. In its war on Yirenunyirin in 2045–48 Yennga lost 16,427 men and its enemy lost twenty, losses that can only have come from marching, hunger and fever. Yeda chose another road. In the [[evt:16372|First Yeda–Tiy War]] (2037–44) Ololomen IV attacked [[pol:623|Tiy]] to force it to pay tribute. He won the first battle, at Chezhietle in [[reg:11|Tsiey]] in 2038, and raised a stele claiming 9,400 enemy dead (PAL·IN·01185); the record gives 4,292. Then he lost the next four. The Tiy account (PAL·CH·03036) leaves out that first battle and inflates the rest. For 2039, for example, it gives Yeda 68,930 men and 33,444 dead, where the record has 32,463 and 12,330. Each side kept the battle that flattered it. In 2044 the king who had come to collect tribute agreed to pay it instead: 7,697 kg of silver a year for thirty-six years. The payments show in the two treasuries. Yeda's fell from about 428,000 kg in the 2030s to 179,000 by the 2080s, while Tiy's nearly tripled.

In the 2060s the small kingdom of [[pol:693|Ana]] fought two empires at once, at a single river crossing: the ford of Fyinan in [[reg:13|Iiyyi]]. In 2045 an Ana man named Khi'ua is credited with inventing mounted cavalry, independently of anyone else. In the [[evt:16582|Eshueethie–Ana War]] it won four battles in a row at the ford between 2068 and 2071. In the last of them an Eshueethie army of 39,550 lost 12,947 men, and Ana lost 946. No source says whether horsemen won those days. The Eshueethie chronicle (PAL·CH·03085) blames hired men, storms and treachery. The Ana account (PAL·CH·03086), written forty-two years later, swells the enemy at that last battle to 107,846 men.

On the other bank Pibetie pressed the [[evt:16567|Pibetie–Ana War]]. The Song of Bozinsimen at the Ford of Fyinan (PAL·PO·00008) remembers it through the warrior king who died in the river in 2070 and his daughter [[per:16568|Boinieme]], aged thirteen, who said "The river owes me a father" and later ruled Pibetie for thirty-one years. In 2073 Ana ceded ten districts and paid 21,069 kg of silver; the surviving tablet (PAL·TR·01133) calls it 702 talents. A year later Ana's royal city opened its gates to Natlatl.

Nakathash meanwhile took Nilu to war in the name of its One Power. Her [[evt:16569|Holy War of Nilu against Puvu]] (2062–73), fought in [[reg:24|Pyivi]], left one of the most trustworthy royal documents of the age. Writing from camp after the defeat at Lamligul in 2070 (PAL·LT·01661), she gives her own dead as 3,234 ("I have read every name"), exactly the record's figure. On the same field [[per:16411|Napüningi]] of Puvu raised a stele claiming nine thousand (PAL·IN·01207). Nilu lost 29,441 men in the war and Puvu 45,586, yet Puvu had the better of it, and the peace gave Nilu nothing but a fifteen-year truce.

## The great unravelling (AY 2076–2137)

There had been warnings. The [[evt:16309|Onset of the Great Drought in Daadüdhih (AY 2032)]] struck 513 districts, 107 of them Yennga's, and within a decade grain at Lus rose from 15.5 grams to 23. But the empires that fell first fell from within. The record keeps a rough measure of each state's internal order. After 2070 it sank toward zero in one empire after another, while their populations went on rising.

Puvu fell first. Napüningi began a holy war of his own against Luthe in 2080. He lost 37,615 men to Luthe's 14,977 and paid 40,957 kg of silver to [[per:16647|Nandithim]] the Victorious for peace. By 2100 Puvu had more people than ever, 4.2 million, and its measure of order stood at nothing. Napüningi, remembered as the Last, died in 2101 after fifty-one years on the throne. The [[evt:16839|Collapse of the Puvu State]] followed that year and left five successors, among them [[pol:727|Kabave]], which lasted eight years, and [[pol:728|Yif]]. Los came next. Under [[per:16546|Wasiluesh'a]] the Lame (2069–98) the province of Ninshinuesnon seceded, and it seceded again in 2116. Los peaked at 7.5 million people in the 2110s, and in 2120 the [[evt:17025|Collapse of the Los State]] split it six ways, leaving a rump kingdom of [[pol:739|Los]]. Another heir, [[pol:740|Pivish]], would endure until 2652, longer than Los itself had lasted.

The [[evt:16933|Onset of the Great Drought in Baan (AY 2119)]] struck 490 districts, about seventy each in Pibetie and Eshueethie. Two years later [[evt:17015|The Red Cough]] broke out at Pivithie and spread along the trade routes to 42 more cities. It killed about 1.24 million people, nearly a quarter of those it reached. Pibetie had already been bled. In the [[evt:16920|Second Pibetie–Eshueethie War]] (2109–13), fought in [[reg:5|Yiutl]], [[per:16816|Iwshashmovi]] had attacked and lost. The Lament for the Fallen of Yiotl (PAL·PO·00007) mourns the day when "the king went out with forty-four thousand" against sixty-four thousand and "nineteen thousand of ours went down." These are almost exactly the record's figures. The victors' chronicle (PAL·CH·03136) gives Pibetie 91,354 men and 65,825 dead. Here the losers' grief is a better witness than the winners' pride. Pibetie paid 46,610 kg of silver and lost nearly half a million people in the plague decade. In 2134 it broke "in four, like a loaf at a funeral meal."

Yeda's tribute to Tiy had run out in 2080. In 2120 its city of Nimid was sacked and its archives burned, one of fourteen archives burned in the period's 63 sacks, which is one reason the Tiy side of these years is thin. The [[evt:17060|Collapse of the Tiy State]] followed in 2124, and its heirs included [[pol:746|Miuhitl]] and [[pol:747|Nimit]]. Eshueethie, the great winner of the age, fell last. [[per:16679|Weni]] the Silent died in 2119, a usurper named Yewatluee seized the throne in the crisis that followed, and the Red Cough arrived soon after. In the 2130s Eshueethie still counted 7.2 million subjects, more than ever. In 2137 the [[evt:17180|Collapse of the Eshueethie State]] split it five ways. A new [[pol:755|Tomözel]] took Ishwyithie, and beside it rose an empire of [[pol:756|Ana]], back on the map sixty-three years after Natlatl had annexed the old kingdom.

## Prophets, schools and the alphabet

These troubled years were also rich in new ideas. In 2006, at Niuzafammas in Veni, [[per:16030|Vethpemteth]] sat at the city gate and taught that the gods were servants of a single One. Her record of a debate with a priest there (PAL·SC·00118) is the founding text of the [[mov:51|Shenlamtanko]] faith. When the priest argued that defeat followed thin offerings, she answered that she had seen fat oxen led in before a lost battle: "If a god can be bought, priest, that god is a merchant." In 2009 came the [[evt:16124|Conversion of Lininade to Mvivituthpo]], when [[pol:696|Lininade]] adopted the cult of [[mov:31|Mvivituthpo]], already eleven centuries old. In 2038 [[per:16292|Nislo'nide]] founded the [[mov:52|Ya'e'use]] cult at Miviwki.

Philosophical schools followed. [[per:16685|Odyiyshan]] founded the [[mov:53|Vishdetanngom]] school at Nivanga in 2085, and [[per:16766|Nachügü]] the [[mov:54|Güginidü]] school at Athnambi in 2099. Navande founded Mbumbasheli at Shyih in 2108, and Nambazhathu founded Tsiutuzhu at Dadunde in 2148. At their height each of these schools would count between 15 and 31 million followers. The largest movement of the age began in 2166, in the depth of the cold years, when Ihishe, an ordinary man at a threshing floor, founded the I'ishiu faith at Tivintukhe. His sermon (PAL·SC·00119), which gives his name and the town's in another tongue, says he was called at noon under a sky "dark although there was no cloud." It teaches that release from the wheel of lives comes through mercy, not sacrifice: "Of every ten measures of grain in your storehouse, set aside one for the widow and the stranger." I'ishiu would one day count some 174 million believers. In 2120, though, the land still belonged to the old pantheons, with Jinru and La'im each holding about 14 per cent of it.

Writing changed faster than belief. Alphabetic writing was invented independently five times in less than a century: at Nivanga in 2086, the year after Odyiyshan founded his school there; at Iti in 2111; at Jenontono in 2162; at Nimit in 2168; and in Ana in 2173. Iti adopted the Tlixmax script in 2138 and Yiotl the Pieshayestlitl in 2151. Seventy-nine sound changes are dated to the period, and in 2199 Ithim split off from Shimim. The drift shows in the sources themselves: a Turamtu king writes "Shevunuvumalen" for Shefnofmalen, Nakathash spells her enemy "Napiningi," and the priest of the veiled sun calls his city Tallen.

## The veiled sun (AY 2140–2199)

The cold began before the eruption. The record dates the onset of a long cold spell to 2140, and in 2145 a famine in Khishkhih killed or drove away 398,127 people. Then, in 2162, the mountain blew. The average price of grain rose from 18.8 grams of silver in the 2150s to 28.8 grams in the 2160s, and at Dagafu it rose from 22.9 to 74.6. The Burning Plague of 2163 killed about 889,000 people. Famines struck Tamthal in 2161, and Luthe and Dyimbi in 2169. Luthe, under its queen [[per:17119|Namumbiv]] the Old, lost three-quarters of a million subjects in that decade. More than nineteen in twenty of the period's famine losses came after the cold set in.

The wars went on regardless. In the [[evt:17348|Third Turamtu–Yennga War]] (2155–62), [[pol:670|Turamtu]] invaded Yennga's lands in [[reg:8|Diebeguvebenga]]. After his victory at Yivinnimkhu in 2159 the Turamtu king Vikhminin wrote home (PAL·LT·01662) that "twenty thousand of them lay on the plain," and that [[per:17003|Shefnofmalen I]] of Yennga "lay among his dead... His spear is in my tent." For a royal boast this is close to the truth: the record gives 17,956 Yennga dead and confirms the king's death. But Vikhminin himself fell in battle the next year. In 2162 the dead king's son, [[per:17366|Shefnofmalen II]], won back the field and made Turamtu pay 35,009 kg of silver. Yennga won the war, but it lost 47,669 men, nearly twice Turamtu's toll.

Yeda reached its height of 9.1 million people in the 2150s under [[per:17082|Ololomen VI]] the Great, and it lost some 700,000 of them in the cold decade. The priest of the veiled sun says barley sold "for near two Kerel" the measure. That is about 17 grams of silver, and it matches the record's 16.7 grams at Taulin in the 2170s, up from 10.8 twenty years before. Then, in 2180, everything struck at once. Ololomen VI died. The [[evt:17598|Onset of the Great Drought in Daadüdhih (AY 2180)]] struck 428 districts, more of them in Yeda than in any other state. A temple record at Ndulamlule (PAL·RE·00076) says "the wells ran dry." Unhatlmum revolted the same year, and its rebels lost 32 men in five years while Yeda lost 34,795 putting them down. Three rulers followed in nineteen years, and the measure of order fell to 0.08. In 2199 came the [[evt:17760|Collapse of the Yeda State]]. Of its six heirs, [[pol:777|Tallen]] took Taulin itself, while [[pol:779|Yide]] and [[pol:778|Nelenga]] took other shares. To the east, [[per:16942|Navavun]] the Great (2122–89) carried Kazhe from 214 districts to 308 and sacked the city of Nilu in 2177. His death was followed by a war over the succession.

## Heirs and the White Death (AY 2199–2250)

The heirs of Yeda fought over the body almost at once. In the [[evt:17813|First Tallen–Yide War]] (2202–15), fought below Peyekhjeda in [[reg:16|Yidhe]], a camp scribe wrote to Tallen's young king in 2210 (PAL·LT·01663). He reported the enemy dead, counted "by the right hands," at five thousand seven hundred and Tallen's at 2,600; the record gives 5,748 and 2,627. He still calls the enemy Yeda, as if the old name were the prize, and he warns that "a soldier unpaid is a soldier who looks toward home." Tallen's own chronicle of the war survives as a single quoted sentence (PAL·CH·03303). After thirteen years and some 70,000 dead, the Treaty of Binge restored the old frontiers.

In the villages the cold years went on. A farmer in Nukumuku kept a Diary of a Hard Year (PAL·DI·00009) through the [[evt:17774|Famine of AY 2201 in Nuekhmokh]], which took 49,692 people, a quarter of the district, by death or flight. He describes a second sowing from the common store and tally sticks hung at the store door: "Neighbours who have known each other all their lives now watch each other's sacks."

Kazhe reached its greatest extent and 6.7 million people in the 2210s, but its measure of order had fallen to 0.05. In 2216 the [[evt:17983|Collapse of the Kazhe State]] left six heirs, among them [[pol:793|Nambe]] and the kingdom of [[pol:792|Khishkhizi]], a name readers in Khishkhis will know. Two years later came the worst blow of the period. [[evt:17909|The White Death]] broke out at Myizinithie in 2218, spread to 45 more cities and killed about 1,955,000 people, more than a third of those it reached. Its best witness is Mashe Ada, queen of Tomözel. In a letter to the merchants of Ishwyithie (PAL·LT·01699) she ordered the river gate shut and debts held over, so that "no man shall be sold for a debt in the year of the white sickness." Grain was to be sold "at the old weight." Then she admitted, "I am the queen, and I do not know." A temple record from the city (PAL·RE·00081) adds: "Those who nursed the sick fell sick in turn." Afterwards grain grew cheaper, not dearer, falling to an average of 14.4 grams in the 2220s, because there were fewer mouths to feed.

Yennga, the largest empire in the world, broke last. In 2220 it counted 10.1 million subjects, more than ever before, but Nodoruegeremo had seceded twice and the measure of order stood at 0.08. In 2221 the empire split into five, and a new [[pol:801|Yennga]] at Yinnge carried the name on. Commerce outlasted the empires: 114 merchant houses were founded in the period, and in 2246 Mii defaulted on 13,924 kg of silver owed to the House of Mitlta.

A word on the evidence. Of the 804 documents that survive from these years, 327 are inscriptions, mostly royal stones that multiply the enemy dead. Court annals choose what they record: Pivish's annalist mentions two of his kingdom's ten battles (PAL·AN·00338), and Pibetie's five of twenty-four (PAL·AN·00348). The most honest voices are a queen counting her dead, a poet mourning a lost army, a scribe begging for pay and a farmer keeping a tally.

Of the great powers of AY 2000, only Luthe and Nilu still stood in 2250, and they were at war. Under [[per:17783|Nangatsu]], Luthe had been struck again and again by the new droughts, in Dyimbi, Ng'jinaguh and Ayshi. In 2247 Nilu struck, and the [[evt:18351|First Nilu–Luthe War]] began on Luthe's own ground in [[reg:27|Luthe]]. Nangatsu's stele for his victory at Kavu in 2251 (PAL·IN·01338) claims 55,600 enemy dead, three and a half times the record's 15,798. But it was Nilu that prevailed. That war, and the new empires of Tallen, Ana, Tomözel and the second Yennga, belong to the next chapter.
