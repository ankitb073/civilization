*[Copied on parchment for the palace archive; a rubric states that the queen spoke these words standing, before the council, the household and the ordained of the new faith.]*

Lords of the council, captains of the guard, women of my household, and you, the ordained, who have waited at the doors of this hall longer than I have deserved:

It is the custom of rulers, when they change their minds, to pretend that they have not. I will not do so. I was raised in the teaching of [[mov:25|Miyithuyishiu]], and I do not repent of what it taught me. It taught me justice in dealings, and I have tried to deal justly; it taught me moderation, and I have not always been moderate. It taught that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I have watched the wise withdraw, and I have watched the kingdom go on without them. That is the first thing I wish to say to you: the old teaching was not false. It was small. It made good men quiet, and left the suffering where it lay.

Consider what we have done in that teaching's name, and judge whether it was moderate. Eighteen years ago this court sent its young men out in the [[evt:24938|Holy War of Mane against Ini]], and they came home with nothing, neither victory nor peace, the war simply dissolving like salt in rain. Three years ago we went to war again, against Tsiy, for land; you know that it is the [[evt:25127|Mane–Tsiy War]], and you know that it is not ended.

You know too what happened at Chezhietle, and I will not make you say it, for I will say it myself. In the second year of that war our captains met the army of Tsiy at the [[evt:25136|Battle of Chezhietle]], and were turned back. This summer I went there myself. I stood at the head of the host at the second [[evt:25149|Battle of Chezhietle]], and the field was lost, and our men lay down there in numbers I cannot speak without shame. I rode back into [[set:13|Tsivintuha]] behind the wagons of the wounded. I counted them at the gate, as long as I could bear to count.

Here, my lords, is a question the philosophers of the old school never answered for me: what is a ruler to do with the suffering she herself has caused? The old teaching said, be moderate, withdraw. But I cannot withdraw from a widow. I cannot be moderate toward a boy with no hands.

The teachers of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] gave me a different answer. They told me that there is one power above every power, and that the gods we served are its servants or its shadows. They told me that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering, and that this word is not for one people but for all peoples, for the men of Tsiy also. I did not believe them at first. Then I went into the streets of this city and found that the people had believed them long before me. In the wards by the river, in the potters' quarter, among the wives of the very soldiers I had led, the sign of the new faith was on more doors than the old. A queen who will not follow her people where they have already gone is not leading; she is merely late.

Therefore, today, before you all, I, [[per:24322|Huhhimin]], take up the faith of I'ishiu, and with me the crown and this court. This is the [[evt:25221|Conversion of Mane to I'ishiu]]. The ordained will enter the household. The old shrines will not be broken, for their gods, we are taught, are servants still, and a servant is owed his wage. Let the treasury pay the [[cur:540|Makh]] owed to the old priests as before, and let no one in [[pol:987|Mane]] be beaten for the gods he keeps.

I make no promise to you that this will win the war. It may not. The wheel turns, both teachings say so, and what ends will begin again. But I would rather the wheel find us compassionate than find us moderate and cold.

Ordained ones, come forward. I am ready.

*[Rubric:]* Spoken in the hall of Tsivintuha, in the year 658 of the I'ishiu.
