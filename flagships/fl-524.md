*[A hymn in five strophes with a closing refrain, copied for the use of a choir; the rubric at its head names the occasion.]*

*For the first return of the day on which [[per:26840|Nadigi]] taught in the burnt market of [[set:227|Nzhinduvi]]; to be sung at dawn, without instruments.*

**I.**

Sing, city, though your voice is hoarse with weeping;  
sing, though the walls you sing from are new mud over old stone.  
Five summers back you were a city of merchants,  
and every merchant weighed his worth in [[cur:532|Dudvu]],  
and every temple counted its gifts by the scale.  
You trusted the scale. The scale did not save you.

**II.**

Remember the year of the first oath, the [[evt:27114|Treaty of Nzhinduh]],  
the fields we gave, the tribute we promised, year upon year.  
Remember that same year, when other strangers came against us,  
the [[evt:27116|Second Battle of Dyizhih]], where our few stood against their many  
and were broken. Remember how those strangers came again, two summers on,  
the [[evt:27126|Battle of Nzhinduh]] at our own gate,  
and after it the night of the [[evt:27127|Sack of Nzhinduh]], when the market burned,  
and then the second treaty, and the second tribute laid on the first.  
[[per:26698|Navavun]] set his seal to it with a steady hand,  
because a ruler must sign what a city cannot refuse.  
Let no one blame him. Let every one remember.

**III.**

Then, two summers after, while the market still lay in ash,  
one man went and sat down in the ash.  
He did not curse the strangers. He did not curse the gods.  
He said: Hear me, you who have lost your silver.  
There is one Power above all powers, and it was never in the scale.  
What the strangers carried away was never yours to keep;  
what they could not carry is the only wealth.  
The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite,  
not because wealth is wicked, but because it is easily stolen.

**IV.**

So [[mov:104|Vyiminambi]] began, in a burnt place, among the poor.  
It asks of us duty, and order, and right sacrifice;  
not the bull with gilded horns, but the day's work done,  
the debt paid, the neighbour fed, the oath kept.  
It says little of how the world began or how it ends;  
it says much of how a man should stand in the middle.  
And it is not for this city only. Nadigi said:  
the stranger who burned the market may hear this also.  
That was the hardest saying, and the people murmured.  
But the murmuring has turned to singing.

**V.**

Do not think, O city, that we have forgotten the gods of our fathers.  
The priests of [[mov:38|Zhivintsizu]] still keep the altars on the hill;  
they still ask for justice in dealings and for moderation in all things,  
and we still bring them bread at the feasts.  
Many powers share the world, and the one Power does not grudge them their places.  
Only, we no longer weigh the gods in Dudvu,  
nor think a full storehouse proves the favour of heaven.  
A city that has been emptied learns what it can live without.

*Refrain, sung three times:*

> Empty hands can hold the light.  
> Empty hands can hold each other.  
> What the strangers took, let them keep;  
> what they left us, no one can take.

*Copied in the 9th year of Navavun, for the choir of the burnt market.*
