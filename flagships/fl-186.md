*Copied in the year 5344 from the founding of [[set:9|Dagafu]]. The author did not sign it, being fond of his neck.*

**I.**

God save our Lady [[per:42478|Nakhange]], whose name in the old tongue means Night!  
They crowned her at six years old, and she has been playing at soldiers ever since.  
Kneel, barons, and render your service; the Book bids us honour the crown,  
and I honour it, sirs, I honour it: I only count what it costs.

**II.**

Hear of the war we made on our neighbour, a little realm and a proud one.  
At [[evt:43074|Mbambanuduyih]] we went out near thirty to one,  
and we won, God be thanked, and their captain lay dead on the field;  
and our own dead filled the ditches, but the bells were rung for the living.

At [[evt:43082|Mbambanuduyih]] once more, with twice the host, we won again.  
Then at [[evt:43107|Gandagi]] the Lady herself put on her gilded helmet:  
three hundred thousand of us went out to catch seven thousand of them,  
and forty-four thousand of ours came home in the carts, and she called it a triumph.

*Refrain:* Ring the bells in Dagafu! The Night has won the day!  
Ring for the forty-four thousand: they cannot hear you anyway.

**III.**

At [[evt:43121|Dambüzhi]] it ended. They knelt; we let them up.  
By [[evt:43122|the peace of Dambüzhi]] they gave us a town and a province,  
an indemnity in silver, their homage, their markets, fifteen years of quiet;  
and the indemnity came to less, a clerk tells me, than the funerals.

**IV.**

The teachers of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] say: in all things be moderate.  
Moderate in meat, they mean, in wine, in anger, and in love.  
They did not think to say "in armies," the holy men being unworldly;  
our Lady, being worldly, has interpreted the text.

They teach that each may find the truth by his own inner light.  
My inner light, I confess, is a candle and a slate:  
I add the dead at Gandagi to the dead at Mbambanuduyih,  
and the slate is too small, and the candle gutters.

**V.**

And now, God save us, [[evt:43207|the Fuvu war]], the third of that name.  
At [[evt:43219|Paagundi]] our Lady won, as she always wins;  
at [[evt:43227|Khambive]] her captain won, and lost forty thousand doing it;  
and at [[evt:43228|the sacking of Khambive]] they burned the town's old records.

Why burn the records, Lady? Did they keep a count of us?  
A thrifty queen should keep such books; they are cheaper than men.  
Bread is cheap in Dagafu, a loaf for a crumb of a [[cur:923|Ngathe]];  
there are fewer mouths at table, and the bakers thank the Lady.

*Refrain:* Ring the bells in Dagafu! The Night has won the day!  
Ring for the men at Khambive: they cannot hear you anyway.

**VI.**

The little prince [[per:42522|Nashinzhè]] is ten, and plays with wooden soldiers.  
God grant him long to play with wood, and late to play with ours.  
Prosperity is the sign of divine favour, the preachers say;  
then God must love the coffin-makers best of all [[pol:1670|Dagafu]].

**Envoi**

Lady, I am your loyal man; my father served your house;  
I pay my dues at the quarter-days, I kneel when the banners pass.  
I only ask, when you go out to win your next great victory,  
take a smaller army, Lady. You will win it all the same.
