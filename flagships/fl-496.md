To my brother, the son of my father, the son of my mother, say:  
Thus says [[per:8822|Naguvu]], king of [[pol:178|Puvu]], who has no house, who sleeps in the house of a herdsman.

May [[dei:48|Kudze]] of the high sky keep you. May [[dei:53|Bine]] of the hearth keep a fire in your house, for there is none in mine.

Hear me, my brother. Hear it from my mouth, not from the market. The great house of Puvu is broken. Our father's seat is broken. The empire of our fathers' fathers is broken into six pieces, like a jar that falls from the roof. This is the [[evt:8564|Collapse of the Puvu State]]. I have seen it. I am in it. I am the potsherd that is left.

Hear how it was done, and who did it.

The governor of [[pol:378|Kambive]] ate at my table at the feast of the new year. He poured oil with me before [[dei:51|Talu]] of the earth. In the next moon he shut his gate and called himself king.  
The men of [[pol:379|Pievi]] said: "We are not Puvu." They said it in the gate. They said it to my messenger, and sent him back without his beard.  
The lord of [[pol:380|Kankushe]] kept the tribute in his own storehouse. He kept the [[cur:115|Ngüzhin]] in his own chest. He said: "The road is not safe." The road was safe enough for his own carts.  
[[pol:381|Kashuntsi]] went the same way. [[pol:382|Düvnutside]] went the same way. Each man took his piece and ran into his own house and shut the door.

And [[set:12|Kabave]], my own city, the city of the throne, where our father sat and judged: Kabave too shut its gate. The elders stood on the wall. They said: "Go away, king. We will keep our city for ourselves." Now it is [[pol:377|Kabave]], and not Puvu. My own city. My own wall. They did not throw stones. They only shut the gate.

Do not say I was a bad king. Do not let them say it. I gave the gods their due. I poured to [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] of the spear. I poured to Kudze. I poured to [[dei:49|Tuve]] under the earth, that the dead might not be angry. I kept the order of [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]], each god in the god's own place. I did not take a man's field. I did not take a man's wife. Six years I have sat in the seat, and no one went hungry; even now a measure of barley costs less than one Ngüzhin. Let them say whether I lie.

No. This was not my doing. This was the doing of the gods, who turned their face, and of men who smiled at me and sharpened the knife behind their backs.

And it began long before me, my brother. You know the old songs. You know how the spears of Puvu went out to Shimbayi, once in the days of our great-grandfathers and again before our father took the seat, and were broken there. You know the [[evt:8174|Battle of Shimbayi]] and the [[evt:8178|Battle of Hil]] the year after, where the queen of that day led out the spears of Puvu and left thousands of them on the field. From those days the great house was like a tree with a crack in it. The rain went in. The wood went soft. It stood a long time. Then the wind came, and it did not stand.

And when the seat changed hands, and changed again, and our father took it from those who could not hold it, the governors learned a lesson: the seat of Puvu is a thing that may be taken. They have learned it well. They have taken it, each a piece.

Now hear what I ask.

Do not come to me. The roads are full of men who would sell my head to a new king for a sack of barley. Stay where you are. Keep your spearmen near you.

If the wheel turns, as the priests say it turns; if what ends begins again: then send for me. I will come. Until then I sit in a herdsman's house and count the goats, and the goats do not rebel.

Written in the sixth year of Naguvu, in the hills, by a scribe who is paid in bread.
