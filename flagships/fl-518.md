[[per:24211|Dafdovan]], a seeker, to the friends in [[set:10|Navnge]] who have asked me by letter and by messenger what it was that I saw at [[set:157|Pyikhside]]: grace to you, and light, and the patience to read to the end.

You ask whether I have founded a new worship. I have founded nothing; I have only stopped walking in a circle. But since men will give a name to whatever gathers, and since already they call those who listen to me by the name of [[mov:88|Enethnothdon]], I will tell you plainly what we hold, so that no one may tell you falsely.

Begin where I began, with the city. You remember the year of [[evt:24304|the sack]]. You remember the smoke over the granaries, the temple doors broken, the priests of [[mov:2|Sinru]] standing in the square with their hands empty, saying, "The wheel turns; what ends will begin again." And then, in that year and the next, the wheel turned indeed: the enemy at Binge, the enemy before our own walls, the siege, and the king, our lord [[per:24022|Pansipavbukhin]], twice in two years setting his seal to a [[evt:24315|treaty]] he did not write. I do not reproach him. He fought at the walls; I only watched from them.

But I asked myself, in those years, the question that every honest citizen of [[pol:1044|Navnge]] has asked in the night. If all things turn upon a wheel, and what ends must begin again, then the sack will begin again, and the siege will begin again, and our children will stand where we stood, and their children after them, for ever. Is that a teaching, or a sentence?

At Pyikhside, in the spring of this year, sitting alone outside the town, I was given an answer. I will not dress it in fire and voices; it came quietly, as reason comes.

Time is not a wheel. It is a road. It runs from the making of the world to a final judgement, and it does not run twice. What is done on it is done once, and will be weighed.

The gods are not many masters, each claiming his due. There is one divine power, and the gods our fathers named, [[dei:14|Vvumi]] of the spear and [[dei:15|Vinbe]] of the earth and [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky and the rest, are its servants, or the shadows it casts upon the world. I do not ask you to curse them. A servant is not cursed for being a servant.

The truth is not the property of an ordained line. Each seeker may find it by the inner light and by reason, which are the same light seen from two sides. I honour the priests who taught me my letters. But I will not send you to them for what you can find in yourselves.

What does the one power ask? Not bulls on the altar, but justice in dealings and moderation in all things. A merchant who weighs his [[cur:508|Bedu]] honestly offers a better sacrifice than one who burns a fat ox and shaves the coin.

And here the old faith and the new agree in one thing: prosperity is a sign of divine favour. But read the sign rightly. The favour is not bought at the altar; it follows justice as the harvest follows rain. A city that deals justly will prosper, in time, though it be sacked; a city that deals unjustly will fall, in time, though it sack others.

Last, and hardest. This message is not for Navnge alone. It is for all peoples, and therefore also for those who broke our gates. I know what I ask. I ask it of myself first.

Do not quarrel in the market over these things. Do not tear down any altar. Meet in each other's houses, read this letter, and test it by your own light. If it does not shine, put it away. If it does, come to Pyikhside in the summer, and we will speak further.

Farewell. Deal justly; remember that the road runs one way.

Written at Pyikhside, in the year 2996 from the founding of Navnge.
