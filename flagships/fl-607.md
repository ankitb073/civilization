title: Ue'o'ol of Tlayiyan: the conqueror and the farmer's fields

In AY 1968, in the fifty-third year of his reign, the king of Tlayiyan heard a petition at court. The petitioner was Is'anim, son of Masa, a man of Niz. Seven years earlier, under a treaty, his father's fields had passed with part of Niz to Tlayiyan. He asked for them back. The steward of the royal lands answered that the land was the crown's, and that the plaintiff had not sworn allegiance within the year. The judgment let Is'anim keep his 165 measures of sown land as a tenant of the crown, paying rent at every harvest. The clay record of that sitting is one of the last documents of [[per:14974|Ue'o'ol]]'s life. It is also the only one in which we see him face to face with someone his wars had touched. Tradition calls him the Conqueror. The lawsuit shows what conquest meant to the conquered.

## A queen's son

He was born in 1900, a Lizo prince of the Yalni Shimen house, which had held the throne of [[pol:555|Tlayiyan]] since 1764. His mother, [[per:14937|O'e Lieze]], came to the throne in 1909 and reigned seven years. His father, who appears in the record without a name, never ruled and outlived her by twenty-one years. An elder sister had died at ten, the year before he was born. The [[evt:15111|Accession of Ue'o'ol]] in 1916, on his mother's death, made him king at sixteen. The record names no regent. He ruled an empire of some three and a half million people from Tliyiya.

Within a year he was at war. The [[evt:15131|Second Tlayiyan–Mikhuthitsu War]] was a war of conquest against the young kingdom of [[pol:668|Mikhuthitsu]], ruled by the aged [[per:14954|Pavudyiyshan]], whom later ages called the Unfortunate. In 1920 the twenty-year-old king commanded at the [[evt:15209|Battle of Zhaalakhazhash (AY 1920)]], with fourteen thousand men against nine hundred, and won. His stele for the battle claims 1,400 enemy dead, more than the whole enemy army. The record gives 396, and Tlayiyan lost almost four times that number. The [[evt:15211|Conquest of Mikhuthitsu]] was signed at Lininadhe after a siege, and twenty-one districts became Tlayiyan's. The scribe of the defeated palace, writing two years later, closed his account with a line found in more than one losing chronicle: "Let this record keep what the sword has taken." The king's younger sister died that same year, at fourteen.

## Forty years of small wars

Ue'o'ol's wars followed a single pattern for four decades. He chose small neighbours, outnumbered them many times over, won at a high price and took tribute. In the [[evt:15255|Tlayiyan–Bayitl War]] of 1926–1929 he attacked the city-state of [[pol:653|Bayitl]], whose ruler [[per:15179|Yi Foyatl I]] had seized power only that year. The Tlayiyan account of the war, written in 1931, has Yi Foyatl gathering an army "against Tlayiyan" and more than doubles the enemy's numbers. The Bayitl account, written half a century later, has Ue'o'ol "swollen with pride", which is nearer the truth. The [[evt:15287|Treaty of Biyitl (AY 1929)]] took five districts and made Bayitl a vassal.

The [[evt:15369|Tlayiyan–Nibanga War]] of 1937–1939 was fought against a queen, [[per:15046|Linlabolath]] of Nibanga, three years his elder, who had reigned since 1917. He beat her at Lininadhe in 1939. The [[evt:15401|Treaty of Lininadhe (AY 1939)]] took fourteen districts and the town of Kivunge, imposed a yearly tribute of nineteen talents of silver and made Nibanga "a servant of Tlayiyan". Nibanga's own account says only that "some villages of no account were given up", villages "which had always been poor". Linlabolath kept her throne for another thirteen years.

Between these wars the private record is bleak. In 1937 his father died, and so did his eldest child, a daughter of thirteen. His youngest son died in 1942, aged eight, and his brother in 1948.

The [[evt:15465|Tlayiyan–Nie'khonuesnon War]] of 1945–1951 was the longest of the reign. At Athtathe in 1946 he beat [[per:15294|Ine'ash II]], called the Brave, and his stele claims 7,200 dead where the record gives about 1,500. At the [[evt:15502|Battle of Toiyoe (AY 1947)]] Ine'ash was killed. The Tlayiyan account says that "his head was brought to the king". Another commander, Shi'amni, fought on and died at Nyikhunuisnun in 1951. Ine'ash's younger brother, [[per:15296|Yalni Shimen II]], signed the [[evt:15563|Treaty of Nyikhunuisnun (AY 1951)]], ceding five districts and a tribute. His court historian later wrote that "nothing of worth was lost".

## The boy king of Niz

His queen died in 1956. Two years later, at fifty-eight, Ue'o'ol began his last war. In the [[evt:15617|Fourth Tlayiyan–Nizh War]] the young king of [[pol:575|Nizh]], [[per:15396|Ia Imam I]], met him at the [[evt:15643|Battle of Litlitli (AY 1959)]] with 568 men against nearly 33,000. Ia Imam was killed at thirty-two, and once again the court account has the head brought to the king. His son, [[per:15552|Ia Imam II]], was eleven. In 1961, at thirteen, he put his name to the [[evt:15667|Treaty of Litlitli (AY 1961)]]: three districts, eleven years of tribute in silver, and Litlitli itself sacked. The treaty's text invokes "the gods whom Tlayiyan and Niz alike revere". Ia Imam II reigned for forty-two years and is remembered as the Old. He outlived Ue'o'ol by a generation, and Ue'o'ol's dynasty as well.

It was a former subject of Ia Imam who came to the king's court in 1968. The lawsuit of Is'anim is a small text, but it shows how the treaties worked. The court recognised that the land had changed hands, and it did not dispossess the farmer. It made him a tenant and charged him rent. A testament of the same years, sealed by Sho'a Luesh'a, a merchant of Ane, in the king's forty-seventh year, shows the kingdom from the other side: timber and spices in the warehouse, money for the temple, servants freed on the day of the merchant's death.

## The reckoning

Ue'o'ol died a natural death in 1969, aged sixty-nine. He had reigned fifty-three years. In that time Tlayiyan grew from some 236 districts to about 284, and from three and a half to more than four and a half million people. He won every battle he commanded, and in all but one he lost more men than the enemy. Across his five wars his armies lost three and a half times as many soldiers as all his enemies together. Most of his victims were his own soldiers. His stelae, offered in turn to a god and to a goddess, never mention them.

The succession went badly. His son [[per:15037|Esiesh Shimen]], one of twins born in 1926, succeeded at forty-three. In 1972, the year the capital was besieged, he was killed in battle. His twin brother reigned for ten years, and after him came a child under a regent. In 1984 a usurper ended the Yalni Shimen house, and in 1995 Tlayiyan broke into four states. One of them, Lininade, was named after the town where the young Ue'o'ol had signed his first conquest.
