## A common name

Of [[per:46349|Nangash]] the record keeps a frame and very little to hang in it. He was a man of the Khunilile, the people of [[pol:1638|Khishkhis]], born in AY 4849; he died a natural death in AY 4908, at fifty-nine. His name meant *river*. No father, mother, wife or child is entered against him; no school, trade, house or grave. He held one role, inventor, in one year, 4895.

The name itself is a trap. His century was full of men called Nangash. One, born in 4842, founded the Umbi Corporation at Adhizhili; another, born in 4857, founded the Khushimbi Corporation at Yizhi and kept its books. A merchant's testament of 4852 leaves steel "To Nangash, my faithful agent" (PAL·WL·01148), when our Nangash was three. Nothing ties the inventor to any of them, and I will not lend him a firm or a signature that belonged to someone else.

He grew up in a state that went often to war and always won. Khishkhis was a one-party state ruled from [[set:85|Nambi]], and its faith in every survey of his life was [[mov:70|Vimekhulu]], which taught that all things turn on a great wheel and that the good life is duty, order and right sacrifice. In his fifty-nine years it fought six wars and won all six, among them the [[evt:47081|Third Khishkhis–Khanji War]] of his youth. He was nineteen when [[per:46181|Nazhigi]] became chairman in 4868, thirty-three when his term ended. The survey of the 4850s counts 73,762,320 people in 452 districts; that of the 4890s, 104,300,584 in 498.

## The specification

He is visible through one printed page in his own name, *Patent of Nangash: A process for fixing the nitrogen of the air* (PAL·PT·00009), dated 4895, complete, and catalogued in the state archive of Khishkhis in 4898. It opens with the patent office's formula, which also heads a rifle patent of 4802 (PAL·PT·00008): "To all to whom these presents shall come". Then the man speaks: "I, Nangash, have invented a process by which the nitrogen of the air is combined under pressure into a salt fit for the fields". Two clauses follow: "At high pressure and in the presence of iron, the yield of the process is made sufficient for industry," and "A working model is deposited at the office."

That is all: no temperature, no figure, no drawing. The page fixes the claim and keeps the craft. The record calls it the [[evt:47351|invention of synthetic fertilizer in Khishkhis]], and I have checked every state: none had the process before 4895, and his is the only invention of it the record knows. Khanji, beaten in 4868, had it by 4900; Yiethithi by 4902; Fuvu by 4911. Of the states standing in the year I write, every one has it but the Kingdom of Bakh.

## Between declarations of war

The Nambi Clarion (PAL·NP·00729), dated "21 Mbivi 2437 of the Vimikhulu", gave him two sentences: "Nangash has demonstrated synthetic fertilizer before the minister. The inventor declined to explain the details." The weather was "cold, with frost at night"; grain stood at 2.14 [[cur:1109|Agni]] the measure. The minister is unnamed; the chairman that year was Napanjif.

The story crossed four frontiers almost word for word, perhaps from a common source; the record does not say. The Khavu Tribune of Luthe (PAL·NP·00738) and the Khavazhu Tribune (PAL·NP·00739) put the demonstration "before the academy"; the Shimbayi Clarion (PAL·NP·00740) kept "before the minister"; the Yiethithi Messenger (PAL·NP·00741) has "before a crowd of investors". Abroad he became "Nangashu", and in Yiethithi "Nankax". Two papers add, "Shares in rival firms fell." Rivals to whom? The record names no firm of his.

Every foreign paper printed him beside a war, three of them beside their own wars, just declared. The Yiethithi Messenger set him under its own bad news: "After a hard-fought engagement near Lisi our army has fallen back to prepared positions." That was the Battle of Lithi of 4895, in the [[evt:47389|Holy War of Khishkhis against Yiethithi]]. The record gives Khishkhis 581,002 men against 471,183, and losses of 14,478 against 26,718. The Khishkhis general's telegram put enemy losses at 35,500 (PAL·TG·00194); the state's chronicle of 4899 (PAL·CH·08271) counts 95,357 enemy dead and 4,772 of its own. Beside that arithmetic, a man who declined to explain the details looks almost scrupulous.

## What the salt did

Did his salt feed anyone? No document of the time says so, and the record measures it only sideways. Between the surveys of the 4800s and the 4890s, the people per district of Khishkhis rose by 4 to 9 per cent a decade. In the 4900s, the first full decade after the patent, they rose by 17 per cent, and by roughly 10 to 15 per cent a decade since. Conquest muddies the measure, and the record does not tie the rise to him; still, it is the strongest witness it holds.

Bread is a harder witness. At Nambi grain had grown cheaper for three decades before the patent, from about 13.9 grams of silver the measure in the 4850s to 6.4 in the 4880s. After it, the price climbed to 11.6 in the 4900s and 16.4 in the 4910s, before falling back. At Yizhi and Alunzhi grain stayed cheaper than in the 4850s. The Great Drought of Baan, which lay over some thirty of the empire's districts, ended only in 4899; holy war on Fuvu began in 4903. Whatever the process did for the fields, it did not make bread cheap in the capital.

He died in 4908, the year the [[evt:47507|Holy War of Khishkhis against Fuvu]] ended with twenty-four districts ceded at Khambe. No later word of his survives. A year after his death the Khushimbi books at Yizhi were still kept by a man called Nangash; not ours. Of the man named for a river the record keeps one page and five newspapers, and the page is silent where it matters most. The method he declined to explain is now in the fields of nearly every state on earth.
