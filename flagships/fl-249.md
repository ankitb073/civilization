title: Reason Within the Faith: The Historical Mamanen

[[per:28708|Mamanen]] is usually presented in one of two ways. In the first he is a rebel who set reason against the priests. In the second he is a solitary sage whose philosophy came from nowhere. The record supports neither. It shows a philosopher who taught in the capital of a young kingdom whose state faith was the Veshethmekh, who accepted much of that faith's picture of the world, and who argued about one question within it: who decides what is true.

## The record

Mamanen was born in AY 3132, a man of the Jego people. The record places the [[evt:29144|Founding of the Reronga School]] at Taulin in 3170, when he was about thirty-eight. Taulin was then the seat of the Kingdom of Tallen, which had broken away in 3159 in the Peme Civil War and had been independent for only eleven years. Its state faith was the Veshethmekh, and its king at the time was Lovdhan. Mamanen died of natural causes in AY 3190, at about fifty-eight, after some twenty years of teaching. The kingdom outlived him by only nineteen years.

## The teachings and their compiler

Mamanen is better served than most founders of schools. The Teachings of Mamanen (PAL·PH·00029), a parchment dated AY 3207, were compiled seventeen years after his death by the priest Talepavbokhen. Born in 3168, two years before the founding, he was twenty-two when Mamanen died and could have heard him. The heading claims only that the sayings were "set down after his death".

Two features of the collection deserve notice. The first is that its compiler was a priest. A school that locates truth in each seeker's inner light and reason was remembered, in its earliest text, by a member of the profession whose authority it questioned. That alone should make us cautious about the image of Mamanen the anticlerical rebel.

The second is that every one of its six lines has a parallel in older collections of philosophical teachings. "Everything that has a beginning has a cause; seek the first cause" already stands in the Teachings of Odyiyshan (PAL·PH·00002) of AY 2104. "There is no shame in wealth honestly got" appears in the Teachings of Shim'amtlü (PAL·PH·00007) of 2314. Like the sayings literature of the faiths, the philosophical tradition had its stock of formulae. The Teachings tell us which of them Mamanen's pupils thought fitted his school. They do not give us his voice.

## A philosopher of the state faith

The doctrine, as the record summarizes it, is best read beside the Veshethmekh. On the shape of the world the [[mov:111|Reronga]] agrees with it: time runs in a line from creation to a final judgement, and there is one divine power, with the old gods as its servants or shadows. On two points it parts company. The Veshethmekh holds that truth is kept by an ordained priesthood and passed down in order. The Reronga holds that each seeker may find it by inner light and reason. The Veshethmekh makes the good life one of compassion and release from suffering. The Reronga makes it one of duty, order and right sacrifice, and adds that prosperity is a sign of divine favour.

This is not rebellion. It is an argument conducted inside a shared cosmos, about authority and about conduct. It is the kind of argument a capable teacher in a young kingdom might make, where the state faith was established but the institutions of a new state were still forming. No edict against the school is recorded, and nothing suggests that Mamanen suffered for his views. The legend of persecution, where it appears, has no support in the record.

## Conclusion

The historical Mamanen, then, is a Jego teacher of Taulin who for twenty years argued, within the frame of the established faith, that truth belongs to reason and that a prosperous, dutiful life is a mark of divine favour. His pupils remembered him in the formulae of their tradition, through a priest who may have known him. The [[mov:111|Reronga]], which grew to some 37 million adherents at its height, inherited from him not a body of original sayings but a position in a debate. That position has proved durable, and it is a better monument to [[per:28708|Mamanen]] than either of the legends.
