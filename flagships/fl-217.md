title: Brothers at Zhiyiyzhikhti: The Kakhti Account of the Nuvi Civil War, Read Against the Record

The [[evt:3352|Nuvi Civil War]] (AY 715–718) was a war between brothers. The record makes this plain, even though the one chronicle we have does not. The rebel claimant Tsilath and the reigning king, Tathbaluthin the Ill-Starred, were both children of Queen Tsilath, who had ruled [[pol:129|Nuvi]] briefly in AY 695–696. Tathbaluthin, the elder, succeeded her as a boy, under a regent. Tsilath, the younger, broke away in AY 715 at the head of a kingdom of his own. My argument is that the Kakhti account conceals this kinship more thoroughly than it conceals any number, and that the concealment is its most informative choice.

## The tablet

The Kakhti account (PAL·CH·00517, Meta) was recovered in AY 4918 at Akhtse, the seat of the breakaway kingdom, and translated by Navaavun. Several of its leaves are missing. Pimtulukhnen dated it "in the 9th year of Kakhti", AY 723, five years after the suppression. He was a man in his thirties during the war. By AY 723 Tathbaluthin had died, and Nuvi itself was a year from collapse. No Nuvi account of the war has been recovered, so the record must serve as the second witness.

## Field by field

*Who began it.* The record dates the war from the [[evt:3351|Secession of Kakhti]] in AY 715, so the rebels began it. Pimtulukhnen writes: "Tathbaluthin of Nuvi, swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Kakhti. So [...] out to punish him." The lacuna is easily restored from the stock formula, "So the king went out to punish him." That formula concedes, almost against the scribe's intention, that it was Kakhti's king who took the field. More striking is what the sentence omits. A younger brother's claim against an elder is rewritten as one foreign king coveting another's land.

*The battles.* The record has three, all fought before Zhiyiyzhikhti, the Nuvi capital, and all won by Nuvi: the [[evt:3367|Battle of Zhiyiyzhikhti (AY 716)]], the [[evt:3377|Battle of Zhiyiyzhikhti (AY 717)]] and the [[evt:3381|Battle of Zhiyiyzhikhti (AY 718)]]. The tablet as we have it records the second and third as defeats, each with an excuse, and has nothing for AY 716. Here the archaeologist must be careful. With leaves missing, physical loss cannot be ruled out. The surviving entries run from the first year of Tsilath to his third without a break in the text, which favours deliberate omission, but I would not put it more strongly than that.

*The dead.* [[pol:142|Kakhti]] sent 20, 18 and 34 men against the capital. Nuvi met them with 333, 203 and 189. The record gives Kakhti 26 dead (8, 5 and 13) and Nuvi 108 (56, 27 and 25). Pimtulukhnen gives his own side 2 and 4 dead, and Nuvi 81 and 75, three times the record's 27 and 25 in both battles. The scale is small, but the method is the same as at any royal court.

*Commanders.* None was killed, and the tablet claims no deaths on either side.

*Outcome and land.* Nuvi won. The [[evt:3382|Suppression of the Kakhti Revolt]] in AY 718 annexed all twelve of Kakhti's districts in [[reg:19|Mana]]. Pimtulukhnen presents an honourable peace in which nothing that mattered changed hands. One fact in the record makes this less absurd than it sounds: Tsilath himself signed the suppression and lived until AY 735. He was not executed, as rebel leaders against foreign overlords often were.

## What kinship explains

Read with the family in view, the chronicle's choices make sense. A scribe at Akhtse could not present the war as a brother's bid for his brother's throne without conceding that the throne was Tathbaluthin's to lose and that Tsilath had lost the bid. Presenting it as a defensive war against a covetous neighbour preserved the dignity of a claimant who was still alive. It also explains why no head is brought to any king in this text. Nobody died at the top, and the peace was settled within one house.

What the record cannot tell us is how the brothers themselves regarded the war, or whether Tsilath's survival was mercy, policy or indifference. Kinship is a fact. Its feeling is not recoverable from a muster roll or a treaty.
