Thus says [[per:29051|Ai Mix]], king of [[pol:1259|Owa]], servant of the One, in the seventh year of his reign.

When my envoy came back from Tizha, I said to him: "Speak, and the scribe will press your words, so that the elders in [[set:153|Owa]] may hear how the peace was made, and so that no liar in the market may tell it otherwise." And my envoy spoke, and these are his words, and I have set my own words beside them where it was fitting.

**The envoy's words.**

"My lord, I went down to Tizha with twelve men and a white ox, on the third day after the new moon. An eagle flew up on the right hand as we set out, and the priests said: 'Go; the One goes before you.' The road was full of the signs of your victory. At every ford there were broken shields of Tizha. At every well the people bowed and would not look up.

"When I came before the wall, I saw the gate burned black from the sack of last year, and the new gate not yet hung. Your spearmen sat about the city in their camp, as they have sat since the spring. This is the [[evt:29542|Siege of Shish]]. No cart went in. No cart came out. The men on the wall were thin.

"They opened a small door and brought me in. I was taken to the hall of the king. On the king's seat sat a boy, [[per:29141|Asiüp]], son of the king who fell. His feet did not reach the floor. Beside him stood his mother, who holds the seat for him, and the elders of Tizha behind her.

"I said what you commanded me to say: 'The king of Owa does not want your city. He has had it once already. He wants your silver every year, and your road open to his merchants, and your spears never again turned toward Owa.'

"The elders murmured. One of them said: 'We have fought for three years. Shall we give in now?' I said: 'Your king fought, and he lies under the earth. Your army fought at Ninshinuesnun this very spring, and where is it now?' And they were silent, my lord. For the [[evt:29541|Second Battle of Ninshinuesnun]] had broken them, and their dead still lay unburied in the fields.

"Then the mother of the boy spoke. She said: 'Let it be done. Let my son live and keep his seat, and we will pay.' So we sat, and we counted. They will pay [[cur:681|Tlani]] of silver each year at the harvest; the weights are written on the second tablet. They will open the road. They will send no spears against Owa while the boy lives or after.

"On the seventh day the boy came out to the gate with his mother, and you came up from the camp, my lord, and he put his hand to the clay, and you put your hand to the clay. This is the [[evt:29543|Treaty of Shish]]."

**The king's words.**

So my envoy spoke, and he spoke truly, for I saw the last of it with my own eyes.

Let the elders of Owa hear also what my envoy was too modest to say. The men of [[pol:1271|Tizha]] brought this war upon themselves with insults. They mocked the One and the justice of the One. They thought their walls were high. At the [[evt:29525|Battle of Shish]] last year their king came out with his whole strength against us, and he fell, and the ditches of Tizha were filled with his men, more than the scribes could count. Then their city was opened, in the [[evt:29526|Seventh Sack of Shish]]. Seven times now that city has been opened by strangers. Will its elders never learn?

I did not take the boy's head. I did not burn the house of their gods. The One asks for justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and I have been moderate. Let the priests of [[mov:36|Mitoaiy]] say whether I have been just. The silver of Tizha will come to Owa every harvest, and prosperity is the sign of the One's favour. Let any man who doubts that favour look at the carts on the road.

So ends the [[evt:29497|Owa–Tizha War]]. I have written it. Let it stand.
