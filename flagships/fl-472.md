**SPECIAL EDITION. — THE REPUBLIC PROCLAIMED. — FULL TEXT OF THE ADDRESS.**

*[[set:136|Mbilu]], 3 Ndazhe 4706 of the Uthungala. — At four o'clock this afternoon Citizen [[per:45529|Navande]] appeared on the steps of the old palace before a crowd which our reporters estimate filled the square and every street leading into it. The following is a verbatim account taken down by our shorthand writers.*

Citizens! (*Loud and prolonged cheering.*)

Citizens of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]]: for the first time in the history of this nation, a man may stand on these steps and call you by that name, and no guard will drag him down for it. (*Cheers.*)

This morning, at seven o'clock, the delegates of the districts, of the workshops and of the regiments informed [[per:45281|Nashièmbangu]] that the nation no longer required his services. (*Laughter and cheers.*) He received the message in his study. He did not resist. I will say this for him, and you will permit me to say it: he heard the sentence of his deposition read aloud without a word of complaint, and he asked only that his household be treated with decency. It will be. (*A voice: "Better than he treated us!"*) Yes, friend, better than he treated us; that is the whole point. (*Cheers.*)

Let no one say that this is a riot, or a mutiny, or the work of a faction. The [[evt:46232|Mbilu Revolution]] is the work of the whole people, and it was accomplished, I am proud to tell you, with fewer broken windows than a feast-day. (*Laughter.*)

Now hear me on three matters.

**First: the soldiers.** Two years ago our army won the [[evt:46227|Treaty of Dalithimi]] after holding the field at Dalithimi twice over. The palace newspapers gave the glory to the king. I give it where it belongs: to the men in the trenches, to the carters who brought up their shells, and to the women who made the shells. (*Great cheering.*) The Republic will not forget them. Every man who served at Dalithimi shall have his pension doubled, and paid in good [[cur:926|Dathu]], not in promises. (*Cheers.*)

**Second: bread.** I know the price of a measure of grain in this city. It stands above two Dathu, and wages have not followed it. Under the old system the granaries answered to the palace. From tomorrow they will answer to a commission of the districts, and the books will be printed in the newspapers every week so that any citizen may read them. (*Cheers.*) Justice in dealings, moderation in all things: these are not my words. You learned them as children, in the teaching of [[mov:24|Uthungala]], and so did I. (*"Hear, hear!"*)

**Third: the constitution.** No one has made me king, and no one will. (*Prolonged cheering.*) The delegates have asked me to serve as provisional President until a National Assembly can be elected. I have accepted, for so long as the Assembly wishes and not one day longer. Within sixty days, every householder in Mbilu shall vote. (*Cheers.*) The Assembly will write the law. I will obey it. And if I do not, you know the way to these steps. (*Laughter and cheers.*)

Citizens: our faith teaches that time runs in a line, from creation to the final judgement, and does not turn back on itself. I believe it. The age of kings in Mbilu is behind us on that line, and we shall not walk backwards to it. (*Tremendous cheering.*) Each of you may find the truth by your own inner light and by your own reason. Today, a whole people found the same truth on the same morning. That is what a republic is.

Go home now. Keep order. Open the shops tomorrow at the usual hour. Let the foreign correspondents write to every capital that Mbilu changed its government without changing its manners. (*Laughter.*)

Long live the Republic! (*The crowd took up the cry, and the singing continued for some time after the speaker had withdrawn.*)
