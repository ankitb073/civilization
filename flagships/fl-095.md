In the 26th year of [[per:19213|Vavukhin]], king of [[pol:773|Tunnga]], in the month of the barley harvest, [[dei:138|Tuel]] the moon rose dark as a bruise over [[set:151|Nuthuruekhrim]], and the dogs howled all night. The priests of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]] said: "The gods are hungry. Give more." In the morning a soldier came home from Nidhinge with his arm bound up, and he found me at the well, and he asked me questions. I, [[per:19520|Tuethuyin]], have written his words and mine, as near as I remember them.

**The soldier said:** Teacher, last year I went out to Nidhinge, and we were broken there; [[evt:19595|the first battle]] was the enemy's. This year I went out again to the same field, and in [[evt:19603|the second battle]] the enemy was broken. Both years I poured beer to [[dei:136|Dueminu]] of the storm. Both years men died at my side. Tell me: in which year did the god love us?

**Tuethuyin said:** In neither year, and in both. Dueminu does not love and does not hate. He is a servant, as the king's runner is a servant: he carries what he is given. Above the storm, above the moon, above the grain and the river and the dark house of [[dei:137|Shyethartsi]], there is one Power, and the gods stand before it as stewards stand before a king.

**The soldier said:** The priests say the good life is duty and right sacrifice. I did my duty. I stood in the line. I killed the men in front of me. Is that not the good life?

**Tuethuyin said:** You did what the line asked. I do not blame you. But hear me. Duty is a rope; it binds a man to his place, and that is something. Compassion is a lamp; it shows him the man in front of him. The man you killed at Nidhinge had a mother at a well like this one. Did your duty ease her suffering, or yours?

**The soldier said:** Neither. My arm hurts, and I cannot sleep.

**Tuethuyin said:** Then that is where the teaching begins. The good life is to lessen suffering, yours and the other's, until a man is released from it. I learned this when I was fourteen years old, and saw [[set:129|Nuwtennga]] taken in the [[evt:19275|Sixth Sack of Nuwkennga]]. The priests poured to every god in the city, and still the gates were broken. I saw a woman of our enemies give water to one of our children in the street. Her gods were not my gods. Her mercy was the same mercy.

**The soldier said:** Then your teaching is for our enemies also?

**Tuethuyin said:** For every people under the sky. Is the Power smaller than the border of Tunnga? When the king set his seal to the [[evt:19591|Treaty of Zhinlampive]], the men of that country paid him silver; they did not stop being men.

**The soldier said:** Be careful, teacher. The king won that war at [[evt:19590|Zhinlampive]], and the king is generous.

**Tuethuyin said:** May the king live long. I do not speak against the silver. The silver of Zhinlampive was weighed out in [[cur:408|tarmi]] in the treasury, and the scribes counted it. I say only that the tears of Zhinlampive are not weighed, and someone must count those too.

**The soldier said:** And who will keep this teaching when you are dead? You are one man.

**Tuethuyin said:** I will lay my hands on those who learn it well, and they will lay theirs on others, in order, from hand to hand, so that the word is not bent in the passing. A teaching without keepers is water poured on sand.

**The soldier said:** Then lay your hand on me, though my hand is not good for much.

So I laid my hand on him, and he was the first. On that day, at Nuthuruekhrim, the way of [[mov:67|Dankhüthil]] was opened to all who would come, and the people who stood at the well were witnesses.

Written in the 26th year of Vavukhin, at Nuthuruekhrim, in the year of the [[evt:19656|Founding of the Dankhüdhil Faith]].
