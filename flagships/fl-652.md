title: The Man Who Left on Time: Nandune and the First Merchant Republic

In AY 2103 the ruler of [[pol:720|Dunjènile]] gave up power for no better reason than that his term had run out. Of the 486 elections in the record, this is the first. The man who stepped down, [[per:16712|Nandune]], had twelve years earlier turned his city-state into a merchant republic, the first state of that kind the record knows. The next would not appear until AY 2240. This series counts him among founders, though he founded no faith. What he founded was a way of governing. Yet no surviving document names him, and his life must be read from the record and from his successors' stones.

## A failing kingdom

Nandune was born in AY 2049, a Khufiènji of the old Kingdom of Dunjènile. The record names no parents, wife or children, and it is silent on his trade and on how he rose. Twenty years before his birth Nilu had sacked [[set:109|Dunjinil]] and burned its archives. When he was six, the Treaty of Khanindu of 2055 made the kingdom a vassal of Luthe and laid an indemnity on it. From the survey of the 2050s until the kingdom's end, the record's measure of its cohesion stood at zero.

A school exercise from the port of Dunjinil, dated AY 2071, when Nandune was twenty-two, has a pupil named Nangashu copy a model letter to King Nagième: "The grain of my lord is safe in the granary. The boat of my lord has arrived at the quay." The teacher's note reads: "Write it again, ten times." The city's children learned to write about grain and boats, and to address a king.

In 2085, under a ten-year-old king and his regent, the kingdom collapsed. Of its twenty-six districts, sixteen passed to a city-state ruled from Dunjinil by [[per:16666|Nakhiènde]]. The other ten went to a rival city-state, [[pol:721|Gane]], with its seat at Gyi.

## The reform of 2091

In 2091 Nandune, then forty-two, led the [[evt:16762|Merchant republic Reform in Dunjènile]], and Nakhiènde was deposed. The record lists no battle, execution or exile. Nakhiènde died a natural death in 2106, outliving the man who displaced him.

Why a merchant republic, and why then? The record does not say. The state's faith, the cult of [[mov:24|Uthungala]], asked for justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and held prosperity to be a sign of divine favour. Under that creed rule by men of trade need not look impious, though the record does not connect the two. What it does show is hardship: grain in Dunjinil's market cost nearly twice as much in the 2090s as in the 2080s, the second-steepest rise among the 118 markets the record prices, while the typical market barely moved. Even so, by the end of the 2090s the record's measure of the republic's cohesion stood at 0.51, where the kingdom's had stood at zero.

## Kavu and Gyi

The one war of his tenure was the [[evt:16817|Dunjènile–Gane War]], a war of conquest begun in 2099. Gane was already losing a war to Nilu; in 2100 the Treaty of Gyi took four districts from it, laid an indemnity on it and made it Nilu's vassal. Dunjènile's war looks like a neighbour seizing on a bleeding rival.

Nandune commanded in person twice. At the [[evt:16848|Battle of Kavu (AY 2101)]] he led 2,907 men against 1,089 under Gane's young ruler, [[per:16711|Nakathambe]], then twenty-seven. Nandune lost 31 men, Gane 208. A year later, at the [[evt:16853|Battle of Gyi (AY 2102)]] before Gane's capital, he brought 1,735 men against 550 and was beaten. He lost 539 men, nearly a third of his force, against 103 of the defenders. Of the 682 men the republic lost in the whole war, 539 died that day.

Gane's side of these years survives in one chronicle, set down in 2117 by Nambugthu, who still called himself "scribe of the palace of Gane" thirteen years after Gane had ceased to exist (PAL·CH·03108, a clay-tablet copy with several leaves missing). Its surviving leaves end with the peace with Nilu: "at the urging of the priests, the king agreed to terms with Nilu, and the Treaty of Gyi was sworn. The border was settled." They say nothing of the war with Dunjènile, or of Gane's one victory in it.

## A term served

In 2103 the record's first election replaced Nandune with [[per:16801|Najiègimbu]], two years his senior. Najiègimbu too served twelve years and left when his term ran out, which suggests a fixed term. He took over the war and won it: at Kavu in 2103 and Gyi in 2104 his armies outnumbered Gane's four or five to one, and the conquest of 2104 added Gane's last six districts to the republic.

He took the glory as well, and inflated it. His stele at Kavu (PAL·IN·01222, weathered but legible) reads: "Najiègimbu, king of Dunjènile, defeated Gane in Kavu. He killed 500 soldiers." The record gives Gane 233 dead in that battle. The title tells us more than the number. The stele makes the chief magistrate of the first merchant republic a king, and so do his palace inscription of 2112 (PAL·IN·04659) and the epitaph of a later ruler, Navavun, from the royal tombs (PAL·IN·04660). In its three surviving inscriptions the republic Nandune made never found, or never wanted, another word for its rulers.

Of Nandune himself, nothing is inscribed. The seventeen inscribed finds from Nanjil's excavation at Dunjinil (PAL·EX·00163) include nothing between the school exercise of 2071 and Najiègimbu's palace of 2112. Nandune died in 2104, aged fifty-five, of natural causes. Gane was annexed that same year, and the record does not say whether he lived to hear of it. His republic lasted forty years, until Nilu conquered it in 2131.

As a general he was poor: he won one cheap victory, then suffered a costly defeat against a force a third the size of his own. As a founder he made something no one in the record had made before. And he did what no king around him ever did: when his time was up, he went.
