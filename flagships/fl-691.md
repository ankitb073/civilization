Three stones carry [[per:7601|Udatlon]]'s own account of his wars, and they say much the same thing three times. On a rock relief near [[set:129|Nuwkennga]], dated AY 1170 and excavated by Nambili in AY 4944, the king of [[pol:286|Ibun]] "defeated Birnado". "He killed 1,000 soldiers. He erected this stele for Shyedartsi, his lady. May Shyedartsi destroy the name of the enemy." A second relief, at Jerathnu in AY 1175, raises the count to 3,100; a limestone stele back at Nuwkennga, in AY 1179, to 4,200. Read against the record, they tell a story their author did not intend: the dead multiplied as the king's own part in the fighting shrank.

He was a man of the Tueng, born in AY 1138. The record names no father, mother, wife or child, and is silent on his life before AY 1168, when, aged thirty, he seized the throne. The only hint of a family comes from an enemy chronicler, who says he acted "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore". He founded a dynasty of one, Ibun's last.

## A throne taken in a bad year

Ibun's law made its kings by election, but since AY 1127 every king had taken the throne by usurpation, and Udatlon was the fifth. His predecessor [[per:7317|Tadenen]], himself a usurper, died a natural death in 1168 at sixty-eight. The record calls the seizure a crisis but does not say whether the old king still lived. Someone gave Tadenen a royal tomb; its lintel from the royal tombs at [[set:197|Ivun]] reads: "Tadenen, king of Ibun, dwells in this tomb. May Shyedartsi protect his soul."

Tadenen had opened the [[evt:7213|Holy War of Ibun against Namlo]] in 1166, and in 1168 the general Yelin lost 932 men at Bimnge. In the same year [[pol:316|Birnado]] broke away under Kutluknen, beginning the [[evt:7228|Ibun Civil War]]. At the end of the 1150s Ibun had held 121 districts and 1,474,104 people; the survey of the 1160s counts 67 districts and 876,615.

## Three stones for Birnado

Udatlon fought thirteen battles in person and was outnumbered in eleven. Three were against Birnado. At Nuwkennga in 1169 he held 2,879 men against 3,541; Birnado lost 402 to his 160, and its general [[per:7618|Malnun]] fell. The first stele's 1,000 dead are some two and a half times the record's. At Nuwkennga in 1174 he lost, outnumbered almost six to one, at a cost of 110 men. At the [[evt:7312|Battle of Jerathnu (AY 1176)]], with 2,191 men against 5,714 under [[per:7602|Kutluknen]] himself, he dealt Birnado its heaviest defeat of the war: 1,269 dead against Ibun's 434.

No stele of his survives for that day. The Jerathnu relief is dated a year before it and catalogued under the fight of 1173, which the record gives to his general Uluduemin; Birnado lost 593 there, not 3,100. The third stone celebrates Nuwkennga in 1177, won by Yelin, where Birnado lost 550. Of Ibun's own chronicle of the war one fragment survives, and it too credits a general: "Yelin waited until their water ran out."

Birnado's chronicler Igun wrote in AY 1184; a copy survives, burnt at the edges. Birnado's defeat at Nuwkennga came "because the omens had been neglected", and he reversed the dead: "We lost 132; the enemy, 611." Of Jerathnu 1176 he says nothing. His last entry, for 1177, reads: "the gates were opened to [...] gods had turned their faces from us." Kutluknen had died that year, and a usurper of Birnado, [[per:7694|Lulbalütin]], signed the realm away at Nuwkennga: one usurper surrendering to another. Ibun took back fifty-four districts.

Every enemy on the stones was Birnado, its commanders Tueng like himself. The record does not say why, but a usurper fighting a rival claimant had more reason than most to cut his name into rock.

## The faith he imposed and the faith he took

Against [[pol:261|Namlo]] he raised no stones, though he won there too. Three times he led Ibun out against Namlo armies near Ivun, his own capital. [[per:7617|Ebyebun]] beat him in 1169; in 1170 he beat Ebyebun, outnumbered nearly two to one. At the [[evt:7307|Battle of Ivun (AY 1175)]] he took 1,250 men against 6,494 under [[per:7671|Edonantsen]]; Namlo lost 2,321, Ibun 313, the most one-sided battle he ever fought. Namlo's chronicle, written in 1204, remembers only the first, in which Udatlon "came against us at Ibun with 6,900 men and spears beyond counting".

No text of the [[evt:7309|Treaty of Nudhuruigirimu (AY 1175)]] survives. The record keeps its terms: Namlo ceded nine districts, [[set:161|Linidhagshal]] among them, accepted a ten-year truce, and was to adopt Ibun's [[mov:9|Menibe]], a faith that taught it belonged to one people and sought no converts. Namlo's signatory was [[per:7611|Lueduen III]], then seven years old. Her chronicler wrote that "at the urging of the priests, the queen accepted the pleas of Ibun", and added: "Nothing of worth was lost." By the end of the 1170s Namlo held nineteen districts instead of twenty-eight, and its state faith was Menibe.

Seven years later Udatlon's own tablet, found at Ivun complete with minor losses, turned that victory inside out: "From this day the Kunelmu is the faith of Ibun. Its feasts shall be kept as the feasts of the king. The rites of the Myenibe may continue in private." The [[mov:21|Kunnelmu]] was Namlo's old faith, the one Ibun's holy war had been fought against. The record gives its reason for the [[evt:7444|Conversion of Ibun to Kunnelmu]] in one word, majority: the king followed his subjects rather than led them. A queen's edict for the same faith in AY 1150 (PAL·DE·00074) ordered that "Its shrines shall be built in every city of the realm"; Udatlon asked only for feasts. The old enemies had exchanged faiths, though Ibun's chronicler of 1197 still credited the king's last victories to Novegu, a Menibe goddess.

## The queen at Linidhagshal

[[pol:310|Pome]] attacked in 1177. Its queen, [[per:7432|Pemtongath]], was a year older than Udatlon and had reigned since 1154. Of his six battles in the [[evt:7318|Pome–Ibun War]] she led against him in five, and he won four. Both courts' chronicles, of 1197, bend the record. Pome's says Udatlon, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Pome", though Pome attacked. Ibun's puts 11,251 Pome men at Linidhagshal in 1186, where the record counts 4,353, and claims "Of the enemy 2,575 were slain; of ours, 87", against the record's 880 and 169.

The figures hold a sharper truth. In his own six battles Ibun lost 1,687 men and Pome 2,932. In the two his generals fought, in 1180 and 1182, Ibun lost 3,083 and Pome 1,326. At Tinlamvukhnir in 1182 Uluduemin lost 2,103 men to Pome's 666; Ibun's chronicler wrote, "It cost us 1,081 men, and them 1,949."

The [[evt:7421|Treaty of Linidhagshal (AY 1187)]] was sworn in the town Namlo had ceded to him twelve years before. It left the frontier where it stood, with a ten-year truce, and each chronicler wrote that the other side, "having gained nothing", gave up. Nambili's excavation there in AY 4942 found that "the city seems never to have been sacked."

Ordinary life ran on. The day-book of the House of Bili, kept at Ivun in 1181, prices bronze at the market of [[set:151|Nudhuruigirimu]], the enemy capital Ibun had sacked in 1173 and 1175. Grain at Ivun cost 2.11 [[cur:152|Uguy]] the measure, against 1.57 in 1167. In 1174 a Nudhuruigirimu house, Buedu, had written in its margin: "Losses written off after the Sack of Nudhuruigirimu."

## After the name

In 1189 [[pol:175|Yinnga]], with nearly three times Ibun's people and soldiers, came for tribute. At the [[evt:7456|Battle of Virnadhu (AY 1190)]] [[per:7828|Malomen]] brought 11,966 men against Udatlon's 6,395; Ibun lost 820, Yinnga 202. In 1191 Ibun broke into three kingdoms, one seated at Virnadhu, Birnado's old capital. The record traces the collapse to five events, three from 1168 and 1169: Birnado's secession, Yelin's defeat at Bimnge, and Udatlon's own at Ivun. The [[evt:7436|Yinnga–Ibun War]] ended without a settlement; there was no Ibun left to settle with.

He died of natural causes in 1195, aged fifty-seven. The record does not say where, and no epitaph of his has been found. His stones asked the gods to destroy the name of the enemy; Pome's chronicler wrote his as "Odatelon". At Ivun in 1197 a chronicler still signed himself "scribe of the palace of Ibun, in the 87th year of Ibun", six years after Ibun ended, as though the count, like the king, had outlived its realm.
