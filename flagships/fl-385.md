*[A single folded leaf, small and much handled, found sewn into the lining of a garment. The hand is cramped; the ink is poor.]*

To be said in the dark, by the faithful of [[mov:25|Miyithuyishiu]], when two or three are gathered and the door is barred.

O you gods who share the world, each in your place and each with your due; you of the threshold and you of the hearth, you of the field and you of the road, you of the river and you of the grave; we do not name you aloud tonight, for the walls of [[set:13|Tsivintuha]] have ears, but you know your names, and you know ours.

Hear us, who are few, from under the floor.

Last year the officers of the kingdom came to our houses of prayer. They sealed the doors with the king's seal; they carried away the lamps and the books; they took the stoles from the shoulders of our priests and asked them, before the whole street, whether they would bow to the new altar. Some bowed. Do not judge them harshly, for they have children. Some did not bow, and where they are now, you know better than we.

We do not curse [[pol:987|Mane]]. Mane is our country; our fathers' bones are in it; our bread grows in it. We do not curse the king, [[per:23136|Ihi]], whom we saw ride in triumph after the [[evt:23557|battle of Chezhietle]], and for whose safety we offered incense in those days at the open altar, before it was closed to us. We prayed for the peace that came by the [[evt:23582|Treaty of Chezhietle]], and we were glad of it with all the city. Is this the reward of loyal subjects? We ask it of you, not of him; he will not hear us.

The priests of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] say that there is one divine power, and that you are its servants or its shadows. Be it so; we will not quarrel with them about the furniture of heaven. Our teachers say little of the world's beginning or its end, and we have always thought it a kind of modesty. But we know this: that each of you has had your place and due among the faithful since the days of our fathers' fathers' fathers, for two thousand years and more, before there was a Mane, and before the I'ishiu were heard of. A shadow is not worshipped for two thousand years.

They say, too, that all things turn on a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. Then let them reflect, when they are alone, that the wheel which has brought them up will carry them over, and that the altar they have closed may one day be opened by the hand that closed it. We do not wish them evil. We wish them only the turning of the wheel they preach.

Teach us now what you have always taught: justice in our dealings, moderation in all things.

Justice, so that we do not lie to the officers, nor cheat them in the market to repay them, nor inform on one another to buy our own safety.

Moderation, so that our grief does not become hatred, and our fear does not become cowardice, and our courage does not become folly.

Withdrawal from wealth and appetite, which was always our counsel, and is now become our necessity. They have fined our households in [[cur:540|makh]] until there are few makh left to fine; well, then, we are the wiser by force, and we thank you for the lesson, though we did not ask it.

Keep the old priests who are hidden among us. Keep the books we buried. Keep the children, who learn the prayers at night and forget them in the day, as they must.

And if any of us is taken, let them be taken quietly, and let them say nothing, and let them be received by you in their place and with their due.

Here the faithful answer: *In their place, and with their due.*

Written in the year 543 of the I'ishiu, as the kingdom now counts; we count otherwise.

*[Below, in a different hand:]* Burn this after learning it.
