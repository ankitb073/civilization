The name [[per:19610|Bozinibe]] means "victory water", and his court called him "the Victorious". For once the epithet is no exaggeration. In forty-six years as king of [[pol:755|Tomözel]], from AY 2366 to 2412, he fought five wars and won all five. His armies lost no battle that the record knows. He annexed two kingdoms and a city-state, took land from two more kingdoms, and added some sixty-three districts to his realm. And in 2412, five years after his last conquest, the empire he had enlarged broke into six pieces while he was still alive to see it. He lived four more years as a king without a kingdom. This chapter tries to explain how both things could be true of one reign.

## The son of a brief queen

Bozinibe was born in AY 2350, a La'im of the house of Shoe Simen, which had ruled Tomözel since its founding more than two centuries earlier. His grandfather Tsisfee reigned for a generation and fought his own wars. His mother, [[per:19605|Tieitl]], succeeded in 2365 and is remembered as "the Brief", for she reigned barely a year. She declared the war on I'a'ie'i that her son would finish. The Annals of Tomözel, AY 2300–2399 (PAL·AN·00352), which promise to run "to the 34th year of Bozinibe", do not give her a single line. She died in 2366, aged forty-four.

The [[evt:19731|Accession of Bozinibe]] followed. He was sixteen and needed no regent. The realm he inherited held some 281 districts and six and a quarter million people, and its capital was the old city of Shue'etie. The record's measure of its internal order was already poor.

## Five wars

The [[evt:19722|Tomözel–I'a'ie'i War]] (2365–2369) set the pattern of the reign. Its battles were won by veteran generals against forces that the record numbers in the low thousands, 4,949 men at Athtathe, 2,497 at Toiyoe. Tomözel lost 391 men in the war and the enemy 1,952. By the [[evt:19772|Treaty of Toiyoe (AY 2369)]], sworn with [[per:19569|Nitllo Atha I]] at [[set:111|Toiyoe]], [[pol:860|I'a'ie'i]] gave up seven districts and the town itself, paid an indemnity and became a vassal. The young king set up a boundary stone in the ceded land, with a curse on the enemy.

In 2375 two neighbouring kingdoms swore a thirty-year alliance against Tomözel. The [[evt:19851|Holy War of Tomözel against Yimola]] followed that year against [[pol:871|Yimola]], a kingdom only five years old. At Nirun in 2377 a general's army of 31,092 beat a defending force of 578, and the king raised a limestone stele claiming "He killed 800 soldiers" (PAL·IN·01453). The record gives 121. The Yimola account of the war (PAL·CH·03650), written in 2386, claims for the defenders a loss of 42 men at Nirun and 77 the next year, against 34 and 49 of the enemy. For once the defeated came closer to the truth than the victor. After the sack of [[set:207|Niuzafammas]], King [[per:19678|Kuthvunen]] signed the [[evt:19909|Conquest of Yimola]], which annexed twenty-seven districts.

The [[evt:19999|Holy War of Tomözel against Nebe]] (2384–2390) was the war in which the king fought hardest himself. At the [[evt:20013|Battle of Lienevkan (AY 2385)]] he led 34,733 men against 1,339 under [[per:19805|Ovanaven]]. At the [[evt:20040|Battle of Lienevkan (AY 2388)]] he beat King [[per:19733|Jeyshavanaven II]], who died on the field. The palace account of the war (PAL·CH·03679), written two years later, says "Bozinibe broke their line at the first charge" and gives his losses that day as 42. The record gives 77. The [[evt:20077|Treaty of Lienevkan (AY 2390)]], sworn with the dead king's younger brother [[per:19734|Jeyshavanaven III]], survives in its clay text (PAL·TR·01357). Nebe gave up thirteen districts, "its fields and its people". It owed twenty-four talents of silver a year for thirty-one years and became "a servant of Tomözel".

## Tononen's fields

One document from these years shows what a treaty meant at the level of a family. In 2401 a man of Nebe named Tononen, son of Melitanan, came before the king's court at Eshueethie. He said that his father's fields, "195 measures of sown land", had been taken when Nebe's land was given to Tomözel, and asked for them back. The steward of the royal lands replied: "No private claim survives the treaty." The claim was dismissed, and Tononen was ordered to pay the costs of the court (PAL·LS·00350). Of all the documents of the reign, this record of a lawsuit may be the most honest. In it a royal court recorded a subject's complaint that his land had been taken, and refused him without embarrassment.

The last two wars were short. In the [[evt:20124|Fourth Tomözel–Yiotl War]] the generals besieged [[set:174|Faamni]] twice, and the city's queen, [[per:19862|Niekhobibe]], died in its defence. The [[evt:20155|Conquest of Yiotl]] in 2398 took three districts at a cost of fifty-nine of Tomözel's men. The defenders' scribe ended his account (PAL·CH·03708): "The gates were opened to the enemy. Let this record keep what the sword has taken." In the [[evt:20211|Holy War of Tomözel against Shivilna]] the king, now fifty-six, commanded once more at the [[evt:20237|Battle of Shidyun (AY 2406)]], defeating [[per:20099|Yimosna]]. In 2407 the [[evt:20248|Conquest of Shivilna]] ended a rebel kingdom that had stood for almost a century since breaking away from Vakha. Its young king, [[per:20023|Nistlo'ilwesh'a]], signed it at the age of twenty-two. The record says he was executed that same year.

## The victorious king without a kingdom

Over the five wars Tomözel lost about 1,600 soldiers and its enemies some 6,600. The empire grew to 344 districts and nearly eight million people. Yet the record's measure of its order fell, with only one partial recovery in the 2390s. By 2412 it stood lower than at any earlier point in the reign, and the army had grown to over 60,000 men. No revolt, plague or invasion is recorded as the cause. In AY 2412 the empire simply came apart into six successor states, each with a new ruler. One of them was a city-state of I'a'ie'i, centred on Toiyoe, the town Bozinibe had taken in his first treaty.

His wife had died in 2394, and the record knows of no children. Bozinibe lived on until AY 2416 and died of natural causes at sixty-six, holding no office in any of the successor states. The epithet "the Victorious" was never wrong. It describes the battles truly and leaves the state out. His victories had added people, land and tribute that the Tomözel court could not hold together. Tononen's lawsuit suggests why. A state that answered a farmer with "no private claim survives the treaty" had won the land and lost the loyalty of the people who worked it.
