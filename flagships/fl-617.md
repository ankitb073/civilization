The name [[per:24169|Tashefen]] means "bright glory", and few rulers have lived so fully into a name. In forty-six years on the throne of [[pol:1004|Yeda]], from AY 2765 to 2811, he fought or presided over six wars, swore six peace treaties and never lost a war. His court called him great, and later generations kept the epithet. Of the conquerors in this series he is the one whose reputation the record does least to undermine. Precisely for that reason it is worth asking what the brightness cost, and whom it left in shadow.

## The usurper's son

Tashefen was born in AY 2735 into a Jego family that was not yet royal. In 2757, in a year the record marks as a crisis, his father [[per:24083|Toshen I]] seized the throne of Yeda from the reigning king and founded a new house. The son was twenty-two. He therefore grew up as a subject and came of age as a usurper's heir, and he learned early that a throne in Yeda was kept by success. Toshen reigned eight years. His tomb lintel asks only that a god "protect his soul".

The [[evt:24380|Accession of Tashefen]] in 2765 passed without recorded challenge, though Yeda's law favoured seniority over sons. He was thirty. His first wife, whose name the record does not keep, bore his heir, [[per:24362|Toshen II]], in 2767 and died in 2779. The empire he inherited held some eleven and a half million people and was settled enough to go to war almost at once.

## The western wars

His first war was of his own choosing. In the [[evt:24421|Yeda–Rieney War]] (2768–2771) he attacked the old kingdom of [[pol:939|Rieney]]. At the [[evt:24455|Battle of Riniy (AY 2769)]] he commanded 124,469 men against 13,922 under Bavalothen. He won, at a cost of 18,093 dead against the enemy's 4,156. The diorite stele he raised near the field (the Victory stele of Tashefen: Battle of Riniy) claims 17,500 enemy dead, four times the true figure. When [[pol:1013|Diebegovebenga]] came in on Rieney's side, he met its army at the [[evt:24486|Battle of Budh (AY 2771)]], 83,183 men against 32,094 under Duemagonen. This was his best day as a commander: the stronger enemy of the war lost 9,674 men to his 6,768. By the [[evt:24487|Treaty of Budh (AY 2771)]], sworn with [[per:24024|Tuebanaven]], Diebegovebenga gave up twenty districts and two towns and promised tribute for thirty-eight years. Rieney itself collapsed that year into two city-states. A fragment of the lost Rieney account of the war says its king made peace "out of mercy for the common folk". That is the consolation of the defeated, and it may also have been true.

The next war came to him. In 2775 the kingdom of [[pol:1007|Yarel]] attacked, and the [[evt:24520|First Yarel–Yeda War]] turned on two battles fought by the king himself. At the [[evt:24543|Battle of Vuvu (AY 2778)]] he defeated [[per:24324|Vakolvan I]], who had seized Yarel's throne that same year and died on the field. The Yeda account of the war, written more than half a century later, gives Vakolvan 74,814 men and 23,611 dead. The record gives him 30,971 men and 7,376 dead, and Yeda nearly as many. The following year, at the [[evt:24554|Battle of Nin (AY 2779)]], Tashefen's 120,692 men beat 13,138 under Vakolvan's son [[per:24347|Tovanaven]], and lost 16,578 of their own to the enemy's 4,856. The [[evt:24555|Treaty of Nin (AY 2779)]] secured only a truce of fifteen years. Yeda had lost more men than the invader, and nothing was gained but quiet.

## From conqueror to overlord

The middle of the reign shows Tashefen learning a different art. In the Fourth Yeda–Jenontono War he won the [[evt:24675|Battle of Ngiunge (AY 2788)]] cheaply, losing 4,064 men to the enemy's 9,319, and his army sacked Zhinontunu. The city's archives burned. A soldier of Yeda cut a line into the plaster of a temple wall there that has outlived them: "Lometathrelen took the wine of this house. There was not enough." It is the only voice from the ranks in the whole reign. The bronze text of the [[evt:24689|Treaty of Zhinontunu (AY 2789)]], granted "by Tashefen, emperor of Yeda, to Tokhshen of Jenontono", took twelve districts and a yearly tribute for sixteen years. It also made [[per:24301|Tokhshen]]'s kingdom acknowledge Yeda's overlordship and send its levies when summoned.

That was the pattern of the later treaties. The [[evt:24824|Treaty of Yishdhagshal (AY 2798)]] took seven districts and an indemnity from Queen [[per:24291|Takhadhath]] and made her kingdom a vassal. When Yarel attacked again, the [[evt:24892|Treaty of Nin (AY 2801)]] made the same Tovanaven pay an indemnity and accept vassalage. Tashefen did not command in these wars. Others won the battles, but the steles bear his name. One raised near Ture for the victory of 2796 claims 19,300 enemy dead, where the record has 3,196. The empire was ringed with dependants who paid, sent soldiers and did not need to be governed. The cost fell on the army. In the second Yarel war alone Yeda, the defender and the victor, lost 39,166 men to Yarel's 28,637.

## An old king's marriage

In 2803, at sixty-eight, Tashefen married again. The [[evt:24911|Marriage of Tashefen and Povvanath]] joined his house to that of the city-state of [[pol:1060|Rieney]], the remnant of the kingdom he had broken in 2771. [[per:24625|Povvanath]] was the daughter of its ruling queen. She was fourteen. The record calls it a joining of houses, and so it was: a small city bought itself a protector with a girl. We know nothing of her years at the court of Yeda, except that she was long-lived and that she outlived her husband by twenty-four years. Her mother's city did not survive her. In 2826 it fell to Diebegovebenga.

The last war of the reign, the Yeda–Diebegovebenga War, was again fought by generals, and again commemorated by a royal stele that multiplied the enemy dead almost fivefold. The [[evt:24983|Treaty of Biniynge (AY 2809)]], sworn with Queen [[per:24665|Pavmanen]], brought fourteen districts, two towns and a heavy indemnity.

## The ledger

Tashefen died of natural causes in AY 2811, aged seventy-six, and the Accession of Toshen II was peaceful. The record allows a fair accounting. Over his reign Yeda grew from 187 districts to about 279 and from some eleven and a half million people to more than sixteen million. The treasury grew by more than half. The record's measure of order in the realm stood markedly higher at his death than at his accession. Against that stand more than 150,000 Yeda soldiers dead in battle, three steles that told lies about the enemy dead, and a fourteen-year-old bride.

The structure he built lasted a century. Yarel, his vassal, was annexed outright in 2864, and Yeda broke into six successor states only in 2911. Few founders of an overlordship can claim as much. The court's "great" is a fair word for Tashefen if it is read as a judgement of scale and endurance, and not of the men, at Nin, in a burned archive and in a temple with too little wine, who paid for it.
