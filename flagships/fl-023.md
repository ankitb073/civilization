[[per:26578|Nathuth]], queen of [[pol:1052|Turamtu]], to the elders of the council and to the people of the city of [[set:16|Turamtu]]: greeting, and the favour of the gods.

I write to you from the camp before Nuidaduth, with the mist of the river still upon the tents and the smoke of the pyres upon the wind. We have conquered. Let the priests of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]] open the doors of every house of the gods, and light the lamps from the gate to the citadel.

You know with what grief I took up the rod of my father in this same year, while his tomb was still unsealed; and you know that many of you counselled me to remain in the city and rule the war from a cushion. I answered then, and I answer now, that a ruler who inherits a war inherits also the duty to look upon it. A commonwealth is held together not by walls but by the example of those who stand first in it. Had I sat at home while the sons of the city bled, I should have deserved neither their obedience nor yours.

Let no one say that this is a war of greed. The men of [[pol:1122|Eshuethye]] have shut the roads of [[reg:14|Yimla]] against our merchants, laid tolls upon our boats that no just people lays upon its neighbours, and made the markets of that country into their private storehouse. Commerce is the common inheritance of all who travel; whoever closes a road makes war on every honest man who walks it. Eight times before this the trade of Yimla has been fought over, and now we fight again in what the clerks already call the [[evt:26984|Ninth Yimla Trade War]]. We did not choose the quarrel. We chose only not to be robbed.

I do not hide from you the [[evt:27006|first day at Nuidaduth]], three summers past, when [[per:26542|Nuwbitharin]] was driven from the field. He was overcome not by the valour of the enemy but by the marshes and by guides who led him false, and [[per:26300|Ashtheyatsu]], their queen, boasted of it in every town of her country. Yet she did not boast long, for the gods took her within the year, and [[dei:137|Shyethartsi]], lord of the underworld, keeps a just account. In the summer following Nuwbitharin came again to the same ground and broke [[per:26379|Inemamshe]] so thoroughly that his men threw away their shields to run the faster: that was the [[evt:27015|second day at Nuidaduth]].

Now hear the third. Four days ago we came upon the Eshuethye under a new general, [[per:26684|Yuni Mevi]], a man of more courage than judgement, drawn up on the open ground below [[set:247|Nuivthuvu]], and we went against them. From dawn until noon the lines pressed upon one another, and for an hour, I confess, the issue was in doubt. Then [[dei:136|Dueminu]] sent wind and rain into their faces, Nuwbitharin brought the reserve along the riverbank, and their line opened like a rotten sack. They left upon the field, by the count of my heralds, more than twenty thousand dead. Our own I will not number in a letter that an enemy might read; we have burned them with honour, and their names will be cut in stone. So ended the [[evt:27049|battle of Nuidaduth]], the greatest this war has seen.

Do not think, however, that one victory finishes a war. The philosophers teach that fortune is a river and not a pool: it does not stay where it is poured. The enemy still holds walls and has silver to hire more men. I ask therefore three things of the council. First, that grain be sent before the autumn rains, with offerings to [[dei:139|Nithikhti]] that the harvest may bear it. Second, that the treasury send [[cur:578|lyembi]] enough for the soldiers' pay, since a soldier who is not paid becomes a thief, and a thief becomes a deserter. Third, that sacrifice be made to [[dei:141|Navthin]] of the river, who carried our boats and did not drown our horses.

The gods favour those who keep order, and prosperity follows their favour as the crop follows the rain. Keep order in the city as we keep it in the camp, and the markets of Yimla will be open to your children.

Written with my own hand in the camp below Nuivthuvu, in the year 3193 from the founding of Turamtu.
