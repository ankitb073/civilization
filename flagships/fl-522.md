*[Preserved in a copy made by the founder's own scribes and kept by the community at Eshueethie; the original, if delivered, has not survived.]*

To [[per:25233|Amanfoyatl]], queen of [[pol:994|Tomözel]], mistress of Eshueethie and all its lands, from [[per:25596|Tlamfen Ibe]], a man of her city who asks nothing of her but a hearing: health, and the favour of the One.

Majesty, you will have been told by now that a certain teacher has begun to gather men in the lower town, that he speaks against the gods, and that he must be watched. The first is true. The second is not. The third I welcome, for I have nothing to say in a cellar that I would not say in your hall. I write so that you may hear it from me before you hear it from your informers, who are diligent but not philosophers.

I have lived the most of my two-and-forty years in [[set:3|Eshueethie]]. I was a young man when our army was broken at the [[evt:25730|Battle of Khash]], when our general fell, and the carts came back for many days, full. I helped to unload them. I tell you this not to wound you, though you sat on the throne that year, but so that you know where my teaching began: in a courtyard full of the dead, asking which of the many gods had failed them, and why.

The priests of [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]] answered me kindly. Many gods share the world, they said, each in his place; it was not this god's place to save them, nor that one's. Be moderate; withdraw from appetite. It was a gentle answer, and I could not live on it.

Here is what I have come to believe, and now teach.

There is one divine power, and the gods your fathers honoured are its servants, or its shadows cast upon the wall. I do not mock them; a servant is to be respected for his master's sake. Time does not wander, as the old teaching suggests by its silence; it runs, like a road, from a first morning to a last judgement, and every deed is a milestone upon it. The good life is duty, order, and right sacrifice, not the shrug of the moderate man. And the One shows favour plainly: a people that walks the road prospers, and a people that wanders does not.

Consider, Majesty, your own victories, and forgive me for doing so. At the [[evt:25824|Battle of Foyitlithyi]] and again, under your own banner, at the [[evt:25829|Battle of Foyitlithyi]], our men won the field; and near two thousand of them fell to take it. At the [[evt:25830|First Sack of Eol]] the house of books was burned, and the learning of a city went up in an afternoon. At the [[evt:25831|Treaty of Eol]] we gained fields, and peace. Was that favour? I do not say it was not. I say only that a people which cannot tell victory from loss is a people without a road.

This year, before witnesses, in the old market of Eshueethie, I set apart the first of those who would keep the teaching, and laid my hands on them, so that the teaching may be passed down in order and not dissolve into the opinions of whoever shouts loudest. The scribes of the city have recorded it as the [[evt:25953|Founding of the Setlietlpovi Faith]]. I accept the name. [[mov:96|Setlietlpovi]] means bright justice in our tongue, and justice walked out along a straight road is what I offer.

I do not ask you to leave your gods. I ask you to let me speak in your markets, and to let those who listen keep the teaching without fines in [[cur:543|Nuesha]] or worse. The message is for all peoples, and so it is for yours, and so, if you will have it, for you.

Your servant on the straight road,

Tlamfen Ibe

*Written at Eshueethie, in the year 2369 of the Mietseyeshio.*
