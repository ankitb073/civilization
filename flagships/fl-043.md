title: The Song of Tadudhiv, from the Fourth Canto

*. . . and the trains went east by night, and the trains came west by day,*  
the east-bound loud with singing, and the west-bound loud with none;  
and the wire beside the railway hummed the names, and still the names,  
and the presses of [[set:0|Taulin]] set them in columns, black on grey.  

O Thou who art One, before whom the old gods stand as shadows stand,  
Thou who hast drawn the line of time from the first dawn to the Judgement,  
give me a voice of iron, give me the voice of the foundry whistle,  
that I may sing of [[reg:21|Tadudhiv]] and the seven winters there.  

[[pol:1959|Yidhe]] went out to take the plain, and the plain was wide and black,  
and across it, sunk in the clay to the shoulder, waited [[pol:1638|Khishkhidh]].  
So began [[evt:48251|the war for Tadudhiv]], and the mothers put on grey.  

Hear the first winter. At [[evt:48261|Nudulamlul]] [[per:46989|Lotafbafin]] led,  
with twenty-two millions at his back and the guns in rows behind,  
and against him [[per:47063|Nadhash]], the iron woman of Khishkhidh,  
with fifty millions in her trenches, two of theirs for one of ours.  
Eight millions of ours went down into the clay of Nudulamlul,  
and eight millions of theirs, and the clay did not ask whose sons.  
That field was theirs. But mark it, you who read: they paid coin for coin.  

Hear the second winter. Lotafbafin came again, and Nadhash met him,  
man for man, the two hosts even as the pans of a merchant's scale,  
and Yidhe tipped the scale: [[evt:48268|the second Nudulamlul]] was ours.  
Hear the third winter. They held, and held by a hand's breadth only,  
and [[evt:48272|the third Nudulamlul]] was theirs, and the dead lay even.  

A year the guns were silent, and the fields grew rank with nettle.  
Then came Khangushe. Read it on the monuments, children; read it twice.  
Twenty millions of Yidhe went against twice their number,  
and when the smoke lifted from [[evt:48285|the field of Khangushe]]  
six millions of Khishkhidh lay on it, and two millions of ours.  
The bells of Taulin rang three days. The wire sang nothing but our name.  
The next winter Lotafbafin broke Naguf where the others had been broken:  
[[evt:48291|the fourth Nudulamlul]], our third field, and theirs the greater loss.  

But who can hold the spring tide back with his two hands?  
In the eighth year the remnant went out under [[per:47098|Anuyin]],  
ten millions, hollow-cheeked, the boots of the last levy on their feet,  
against [[per:47099|Nalizhikh]] and twice their number fresh from the rear.  
The rain came. The shells ran short. The wire was cut at the river.  
I will not sing [[evt:48298|the last Nudulamlul]]. Let the rain sing it.  

Then came the men in frock coats with their pens and with their seals,  
who had never smelled the clay, and they sat down at Nudulamlul,  
and by [[evt:48299|the paper of Nudulamlul]] they signed away our claim,  
and counted out the [[cur:1137|nivbe]] of the poor to Khishkhidh's treasurers,  
and opened the markets of Yidhe, and bound the guns for twenty years.  
Three fields we won and three they won; of theirs the more were fallen;  
yet the plain stayed theirs, for the pens gave up what the guns had not. Mark it.  

O Thou who teachest compassion and the release from suffering,  
forgive us that we cannot yet forgive the men with pens.  
Thou hast drawn time as a line, not as a wheel; it does not come again.  
Yet a line may rise. Under [[per:47087|Bivbuvun]] the guns have spoken,  
and Anuyin, who lost the last field, won the next before he slept,  
and [[evt:48414|Virathkhanu is ours]], and the sons of Nudulamlul stand on its walls.  
The faithful of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] shall wait upon the Judgement.  
Till then, O Yidhe, remember Tadudhiv. Remember. Remember.  

*Printed at Taulin, 23 Baadhu 2739 of the Vishithmikh.*
