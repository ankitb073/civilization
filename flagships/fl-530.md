*[Chancery copy on parchment, with the enclosed letter copied below the king's in a smaller hand. Both were sealed with the royal seal of Miohitl before dispatch.]*

[[per:36203|Nihtlihuem]], by the favour of the gods and the ordination of their priests king of [[pol:1536|Miohitl]], lord of [[set:28|Tlue]] and, by right of arms, of the fields lately ceded by Linath, to [[per:36539|Shitlihuem]], queen of [[pol:1564|Linath]], greeting and peace, which is now at last possible between us.

Madam,

The ink is not yet dry on the [[evt:37082|Treaty of Lininadhe]], which you and I set our hands to in the hall of [[set:47|Linath]] while the smoke of my siege-fires was still in its rafters. I write to fulfil the promise I made you there, that you should have word of the young persons of your court whom you have given into my keeping as surety for the indemnity and for the conversion of your realm. They arrived at Tlue on the ninth day. They are lodged in the old tower of my palace, which has stood quiet for many years and is the better for the noise.

Before I come to them, Madam, permit me a word on the war, since your envoys at Lininadhe were pleased to call it a war of aggression.

It was not. It was the [[evt:36898|Holy War of Miohitl against Linath]], undertaken on the advice of the ordained priests of [[mov:25|Miyithoyishio]], to bring your people to the gods who keep each thing in its place and ask of men justice and moderation. It lasted thirteen years only because your fathers' captains were stubborn and your fathers' priests were proud. I do not deny that at the [[evt:37011|Battle of Lininadhe]] in the sixth year of it, the field was yours; your commanders that day fought from behind their earthworks, and I do not count it a fair trial. But at the [[evt:37027|Battle of Lininadhe]] three years later, the field was mine and your host was broken entire; at the [[evt:37055|First Battle of Khash]], your dead lay so thick that my heralds gave up the counting; and this spring at the [[evt:37080|Battle of Lininadhe]], where I commanded in person, I saw your last army melt away before noon. Then came the [[evt:37081|Siege of Lininadhe]], and you know the rest better than I.

The terms are just. The fields named in the treaty are mine. The indemnity will be paid in good [[cur:831|Paya]], at the weights of Tlue. Your temples will receive our priests, and your people will learn moderation, which, if you will forgive me, Madam, they have long needed. Fifteen years of truce lie before us. Let us use them.

Now, as promised, I enclose a letter written by the eldest of your hostages, in his own hand, unread by my officers save to see that it carries no secret matter. I have added nothing to it.

> *To my lady the Queen, from her servant at Tlue.*
>
> *Madam, we are well. We were afraid on the road, and the little one cried, but no one has struck us or spoken roughly. We eat at the king's second table. The bread is whiter than ours and the wine is thinner. A priest comes each morning to teach us the names of their gods, and he is patient when we forget them. The king spoke to us on the first evening. He is not so tall as they say at home. He told us that if the silver comes on time we shall see Linath again before we are grown, and I believe him, because he said it in front of his priest.*
>
> *Pray for us, Madam, and send the silver.*

You see, Madam, that your young people are in good health and good hands. Keep the terms, and they will remain so. Break them, and the gods, whom you now serve as we do, will judge between us.

Given at Tlue, in the year 4211 from the founding of Tlue, under my seal.
