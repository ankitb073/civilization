*To my cousin in the hill village, say: thus says your cousin, the daughter of your mother's sister. May [[dei:15|Vinva]] of the earth keep your field, may [[dei:18|Iva]] of the fire keep your hearth. In the 9th year of [[per:21543|Mashieven]].*

I write to you from the barley-shed of a stranger, on the road from the city. My hand is not steady. The scribe at the crossroads writes for me for a cup of meal, so I will be short where I can and long where I must.

[[set:168|Dviminnga]] is taken. Our city is taken. You will have heard it; I tell it so you know it from one who saw.

There were omens. In the month of the new barley a raven sat three days on the gate of [[dei:19|Yinan]], and the priests did not drive it away. A calf was born in the street of the tanners with two heads. The old women said: the gods are looking away. We said: the old women always say so.

The men of [[pol:887|Turamtu]] came up the river road with more spears than there are stalks in a field. At the [[evt:21808|battle of Diviminnge]] our men stood before the gate. They were a handful, cousin, a handful, the guard of the king's house and the young men of the streets. My husband's brother was among them. We have not found him.

Then the gate. Then the fire. They went from house to house. They took the bronze and the cloth and the girls they could catch. They drove off the oxen. I took the children and the grinding stone and went out through the water-gate in the dark, with the people of our street, like goats through a hedge.

From the hill I looked back. The house of tablets was burning, the great house where the scribes kept the writing of the city. It burned all night, with a white light. In it were the tablets of the king's grain, the tablets of the temples, the tablets of every field and every debt. My father's field was written there. The moneylender's tablet of our debt was written there. Now neither is written anywhere. Who will say the field is ours? Who will say we owe the moneylender? The gods know, and they are looking away.

This is the [[evt:21809|Sack of Diviminnge]], the scribe says, and he says the men of Turamtu do it to a city as a man breaks a jar he means to own.

When I was a girl we were people of Yennga, and paid our tribute to Yennga. Then came the [[evt:21719|Collapse of the Yennga State]], and the king of our own city stood up, and there was the [[evt:21725|Rise of Dviminnga]]. Nine years we had our own king. Nine years we paid tribute to our own house, and sang in our own temples. Some say the king Mashieven is in the hills, gathering men. I pray it is so. I do not know it.

Here a basket of barley costs a bronze bowl. I have one bowl left. The woman of this shed gives the children whey in the morning and asks nothing. May [[dei:17|Nidshüdi]] of the grain fill her bins for ever.

Cousin, I ask you by the bones of our grandmother: may we come to you? I can grind, I can spin, the boy can keep goats. We will sleep with the animals. We will eat what you give and not ask for more. If you say no, I will not curse you. Send word by the salt-carrier who comes on market days.

Let [[pol:942|Dviminnga]] stand again. Let the raven fly off the gate.
