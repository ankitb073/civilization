*To my wife at [[set:11|Vieneynga]], from your husband in the camp before the hills, greeting. In the year 286 of [[pol:1013|Diebegovebenga]].*

I am alive and whole, which is the first thing, and I have all my fingers, which is the second. The rest is detail.

You will have heard the criers in the market shouting the victory at [[set:185|Tienlamvokhner]]. I was there. I will tell you what the criers did not, because a wife should know what her husband has done.

Our general [[per:26392|Ononvavsheyen]] brought us down to the crossroads in four columns, and we were four men for every one of theirs. The old soldiers say this is the thirteenth war fought over the road through Baan, the [[evt:26699|13th Baan Trade War]] the clerks call it, and that their grandfathers' grandfathers fought on the same ground for the same tolls. When I heard that I thought: so this is what a road is worth, thirteen wars and still unsettled. A philosopher might make something of that. I made a fire.

The [[evt:26736|Battle of Tinlamvukhnir]] lasted from morning until the light went. Their general, [[per:26366|Zhedhoman]], did not run, and his men did not run, and that is why we lost more men than they did, although we won. I do not know how the clerks will write that. Victory, I suppose. The man who slept beside me on the march was a carter from the harbour quarter, with a laugh like a donkey. He is buried by the road.

Then we went on to their city, [[set:170|Zhieyeyzhekhte]]. The men of Peme had broken them four years ago, and their walls were still half-mended, with new stone set on old. We were in by the third day. The [[evt:26737|Sack of Zhiyiyzhikhti]], the clerks say. I will tell you only this: I took a blanket, because the nights are cold, and a bronze lamp, which I will bring home. I did not take anything else, and I did not do anything else. I swear it by Buevme and by your bread.

Their temples are strange to us. Since the [[evt:26672|Conversion of Zheyeyzhekhte to Anatofpil]] they have followed a new cult, and our priests say that is why the gods of our fathers gave them into our hands. I saw an old woman of the city on her knees in the ash of one of those temples, praying in a tongue I could not follow. She did not look like a woman whom the gods had given into anyone's hands. She looked like my mother.

You write that your sister goes now to hear the Taletuetpoth preachers by the harbour gate. Let her. A new faith in a hungry city is a cup of water; nobody asks the cup where it was made.

Here grain is a [[cur:551|Niesh]] and a tenth the measure, as at home, but the sutlers cheat on the weight, and we are paid late. Do not sell the loom. Sell the brass pot if you must.

The war is not finished. The queen [[per:26164|Makhsheshienmomath]] has not called us home, and the captains say we march again after the feast. Kiss the girls. Tell the eldest I have a bronze lamp for her, and she is to stop asking whether I have killed anyone.

Your husband, who thinks of the fig tree in our yard.
