*[Obverse. A small, roughly made tablet; the clay is coarse and full of straw, as though shaped in haste by the roadside.]*

To my mother, say:

Thus says your child, who went out from [[set:31|Waga]], the city of the kings.

May [[dei:0|Etlie]] of the sky keep you. May [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river keep your field. May [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth keep the graves of my fathers, for I cannot.

I am alive. Do not weep. The little ones are alive. The ox is alive. The cart is broken in one wheel; we mended it with rope. Hear what has happened, and do not believe the traders, who say more than they saw.

My mother, you remember what the old men said: that in the days of their grandfathers the throne was split, when Mieshlethie went out from under Waga and set up its own seat. That was the first crack in the wall. You remember also that four years ago the father of the king took the seat by force. That was the second crack. The old men said: a wall with two cracks stands until the third rain.

The third rain has come.

The king, [[per:9622|Yueppa'obi]], is a boy. His father has gone down to Muevas'e. Who listens to a boy? The great ones of the provinces did not listen. They went out from the gate of [[pol:153|Waga]], each to a country of their own, and each said: "This country is mine." Six heads now grow where one head grew. Hear their names, and tell them to my uncle, so that he knows whom to pay:

[[pol:407|Waga]], whose name is raised again in the city by new hands.  
[[pol:408|Eshue'ethie]].  
[[pol:409|Yi'otl]].  
[[pol:410|Pibetie]].  
[[pol:411|Tlalesyi]].  
[[pol:412|Piew'ana]].

The scribes of the palace write it on their tablets as the [[evt:9346|collapse of the Waga state]]. I do not know that word. I know that the roof fell, and we were under it.

In the city there was no bread for three days. The storehouse of the king was shut, and then it was opened, and then it was empty. Grain that was a little more than one [[cur:85|i'a]] the measure went for three, and then for any price. The soldiers of the king did not know whose soldiers they were. Some went to one lord, some to another. Some went to no lord, and took what they wanted in the streets. I saw them take the doors off the houses for firewood.

So we went out. We went out at night by the river gate, with the cart and the ox and the little ones, and the bronze pot of my father, and the gods of the house wrapped in a cloth. We went out with many others. The road was full, as on a feast day, but no one sang.

*[Reverse.]*

Now we are at the ford, three days from the city. At the ford there is a toll. Yesterday it was the toll of the king. Today it is the toll of a new lord, and his men did not know the price, so they took what they liked. Tomorrow, who knows whose toll?

My mother, I will come to you if the road is open. If the road is shut, I will send word by the potter's son, who travels with us.

Make the offering for us to [[dei:1|Uewtsi]], lord of wisdom, that he show us the road. Make the offering to [[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, that she turn the spears away from us. Make the offering to [[dei:4|Lashla'e]], who lights the road at night, that he light it for us.

All things turn on the great wheel, say the priests of [[mov:0|Le'im]]. What ends will begin again. I do not know what will begin. I know what has ended.

> Written at the ford, in the 2nd year of Yueppa'obi, king in Waga, if he is king. Whoever breaks this tablet on the road, may Lashla'e hide his light from him.
