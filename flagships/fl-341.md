[…] the city, the city, its gods have left it like a sheepfold.  
[[set:115|Unjinlathu]], your name is spoken, but your seat is empty.  
Unjinlathu, your walls stand, but your lord does not stand within them.  
Your gate is open; it was not opened by your hand.  

[[dei:28|Khuthaze]] of the high sky, why did you turn your face?  
[[dei:30|Pugazhu]] of the sun, why did you hide in the dust?  
[[dei:32|Nijinande]], lady of the spear, why did you put down the spear?  
They do not answer. The wind answers. The wind goes through the house of the gods.  

*Weep, Unjinlathu, weep, for the fire came in the summer.*  
*Weep, Unjinlathu, weep, for the fire came again.*  

The men of [[pol:15|Kavu]] came down from their hills,  
they came down in the season of threshing, they came down with bronze.  
This was the [[evt:502|Kavu–Unjinlathu War]]; four summers it ran,  
four summers the barley of Luthe was trampled, four summers the oxen were driven.  
In the fifth summer they came to our walls.  

Before the walls our general went out, our men went out,  
five hundred and more went out, they went out singing.  
Two hundred and more did not come back. Two hundred and more lay down at the gate.  
This was the [[evt:510|Battle of Unzhinlathu]]. The field was not ours.  
Nijinande turned her face. The gate was left without a man.  

*Weep, Unjinlathu, weep, for the fire came in the summer.*  

Then they came in. This was the [[evt:511|First Sack of Unzhinlathu]].  
They came in at the gate of the barley-sellers; they came in at the gate of the water.  
They took the bronze from the house of the king.  
They took the cloth from the house of the women.  
They took the oil from the jars; what they did not take, they broke.  
The holy bread of [[dei:31|Kumbe]] they threw down; dogs ate it.  
The old men sat in the dust. The children were not counted.  
The mother called for her son; her son did not answer.  
The son called for his mother; his mother was led away.  

[[dei:29|Bumbe]] of the sea, you saw it from afar.  
Kumbe of the night, you saw it close. You did not close your eye.  

*[Column ii.]*

In the next summer they came again; the wound was opened again.  
This was the [[evt:516|Second Sack of Unzhinlathu]]. What was left, they took.  
Then the elders of [[pol:24|Unjinlathu]] went out with dust on their heads,  
then the lord of Unjinlathu set his hand to the oath.  
This was the [[evt:517|Conquest of Unjinlathu]]. The land was given to Kavu.  
The seat of our lords was given to Kavu. The fields of Luthe were given to Kavu.  

*Weep, Unjinlathu, weep, for the fire came again.*  

Now the boy who saw the fire is a man with grey in his beard.  
Now the girl who hid in the jar is a mother of sons.  
Their sons speak the speech of the market; they bow to Kavu.  
Their sons do not know the names of the gate. I know the names of the gate.  
I sing them. I sing them in the 51st year of Unjinlathu,  
for I count the years of Unjinlathu, and not the years of Kavu.  

All things turn on the great wheel, say the priests of [[mov:4|Khupande]];  
what ends will begin again; what falls will rise.  
Let it be so. Khuthaze, turn the wheel.  
Pugazhu, rise over Unjinlathu as you rose before the fire.  
Nijinande, lady of the spear, take up the spear you put down.  

*Weep, Unjinlathu, weep, until the wheel turns.*  

> This lament is sung on the day of the first fire. Whoever scrapes it from the clay, may Kumbe withhold her light from him.
