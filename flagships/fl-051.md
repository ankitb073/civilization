To [[per:4841|Huzhol'igwi]], the man of [[pol:157|I'emim]], my servant, the dust beneath my feet, say: thus says [[per:4809|A'mapayal]], the great queen, queen of [[pol:155|I'ith]], your lady, beloved of [[dei:11|Nizhashta]], whose foot is lame and whose arm is long.

With me it is well. With my house it is well. With my soldiers, my asses and my granaries it is well. With [[set:152|Tantethi]], my city, it is well. With you, now that you are my servant, may it be well also.

Hear concerning the hostage. The young man of your house, whom you gave into my hand at the gate of I'emim as the tablet commands, has come to Tantethi. I have seen him with my eyes. On the road he wept. Now he weeps no more. He eats bread at my table. He drinks beer from my jar. He wears a cloak of good wool, which my own women wove. He sleeps in the house of the queen's servants, and a lamp burns by him. No man strikes him. No man shames him. Thus I keep my word, as a great queen keeps it.

But hear also this. So long as the tribute of I'emim comes to Tantethi in its season, so long will the young man eat bread at my table. If the tribute does not come, if it comes light, if the silver is mixed with lead, then the bread will be taken from him. Then the lamp will be taken from him. Do not make me write you a second tablet about this.

Why did you go against me? Why did I'emim hold back the gifts that were due to I'ith? Year upon year my messengers went to you, and year upon year you sent them back with empty hands. So [[evt:4484|the war]] came upon you. It was not I who began it. It was the hand of I'emim that began it, by holding back what was due.

At [[evt:4504|Fiezi]] your men came out against me like a cloud of flies over a dead ox. My soldiers struck them. Their captain fell in the field. Their dead lay in heaps, one upon another, and the vultures of [[dei:8|Nish'il]] grew fat. At [[evt:4548|the city of I'emim]] you shut your gate. My soldiers broke your gate. As your city was taken once in the old days, so I took it again: [[evt:4549|it was sacked a second time]], and the smoke of it went up to the sky, and you saw it from the wall.

Then you came out to me. You bowed seven times and seven times on your belly and on your back. You set your seal beside my seal on [[evt:4550|the tablet of I'emim]], in [[set:118|I'emim]], before the gods. The fields that I took I hold. The tribute is fixed upon the tablet, so much each year, for many years. You are my servant. Your land is the footstool of my land. For ten years no spear of I'emim shall be lifted against I'ith.

You sat down on a seat that was not your father's. I do not ask how you came to it. That is a matter between you and your gods. I ask only for the tribute, and the tribute I will have.

[[dei:6|Nili]] the river saw it. [[dei:7|Yuf]] the fire saw it. Nish'il the sky saw it. Shiminto saw it, and Masto, and Nizhashta. The gods of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]] are witnesses between us. If you keep the tablet, they will keep you. If you break the tablet, they will break you, and I am their hand.

Send your messenger to me quickly. Let him bring the first tribute in [[cur:105|ikhal]] of good silver, weighed on a true balance, and let him bring a word for the young man from his kin. I will give it to him myself, and I will see his face when he hears it.

Thus says A'mapayal, queen of I'ith, in the 8th year of A'mapayal. The tablet is finished.
