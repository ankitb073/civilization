*[Obverse. A school-hand tablet, the signs large and careful. The beginning is complete.]*

In the name of [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the high sky, who sees all things.  
In the name of [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the black earth, who takes all things back.  
In the name of [[dei:19|Yenan]], lord of the moon, who counts the nights.

I, a servant of the house of tablets in [[set:18|Lenunushal]], write what I saw. I write it so that it is not forgotten. I write it in the 28th year of [[per:190|Venululath]], our lady, the queen of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]], may she live.

Hear it. The men of [[pol:1|Tallin]] came down into our land. They came down year after year, with spears, with donkeys, with dust. In the year before the fire, at the [[evt:243|Battle of Tathriubu]], they broke our spears. Then they came to our walls and sat down in a ring. That was the [[evt:244|First Siege of Linnshal]]. The ring was around us all the winter. The barley in the storehouse grew less. The barley in the storehouse grew less. The children cried at night, and the mothers had nothing to put in their mouths.

In the spring our men went out of the gate to drive them off. That was the [[evt:249|Battle of Linnshal]]. I stood on the wall. I saw it. Our men went out with their spears bright, and the men of Tallin were many, many, as the locusts of the field are many. Our men did not come back in through the gate. Some came back. Not many came back.

Then the men of Tallin came in.

I saw it. I write it. They broke the gate at the place of the potters. They came in at the potters' gate, and the pots were broken under their feet. They came into the street of the bronze-workers. They took the bronze. They came into the street of the weavers. They took the cloth. They took the goats from the pens. They took the grain that was left. They took the young men and tied their hands with cords. They took the young women. My sister's daughter they took. I do not know where she is.

I saw the house of [[dei:14|Vulme]], our lady of the spear. They pulled down her doorposts. They took the bronze that our fathers had hung in her house. They took it for their own Vulme, as if she were theirs alone. Vulme, see it. Vulme, remember it.

I saw the fire. The street of the reed-cutters burned. The street of the oil-pressers burned. The smoke went up to Dumamnul, and Dumamnul looked down and did not stop it.

But hear this also. The house of tablets did not burn. They came to the door; a captain of Tallin stood at the door; he said, "Leave it." The tablets of our fathers are whole. [[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire turned his face from that door. I give him thanks. I put oil on his altar every morning.

This was the [[evt:250|First Sack of Linnshal]]. Let the scribes after me call it so.

*[Reverse.]*

After the fire, our lady the queen went out. Venululath went out to them, in a plain cloak, with dust on her head. She did not weep before them. She sat with the king of Tallin, and they swore. That was the [[evt:251|Treaty of Linnshal]]. Fields that were ours are theirs now. Silver goes to Tallin each year, a long count of years. So the wheel turns. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] say: what ends will begin again. I hope they are right. I write it so that I remember.

The [[evt:236|First Tallin–Lenunushal War]] is ended. The walls are mended. The potters' gate has a new door. But the street of the reed-cutters is still black.

Whoever reads this tablet, pour water on the ground for the men who did not come in through the gate. Pour water for my sister's daughter. [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]], lady of the grain, fill our storehouse again.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in the 28th year of Venululath, in the month of the first threshing, in Lenunushal.
