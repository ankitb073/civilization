In AY 1001 a scribe marching with the army of [[pol:171|Tallin]] wrote to his sovereign from a battlefield in enemy country. The tablet survives almost whole. He describes the fear of the young soldiers, the ram sacrificed before the army, and the counting of enemy right hands. Then he turns to his own side: "Six hundred and seventy-seven men of Tallen fell. We burned them... Their names are written on a second tablet. Let my lady send it to their fathers." The lady was [[per:5808|Kalidath]], queen of Tallin for fifty-five years and later called the Great. The scribe assumed she would want the names. Nothing else in the record tells us so much about how her subjects thought of her.

## The eldest surviving daughter

She was born in 965, a Jinru woman, and her name means "pure light". Her father, [[per:5491|Yathshiyin]], had reigned since 944. Her mother died when she was four. Three elder sisters, all born in 962, died in childhood, the last when Kalidath was eleven. A sister born in her own year lived long, and a brother followed in 968. Tallin's law gave the crown to the eldest child, son or daughter, so when her father died in 981 the [[evt:5530|Accession of Kalidath]] made her queen at sixteen.

She married a man a year older. Their son [[per:6045|Pavbokhmanen]] was born in 990. A daughter born in 995 died at nine, and a younger son, born in 998, at fourteen.

## A queen in the field

Within two years she was at war. The [[evt:5546|Tallin–Ramthulnga War]] (983–985) was fought against [[pol:226|Ramthulnga]], a kingdom founded in 958. Tallin's palace chronicle claims that its queen, [[per:5800|Pimpadiyath]], "broke the peace", but the record names Tallin as the attacker. At the [[evt:5558|Battle of Nunuunge (AY 984)]] Kalidath, nineteen, led 15,235 men against 719. Tallin lost 1,232 and Ramthulnga 290, and Pimpadiyath, aged twenty-one, was killed. The chronicle adds that "her head was brought to the queen". As a translator I note that its scribe calls Kalidath "the queen", and that her victory stele, cut from the old formula, says that "she" killed 1,300 soldiers and set it up for "her lord". The old formulas had been made for kings, and the scribes changed only the pronouns and titles for her.

Pimpadiyath's daughter, about four years old, succeeded under a regent. In 985 a Tallin general took her town. The [[evt:5566|Conquest of Ramthulnga]] was sealed between a queen of twenty and the child [[per:5838|Nurpimthuth]], the last ruler of her kingdom, and fourteen districts passed to Tallin.

## The long war with Yida

The [[evt:5693|First Tallin–Yida War]] (999–1008) was the test of her reign. Each side's chronicle says the other broke the peace; the record names Tallin as the aggressor against the [[pol:165|Yida]] Empire. Her chief general won at Virathkanu in 1001, where the dispatch was written. Then he lost three times, in 1002 at a cost of 9,144 men, in 1004 within sight of Tallin's own capital, and again in 1006. In the year the fighting reached her capital, her nine-year-old daughter died.

The queen herself did not lose. At the [[evt:5750|Battle of Shivange (AY 1003)]] she led 15,415 men against 12,693 and lost 797 to the enemy's 5,415. At the [[evt:5767|Battle of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 1005)]] she was outnumbered, 11,084 to 12,284, and still won, losing 1,691 men to 3,980. Yida's king, [[per:5678|Beroybethun II]], fell on the field, and Tallin's chronicler again records a head "brought to the queen". His death threw Yida into a usurpation, and [[per:6021|Luvanaven]] took its throne. At the [[evt:5797|Battle of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 1008)]] she won a third time. Yida's own scribe blames "a great storm" for that defeat.

The [[evt:5798|Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 1008)]] gave Tallin forty districts and two towns, Binge among them, with tribute for thirty years and a ten-year truce. It also bound the two houses together: her son, eighteen, was to marry Luvanaven's daughter [[per:6049|Nauduth]], who was fourteen. Yida's scribe says the peace was made "out of mercy for the common folk" and that "nothing of worth was lost". The war had cost Tallin 17,145 men and Yida 23,215.

## Birnadu, and the war with a daughter-in-law

Her younger son died in 1012 and her husband in 1015. In 1014, after [[pol:199|Birnadu]] had been beaten by another neighbour, she opened the [[evt:5855|Tallin–Birnadu War]] for tribute, with [[pol:184|Egu]] as her ally. At the [[evt:5868|Battle of Zhivampive (AY 1015)]], at fifty, she defeated a force under Birnadu's king, called the Red, losing 2,161 men to 718. Her generals won the remaining battles. The [[evt:5889|Treaty of Yishdhagshal (AY 1018)]] gave Tallin nine districts and twenty-one years of tribute. On the other side of the table sat [[per:6138|Tumningun]], a king of eleven.

The [[evt:5964|Second Tallin–Yida War]] (1027–1033) was fought by her generals, while Yida's scribe called her "swollen with pride". In 1032 Luvanaven died and Nauduth became ruler of Yida. In 1033 she led Yida's army at Binge against her mother-in-law's general and lost. The [[evt:6018|Treaty of Binge (AY 1033)]], sworn at [[set:147|Binge]], survives on a fragmentary clay tablet: "Thus says Kaledath, great queen, queen of Tallen... The war which Navodoth began is ended." The record says Tallin began it. Yida gave up thirty-two districts and three towns, with the river as boundary: "No man of the one land shall plough beyond the stones." Tallin's merchants were to pass "unhindered". It was the fifty-third year of her reign.

## The long shadow

Nauduth died in 1035. Kalidath died in 1036, aged seventy-one, of natural causes, and the [[evt:6041|Accession of Pavbokhmanen]] passed the crown to her son, then forty-six. Yida collapsed the next year. The record lists among the causes her victories at Shivange and Piyikhzhidhe and both her treaties. A Yida king of those last days wrote from exile that "at Shivange they fell. At Piyikhzhidhe they fell", and protested, "I did not give those towns." In 1049 the son of Pavbokhmanen and Nauduth became king of Tallin, the grandson of both the conqueror and the usurper she had beaten. Tallin itself lasted until 1141.

Over her reign Tallin grew from about 151 districts to 246, and from fewer than six million people to about eight and a half. She commanded in five battles and won all five, once against a larger army and twice against near-equal ones, while her best general lost four. Few epithets in this series are so well supported. The cost is also on record: a queen and a king killed in battle, a child queen who signed away her kingdom, and the burned dead of Virathkanu. Their names went to their fathers on a second tablet, which has not survived.
