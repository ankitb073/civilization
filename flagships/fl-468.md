*In the name of the One Power, Maker of the world and Judge of its last day, before whom the old gods are servants and shadows, and by whose light every soul may see its way.*

**To all the faithful of the [[mov:24|Udunkala]], to lords spiritual and temporal, to the cities, the free villages and the sworn men of every degree, and to whomsoever these letters shall come, greeting.**

Be it known, in the year 3667 of the Udunkala, that the realm of [[pol:1496|Tadudüvi]] is free, and owes neither homage nor fealty, neither service of host nor service of hearth, neither tithe nor toll nor any due whatsoever, to the rulers of [[pol:1451|Kabaf]] or to any who claims through them.

**I. Of the yoke.** It is written that the One weighs every deed in a just balance, and that no lord may demand of his man more than he gives. For many generations the rulers of Kabaf took from this land its sons for war and its grain for tribute, and gave back neither justice in dealings nor moderation in demands. Our fathers rose against that yoke twice before, and twice it was laid on them again. They did not cease to pray, nor the One to hear.

**II. Of the rising.** The people of the land of [[reg:21|Tadudüvi]] stood again, in the [[evt:35267|Revolt of Tadudüvi]], under our lord [[per:34777|Nadigi]], whom the One raised up to be their king; and when the rulers of Kabaf sent their hosts to bring them back into bondage, there began the war that is now ended, the [[evt:35268|Third Tadudüvi War of Independence]].

**III. Of the judgement of arms.** Let these be remembered in every church of the realm on their days:

- At [[set:37|Ntsivi]], the king himself led the host against [[per:34605|Natadtsoth]], captain of Kabaf, and the field was ours; that is the [[evt:35332|Battle of Nzhivi]].
- At [[set:227|Nzhinduvi]], the king's captain [[per:34824|Navande]] met Natadtsoth again, and the field was ours.
- Before [[set:140|Adunambi]], in the fifth year, Navande was thrown back by [[per:34844|Nangatsu]], and many of ours fell. We do not hide it. The One chastens those whom it would keep, and pride was in our camp.
- Before Adunambi in the sixth year, Navande met Natadtsoth a third time, and Natadtsoth fell in the field, and the field was ours.
- Before Adunambi in the seventh year, Navande met Nangatsu again, and Nangatsu broke, and the field was ours.

We confess that the price was dear. Our dead outnumber theirs more than twice over, for the men of Tadudüvi went against walls and well-armed companies with bills and staves and the bare hands of free men. Let no one say freedom came cheap. Let every lord of this realm who holds a manor remember on whose graves it stands.

**IV. Of the peace.** Thereafter the rulers of Kabaf, seeing the hand of the One against them, sent their envoys to Adunambi, and there was sworn the [[evt:35378|Treaty of Athnambi]], by which Kabaf renounced all lordship over this land, and a truce was set between the two realms for fifteen years.

**V. Of what follows.** Wherefore we declare:

that Nadigi, our lord, holds this realm of no man, but of the One Power alone, and shall answer for it at the Judgement and nowhere else;

that every oath sworn by any man of this land to the rulers of Kabaf is loosed, and that whoever keeps such an oath in secret is forsworn against his true lord;

that the lords of this realm shall do homage to Nadigi at Adunambi before the next feast of harvest, and that their men shall do homage to them as of old;

that justice shall be done in dealings, and moderation kept in dues, as the faith commands, so that it may never be said of Tadudüvi what we have said of Kabaf.

**Given at Adunambi, under the great seal of the realm, in the year 3667 of the Udunkala. Let it be read aloud in every church and market of the land on three successive feast-days.**
