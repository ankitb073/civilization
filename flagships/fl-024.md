I'shil Ash, queen of [[pol:1119|Imshi]], to the council of the city of [[set:116|Imshi]] and to all who keep its laws: health.

The old teachers say that fortune, like a river, does not flow twice over the same stones. Whoever wrote that never campaigned in [[reg:6|I'ith]]. Three times now our army has stood before [[set:152|Tanshethi]], and three times the same stones have watched us. The first time the gods gave the field to the enemy. The second time I commanded myself, and I will not pretend to you that the day was ours. This summer, in the fourteenth year of the [[evt:27036|War of the Iethithi Succession]], we came to Tanshethi a third time, and the river has turned.

[[per:26701|Nadal]] held the command, and I have nothing but praise for him. The usurping house of [[pol:1116|Iethithi]] brought nearly twenty-seven thousand men to the field. Ours were a little fewer, about twenty-six thousand. Their general [[per:26716|Mumawlo]], who has stood against us in every battle these seven years, set his line along the ridge as he always does. Nadal did not attack the ridge. He let the enemy come down to him. By nightfall the slopes belonged to us, and we buried one thousand nine hundred of our own. Of theirs I am told more than three thousand fell. I believe the figure, since our burial parties have not finished counting.

Do not think I am drunk on this victory. The teachers of the [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] tell us that the wise man takes no pleasure in plunder and tries to keep his appetite as moderate as a harvest in a dry year. I have taken their teaching to heart. I have forbidden the sale of captives, and the spoil of the camp has gone to the temples so that each god may have its due. A war over an inheritance is still a war over justice. If we fight it greedily, we lose the cause even while we win the ground.

You know the cause. The throne of Iethithi was never the property of [[per:26365|Luesh'ayof]], to hand on as he pleases. Our claim stands in the oldest law. Since the [[evt:26578|First Imshi–Iethithi War]] they have mocked it, behind treaties that were nothing but a pause for breath. They answer argument with walls and a law with an army. Let them now answer Tanshethi.

I keep the whole account in front of me, and I ask you to read it too, the good and the bad. In the third year [[per:26695|Mumawim'am]] broke them at [[set:176|Azha]] and left more than six thousand of their men in the fields. At [[set:118|I'emim]] he won twice. In the first of those victories their general [[per:26694|Im'ampaya]] fell, and the house of Iethithi has never found another like him. Nadal beat them at [[set:26|Ba'mi]], and last year he beat them again before [[set:139|Bathshi]], where we put twenty-eight thousand men into the field. Against these I set our misfortunes: Iemi once, Tanshethi twice, and [[set:283|Khasnin]]. At Khasnin the enemy would not meet us in the open. They waited in the defiles like thieves. I have heard men call that generalship. I call it what a thief does on a dark road.

In all this I have had to look behind me as well as ahead. When the rebels rose in Auba, the army did not falter. The [[evt:27125|suppression of the Auba revolt]] cost us a season and not a single province. A state that can put down treason at home while it presses its rights abroad is not a tired state, whatever the gossips in the market say.

So I ask you for constancy. Grain is cheap this year, under a [[cur:603|shishli]] the measure, so feeding the army is not beyond the treasury, and I ask you not to cut the supply to save a little. The levies should march before the autumn rains. The festivals should be kept as usual, so that the enemy's spies report a city at ease rather than a city afraid.

The gods are many, and each keeps its own place. Ours has been the place of patience. Patience has now begun to pay.

Farewell. Written in the camp below Tanshethi, in the year 2834 from the founding of Imshi.
