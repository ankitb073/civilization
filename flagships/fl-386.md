*[A royal letter on fine parchment, with a second, coarser leaf stitched to it. The second leaf is in a different and unpractised hand.]*

[[per:26252|Ashni]], king of [[pol:1098|Mane]], to the Keeper of the Ordained in [[set:13|Tsivintuha]], greeting, and the peace of the One.

You will have heard, Reverend Keeper, that the man from the potters' ward was put to death yesterday at dawn, outside the river gate. You will not have heard what I now send you, because no one but the captain of the night watch and I have seen it. He found it folded inside the man's tunic and brought it to me unread, as he ought. I have read it twice. I enclose a faithful copy; read it before the rest of my letter.

> *To the brothers and sisters who keep the straight road, from one who walks the last of it.*
>
> They tell me I will die at dawn, and I believe them, for the king's men are honest in this at least. Do not grieve. Grief is a coin for those who think the wheel will bring the dead round again. We do not think so. We hold that time is a road, not a wheel, and that it runs from the first morning to the last judgement, and that every step is counted once and never walked again. Therefore I will not waste this step.
>
> They asked me to kneel to the ordained and be forgiven. I told them what we have always said: teachers are to be honoured, but no one owns the truth, not the ordained, not the king, not I. If the One wishes to speak to me, the One does not need a man in a robe to carry the message.
>
> They asked me whether I did not fear pain. I said that the good life is duty and right sacrifice, and that a duty which costs nothing is only a habit. This will cost something. Good.
>
> Keep the meetings. Keep them quietly. Pay the fines in [[cur:592|Midnatl]] when they demand them; do not lie about your names. The One is not served by lies. Our message is for all peoples, the men who hold my chains among them. Tell them so, gently, when they are off duty.
>
> I go straight on. Do not look for me on the wheel.

Now, Reverend Keeper, hear the rest.

I do not regret the edict. A kingdom is a single body, and a body cannot answer to two heads. The [[evt:26787|Persecution of the Udushmamash in Mane]] was ordered by me, in council, with your counsel; the [[mov:94|Udushmamash]] meet in secret, refuse the ordained, and teach the young that the order passed down from teacher to teacher since the founding of our faith is no order at all. Where every man is his own priest, every man will soon be his own king. I believed that in the spring, and I believe it still.

But I am obliged to tell you what troubles me, since I am obliged to tell you everything.

We of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] are taught that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering. I have released this man from suffering, it is true, in the bluntest way available to a king. I cannot say that I have shown him compassion. And I notice that he, whose teaching we call harsh, went to his death speaking of the men who chained him as people to be told the truth gently. I set it before you as a physician sets a symptom before a colleague.

Here is what I have decided, and you may correct me if it offends the order. The body is to be given to his household, not thrown into the river. His letter is not to be published, nor burned; this copy and the original are to be kept in the archive of the ordained, under seal, for whoever comes after us. Let them judge whether we were right. The wheel turns, we say; if so, they will have their own edicts to regret.

The edict stands. I will sign no new death warrants this month. Pray for the man from the potters' ward, and, if you think it proper, for the king.

Given at Tsivintuha, in the year 3761 from the founding of Tsivintuha, under my seal.
