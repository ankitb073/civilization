*To the Reverend Fathers, Teachers and Delegates assembled in Council at [[set:85|Nambi]], from one delegate among them, written at the palace on 20 Bini 2183 of the Vimikhulu, and to be read aloud at the closing session.*

Reverend Fathers,

I write to you as a delegate, and I beg you to read me as one. It is true that I am also [[per:43997|Najimi]], and that I wear the crown of [[pol:1638|Khishkhis]], under whose roof you have sat these many weeks; but our faith teaches that teachers are to be honoured and that no one owns the truth, and if no teacher owns it, still less does any sovereign. I therefore lay the crown aside for the length of this letter.

You will forgive me if I begin with what is plain. The [[evt:44955|Council of Nambi]] was called to reconcile the brethren of [[pol:1881|Angushi]] with the body of the faithful. It has not done so. Their delegates left the hall yesterday before the final reading, and I am told by those who accompanied them to the gate that they will not return. Let the acts of this Council say so first, and without ornament: we failed.

I have heard it said, once to my regret in open session, that the fault lies wholly with them: that the men of Angushi came to dispute and not to be persuaded, that they mistook obstinacy for conscience. I do not believe it. I saw men who had travelled far to sit in a city not their own, under the eye of a crown that has not always been their friend. Let us be honest, Fathers. Some of them remember that their fathers lost fields to this crown. They have heard, as all the world has heard, of the [[evt:44849|First Battle of Jyijinjivji]] and of the [[evt:44860|Twelfth Battle of Yaadhi]], and they have read the terms of the [[evt:44861|Treaty of Yaadhi]], to which I set my own hand. Is it so strange that they wondered whether a council held in Nambi was a council of the faith, or a council of the empire wearing the faith as a cloak?

I do not say they were right to wonder. I say that we gave them no great reason to stop.

Consider, Fathers, how this Council was arranged. Its sessions were held in a hall of my palace. Its delegates were lodged at the expense of my treasury, to the sum of many thousands of [[cur:901|Shikh]]. Its order of business was drawn up by my chancellor's office. Each was done with good intentions. But the wise, our teachers say, withdraw from wealth and appetite; and a council that eats at the empress's table should not be surprised if its guests suspect that it also speaks with the empress's tongue.

What, then, do I propose? Not a reproach to those who have gone, nor a decree against them. I have no power to make decrees in the faith, and I would refuse it if it were offered. I propose three things only.

First, that the acts of this Council record the arguments of the brethren of Angushi as fully and fairly as our own, in their own words, so that those who come after may weigh them without our gloss.

Second, that the door be left open. Let it be written that any delegate of Angushi may return to the body of the faithful without penalty and without first confessing error. The wheel turns; what ends will begin again. A council that has ended may be followed by another.

Third, that if another council be called, it be called somewhere other than [[set:85|Nambi]], and at no cost to my treasury, so that no one may say that the crown bought the outcome.

Some of you will think these proposals weak. I think them merely honest. The faith of [[mov:70|Vimikhulu]] is for all peoples; it was never meant to be the possession of one city or one throne. If we cannot persuade our brethren of that by argument, let us at least persuade them by conduct.

Your servant and sister in the faith,

Najimi, delegate

*Postscript, in her own hand:* I ask that this letter be read aloud and not merely entered in the acts. A letter that is only filed has not been sent.
