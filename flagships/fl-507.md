*[Obverse]*

Thus says [[per:13514|Nanüthnan]], king of [[pol:414|Tagath]], king of the four winds, son of Naguvu the king. Thus says the king in the fifth year of his seat, in the hall of the seat, in [[set:9|Tagath]], the city of the seat.

Hear it, [[dei:77|Pugazhu]], sun who sees all. Hear it, [[dei:73|Tithu]] of the storm. Hear it, [[dei:74|Binge]], mother of the barley. You turned your faces. I do not know why you turned your faces. I fed you. My father fed you. The smoke went up every morning. Yet you turned your faces.

Hear what was done to Tagath. Let it be written, so that no one says the king did not see it.

First the gods sent the sickness. The [[evt:13436|Silent Ague]] came into the cities without a sound. It came into the house of the potter and the house of the governor. Of every three who sat at the hearth, one went down. Four years it walked in the land. The fields had no reapers. The priests had no voices left for weeping.

And all those years the strangers were coming. In the [[evt:13315|Migration of the Khukhudhul into Ng'jinaguh]] they came down in their thousands and their tens of thousands, and Tagath received them, as a great house receives the hungry. They ate our bread. They did not keep our oaths.

And the men of the old house never forgot their grudge. They said: "Naguvu took the seat; the seat was not his." They called it the [[evt:13459|Usurpation of Naguvu]]. I say: the gods gave my father the seat, and the gods do not give in vain. But the old house whispered, and the whisper went from city to city.

*[Reverse]*

Then the governors broke their oaths. In the [[evt:13524|Secession of Kambizhin]] the first one stood up and said: "I am a king." The rebel came against me with spears. At the [[evt:13554|Battle of Dadavli]] my host met the rebels, and we were many and they were few, and we broke them. Let it be written: Tagath won at Dadavli. But the rebel did not bow.

Then in the [[evt:13540|Secession of Kayagu]] a second stood up and said: "I am a king." And the governors saw that the seat was weak, and in this year each one stood up. [[pol:598|Luthe]] stands up, in the very streets of Tagath. [[pol:599|Kane]] stands up. [[pol:600|Tyimbi]] stands up. [[pol:601|Pyivi]] stands up. They were my governors. They ate my bread. They wore my seal on their necks. Now each says: "I am a king." This is the [[evt:13555|Collapse of the Tagath State]]. It was not done by an enemy. It was done by the hand that was fed.

Now hear the last words of the seat.

The seal of the king I lay down in the house of the gods, before the image of Pugazhu. Let no traitor wear it.

The tablets of the court I give to the priests of [[mov:11|Kyif]]. Let the teachers keep them. Let them dispute over them in the days to come, and let them know who was faithful and who was not.

The silver of the treasury, the [[cur:228|Gathu]] that remains, I give to the soldiers who stood at Dadavli. Each man his share. Let no governor take it.

The soldiers who stood by me, I release from their oath. Go to your houses. Go to your fields. Tell your sons that you did not break faith.

*[Lower edge]*

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The seat of Tagath ends. Let it begin again.

Whoever broke his oath to the king, may Tithu break his roof. May Binge burn his barley. May his sons sit in the dust and his name be forgotten.

Thus says Nanüthnan. The scribe has written it. The seat is empty.
