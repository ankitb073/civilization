---
title: The Tablet in the Reed Basket
---
*[Small tablet, found folded inside a clay case shaped like a loaf]*

To the chiefs of the hill villages, to the keepers of the upper fords, to every man of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]] who still holds a spear outside the walls, say: thus says [[per:190|Venululath]], your lady, she who sits in the high house of [[set:18|Lenunushal]].

By [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear, by [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky, by [[dei:15|Vinba]] who holds the dead, I write with my own hand, because the scribe is on the wall. Hear me.

The men of [[pol:1|Tallin]] sit around the city. They sit at the river gate. They sit at the gate of the threshing floors. They sit at the gate of the tombs. At night their fires are many, like the stars that [[dei:19|Yenan]] leads across the sky. This is the twenty-fifth year that I sit in the high house, and never have I seen so many fires.

You know of [[evt:243|Battle of Tathriubu]]. I do not hide it. Our men went out, one thousand and seven hundred and more, against three thousand and four hundred of Tallin. Seven hundred of ours lay down. Three hundred and more of theirs lay down; let that be counted also. The field was theirs. The remnant came back to me through the river gate, and the gate was shut behind them, and the next day the men of Tallin came. So it is now: the [[evt:244|First Siege of Linnshal]].

Hear what is in the city. The barley of [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] is in the storehouse; it will last until the moon is twice full, and not longer. The water of the well is sweet. The walls are whole. The men on the walls are tired, but they are not afraid. The women carry stones to the walls in baskets. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] burn fat to [[dei:18|Eva]] at every dawn, and the smoke goes up straight.

*[Reverse]*

Do not forget who I am. I am she who stood at Tallin in the fourteenth year of my seat. I am she who set her seal in their own city at the [[evt:204|Treaty of Taulin]]. Then Tallin paid silver into my hand, heavy silver, and opened its markets to our boats, and swore ten years of peace by the gods. Their old men remember it. That is why they have come. They have not forgotten their shame; let them not forget their fear.

Now I command you. Gather the men of the hills. Gather the herdsmen with their slings. Gather the young men who are of age, and the old men who can still throw. Come down by the high road on a moonless night. Fall upon their camp at the gate of the tombs, where their fires are thin. When I see your torches on the hill, I will open the river gate, and we will strike them from two sides, like the two jaws of a dog.

If you cannot come, send barley. Send it on rafts by night down the small channel, where the reeds are tall. The boy who carries this tablet knows the way. Feed him. Do not beat him. He is the son of a fisherman, and he swims like an otter.

The [[evt:236|First Tallin–Lenunushal War]] is not finished. The wheel turns. Tallin has been beaten before, and it can be beaten again. Do not let it be said that Lenunushal was given up while its lady still breathed.

*[Edge, in a different, hasty hand]*

Break the case. Read it. Then break the tablet.
