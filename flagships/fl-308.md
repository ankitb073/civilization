*[Obverse.]*

To the elders of [[set:25|Kavu]], to the priests of the high house, to the mother of the king, say:  
thus says [[per:649|Nambave]], your king, king of [[pol:15|Kavu]], beloved of [[dei:28|Khuthaze]] of the wide sky.

With me it is well. With the spears it is well. With the donkeys it is well. With the captives it is well.  
May it be well with you. May it be well with the high house. May it be well with the granary.

Hear, elders. Hear, priests. The men of [[pol:24|Unjinlathu]] are men of the crooked mouth. In my fifth year they shut their hand. They said: the land of [[reg:27|Luthe]] is ours, the pastures are ours, the threshing floors are ours. They lied. Their fathers lied, and their sons lie. So I went down. I went down with the bronze, I went down with the spears, I went down with the young men of Kavu. This is the [[evt:502|Kavu–Unjinlathu War]]; they began it with their lying mouth, and I will end it with my hand.

In this year, my sixth year, I came to [[set:115|Unjinlathu]], to the walls of Unjinlathu. Many were the spears of Kavu. Few were the spears of Unjinlathu. [[per:662|Nabule]] stood against me, Nabule the boaster, Nabule the general of Unjinlathu, who wears his bronze like a bride wears her beads.

At dawn I made the offering to [[dei:32|Nijinande]], lady of the spear. I gave her a white ram. I gave her a black ram. I gave her beer from the jar of the king. And [[dei:30|Pugazhu]] the sun rose behind my back and stood in the eyes of Unjinlathu, so that they could not see.

I went against Nabule. My brothers went against Nabule. The war-carts of Kavu went against Nabule.  
Nabule broke. Nabule ran. Nabule left his shields in the field.  
The field was Kavu's. The field was Kavu's. Let the scribe write it twice: the field was Kavu's.

Our dead were few. Their dead were many. Their dead lay in the barley like stones in a riverbed; the vultures of the hill came down and were glad. The men of Unjinlathu say that they lost little. Do not believe them. They lie about the dead as they lied about the pastures.

Hear, elders. I have not yet taken the walls. The gate of Unjinlathu is shut, and Nabule sits behind it and weeps. The land of Luthe is not yet under my foot. I will not come home until it is.

Therefore send me barley, two loads for each ten men.  
Send me oil. Send me sandals. Send me the young men of the second muster.  
Send me the arrow-makers, all of them; let none stay behind to sleep.

And you, priests, open the high house. Pour to Khuthaze of the wide sky, that he hold back the rain until the harvest is in. Pour to [[dei:29|Bumbe]] of the sea, that the salt-boats come in safely. Pour to [[dei:31|Kumbe]] of the moon, that she light the road for the donkeys at night. Pour again to Nijinande, the lady of the spear, for she has been good to me. Let none of the gods be forgotten, each in their place, each with their due, as it was in the days of our fathers. What ends will begin again; what was ours will be ours again.

To the mother of the king, say: your son is whole. He has no wound. He eats bread and drinks beer. He has a cloak of Unjinlathu wool, taken from a dead captain. He sends it to you.

*[Reverse, lower edge.]*

> Written in the camp before Unjinlathu, in the 6th year of Nambave.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet on the road, may Nijinande break his spear.
