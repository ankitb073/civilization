*Words which Ish Tliwi, king of Litlatlti, spoke at the swearing of the peace before the gate of Litlitli. The scribe of the house of the queen wrote them on this clay in the 2nd year of Nitllu Tli.*

Hear me, [[per:12975|Nitllu Tli]], queen of [[pol:559|Nimit]], daughter of a great queen. Hear me, priests of [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]], who keep the oaths. Hear me, [[dei:83|Hath]] of the sky, who sees. Hear me, [[dei:88|Mahtu]] the sun, who sees by day. Hear me, [[dei:84|Lashli]] the moon, who sees by night.

I am [[per:12836|Ish Tliwi]], king of [[pol:484|Litlatlti]]. Sixteen years I have sat on the seat of my fathers. I did not ask for this day. I did not want this day. But the day has come, and I stand in it.

Queen, you are small. Your feet do not reach the ground. The queen your mother came against me with spears, and now she has gone down to [[dei:87|Hutlpi]], to the house below, and you sit in her seat. I do not speak against her. The dead are the dead. But hear how the [[evt:12920|war of Nimit and Litlatlchi]] went, and let the gods judge.

At [[evt:12935|Izimue]] I stood myself against the queen your mother. My men were few. Her men were many, two for each of mine. The river rose behind us; [[dei:85|Niwi]] of the river did not hold her water for us. So we gave ground. At [[evt:12944|Mivimäkhi]] my general stood, and he too gave ground, for the sun was in his eyes. At [[evt:12963|Litlitli]], before this gate where I stand, we fought a third time, and the gate was opened. It was not opened by the spear. It was opened by men of my own house, who had eaten silver from Nimit. Let Hutlpi take them. Let their names be dust. So came the [[evt:12964|sack of Litlitli]]. My granaries burned. My storehouses were emptied. My women wept on the roofs.

Now I swear. Hear the words of the [[evt:12965|peace of Litlitli]].

I give to Nimit the town that is given. I give to Nimit the land of Nis, its fields, its water, its threshing floors. I bow my head.

I give to Nimit tribute, [[cur:297|Antli]] of silver, each year at the harvest, for sixteen years. As I have sat sixteen years on my seat, so for sixteen years Litlatlti will bring silver. Let it be counted. Let it be weighed. I bow my head.

I open my markets to the merchants of Nimit. Their asses will pass my gate. Their boats will lie at my quay. No toll will be taken that the treaty does not name. I bow my head.

For ten years no spear of Litlatlti will go up against Nimit. For ten years no spear of Nimit will go up against Litlatlti. So it is written. So it is sworn.

If I break this oath, let Hath break my seat. Let Mahtu burn my fields. Let [[dei:86|Shu]] of the grain wither my barley in the ear. Let Hutlpi open her mouth and take me.

If Nimit breaks this oath, let the same gods do the same to Nimit. That also I say. That also the scribe will write.

Queen, I am a man of many harvests, and you are a child of few. I do not weep before you. I weep before the gods, who turned their faces from Litlatlti and showed them to Nimit. But the gods turn. What is low is lifted. What is lifted is brought low. Remember it, little queen, when you are grown.

I have spoken. Let the priests pour the water. Let the lamb be cut. Let the peace be sworn.
