title: Ruined by Victory: The Last Half-Century of Yida

The [[evt:1589|Collapse of the Yida State]] in AY 481 is usually told as the end of a long defeat. The record does not support that story. [[pol:25|Yida]] won every war it fought in its last half-century, and the four battles the documentary consensus counts among the causes of its fall were defeats inside victorious wars. I shall argue that they mattered for what they cost and revealed, not for ground lost, and that the decisive blow was political: a crowded succession ending in the [[evt:1553|Usurpation of Talon]] in AY 476.

## Four defeats in two victories

In the Fourth Diebeguvebenga Trade War (AY 434–445) Talenonoy attacked and Yida prevailed, winning six of eight battles and the Treaty of Bimnge. The two losses were costly. At the [[evt:1322|Battle of Bimnge (AY 438)]] Yida fielded 19,756 men against 8,245 and lost 5,029 against 1,173. At the [[evt:1339|Battle of Biniynge (AY 441)]] it brought 11,118 against 6,355 and lost 3,272 against 1,489. Over the whole war Yida lost 9,924 men, more than the attackers' 8,042.

The Yida–Egu War (AY 455–463) reversed the roles. Yida attacked for tribute, again won six of eight battles, and imposed the Treaty of Shiniy. Yet at the [[evt:1440|Battle of Bimnge (AY 457)]] (14,321 against 8,000; losses 3,030 against 1,384) and the [[evt:1458|Battle of Ture (AY 459)]] (16,359 against 9,937; losses 9,319 against 2,484) it was beaten by armies little more than half its size. Its total losses, 14,171, again exceeded the 10,281 of the side it defeated.

In all four defeats Yida had the larger army, so these look like failures of command, not of manpower. And one commander, Shikhyin, led Yida's army in three of them: Biniynge in 441, then Bimnge and Ture. Together the four cost Yida some 20,700 men.

## What the court remembered

The court's monuments are silent on all this. The Victory stele of Nonakhlun: Battle of Ture (AY 439) and its companion for Bimnge in 445 commemorate the wins of the first war, and two steles of Lulrindominin I, for Zhivampive and Riniy, those of the second. The Yida account of the Yida–Egu War goes further. It makes Egu the aggressor, "swollen [...] coveted the lands of Yida", where the record has Yida marching for tribute, and it has the army at Bimnge withdraw "in good order", losing 1,562 men against 3,680 of the enemy. The record's figures for that day are 3,030 against 1,384. The motive is plain. The account was set down in AY 487 by a scribe who still dated by "the 254th year of Yida", six years after there was no Yida to date by. A vanished court's servant had every reason to remember its wars as just.

## Succession, not scarcity

Climate and economy are the weakest candidates. The fifth century cooled somewhat, but no famine or pestilence in the record touches Yida in its last fifty years. On the record's estimates the empire was larger when it broke than before the Egu war. Its population rose from about 6.7 million in AY 450 to about 8.7 million by 480, its territory grew by roughly a third, and its army in 480 was larger than at any point in the previous century.

What changed was the throne. Nonakhlun the Victorious reigned from 405 to 450. In the next twenty-six years Yida had four more rulers: Lulrindominin I, Ngithnoth, Lulrindominin II and finally Talon, who seized power in 476. Yida had a usurper for its last five years, and Talon outlived the empire he seized by nine.

The breakup was a partition along old seams. [[pol:61|Tallin]] kept the capital at Taulin and lasted until 825, under the name of the kingdom that had held Taulin before Yida's rise in 234. [[pol:62|Buneynga]] at Biniynge and [[pol:65|Chinontonu]] at Zhinontunu likewise revived the names of kingdoms Yida had absorbed, Buneynga as recently as 411. [[pol:63|Pumi]] formed at Himi, annexed only in 470, and [[pol:64|Tupchinga]] and the short-lived city-state of [[pol:66|Punennushal]] (481–504) complete the six. The battles did not destroy Yida. They taught its provinces that the centre could be beaten, and the usurpation taught them that it could be seized.
