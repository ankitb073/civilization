*Official text of the lecture delivered by Chairman [[per:47103|Nazhigi]] before the assembled teachers of the national schools, in the Hall of the People at [[set:85|Nambi]], 18 Nikh 2528 of the Vimikhulu. Printed by order, for use in every classroom of [[pol:1638|Khishkhih]].*

Comrade teachers,

You have asked me to speak about history, and I have agreed, because a nation that does not know how states die will not know how to keep its own alive. I shall speak tonight of what our scholars call the Great Collapse: the age, four thousand years ago and more, when the old kingdoms of the world fell one after another like the carriages of a derailed train.

First, the figures, which the Bureau of Antiquities has compiled from the surviving tablets. Between [[evt:1589|the fall of Yida]] and [[evt:6958|the second fall of Tallin]], a span of some six hundred and sixty years, fourteen great states broke apart into more than sixty fragments. Fourteen! One every forty-seven years, on the average: so that a man might be born under one crown, marry under a second, and be buried under a third. [[evt:2822|Talenonoy]] fell into three. [[evt:4038|Navnga]] fell into six, and [[evt:4077|Tallin]] six years later into five, and [[evt:4130|Nelo]] and [[evt:4213|Egu]] within the next fifteen years. Then [[evt:5441|Mii]], then [[evt:5573|Luthe]], then Nelo a second time and Yida a second time, then Khie'a, then Bäni, then [[evt:6753|I'ith]] into six, and at last Tallin again.

Why? Our enemies' historians blame drought, or plague, or the anger of gods. But drought falls on every field alike, and not every realm fell. Those that fell had one thing in common. Each was ruled by men who wanted more than they could hold. Each broke itself in petty wars over a few districts, and each, when the bill came, found that its provinces would sooner have six masters than one. The faith of [[mov:70|Vimikhulu]] teaches us that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. The kings of the Great Collapse never learned it. The wheel turned, and it turned over them.

Second, a lesson in reading. You will teach children from these tablets, and you must teach them to doubt. Consider [[evt:548|the first war of Navnga against Vuvo]]. Our scholars, comparing every source, have established that Navnga began it, fought two battles, won both, lost some seven hundred men to Vuvo's three thousand, and took eight districts. Yet one tablet swears that Vuvo began it and that Navnga took fourteen districts; and another swears that no battle was fought at all, and that an honourable peace changed nothing. Consider [[evt:1110|the war of Talenonoy and Shue'ethie]]: six battles, three won by each side, a captain named Luesh'anieme among the dead, and six districts gained by Talenonoy. One scribe gives his own side thirteen thousand enemy dead for fewer than nine hundred of its own; another calls the whole affair a stalemate and says only that the border was settled. Or [[evt:1416|the second war of Virnado against Talenonoy]], where nineteen districts became forty-five on one tablet, and on another a border quietly settled.

The ancients lied, comrades, because each lord kept his own archive and answered to no one. That is the deepest cause of the Great Collapse, and I state it plainly: a divided record makes a divided realm.

And now, permit me, the contrast. Seven years ago foreign armies crossed our frontier, thinking us the kind of state that breaks. At Dambüzhi they were thrown back. At [[evt:48362|Dadunde]] they were broken, and by [[evt:48363|the treaty of Dadunde]] the invader became our vassal and swore twenty years of peace. Last year, by [[evt:48386|the founding of Yindu]], our flag was planted across the sea. I do not boast of these things; the One gave them. I say only that they were given to a people that had learned the lesson of the Great Collapse: one record, one purpose, one Party, one nation.

The faith teaches us that teachers are honoured, but that no one owns the truth. Quite so. That is why the truth must be kept by those who serve the whole people, and not by every petty lord with a scribe in his pocket.

Go back to your schools. Teach the figures. Teach the doubt. And teach the children that the wheel turns only over those who let it.
