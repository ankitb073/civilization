In AY 2258 the citizens of [[set:5|Nilu]] gathered to sing a victory. The anonymous Hymn for the Victory at Unzhinlathu (PAL·PO·00006), which survives almost whole on parchment, celebrates the end of the war with which the previous chapter closed. The counsellors of a king "given the crown before he could speak" had sent the army into Luthe, and before the walls of Unzhinlathu the queen of Luthe had asked for terms. The poet is modest about it. "Of theirs, seven-and-twenty thousand lie in the earth of that country; of ours, fewer," the hymn says, and for once a victory song does not exaggerate: the record counts 26,822 dead for Luthe and 16,555 for Nilu. It closes with a warning to the proud:

> This is the victory that does not rot: a just peace, honestly kept.

The peace rotted within a generation. Five years later the defeated kingdom broke apart, and thirteen years later the victor followed it. That pairing sets the theme of the quarter-millennium from AY 2250 to 2500. It was an age of growth, of almost ceaseless war and of new prophets, and above all an age in which great states fell at the height of their power, not in decline.

## A just peace, and its rot (2250–2275)

The kingdom of [[pol:598|Luthe]] had been ruled since 2252 by [[per:18330|Nabathu]], remembered as the Last. Her reign opened with the secession of Lamlivtse and went on through the defeats at Khanindu and Unzhinlathu, and the record lists all of these among the causes of the [[evt:18540|Collapse of the Luthe State]] in 2263. Five states rose from the ruins. One kept the name of [[pol:820|Luthe]] and the old capital at Dagafu, which was sacked for the first time in its history four years later. Another, the kingdom of [[pol:823|Tangath]], would last until 2690 and will loom large in the next chapter.

Nilu's king was [[per:18227|Navave]], also called the Last. He had been crowned in 2246 at the age of one. In 2271 came the [[evt:18638|Collapse of the Nilu State]], and its recorded causes are battles of the very war the hymn celebrated (Khanindu in 2249, Yave in 2250, Kavu in 2251), along with older defeats reaching back to 2212. The record, in other words, puts the collapse down to the price of winning. Navave lived another fifteen years, a king without a kingdom. Of Nilu's five heirs, [[pol:826|Puvu]] took the capital itself and endured until 2612. [[pol:827|Kabave]], Kavu, Tabüthif and the city-state of Tadudüvi shared the rest. In 2407 Tabüthif threw off the overlordship of Kavu, a quarrel between two orphans of the same house.

The larger picture is one of growth. The people living under states numbered 70.2 million in 2250, 77.4 million in 2370 and 82.4 million in 2490. There were 44 states at the start of the period, 48 at its middle and only 31 at its end, as the small were swallowed. One absence deserves stress. After the White Death of 2218, the record knows no great pestilence for the whole of this period. Whatever broke the empires of this age, it was not plague.

## Ash and dry years

Climate did strike, though. A one-year cold spell in 2270 was followed by the [[evt:18604|Onset of the Great Drought in Yizamashti (AY 2272)]] across 311 districts, and in 2273 by the [[evt:18603|Eruption of Mount Fimiy (AY 2273)]], a blast of magnitude VEI 7 that cooled the world by about 1.05 °C. The 2270s were the coldest decade of the age, 1.31 °C below the long-run mean. A priest of Masto at Vami, in the empire of Iti, left the best account (PAL·CH·08450, The Priest of Masto on the Years Without a Sun). The sun rose "red as a coal". Grey dust fell on the altar each morning after he had swept it, frost came in the month of threshing, and grain sold "for five witlsa and more, where in my youth it cost one".

The prices bear him out, and show how uneven the hunger was. At Vami a measure of grain rose from about 19 grams of silver in the 2260s to 75 grams in the 2270s. At Dagafu it went from 17 to 60, and at Ishwyithie from 14 to 30. But at [[set:0|Taulin]] and Navnge, the two capitals of the region of Yidhe, grain hardly moved from 10 or 11 grams. The ash fell on some peoples and not on others.

After the eruption came a run of droughts. The worst for length was the [[evt:19263|Onset of the Great Drought in Yimla (AY 2323)]], which lasted until 2405. The widest was the [[evt:19557|Onset of the Great Drought in Baan (AY 2353)]], across 370 districts. A keeper at Nivanga wrote in 2354 (PAL·RE·00090, Record of the dry years from AY 2353): "Year after year the rains failed. Grain stood at 4.43 Inith the measure. Water was sold in the market by the jar." The sum works out to about 44 grams of silver, almost exactly what the market series gives for Nivanga in that decade. At [[set:155|Yinnge]], under drought for most of the years from 2289 to 2360, grain cost between 30 and 40 grams from the 2300s to the 2370s, and fell to about 11 by the 2400s.

Hunger killed less often than drought threatened. The droughts returned in the 2450s, with the [[evt:20728|Onset of the Great Drought in Baan (AY 2452)]] and the [[evt:20729|Onset of the Great Drought in Daadüdhih (AY 2457)]]. The record lists three famines in the whole period. In Ayshi in 2333, 101,749 people died or fled (8 per cent). In Khanji in 2459, where the record ties the famine to the drought of 2457, 109,719 were lost. In Mikhukhizhi in the same year 377,052 were lost, a sixth of the people, and the record gives no cause. A village song from [[pol:834|Tivinnu]] (PAL·PO·00096, The Granary That Answers Nothing) prices grain at "six Ninkil the measure" (the market says 66 grams) and blames not the queen but the tribute-men who came after the war: "His thumb was on the pan." We should remember how thin this evidence is. The Record of the dry years from AY 2272 (PAL·RE·00084) is lost except for its heading, quoted sixty-two years later. Besides nearly thirty literary works, 988 documents survive from the period; 430 are inscriptions and only 48 are ledgers. We see this age largely through what kings carved in stone.

## Two heirs of Yeda

The longest rivalry of the age was a family quarrel. [[pol:777|Tallen]] and [[pol:779|Yide]] were both born of Yeda's collapse in 2199. Tallen held the old capital at Taulin, Yide sat at [[set:10|Navnge]], and their battles were fought in [[reg:16|Yidhe]], the heartland they had both inherited. A Tallen scribe of the 2360s still calls the enemy "Yeda".

[[per:18210|Balsheyramadhen I]] came to Tallen's throne in 2243 at the age of two and reigned for seventy-five years. In 2315, aged seventy-four, he began the [[evt:19214|Third Tallen–Yide War]]. His stele at Piyikhzhidhe (PAL·IN·01397) says that "he killed 26,000 soldiers". The record gives 4,713. From 2318 Tallen lost at Himi, at Rurin, at Himi again and twice at Piyikhzhidhe. The old king died in the first of those years, and his namesake and successor signed the peace. The Yide palace account (PAL·CH·03542) is just as boastful from the other side. At Rurin in 2320, it says, the enemy came with 52,841 men and left 17,357 dead to Yide's 219. The record has 22,564 men, 4,465 dead and 1,022 Yide losses. Each side inflated the numbers, but neither faked the result. The Yide chronicle also says nothing of two of the war's seven engagements, which happen to be the two it lost. By the Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe in 2324 Tallen paid 54,435 kilograms of silver. Yide's signatory, [[per:19084|Bunethin]], had been born in 2319. He was five years old.

Bunethin, later called the Great, reigned for forty-eight years and was never beaten in a war. In the [[evt:19377|Iti–Yide War]] (2331–2340) [[pol:717|Iti]] attacked him and lost, and paid 1,649 talents by the Treaty of Nuidaduth (PAL·TR·01316). The Iti account (PAL·CH·03573) ends with its king "wearied of bloodshed" and adds: "The honour of the realm was preserved." It does not mention the silver. Then came the [[evt:19588|Fourth Tallen–Yide War]] (2352–2361). Tallen's own chronicler (PAL·CH·03609) opens by making Yide's king the oath-breaker who "set his hand against Tallen". In the record Tallen was the attacker. Tallen lost 28,836 men to Yide's 21,598 and paid another 1,850 talents (PAL·TR·01332). Across the two wars Tallen lost some 47,000 soldiers and 110 tonnes of silver, and lost both of them. As we shall see, Tallen still won the century.

## Broken at the summit

Yide reached its greatest size in 2360, with 6.1 million people in 265 districts. Ten years later it was gone. The causes the record gives for the [[evt:19802|Collapse of the Yide State]] in 2370 are telling: not a lost war, but the battles Yide lost on the way to winning them (Piyikhzhidhe in 2316 and 2317, Nuidaduth in 2340, Himi in 2356, Nodhinge in 2358). Its measure of internal order fell from 0.38 in the 2260s to 0.07 a century later. Six states rose from it. A smaller [[pol:866|Yide]] held Navnge until 2554, beside the kingdom of [[pol:867|Mane]]. Bunethin outlived his empire by eight years.

Iti paid twice. After Nuidaduth it faced the secession of Vakha in 2344, and in 2355 [[per:19122|Kobon II]] of [[pol:801|Yennga]] launched a holy war against it. By the Treaty of Bami in 2359 Iti gave up nine districts and 49,560 kilograms of silver, and Bami, the Vami of the priest of Masto, was sacked. The [[evt:19972|Collapse of the Iti State]] followed in 2382, after its measure of order had fallen almost to nothing. Among its six heirs, [[pol:876|Vami]] made the sacked city its capital, and [[pol:877|Imshi]] would strike some of the age's first coins. In 2399 came the [[evt:20157|Collapse of the Turamtu State]], whose recorded causes go back to a secession of 2341. Its three heirs included a new [[pol:887|Turamtu]] empire, one of the giants of the next chapter.

The widest of these empires was [[pol:755|Tomözel]], seated at [[set:3|Ishwyithie]] in the old land of [[reg:18|Tamthal]]. Under [[per:18381|Yewia II]], who reigned from 2257 to 2302, and [[per:18592|Pitselobi]] after her, Tomözel made war almost without pause. The [[evt:19299|Third Ana–Tomözel War]] (2323–2333) against [[per:18792|Ma'sha Atha II]] of [[pol:756|Ana]] left both kings with victory steles. Ma'sha Atha claimed 6,600 enemy dead at Tiyinmiw in 2325, where the record has 2,384 (PAL·IN·01406). Pitselobi claimed 25,100 at Foyitlithyi in 2331, where the record has 7,937 (PAL·IN·01409). The Treaty of Tliyiya restored the frontier as it had been. About 39,000 men died for nothing.

Under [[per:19610|Bozinibe]], called the Victorious (2366–2412), Tomözel fought holy wars against Yimola, Nebe and Shivilna. The war on Yimola cost it 49 soldiers, against 340 lost by the defenders. In its fifteen wars between 2267 and 2407 Tomözel lost some 36,000 men. In 2370 it was the largest state on the map by territory, and in 2410 it reached its peak, with 7.8 million people in 344 districts. Its order stood at 0.07. In 2412 came the [[evt:20301|Collapse of the Tomözel State]] into six. The record names no single blow: no defeat, no drought, no plague. I can only read it as exhaustion. [[per:20197|Setlietlsima]], called the Victorious, refounded [[pol:890|Tomözel]] at Ishwyithie, and it lasted until 2560. Bozinibe died four years after the empire he had enlarged. Yide and Tomözel both broke within a decade of their greatest extent. In this age, size was not safety.

## Silver by the talent

Tallen, meanwhile, grew. It had 4.1 million people in 76 districts in 2250. It conquered Nelenga in 2295 and put down the secession of Vovo between 2369 and 2375. After Yide's fall it grew steadily, from 100 districts in the 2370s to 138 by the 2410s. In the [[evt:20480|Fourth Tuvizhinge Trade War]] (2423–2426) [[per:20183|Balsheyramadhen VI]] turned on Yennga, which had just reached 8.5 million people, its largest yet. At Baaliurinu in 2425 some 33,000 men met 34,000, and Yennga lost 9,255. By the Treaty of Tathriubu (PAL·TR·01387) [[per:20090|Dodemenen I]] gave up 49 districts in [[reg:9|Tuvizhinge]] and beyond, with six towns. He promised 351 talents of silver a year for twenty-two years and let Tallen's merchants pass "unhindered" and free of tolls. The Yennga palace account (PAL·CH·03776) mentions none of the three battles and sums up: "Nothing of worth was lost." Yennga's treasury had held about 748 tonnes of silver in the 2410s. By the 2440s it held 343.

Dodemenen passed the loss downward. He made war on Yirenunyirin, a fragment of fallen Turamtu, and in 2440 wrote to the young man he held as a hostage (PAL·LT·01688): "When the tribute does not come, I will send her no word. Let her ask your king why." The record prices that tribute at 99 kilograms a year, bought with 185 Yennga dead against 316 of the enemy. By 2490, under the queen [[per:20661|Rathtothsheth]], Tallen held 11.9 million people in 240 districts and kept an army of 116,299. The loser of the Yide wars had become the greatest power in the world.

All of this was paid in weighed silver. Indemnities and tributes were reckoned in talents of thirty kilograms, and 145 merchant houses were founded in the period to carry the metal and the goods it bought. The record shows no debasement in these 250 years, and for a simple reason: money was weighed at every sale, so only the thumb on the pan could cheat it. That changed in 2439, when I'ayof, assayer to the king of Pivish, struck silver of a fixed weight with the king's mark. His public notice (PAL·NP·01080) is the clearest early defence of coinage in the archive: "The mark adds no silver. It adds trust, which is rarer." Pivish's first coins of 2446 held 13.1 grams. Imshi followed in 2447, Vami and Tivinnu struck gold, and Tomözel and Pibetie had coins by 2474. Tallen came to the idea on its own through Dopemten in 2482 and struck its first pieces, of 8.9 grams, in 2498. There were ten new coinages in fifty-three years. I'ayof also asked what would happen "if a king should one day put less silver in the piece". The next chapter gives the answer.

## The One above the gods

The age was crowded with prophets. [[per:18406|Tonofmalen]] founded [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] in 2256, [[per:18640|Nabandangi]] founded [[mov:61|Langikath]] in 2272 and [[per:18639|I Ash]] founded [[mov:60|Shimdish]] in 2275. [[per:18752|Nanüdi]] taught [[mov:62|Nidunde]] from 2281, [[per:19129|Nambadhe]] taught [[mov:64|Sinimu]] from 2310, and [[per:19521|Namumbi]] wrote to a queen about [[mov:68|Tsiumu]] in 2353. Kievi and Vimekhulu followed in 2382 and 2459. Several of the founding texts come straight from the battlefield. [[per:19223|Bethin]] wrote to his king in 2321 (PAL·LT·01693) that after Piyikhzhidhe he had given water to the wounded on the carts at Navnge and heard a word that "was not a word of war": above the gods "there is one Power", and it weighs mercy. He opened the house of [[mov:65|Kankhu]] by the river gate. In 2354, on the occasion of the [[evt:19656|Founding of the Dankhüdhil Faith]], Tuedhuyin answered a soldier back from Nidhinge (PAL·SC·00125, Dialogue of Tuethuyin and the Soldier from Nidhinge): the storm-god "does not love and does not hate".

We should not mistake survival for success. These texts survive, some stained or incomplete, because their communities copied and kept them. The temple priests they argued with appear only as foils. On the map, the old order held. In 2370 the Jinru gods held 15 per cent of the land and Mie'to'ieshio 13. By 2490 Mie'to'ieshio led, with about a sixth of the land, and the ten new faiths together held barely seventy districts. Several of them would outlive every empire named in this chapter.

Languages drifted too. Matlföme and Minlu emerged as distinct tongues around 2299, Kuvage, Shüthi and Pi'ishim around 2399, and Khukhuyli around 2499, and sixty-nine sound changes are dated to the period. In 2256 Turamtu adopted the Ninlatuwne alphabet.

By AY 2500 the map had settled into fewer and larger states. Tallen ruled from Taulin, Ana held 315 districts, and a usurper, Tlamfen Yueva I, sat on its throne. [[per:20911|Natdatenoth]] ruled Yennga, and in Puvu [[per:21046|Navuvage]], to be called the Conqueror, had just been crowned. In 2497 drought came again to Pyivi. Two heirs of this chapter's collapses, Turamtu and Tangath, were still modest states. In the coming age they would stand beside Tallen as the largest in the world. How they used the stamped silver, and what the next war between Tallen and its neighbours cost, is the business of the following chapter.
