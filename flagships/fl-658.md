Two sentences survive in the name of [[per:23224|Nakaadhash]]. They are cut on a foundation tablet from the palace at [[set:65|Adhizhili]], excavated in AY 4889 by Nanjil and translated by Nalilimi (PAL·IN·05242). The stone is weathered but legible, and the text is complete:

> Nakaadhash, king of Kaadhezhile, built this palace in Kaadhezhile. May Bumbe bless the city.

The edition dates it to AY 2691, the tenth year of the state. Five years earlier this man had led the reform that made his city a merchant republic, and that is why he stands in this book among the founders. He founded no faith, only a way of choosing who governs. Yet in the one text he left, he calls himself king. His name means "stranger". This chapter tries to make him less of one, and says plainly where the record cannot help.

## A child of the Kaazhe wars

He was born in AY 2645, a man of the Ninzhe. The record names no parents, wife, child, trade or birthplace, but it tells us what kind of year it was. In 2645 the First Kaadhezhile–Kaazhe War ended at Mungu, where 5,432 men of the old Kingdom of Kaadhezhile attacked 3,822 and lost 1,781 of them against 183 of the enemy's. The Treaty of Mungu gave the kingdom nothing but a fifteen-year truce.

Queen Navande had then reigned forty-seven years. Her silver Vazhve, struck around 2633, shows her diademed head and a walking bull (PAL·NU·00060). She died in 2661 after sixty-three years on the throne. The record marks a crisis: Nanigivi I seized the crown and that same year opened the Second Kaadhezhile–Kaazhe War. It ran fourteen years. In 2672 the king led 4,146 men against 5,459 at Mungu, lost 906 to the enemy's 387, and was killed. The Treaty of Luyululi in 2675 restored the frontier as it had been; the kingdom had lost 8,049 men to Kaazhe's 6,930 for nothing. Nakaadhash was sixteen when that war began and thirty when it ended. Whether he fought in it, the record does not say.

The record's index of internal order stood at zero in the kingdom's survey of the 2660s and barely above it in the 2670s. In 2682 the kingdom of forty-seven districts and some 1.36 million people broke apart. Adhizhili and sixteen districts became the city-state of [[pol:1019|Kaadhezhile]] under [[per:23186|Nambu]]; twenty-nine districts became the Kingdom of Bandidadhu.

## The reform of 2686

In 2686, aged forty-one, Nakaadhash led the [[evt:23424|Merchant republic Reform in Kaadhezhile]], and Nambu was deposed. The record says no more: no battle, no named allies, no deaths. One silence speaks. Nambu lived another twelve years and died a natural death in 2698, a year after the man who displaced him. The reform, it seems, did not need his blood.

The neighbour shows the alternative. Bandidadhu kept its crown and was attacked by Kaazhe in 2685. By the Treaty of Luyululi of 2689 it ceded nine districts and bound itself to tribute for thirty-five years, and from 2687 its king was a child of three ruling through a regent. In Nakaadhash's eleven years Kaadhezhile fought no recorded war and kept its sixteen districts.

What "merchant republic" meant in daily life is harder to see. The record names no merchant house seated at Adhizhili in his lifetime; the House of Ulangu was founded there in 2719, twenty-two years after his death. The city went on paying in the dead queen's Vazhve and struck no coin of its own until the Njizhin, around 2760. The state faith, [[mov:16|Nenje]], taught that prosperity is a sign of divine favour and that no one owns the truth. The record does not say Nakaadhash invoked either teaching, but a creed that read wealth as the gods' approval offered no objection to rule by the wealthy.

## King of a republic

Why, then, the royal title? It is Nalilimi's rendering, and it may say less than it seems. A school exercise from Adhizhili, written in 2753, is headed "The kings of Kaadhezhile, as copied by the pupil Nambave" (PAL·SX·00439), and every name on it belongs to an elected head of the republic. The title outlived the monarchy. The list begins with Navaaze, who took office in 2704; Nakaadhash is not on it, and neither is anyone before Navaaze. The teacher wrote only "Better than yesterday." Fifty-six years after his death, at least one pupil in his own city learned its kings without him.

A palace, though, is a palace. Its blessing went to Bumbe, whom the edition identifies as Bombe, goddess of the sea: the republic's founder asked the sea for the port's luck.

## What the stranger left

He died in 2697, aged fifty-two, of natural causes. Then came the first election the record shows in Kaadhezhile, which chose [[per:23411|Navune]], aged forty-seven. Forty heads followed Nakaadhash, every one of them elected, until conquest ended the state in 3016. His constitution lasted 330 years. In the survey of the 2690s the republic held 685,440 people and 6,854 men under arms, and its index of internal order stood at 0.53.

It could not defend what it had. In the Ninth Khishkhih Trade War (2702–2706) the Khishkhis Empire fielded from more than twice to more than five times the republic's numbers in each of four battles, and won them all. By the Treaty of Yizih in 2706 the republic gave up four districts and 530,462 Vazhve, and opened its markets to Khishkhis merchants "free of all tolls" (PAL·TR·01581). By the end of the 2700s it counted 508,386 people in twelve districts. That empire's name is my own country's; I set down its terms without pleasure.

Nakaadhash remains a stranger. We do not know his face or his reasons. We know that he took the remnant of a broken kingdom, after two fruitless royal wars, and gave it a way of choosing its heads that outlasted him by more than three centuries. We also know that the man he displaced died a natural death. That, too, belongs to what he founded.
