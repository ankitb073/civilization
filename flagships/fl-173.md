In the name of all the gods, each in his place and each in her due, and of the holy priesthood of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]], which keeps their truth in order from generation to generation.

I, [[per:32887|Shoe Tluie]], by the favour of the gods Emperor of [[pol:1371|Tomzel]], son of the lady Baalmamshi, of the house of Nitllu Mov the First, being old in body but sound in mind, set down this my testament in my palace at [[set:264|Ölie]], in the year 3103 of the Myitiyishiu, that those who come after may know what I received, what I did, and what I leave.

**Of what I received.** My mother named me Six Raven, for, she said, the raven is the bird that outlives the battle. I came to the throne in the year 3047, a young man, and found the realm smaller than in the days of my grandfathers, its frontiers quarrelled over, its treasury poor. I took counsel of the priests, and the priests told me what the wise have always said: deal justly, live moderately, and do not love gold. I have tried to keep that counsel. Let the gods judge whether I kept it.

**Of what I did.** The gods gave me victory in many wars, and the chronicles of Ölie record them all; here I set down only the last of them, which are freshest in the memory of the living. When the lords beyond the frontier withheld what was ours, I sent out my armies. At the [[evt:33966|battle of Zhükhi]] they prevailed, and at the [[evt:33976|battle of Kadhdanul]] they prevailed again; and by the [[evt:33977|peace of Kadhdanul]], which I sealed with my own hand, the city of Kadhdanul and its country came under the crown of Tomzel, and fifteen years of quiet were sworn.

When afterward the upstarts of Nyinsitlasa, who had thrown off their own lords, set themselves against me in the [[evt:34032|second war of Tomzel and Nyinsitlasa]], I did not need to destroy them; within the year the gods destroyed them, and they were no more. I record it, that my heirs may learn that pride is its own punishment.

In these last years, when I was already old, the merchants of Tomzel cried to me that their goods were stopped and taxed on the roads of Yiutl. I answered them with the [[evt:34119|fifteenth war for the trade of Yiutl]]. At the [[evt:34129|battle of Onithie]] the enemy fled before my banners; we sat down before Ivu at the [[evt:34130|first siege of Ivu]]; at the [[evt:34139|battle of Pivithie]] they fled again; and at the [[evt:34140|second siege of Ivu]] they came out to treat. By the [[evt:34141|peace of Ivu]], this very year, the city of Pivithie and its lands are given to Tomzel, the roads are opened to our merchants, and the sword is sheathed for fifteen years. So the old Emperor ends as the young Emperor began, with the frontiers made whole.

I do not write here of the years when things went less well. A testament is not a chronicle, and the chroniclers will write what they will.

**Of what I leave.** The gods gave me two daughters and a son, and the gods took all three before me. I do not complain of it. I name them here so that the priests will remember them in their prayers, as I have remembered them every evening. I leave no heir of my body. I commend the throne to the lords and the priesthood, that they may choose, according to the law and the custom of the realm, one worthy of it; and I charge whoever is chosen to keep the counsel that was given to me.

To the temples of every god in Ölie I leave a thousand [[cur:749|Sozhne]] each, for lamps and for the poor. To the soldiers who served in the war of Yiutl I leave a year's pay. To the widows of those who did not return I leave two. Of the rest of my treasure, let one third go to the crown, and let two thirds be given away, for the wise withdraw from wealth, and I would not go before the gods with my hands full.

Let me be buried without gold, beside my children.

Whoever alters this testament, let the gods whom he has sworn by forget his name.

Sealed at Ölie, in the year 3103 of the Myitiyishiu, the fifty-seventh of my reign.
