*[Obverse.]*

In the 4th year of [[per:955|Kokulufath]], queen of [[pol:25|Yida]], lady of the land, daughter of the king who went to the gods, I set down what my eyes saw in the war of her father. Let [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear hear it. Let Dumamnul of the high sky hear it. Let Yenan of the night hear it, who saw us march by his light. Let the scribe add nothing. Let the scribe take nothing away.

**The cause.** The men of [[pol:12|Jinontonu]] had been beaten before, in the [[evt:589|First Yida–Chinontonu War]], when my father carried the spear. They bowed their necks then. Then they lifted their necks again. They sat in [[reg:9|Tupujinga]] like a fox in the fold. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] read the liver of a black goat. The priests said: "Tupujinga is for Yida." The king in Tallin said: "Go out." So began the [[evt:656|Second Yida–Chinontonu War]]. We gave the bull to Vulme. We gave the cake to Nidhikhti. We went out.

**Dininge.** In the third summer the general [[per:894|Jeytathrelin]] led us. We were 5,931 spears. We were many as the barley of the river plain. At [[set:67|Duninga]] their king came out against us, [[per:789|Dolulrinin]], lord of Jinontonu, with 1,316 spears. He did not run. I say it: he did not run. 39 of ours lay down. 309 of theirs lay down. Vinba of the earth drank them all. This was the [[evt:662|Battle of Dininge]]. The field was Yida's.

**The first burning.** Then we went to their city. The gate was broken. This was the [[evt:663|Sack of Zhinontunu]], the first time. We took the bronze. We took the oil. We took the cloth of the women. I took a cup of silver; I gave it to the temple of Vulme. The king of Jinontonu did not bow. The war went on.

**The years between.** Two summers we sat in Tupujinga. We burned their threshing floors. They burned ours. My friend from the same street died of the belly-sickness. No song is sung for that. I sing it here.

**Zhinontunu.** In the sixth summer Jeytathrelin led us again, before the walls of [[set:6|Jinontonu]]. We were 6,707 spears. [[per:916|Jitan]], their general, came out with 881. Few were their spears, but hard. Hear it: this was the bitter field. 55 of ours lay down before the walls. 162 of theirs lay down. Vulme turned her face away until evening. Then she turned it back. This was the [[evt:673|Battle of Zhinontunu]]. The field was Yida's.

**The second burning.** Then the gate was broken a second time. This was the [[evt:674|Sack of Zhinontunu]], the second time. What we had not taken before, we took. Their old men sat in the ashes and covered their heads.

**The oath.** Then Dolulrinin bowed his neck. At Jinontonu he swore before the gods, and the king, the father of our queen, swore. This was the [[evt:675|Treaty of Zhinontunu]]. Jinontonu gave Tupujinga to Yida. Jinontonu would send its silver to Yida twenty-three years, niga upon niga. Ten years the spear would sleep. We came home to Tallin singing. The women threw barley.

**The count.** Six summers. Two battles. Two burnings. The scribes say: 94 of Yida went down to Vinba; 471 of Jinontonu. Fewer of ours than theirs. And the land is ours. The silver is ours. So the gods judged.

*[Reverse.]*

**Now.** The king has gone to the gods; his daughter Kokulufath sits on the seat. Again the spears are out. Again Jeytathrelin went out, against the men of Lenunushal; this is the [[evt:699|Yida–Lenunushal War]]. At the [[evt:707|Battle of Linnshal]] the gods hid their faces. I was not there; my knees are too old. Men came home and did not speak. I do not speak of it either.

What ends will begin again, say the priests. What is lost will be found. Vulme, lady of the spear, remember Dininge. Remember Zhinontunu. Turn the wheel for Yida.

> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Vulme break his spear; may Yenan withhold his light from him.
