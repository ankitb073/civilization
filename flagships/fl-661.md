title: The Victory Nobody Carved: Nandithim and the Last Wars of Tambakh

In AY 4926 the archaeologist Nanjil dug along the old wall of Mbiyuduyizu and published fourteen inscribed finds (PAL·EX·00212). Among them are a Tambakh victory stele of 2812, the bronze treaty of 2813, and two steles set up in 2859 by [[per:24837|Nakumbèn]], emperor of [[pol:979|Khambe]], for victories there in 2857 and 2858. Nothing marks the battle of 2841, when a Tambakh army of 5,516 men met 34,737 Khambe soldiers on the same ground and Khambe lost 15,286 of them. The commander that day was [[per:25016|Nandithim]]. No surviving document of his age names him. He exists in the record chiefly as twelve battle entries, and this chapter tries to read them as a life.

## A kingdom running out of men

He was born in AY 2813, of the Kubis people, and his name means "dawn". The record names no father, mother, wife or child, and is silent on how he was raised or trained. In the year of his birth [[pol:1022|Tambakh]] beat Tangath at Mbiyuduyizu and dictated peace there, with an indemnity and free trade for its merchants. By the end of the 2810s it held 59 districts, some 2.29 million people and an army of 22,590.

His youth saw that kingdom come apart. King Namumbiv fell at Dadifi in 2825, in a battle his army won, and a usurper, Nambathnu I, took the throne. The war against Tathu ended in stalemate in 2832; the war with Luthe, begun in 2826, went badly. Navavun, who had commanded in twelve of the kingdom's battles, died in 2837. Nambathnu II, called the Brave, was killed at Dadifi in 2838, and his younger brother [[per:24984|Nambathnu III]], whom later ages called the Last, came to the throne in 2839. That year Nandithim appears for the first time, as general, aged twenty-six. The record does not say why so young a man was chosen; the deaths of 2837 and 2838 suggest there were few older men left.

His first battle, the [[evt:25342|Battle of Dambakhu (AY 2839)]], was fought under the capital. He had 9,359 men against 7,348 of [[pol:1078|Luthe]] under [[per:24938|Nalavu]]. He had the larger army and was beaten: Tambakh lost 2,886 men, Luthe 693. The city was sacked, and the treaty sworn there that year ended the [[evt:25178|First Luthe–Tambakh War]] with four districts given up, "the river Ligi to be the frontier" (PAL·TR·01684). Luthe's king, another Nakumbèn and not the emperor of Khambe, set up a stele near the city two years later: "Nakumbèn, king of Luthe, defeated Dambakhu in Dambakhu. He killed 23,000 soldiers" (PAL·IN·01872). That is more than twice the men Nandithim had on the field, and the king, not Nalavu, takes the credit.

## Two wars at once

Khambe had been at war with Tambakh since 2837, and in 2838 Kibis, centred on Dadifi, rose for independence. Khambe was a different order of enemy: by the end of the 2830s it had 195 districts and 7.27 million people, with an army of 69,582: four times Tambakh's districts, three and a half times its people and soldiers. At the [[evt:25376|Battle of Dadunde (AY 2840)]] the emperor came in person. His muster roll survives in a medieval copy, burned at the edges: 43,287 men, among them 6,088 light infantry from Dadunde itself. Its last line is the nearest we come to the men in the ranks: "Deserters to be branded and returned to their towns" (PAL·MU·00431). Nandithim had 7,752 men; he lost 1,983, and Khambe 733.

The rebels were another matter. When it fell, [[pol:1094|Kibis]] had seven districts and some 35,000 people. Its general [[per:25029|Natange]] and its king [[per:25008|Nabul]] were Kubis, like Nandithim. Three times near Dadifi, in 2840, 2842 and 2843, he led more than five thousand men against fewer than five hundred, and three times he won. Kibis lost 203 men in those battles and Tambakh 47. In 2843 Nabul took the field himself and was beaten. The terms of surrender included an amnesty, but Nabul, who signed them, was executed.

The Kibis account of the war was written in 2852 by Nayuthu, a woman scribe of the palace, who still dated her work "in the 15th year of Kibis", nine years after Kibis had ceased to exist (PAL·CH·04647). She records one battle, in 2839: by treachery, "our army gave ground. We lost 15; the enemy, 27." The record gives 64 Kibis losses against 10, and Nambathnu III, not Nandithim, commanded that day. His three victories go unmentioned. She ends: "Let this record keep what the sword has taken."

## The field without a stone

Between these came the battle that should have made his name. At the [[evt:25387|Battle of Mbiyuduyizu (AY 2841)]], [[per:25068|Nakathash]], made general of Khambe that year, brought 34,737 men against 5,516, more than six to one. Khambe lost 15,286, some 44 per cent of its army; Tambakh lost 1,379. Of the 30,661 men Khambe lost in the whole [[evt:25310|Second Khambe–Tambakh War]], almost exactly half fell that day. Of the 67 battles of Khambe that the record keeps, only one cost it more.

No one carved it. For his own victory at Dadifi in 2844, which Nandithim did not command, Nambathnu III cut a rock relief: "Nambathnu, king of Tambakh, defeated Khambe in Tadüth. He killed 44,600 soldiers. He erected this stele for Bumbi, his lady" (PAL·IN·01877). Khambe brought 35,157 men to that field and lost 9,050. The king claimed more dead than the enemy had brought, for a victory that cost Khambe under three-fifths of what Mbiyuduyizu had. Steles were the work of kings. At Dambakhu a king took his general's victory as his own; at Mbiyuduyizu a king left his general's victory unrecorded.

At Dadunde in 2842 he lost to the emperor again, with 10,917 men against 25,542, and at Yave in 2843 to Nakathash. In 2844 and 2845 others commanded, and the record does not say why. At the [[evt:25431|Battle of Gyi (AY 2846)]] he beat Nakathash with 5,640 men against 20,013, but it cost him 1,159 men, a fifth of his army, against Khambe's 1,450.

## Three names for one town

Then came three defeats at Dadifi, one a year from 2847 to 2849. In the last he brought 4,266 men, the smallest army he ever led, against 20,862. The treaty that followed gave up the town where he had beaten the rebels three times: "Dambakhu cedes to Khambe the land of Luthe and Dyimbi, with the towns of Dadii, 11 districts in all" (PAL·TR·01689). A translator notices names: the town is Dadifi in the record, Tadüth on the Tambakh relief and in the Kibis chronicle, and Dadii on Khambe's bronze. Six of his twelve battles were fought near it.

By the end of the 2840s Tambakh had 44 districts, 1.84 million people, an army of 15,406 and a treasury of 94,313 kilograms of silver, about half of what it had held at the end of the 2810s. Its state faith, [[mov:11|Kieve]], taught that prosperity is a sign of divine favour; by that measure the surveys read as a judgement. In 2852 the kingdom broke into a city-state at [[set:92|Dambakhu]] and a kingdom of Mbil, and its silver [[cur:566|Nyivi]] ended with it. Khambe annexed the city in 2858, and Nakumbèn put up his two steles at Mbiyuduyizu the next year. Tathu took Mbil in 2883.

Nandithim lived through all of it, and died a natural death in 2890, aged seventy-seven, forty-one years after his last battle. He outlived the king he served, who died in 2876, both successor states, and every commander he faced: Nalavu, Natange, Nabul, Nakathash and the emperor. The record does not say where he spent those decades, or under whose rule.

## The account in figures

His twelve battles are as many as any commander of [[pol:1022|Tambakh]] fought in its 168 years. Only Navavun fought as many. He won five and lost seven. He beat the rebels every time, beat Khambe twice in eight battles, and never beat the emperor in person. Under his command Tambakh lost 13,152 men and its enemies 21,311. Two-thirds of Khambe's losses in the war, 20,415 of 30,661, fell in battles he commanded. These are not the figures of a failure, but of a capable officer given too few men for too long by a kingdom running out of them. The steles went to kings. The general who fought the battles has twelve rows of figures: less than he deserved, and, because they do not exaggerate, more trustworthy than any stele.
