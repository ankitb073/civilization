Weep, O [[dei:109|Nili]], mother of the river, weep with all your waters.  
Weep, O Lashla, lamp of the night, draw the cloud across your face.  
The sons of [[pol:50|Tantöthi]] lie in the land of [[reg:6|I'ith]].  
They lie in the land of I'ith, and they do not rise.  

Who sent them down? The gods of the wheel sent them down.  
Who called them? [[dei:112|Nizhashta]] of the spear called them by their names.  
The gods of [[pol:108|Wa'mi]] are gods without due, gods without right sacrifice.  
The queen [[per:3528|I'a Ash]] stood in the house of the gods and said:  
"Go down, my sons. Make the land clean for the gods of [[mov:17|Nishmam]]."  
They went down. They went down singing.  

At the first battle [[per:3697|Lampin Maf]] went down to Bami.  
At [[evt:3341|Bami]], before the walls of Wa'mi, he set the spears in a row.  
[[per:3473|Shish'o Igwi]], queen of Wa'mi, came out against him.  
She came out with her men, and she went back into her walls.  
Lampin Maf poured beer for [[dei:110|Masto]]; the sun saw it and was glad.  

In the next year Lampin Maf went down to [[evt:3347|Ash]].  
[[per:3592|Yubili'an]] of Wa'mi stood in the way.  
He stood in the way, and he gave way like a reed in the flood.  
The men of Tantöthi took the field. They took the field, and they sang.  

Weep for them, O Nili. Nili, weep for them.  
Those who sang at Bami, those who sang at Ash, where are they?  
They are under the earth. Their mouths are full of earth.  

Then [[dei:111|Siminlo]] of the storm turned his face away.  
At [[evt:3356|Veümin]] the queen went down herself,  
I'a Ash went down, the queen of the wide land, and the storm went against her.  
The rain fell on the bowstrings; the mud held the feet of the young men.  
The young men fell at Veümin as barley falls to the sickle.  
The queen of Wa'mi stood on the high ground and was not moved.  

Three times more they went down. Three times more the gods were silent.  
At Bami the second time, [[per:3706|Mim'apayati]] of Wa'mi held the river road.  
At Veümin the second time, Lampin Maf came home with few.  
At Bami the third time, the river carried the dead to the sea, to Udash'a, lord of the sea.  
Who will count them? The scribe does not count them. The mothers count them.  

Then the queen I'a Ash laid down the spear.  
She made the [[evt:3391|peace of Bami]] with the queen of Wa'mi.  
She set her seal in the clay, and the spears came home without the men who carried them.  
Was it the spear of Wa'mi that did this? It was not the spear of Wa'mi.  
It was the storm. It was the mud. It was the gods who turned their faces.  

Weep for them, O Nili. Nili, weep for them.  
Twenty winters and one the bones have lain in I'ith.  
No one has washed them. No one has laid bread beside them.  

Now [[per:3843|Sholnow]] sits in the seat, Sholnow the strong of arm.  
Sholnow went down to Bami, and the men of Wa'mi ran before him.  
In this year Lampin Maf, grey of head, went down again to Bami.  
Again the young men lie in the mud beside the river.  
Again the mothers cut their hair at the doors of Tantöthi.  
The new dead go down to the old dead. They go down, and they greet their fathers.  

We pour out the beer. We pour out the oil. We break the bread on the threshold.  
O Masto, give them light below the earth.  
O Nili, carry them softly; do not strike them on the stones.  
O Nizhashta, you who called them, call them now to your table.  
The wheel turns. What ends will begin again.  
They will be born again in Tantöthi. They will drink again from the river.  

Weep for them, O Nili. Nili, weep for them.  
They lie in the land of I'ith, and they do not rise.  

Tablet of the weeping for the dead of the [[evt:3337|Holy War of Tantöti against Va'mi]].  
Written in the 4th year of Sholnow. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Siminlo break his house.
