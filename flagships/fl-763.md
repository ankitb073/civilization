*[Obverse.]*

O [[dei:2|Muevas'e]], mother of the earth, black mother, mother of the furrow,  
hear a man who has no name now.  
My name was taken at the gate. They gave me the name of a dog.  
You know my first name. You knew my father's. Say it for me, since I may not.  

O [[dei:3|Niele'i]], lord of the river, lord of the reed-beds, lord of the ford,  
you saw me carried over. You saw the rope on my neck.  
You did not drown the men who held the rope. I do not blame you.  
You are a god. You have your place. I have mine. Mine is in the mud.  

Hear what was. I was of [[pol:87|I'ithi]]. I was of the town of [[set:22|Wal'a]].  
I had a field by the river. I had a wife. I had a house with a door of reeds.  
In the year of the [[evt:3386|Fourth Sack of Bau]] the men of [[pol:30|Yi'otl]] came.  
They were many as the barley grains on a threshing floor. We were few.  
They burned the house of the tablets. They burned the house of the elders.  
They burned my door of reeds. They put the rope on my neck.  

Then our lady [[per:3367|Ta'al]] went down to them and put her seal on the clay.  
That was the [[evt:3387|Conquest of I'iti]]. That was the end of I'ithi.  
The next year our lady went down under the earth. Muevas'e, you have her. Keep her well.  
Her name is called "the Unfortunate" in the market. I do not call her that.  

*[Reverse.]*

Now it is the 12th year of [[per:3699|Ue'manieme]], king of Yi'otl.  
Seven years I have had the rope, and then no rope, for where would I run?  
I plough. I plough the field by the river at Wal'a.  
It is my field. It was my field. It is the field of my master.  
I know every stone in it. My master does not. He lives in the town and comes at harvest.  

Barley is cheap this year. A measure is less than one [[cur:24|Mige]].  
I have no Mige. I have a bowl of barley in the morning and a bowl at night.  
My master's steward says: you eat well, dog. I eat. I say nothing.  

My wife was given to another house. She is across the river.  
At the new moon I see her at the washing-place. We do not speak. We look.  
That is permitted. Looking is permitted.  

Now the master's sons go to the new war. The king calls them to the [[evt:3434|Yi'otl–Yimola War]].  
The steward says: more rope will come back from that war, more dogs.  
O Muevas'e, let them not bring a man of I'ithi to Wal'a who knows my face.  
Let no one see what I am.  

O mother of the furrow, I do not ask to go home. Home is this field.  
I ask this: when I go down under the earth, let it be this earth.  
Let them dig me at the end of the field by the river, under the old tree.  
Let the barley grow over me, and let it be the barley of I'ithi, whatever they call it.  

O Niele'i, carry this clay down to the ford below the town. Hear me.
