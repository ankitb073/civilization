**THE KING'S LETTER FROM THE FRONT**

*Published by order of the Ministry of War. Sent by telegraph from General Headquarters of the Army of Iith and printed in all newspapers of the Kingdom.*

To my people of [[pol:1997|Yiethithi]], in the cities, in the villages and in the works:

You will have read in the foreign press, and you will hear from the enemy's agents, that our army has been beaten before [[set:116|Imxi]]. I will tell you the truth myself, because a people that has given its sons to the war has a right to hear it from its King and not from the enemy.

Our columns did not enter Imxi. After several days of the heaviest fighting this war has seen, General [[per:47107|Iminak]] judged that the enemy's lines could not be carried without a sacrifice out of proportion to the gain, and he drew the army back in good order to its prepared positions. I approved his decision then and I approve it now.

The enemy will ring his bells. Let him count his dead first. By the reckoning of our General Staff, the forces of [[pol:1953|Imxi]] lost upward of twenty thousand men in this single engagement. General [[per:47010|Lamfin Ax]] did not dare to meet us in open country. He buried his army behind concrete and wire and waited for our infantry to come to him. That is not generalship. It is the method of a government that has stopped believing it can win, and has decided instead to make its victory cost more than the victor can pay.

Our own losses are severe. I will not insult your grief by printing a figure before the lists are complete. The lists will be posted at every railway station and town hall in the Kingdom as soon as the Ministry has verified them, name by name.

Do not let the bells of Imxi make you forget the account of these four years:

- At [[set:128|Fiesi]], in the second year of the war, General [[per:46968|Yufilianwu]] was beaten and left nearly forty thousand of his men on the field.
- At Fiesi again, the next year, the same general was beaten a second time, and more than eighty thousand of his men were lost.
- Before Imxi last year the enemy lost thirty-six thousand men, and his commander, General [[per:47139|Vexsayilu]], fell with them.

Add this week's figures to these and the enemy has lost more than one hundred and seventy thousand men in four battles. He did not have them to spare. We have carried the war into [[reg:6|Iith]] for four years, and we have won three of the four great battles fought there. Every one of them was commanded by General Iminak, in whom I place my complete confidence.

We did not choose this war out of appetite. Since the [[evt:48192|Battle of Veümin]] the whole world has known that the state at Imxi is hollow. Its own people turned on their old order, and it has kept itself standing only by drafting more men than it can feed. Iith cannot be left to such a government. It will not be.

I therefore ask three things of the nation.

First, of the factories: keep the night shifts running. The guns and shells we spent before Imxi must be replaced before the autumn.

Second, of the railways: give the military trains the line, even at the cost of delay to goods and passengers.

Third, of every household: subscribe to the new War Loan, in whatever sum of [[cur:1144|Un]] you can afford. Grain stands above two un the measure. I know what that means for a working family's table, and the Ministry of Supply has my order to keep the bakers honest.

The [[mov:69|Kievi]] teaches that everything which ends begins again, and that every man may find the truth by the light within him. I ask each of you to consult that light, and not the rumours of the street corner. The wise man governs his appetite in hard times. A wise nation governs its fear.

I remain with the army.

**I LI, King**

*Given at General Headquarters, 16 Miü 2600 of the Kievi. Copies to be displayed in all public offices of [[set:104|Yiethithi]] and the provinces.*
