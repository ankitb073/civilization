title: Sermon of Nitllu Himim in the Year of the Three Defeats

Citizens of [[set:52|Ihamiw]], men and women of a city that has buried more sons in five years than in the generation before them: I, [[per:21184|Nitllu Himim]], speak to you in the year 2484 from the founding of Ihamiw, and I ask you to hear me not because I am wise, for I am not, but because you are weary, and the weary hear what the comfortable will not.

Consider first what has befallen us. Four years ago the host of our enemies came against us [[evt:21196|at Ashmath]], so many that our scouts could not count them, and we were scattered like chaff. Last year we bought peace by [[evt:21235|the treaty of Iazhash]] with whole provinces, and in the same season [[evt:21236|we were beaten at Iazhash]] by another foe. Our old king fell in the field. This year his son, our king [[per:21124|I Ash]], whom men rightly call devout, stood at the head of his people [[evt:21264|at Ashmath once more]], and was beaten, and set his seal to [[evt:21265|the peace of Ashmath]], by which we shall send our [[cur:387|ufhe]] across the border for twenty-two years. And scarcely had the ink dried when [[evt:21266|Iazhash]] was fought again. There, I am told, our men stood one against eight and did not run; and still the field was theirs.

Mark that, citizens, for it is the hinge of all I have to say. Courage did not fail us. Numbers did not always fail us. Something else failed us.

The priests of [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]] have an answer. They say: prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour, and adversity of their anger; therefore sacrifice more. And so we have sacrificed more. We have burned bulls to [[dei:83|Hath]] of the sky and poured wine to [[dei:84|Lashli]] of the moon; we have heaped the altars of [[dei:86|Shu]] with the grain our children needed; we have given to [[dei:87|Hutlhi]] below and [[dei:88|Mahtu]] above. Has the sky grown kinder? Ask the widows of Iazhash.

Here is the answer I bring, and I bring it as a messenger brings a letter, not as an author. There is one power over all, and the gods you have fed are its servants, as a steward serves the master of a house. You have been bribing the steward. The master is not bribed.

Nor is the world a thing without beginning or end, of which the wise need say little, as our priests are content to leave it. Time runs as a road runs, from its beginning to its end, and at the end is a judgement. A city is judged as a man is judged: not by its granaries, but by its justice. Which of you, being honest, will say that Ihamiw was just in her years of plenty? We grew fat and called it piety. The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, not because wealth is evil, but because appetite is a master that never says enough.

Do not think that I preach disorder. I preach the opposite. The good life is the life of duty, of order and of right sacrifice; but the right sacrifice is of the self, and the order is the order of the one law, and the duty is owed first to the master and only then to his stewards. Neither do I say that every man may make his own truth as a potter makes his own pots. Truth must be kept, and handed down in order, by men set apart for it. I shall set apart such men, and they after me shall set apart others.

And hear this, which will offend you most. The word is not for Ihamiw alone. It is for the men who beat us at Ashmath and the men who beat us at Iazhash. Our enemies too shall stand at the judgement. Let us go to them, when the truce allows, not with spears but with this letter.

So begins [[evt:21328|the Way]] here, in this city, in this year of defeats. Let those who will hear it call themselves of [[mov:72|Ishiu]]. Let those who will not, go in peace. And let our king, if these words reach him, know that a devout man is the first that the one master calls.
