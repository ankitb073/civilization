*[Obverse.]*

To the chief of the priests of [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] in [[set:7|Ana]], say: thus says [[per:700|Silla]], king of [[pol:6|Ana]], the old lion, beloved of [[dei:0|Atlie]] and of Nieshashta, your lord.

With me it is well. With the camp it is well. With the spears of Ana it is well. May it be well with you, with the house of the goddess, with the oil and the barley of the house.

Hear what I say, and let it be read out at the gate, and let it be read out in the house of the goddess.

You know how the road of [[reg:13|I'a'ie'i]] was shut. You know who shut it. [[per:908|Tlamfen Simen]] shut it, he who took the seat of [[pol:2|Mieshlothie]] that was not his, he who sat down where his lord had sat. Since he sat down, the boats of Ana have not gone up the river. Since he sat down, the silver of Ana has not come back. The merchants of Ana sat by the shut road and wept. Four summers they have wept. This is the fiftieth year of my kingship, and I said: it is enough.

Last year at [[evt:684|Toiyoe]] the gods were hard to us. I do not hide it. The dust went up to Atlie, the sun was hidden, and the men of Mieshlothie came out of the reeds like wasps from the nest. There fell [[per:921|Satlietlada]], my captain, the strong of arm. He fell with his face toward the enemy. Many of ours lay down with him. Weep for him in the house of the goddess; give his name to the singers.

But hear what Nieshashta did this year. Hear it and say it twice.

At [[evt:686|Toiyoe]] a second time I sent [[per:924|Simen'ash]], the swift, the son of the spear, against Tlamfen Simen. Simen'ash gave the ram to Nieshashta at dawn. Simen'ash poured beer to [[dei:3|Niele'i]] at the crossing of the river. And the goddess heard, and the river heard. Tlamfen Simen broke. Tlamfen Simen ran. The men of Mieshlothie lay down in the mud by the river, in heaps they lay down, two of theirs for each one of ours. The field was Ana's. Say it again: the field was Ana's. Satlietlada is paid for.

Now I sit before the walls of [[set:2|Mieshlothie]]. Its gate is shut. Its men stand on the wall and shout insults, but their voices are thin. Their granaries are not full. Their wells are low. I have set my camp by their gate, and my spears around their wall, and no donkey goes in, and no donkey comes out. Let the usurper eat his insults.

*[Reverse.]*

Therefore do this. Open the storerooms of the king. Send me barley. Send me oil. Send me beer for the men, for the nights by the wall are cold. Send me rope and timber and pitch. Send me bronze from the house of the smiths. Weigh the silver for the smiths in [[cur:0|Sovosh]], weigh it before the scribes, and let no smith say he was cheated.

Let the priests give a white ram to Nieshashta. Let them give a black ewe to [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth, that the earth may hold the wall of our camp. Let them pour water to Niele'i at the river-gate. Let them keep the lamp of [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] burning in the moon-house until I come home. Let them ask [[dei:1|Uelti]] of wisdom for good counsel for the king, for the king is old and the wall is high.

What ends will begin again; the wheel turns, say the priests of [[mov:0|La'im]]. The road was open before the usurper sat down. The road will be open again. When it is open, the boats will go up and the silver will come down, and I will fill the house of the goddess with the spoil of Mieshlothie.

Send word back with the runner. Let me hear that it is well with the house of the goddess.

*[Left edge.]*

> Tablet of the king, written in the camp before the wall, in the 50th year of Silla.
