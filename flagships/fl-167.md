title: Verses on Five Reed, Our Lady of Victories

*Here follow certain verses that were found nailed to the door of the alms-house of [[set:264|Ölie]], in the 24th year of Nitllu Foyatl. The scribe copies them that the wise may weep and the foolish laugh.*

Sing of [[per:37905|Nitllu Foyatl]], Five Reed, our gracious queen,  
the daughter of [[per:37767|Ie Shimin]], the greatest ever seen;  
a reed that bends to every wind that blows across the fen,  
and counts her many victories, and never counts her men.  

At [[evt:38547|Zhaalakhazhash]] she held the field, as every herald cried;  
they did not cry the other sum: ten thousand of ours had died.  
At [[evt:38553|Kivunge]] she held the field; the heralds cried once more;  
the widows of [[pol:1497|Ölie]] cried too, but softer than before.  
  *So ring the bells of Ölie, ring them loud and long:*  
  *a victory, a victory, and who will count the throng?*  

Those were the men of Unushti, a mighty host and grim;  
she put the [[evt:38554|Unushti Revolt]] down, and set her seal on him.  
Then came the rebels of the Tlue, some forty thousand, by my soul,  
and she went out with thrice their number, men to take the toll.  

At [[evt:38564|Kheye]], says the chronicle, the rebels left seven thousand dead;  
of ours, near seventeen hundred lay with arrows through the head.  
At [[evt:38574|Tivnu]] thirty thousand came, and seven thousand she slew;  
and eighteen hundred of our sons went home in shrouds of blue.  
  *So ring the bells of Ölie, ring them loud and long:*  
  *a victory, a victory, and who will count the throng?*  

The priests of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] say the wise withdraw from wine,  
from wealth and every appetite, from purple and from swine.  
Our lady heard the sermon through, and nodded, and was moved;  
and ordered forty roasted cranes, to show that she approved.  

The tax is laid in [[cur:823|myiu]] on the baron and the serf;  
the baron sells the serf's last cow, the serf lies down in turf.  
The baron owes his lady men, the lady owes him bread;  
she pays him well in banners, and he pays her with the dead.  
  *So ring the bells of Ölie, ring them loud and long:*  
  *a victory, a victory, and who will count the throng?*  

Now in this year, to please the gods, she sends the host to Mane,  
a [[evt:38641|Holy War of Ölie against Mane]], to make the heathen plain;  
and while she marches to the south, or east, or where she please,  
the [[evt:38643|Yiotl–Ölie War]] comes knocking at her knees.  

Young [[per:38166|Wilau]], her son, sits by her side and learns the royal art:  
to call a funeral a feast, and count it from the start.  
The gods keep the boy, the gods keep us, the gods keep the crown from harm,  
and keep the reed from bending till it breaks across our arm.  

You lords who read these verses, do not seek the hand that wrote;  
it is the hand of every man who carried spear or coat.  
We love the realm, we love the gods, we pay the lord his due;  
we only ask, when next the bells are rung, that one be rung for you.  
  *So ring the bells of Ölie, ring them loud and long:*  
  *a victory, a victory, and who will count the throng?*
