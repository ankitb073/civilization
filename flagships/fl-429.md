*[Obverse. To be read aloud at the gate on the day of the new moon.]*

Thus says [[per:411|Nabule]], lord of [[pol:20|Adhugumgi]], beloved of [[dei:41|Khudhaze]] of the high sky, beloved of [[dei:45|Nijnande]] of the spear, chosen of the gods, the strong one, the keeper of the gate.

Hear it, men of Adhugumgi. Hear it, women of Adhugumgi. Hear it, herdsmen of the hills and fishers of the shore. Let the crier cry it. Let the scribe set it in the clay. Let no man say: "I did not hear."

In the days of the founding, the gods set a seat in [[set:49|Adhugumgi]]. This was the [[evt:311|Founding of Adhugumgi]]. [[per:348|Navavun]] sat on the seat. Seventeen years she sat on it. I do not speak against Navavun. She built the wall. She dug the well. She poured to the gods. Let her name be honoured among the dead.

But hear. In the seventeenth year of Navavun the seat shook. The hand of Navavun was weak. The great men of the household quarrelled in the courtyard. The herds were not counted. The tax was not weighed. The gate stood open at night, and the thieves of the hills came in and drove out the sheep. Who would shut the gate? Who would count the herds? The great men quarrelled. The great men did not shut the gate.

Then I, Nabule, shut the gate. I took the spear of the gate in my hand. I stood at the gate three nights. On the fourth day the gods spoke.

[[dei:44|Shidhu]] of the storm spoke: he sent the rain on the dry field.  
[[dei:46|Kalu]] of the earth spoke: she opened her lap; the barley came up.  
[[dei:42|Bumbe]] of the sea spoke: he filled the nets of the fishers.  
[[dei:43|Shilmu]] of wisdom spoke: she put the word in the mouth of the priests.  
The priests said: "Nabule shut the gate. Let Nabule sit on the seat."  

So I sat on the seat. The gods gave it. I did not take it. Whoever says "Nabule took the seat," let him go to the priests of [[mov:6|Khukunji]] and ask them who gave it. They will tell him: the gods gave it.

Now it is the 3rd year of Nabule. Hear what I have done.

I have counted the herds. The herds are many.  
I have weighed the tax. The tax is just.  
I have shut the gate at night. No thief has come in.  
I have mended the wall that Navavun built. I have put my name beside her name.  
I have given the bull to Khudhaze. I have given the ram to Nijnande. I have given the fish to Bumbe.  
I have fed the widow at the gate. I have fed the orphan at the gate.  
The barley came up three years. Is this not a sign? Prosperity is the sign of the gods.  

Hear also this. There are men who sit in their houses and say: "The seat was not his." There are men who whisper at the well. I know their names. I have not yet spoken their names. Let them come to the gate and bow, and I will forget their names. Let them not come, and Nijnande will remember their names.

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The days of Navavun ended. The days of Nabule began. So the wheel turns. Who will stop the wheel?

*[Reverse.]*

Whoever breaks this tablet, may Khudhaze shut the sky on him.  
Whoever scrapes my name from the wall, may Kalu shut her lap to him.  
Whoever honours it, may the barley come up for him, as it came up for Nabule.  

> In the 3rd year of Nabule, lord in Adhugumgi.
