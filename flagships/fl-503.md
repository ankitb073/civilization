title: Lament for Kabave, Mother of Six Lands

*[Obverse.]*

Weep, O [[set:12|Kabave]], great city, mother of lands, weep.  
Weep, O gate of the tribute, the tribute does not come.  
Weep, O road of the envoys, the envoys do not come.  
Weep, O storehouse, your floor is swept and nothing is on it.

O [[dei:48|Kudze]] of the high sky, you saw it and you did not stop it.  
O [[dei:51|Talu]] of the broad earth, you felt the land crack, and you did not hold it.  
O Nüdinande of the war, where was your spear?  
O Tuve of the house below, you are the only one who grew rich.

Once the lands came to Kabave as the rivers come to the sea.  
They came with sheep, they came with oxen, they came with silver.  
They came with the [[cur:201|Limbu]] weighed out in the scales of the great house.  
Once the lord of [[pol:377|Kabave]] sat in the high seat, and six lands bowed down.

How did it begin? Who will tell it? The poet will tell it.  
First came the [[evt:10609|Revolt of Kutsimbüvi]]. One land stood up and said: "I am not yours."  
That was the first crack in the jar.  
Then the enemy came against us at Alunzhi, and at the [[evt:10692|Battle of Alunzhi]] our men fell,  
and the next year at Alunzhi again our men fell, thousands of them fell.  
At the [[evt:10718|Battle of Dambali]] our men fell.  
Our sons went out and came back on their shields, our sons went out and did not come back.  
Then the host of Kabave went out to Azhi to set things right,  
and at the [[evt:11022|Battle of Azhi]] the many were beaten by the few.  
That was the second crack, and the third, and the fourth.  
A jar with four cracks still holds water for a while.  
Then one morning you lift it, and it is in your hands in pieces.

*[Reverse.]*

So came the [[evt:11268|Collapse of the Kabave State]].  
In one year the jar broke. In one year the six lands walked away.

[[pol:506|Puvu]] said: "I am my own." [[pol:507|Nilu]] said: "I am my own."  
[[pol:508|Kankushe]] said: "I am my own." [[pol:509|Nzhinduvi]] said: "I am my own."  
[[pol:510|Tadudüve]] said: "I am my own." [[pol:511|File]] said: "I am my own."  
Six mouths, one word. Six lands, and no mother.

And Kabave, the great city? In the [[evt:11269|Rise of Puvu]] the city became the seat of Puvu,  
the seat of one land, the seat of one shard.  
The great hall is still standing. The great hall is too large for those who sit in it.  
The scribes still write, but they write for Puvu.  
The priests still pour, but the cups are smaller.

Weep for the market of Kabave. The traders of Nilu go to Nilu now.  
Weep for the gate of Kabave. The guards at the gate know every face.  
Weep for the old men of Kabave. They sit in the sun and say: "In my day."  
Weep for the children of Kabave. They do not know what was lost.

Nine years now the city has been small.  
Nine harvests have been cut, and none of them came to the great storehouse.  
But the poet still counts the years of Kabave.  
This is the two hundred and sixty-third year of Kabave. The poet counts it.

O gods of [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]], each in your place, each with your due:  
the wheel turns. What ends will begin again.  
What was broken into six may be gathered into one.  
Let the potter come who can mend the jar. Let him come soon.

Until he comes, let the women of [[set:12|Kabave]] sing this lament at the new moon.  
Let them sing it at the gate of the tribute, where the tribute does not come.

*[Colophon.]* In the 263rd year of Kabave. Written by a servant of the great house, for the great house.
