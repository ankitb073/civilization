In AY 4953 Nambili's trench along the old wall of Linnshal cut through "a thick layer of ash over collapsed walls", which his preliminary report (PAL·EX·00239) dates to AY 4395, the Fifth Sack of the city. The same dig recovered a soldier's scratching on a statue base (PAL·GR·00241): "we came, we took, we go home rich." That year, in that city, a man of twenty-six wrote out the peace. His parchment survives complete, with its seals, and ends with the sentence that is nearly all the archive says of him: "Drafted by Shifpimtin."

## What the record keeps

[[per:49015|Shifpimtin]], whose name means *glory eagle*, was born in AY 4369, one of the Jego, in the old Yidhe Empire's last years. The record says nothing of his birthplace, parents, marriage, children, schooling or face. He was eighteen when the Yidhe state collapsed in 4387 and [[pol:1785|Tallin]] rose from it under [[per:41722|Balshiymaanin I]], with its capital at [[set:0|Taulin]]. Two years later, at twenty, he became the first of Tallin's envoys the record names, and he held the office for thirty-six years, until his natural death in 4425 at fifty-six.

Two documents survive from his hand: treaties on sealed parchment, kept at Taulin in the university library and the palace archive. They are twenty-three years apart and each fills about a page. Everything else is read around them.

By the end of the 4390s the state he served counted 17,732,516 people in 113 districts, with an army of 203,226 men. It professed the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]], a faith of compassion and release from suffering, and paid in [[cur:897|rimi]] of 8.40 grams of silver. It also launched four wars of conquest in his lifetime and won them all. Its envoy likely recorded more than he bargained.

## Six districts or twelve

The first treaty (PAL·TR·02631) ended the [[evt:42209|Tallin–Kuevezhenga War]] of 4392–4395. It was an unequal war: at Nilu in 4393 the general Tupufnen brought 109,568 men against 14,306 and lost 720 to the enemy's 3,548; at Tathriubu in 4395 he brought 68,433 against 5,743 and lost 371 to 1,970. Then Linnshal was sacked, and the peace was made there.

The text promises "a firm, sincere and perpetual peace", then in its last article limits that to a pledge to "refrain from any act of force against one another for a period of 20 years". Kuevezhenga, which the treaty calls Tuvizhinge, "renounces in favour of Tallin all rights and title" to six districts in Diebeguvebenga, Tathriubu among them, and pays 1,283,378 Nuyilith, its own coin: at the record's 14.8 grams of silver apiece, nearly nineteen tonnes. None of this wording was his invention. The Treaty of Zhaalakhazhash (PAL·TR·02604), drafted for other states sixteen years before his birth, uses the same formulas almost word for word, trade clause included.

Beside this plain instrument the chronicles lie in character. Taalidiminin of Tallin, writing in 4440, says "The land which they had stolen was returned to Tallin, 12 districts in all" (PAL·CH·07435). He doubles the cession, turns a conquest into a restitution and gives Kuevezhenga 34,870 men at Nilu. Nuerushienmumath, Kuevezhenga's historian, writing in 4450, says her king made peace "out of mercy for the common folk", and that "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor" (PAL·CH·07436). The atlas frontier shows six districts. Of the three accounts, only the envoy's agrees with the land.

## No less favourable

By 4418 Tallin was an empire, ruled by Balshiymaanin II, son of the first. The [[evt:42479|Tallin–Yidhe War]] went as the earlier war had: four battles, all won by Tallin, the last at Virathkhanu, where Tupufnen's 102,012 men beat 32,139 and lost 1,762 to Yidhe's 8,468. Shifpimtin, now forty-nine, drafted the peace (PAL·TR·02648). It takes sixteen districts with Vuvu and swears the same twenty-year truce. But where his first treaty asked for silver, this one asks only that Yidhe "shall accord to the goods and shipping of Tallin treatment no less favourable than that accorded to any other nation."

The clause was old, but of the three peaces Tallin made in his years this is the only one without an indemnity. The Treaty of Zhinontunu in 4406 had demanded, in the king's own words, "silver to the value of some nine million rimi" (PAL·LT·01799). The record does not say whose idea the change was. A ruler in his first war may have wanted it, or an envoy nearly thirty years in office may have argued that a market outlasts a payment. The chroniclers did not notice. Taalidiminin turned sixteen districts into "38 districts in all" (PAL·CH·07487), and Lukhnenunin of Yidhe fell back on the stock consolation of beaten courts, the same sentence Kuevezhenga's historian used (PAL·CH·07488).

## Envoys came and went

One line in the archive may show Shifpimtin at work, without naming him. The annals of Yidhe, under 15 Ushuth 2163, say: "Envoys came and went; the Treaty of Virathkhanu was sealed" (PAL·AN·00814). The treaty and both chronicles bear the same date. No text survives of the Treaty of Zhinontunu, which closed the [[evt:42347|Tallin–Zhinontunu War]], and the record does not say who drafted it. The old king's letter to the hostage from Zhinontunu defends its terms in an envoy's idiom: "Those are hard terms. They are less hard than the terms the field would have written."

From the survey of the 4390s to that of the 4410s, Tallin grew from 113 districts to 157. Twenty-seven of those forty-four came at Zhinontunu, sixteen under Shifpimtin's second treaty. Both of his peaces outlasted their twenty years. Tallin never fought Kuevezhenga again before that kingdom fell to the Tunnge Empire in 4428, and never fought Yidhe again before Yidhe ended in 4489.

He died in 4425, the year the [[evt:42606|First Tallin–Pienpedhzhe War]] began; the record does not say whether he lived to see it declared. Its peace was drafted by Dutuven, an envoy since 4415. The soldier at Linnshal boasted of going home rich. The envoy left two pages that said, more truthfully than anyone else in Tallin, what had been taken.
