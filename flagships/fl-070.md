title: How the House of Navnga Was Divided
---
I saw it. With my eyes I saw it. Let no one say it is a tale of old women.

I write in the 446th year of [[pol:40|Navnga]]. Others count the years by new kings. I count them by Navnga, as my father counted them, and his father before him.

Hear how the great house stood. From the sea to the hills it stood; from the river to the marshes it stood. One king sat in [[set:10|Navnga]] and the governors came up to him with their tribute, with their oxen, with their silver rafri weighed in the scales. [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] of the river gave water to the fields; [[dei:22|Vinba]] of the earth gave barley; the priests of [[mov:3|Ryethki]] sang at the gate each morning. So it was for four hundred years.

Hear how the house was shaken.

In the days of my grandfather the men of Tallin came against us. At [[evt:3696|Shivange]] the sons of Navnga fell. At [[evt:3713|Taulin]] the sons of Navnga fell. At [[evt:3727|Nilinge]] the sons of Navnga fell. Navnga did not give up its land, but the young men did not come home.

In the same years came [[evt:3665|the Marsh Pestilence]]. It came out of Tallin on the heels of the men of Tallin; it came up out of the wet ground. It went into the houses. In my grandfather's street, of three houses one was emptied. The dead were carried out at night, and there were not enough priests to sing over them. The fields were not sown. The granaries were not filled.

Then the old king went to his fathers, and there was no right order. [[evt:3982|The usurper Bünin]] took the seat by the strength of his spearmen, and the governors looked at one another. One said: "If a man may take the seat by the spear, why not I?" Another said: "The tribute goes up to Navnga, and what comes down to us?" The priests read the liver, and it was black.

Hear now what I saw in the 422nd year of Navnga, when I was a boy of twelve summers.

In the spring the messengers from the provinces did not come. In the summer the tribute did not come. In the autumn there were six banners where there had been one. Each governor shut the gate of his city. Each called himself king. Each made his own seal.

The house of Navnga became six houses. [[pol:165|Yida]] took the city of Navnga itself and the fields around it. [[pol:166|Noni]] took its city. [[pol:167|Kivinkuthakhu]] took its city. [[pol:168|Rela]] took its city. [[pol:169|Bene]] took its city. [[pol:170|Nothinga]] took its city. So was [[evt:4038|the fall of the house of Navnga]], and no great battle was fought for it, and no king was killed for it. It fell as an old wall falls in the rain: first a crack, then a stone, then the whole.

I saw the scribes of the palace carry their tablets out of the great house. I saw the royal standard taken down from the gate. I saw a governor's man stand where the king's man had stood, and the people bowed to him, because what else could they do? I saw my father weep. He did not weep at his own father's grave. He wept then.

Who did this? The usurper did it. The pestilence did it. The men of Tallin did it. And the gods did it, for we had grown fat, and we did not give [[dei:21|Duminu]] of the storm his due, and the storm came.

Now I am a man of thirty-six. I live in the city that was Navnga's and is Yida's. The market is full; the river runs; the barley grows. The young men say: "What does it matter who sits in the great house?" They did not see it. I saw it.

It is the teaching of Ryethki that all things turn on a great wheel, and what ends will begin again. So I set this down in clay, that when the wheel turns, and the house of Navnga stands again, the scribes will know how it fell. O [[dei:25|Novegu]], lord of wisdom, keep this tablet.
