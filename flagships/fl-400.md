**THE HUMBLE PETITION OF THE PEOPLE OF THE DISTRICTS OF [[reg:2|Nüvi]], PRINTED AT THEIR OWN COST AND PRESENTED AT [[set:100|Khuele]] ON 3 NUERNIN 2820 OF THE GIGISHYU**

*To [[per:47097|Kanumalin]], Chairman of the Nation, who was our President before the Revolution and whom our grandfathers, had they lived to see him, would have called King:*

SIR,

We, the undersigned farmers, labourers, carters, mill-hands, schoolteachers and widows of Nüvi, being loyal citizens of [[pol:2001|Khuele]] and faithful children of [[mov:57|Gigishyu]], beg leave to lay before you the condition of our districts in this the fifth year of the [[evt:48328|Famine in Nivi]]. We do so in print because our letters have not been answered, our telegrams have been returned unread, and our delegates have been kept waiting in the corridors of the Ministry until their boots wore through.

**I. THE FACTS.**

1. The rains have failed season after season. The engineers of the Agricultural Bureau, to whom we put the question in writing, reply that the climate of the whole country has been warming for generations, and that our districts, lying low and dry, suffer most from it. They call it the [[cli:251|Industrial Warming]]. We call it the end of our wells.

2. Of every four souls who lived in Nüvi when the hunger began, one is now dead or gone. We do not ask you to believe our own count. Ask your own registrars. Ask the priests who have buried our children without coffins, because the timber was sold for bread.

3. Those who could walk have walked. The roads to the frontier are full of carts and handbarrows. Whole villages have gone over the border; the newspapers call it the [[evt:48329|Migration of the Imu into Mikhukhizhi]], as though it were a holiday excursion. Our sons and daughters are working in foreign mills under foreign masters, and sending home postal orders that do not arrive.

4. Grain is not scarce in the capital. We read in the Gazette that the measure is quoted at about half a [[cur:1148|Mave]] on the Khuele exchange. We do not doubt it. But the railway does not carry it to us at that price, or at any price we can pay, for the merchants hold it back for the cities, and we have nothing left to sell but the doors of our houses.

**II. WHAT WAS PROMISED.**

When the [[evt:48350|Khuele Revolution]] was proclaimed, the posters on our station walls said: *Bread and order for every district.* We believed them. We pasted the posters up again with our own hands when the wind tore them. We have had order. We are still waiting for the bread.

**III. WHAT WE ASK.**

1. That the State requisition grain from the warehouses of the capital and send it by rail to Nüvi, at the State's cost, before the winter.

2. That the soup-kitchens of the temples be supplied by the Ministry, and not left to the charity of the faithful, who are themselves hungry.

3. That the tax upon land be remitted in the stricken districts for so long as the hunger lasts.

4. That those who have gone over the border be permitted to return without fine or penalty, when there is bread to return to.

5. That deep wells be bored by the State engineers, since the old wells no longer reach the water.

**IV. A WORD TO THE CHAIRMAN.**

Sir, the teaching of Gigishyu, which you and we profess alike, is that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering. We do not ask to be released from suffering by death; too many of our neighbours have been released already in that way. We ask to be released by the train, the well and the sack of grain. The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. Let the fields of Nüvi begin again, and let it be said that [[per:47097|Kanumalin]] turned the wheel.

We remain, Sir, your loyal and hungry fellow-citizens.
