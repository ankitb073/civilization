**[[per:24169|Tashefen]], Emperor of [[pol:1004|Yeda]], to the envoys of his household at the courts of friends and neighbours: this description of our wedding, to be read aloud in each hall where you are received, in the year 3636 from the founding of [[set:0|Tallen]].**

It is the custom of princes to let others describe their weddings, so that the praise may seem to come unbidden. We have chosen otherwise. Rumour travels faster than any envoy, and arrives with mud on its boots; we would rather you carried the truth, clean, in our own words.

Know, then, that on the day of the spring offering, in the great court of Tallen, we took to wife [[per:24625|Povvanath]], daughter of the queen of [[pol:1060|Reney]]. Thus the [[evt:24911|Marriage of Tashefen and Povvanath]] was solemnised, and the houses of Yeda and Reney were joined.

**Of the rites.** The priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] kept the ancient order, as is their charge. At dawn a white bull was offered to [[dei:16|Domamnol]] of the sky, and the smoke rose straight, which the augurs pronounced a favourable sign. Bread of the first flour was broken before the altar of [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]]. The bride's hand was placed in ours before the hearth of [[dei:18|Eva]], and the fire was not allowed to sink while the vows were spoken. At night, when [[dei:19|Yenan]] stood full above the walls, the people of Tallen carried torches through every street, so that the city seemed for an hour to have taken the moon's office upon itself.

**Of the bride.** She came from Reney with her mother's household about her, modest in bearing and steady of eye. She is young, and we are not; we will not pretend otherwise, for the whole world can count. But we observe that a vine and an old wall are not ill matched: the wall gives the vine something to climb, and the vine gives the wall a reason to stand. We have charged our household to honour her as they honour us, and to teach her the customs of the court with patience, as one teaches a guest who will soon be mistress of the house.

**Of the alliance.** Let no court suppose that this marriage was made in weakness. Two summers ago our arms concluded the [[evt:24892|Treaty of Nin]], after our generals had thrice turned back the enemy at Nin and at Vuvu. Yeda did not seek this union because it needed a protector. It sought it because peace, like a house, is best built when the builder is strong and the ground is dry. The queen of Reney has a city that the gods have favoured; we have an empire that the gods have tested and not found wanting. Each of us gains what the other has.

**Of the gifts.** We gave to the bride's house a team of white horses, bolts of dyed wool, and silver in [[cur:568|Tonyo]] according to the custom of our fathers. To the poor of Tallen we gave bread for seven days, since grain is dear this year and a wedding feast that leaves the street hungry is a feast the gods do not attend.

**Of our intent.** We are of an age when a man thinks less of his own pleasures and more of what he will leave. What we would leave is this: that Yeda and Reney should remember, long after we have gone where all men go, that they once sat at one table and broke one loaf. The wheel turns; what ends will begin again. We would have this friendship be among the things that return.

Read this, then, wherever you are received; and if any lord asks you whether the Emperor of Yeda is grown too old for weddings, tell him that the Emperor danced until the second watch, and that [[dei:15|Venba]] of the earth did not complain of his feet.

*Given at Tallen, under the great seal, and copied for each embassy.*
