*[Obverse. A lamentation tablet from the temple of Peme. Each stanza ends with the refrain, marked by a double ruling; it is printed here in italics.]*

Woe, the great house! Woe, the house of [[pol:475|Devegobevenga]]!  
Its roof is on the floor. Its beams lie in the courtyard.  
The rain comes into the room of the king. The goats walk in the room of the scribes.  
*Woe, the great house! Who will lift the roof?*

[[dei:16|Domamnol]], father of the sky, you held the roof up with your hand.  
Why did you take away your hand?  
[[dei:14|Volme]], lady of the spear, you stood at the door with your spear.  
Why did you go away from the door?  
*Woe, the great house!*

Hear how the beams were pulled out, one by one. Count them, singer.

The first beam: the [[evt:12760|Battle of Virathkhanu]], in the days of our grandfathers.  
The strangers came to us; the field was theirs. The beam cracked; the roof held.

The second beam: the [[evt:12932|Battle of Muvush]].  
Our spears went out, many against few; the few stood, and three thousand of ours lay down.  
The beam fell. The roof held, but it groaned.

The third beam: the [[evt:13041|Battle of Unushti]].  
Our spears went out, three of ours to one of theirs; the field was theirs.  
The beam fell. The roof sagged.

The fourth beam: the [[evt:13063|Battle of Zhükhi]].  
Our spears went out again against the same enemy; the field was theirs again,  
and the captain of our host lay down among his men and did not rise.  
The beam fell. Dust came down from the roof onto the bread.

The fifth beam: the [[evt:13272|Battle of Biyitl]].  
Our spears went out, five of ours to one of theirs, and the captain of our host fell there also.  
Five to one, and the field was theirs! Weep, women of [[set:17|Peme]], weep.  
The beam fell. The walls leaned out.  
*Woe, the great house!*

Then, three summers since, the roof came down.  
No enemy pulled it. No fire burned it. It came down of its own weight,  
because there were no beams left under it.  
That was the [[evt:13400|Collapse of the Devegobevenga State]].  
*Woe, the great house! Who will lift the roof?*

*[Reverse.]*

Now each room has its own door. Now each room has its own lord.  
The room of the king is [[pol:588|Peme]]; it keeps the old hearth, but the hearth is small.  
The room of the store is [[pol:589|Joke]]. The room of the loom is [[pol:590|Shevanga]].  
The room of the spears is [[pol:591|Khongomno]]. The room of the oxen is [[pol:592|Mevemake]].  
The far room by the gate is [[pol:593|Mekokheje]].  
Six rooms. Six doors. Six lords. No roof.  
*Woe, the great house!*

Once the [[cur:260|Shenan]] of the great house went into every market.  
Once the priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] went up the steps of one temple, and all the lands poured oil there.  
Now the oil is poured in six temples, and the gods must go from door to door.

[[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] of the grain, you are not a stranger in any of the six rooms.  
Feed them all. Feed the room of the king first.  
[[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire, keep the old hearth in Peme burning.  
Yenan of the moon, count the years for us until the roof is lifted.

The priests say: the wheel turns. What ends will begin again.  
Let it turn, Domamnol. Put out your hand again.  
Two hundred years and more the roof stood. It can stand again.  
*Woe, the great house! Who will lift the roof?*

*[Lower edge.]*

> Sung in the temple of Peme on the day of the fallen roof, in the 235th year of Devegobevenga.
