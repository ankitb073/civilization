To my mother, who went out to the hill villages before the siege, say:  
Thus says [[per:782|Tsibulun]], your son, who sat in the seat of [[set:61|Tsinlampiba]], who sits in it no more.

May [[dei:33|Nabudzin]] of the river keep you. May [[dei:34|Tselni]] the moon keep you. May you never see what I have seen.

Hear me, my mother. Do not set this clay aside. Read it to the end, and then break it if you will.

You know the men of [[pol:14|Egu]]. You know their hunger. Two summers ago they came down to our fields, in the [[evt:602|Egu–Tsinlampiba War]], and our fields were not ours to reap. Last year they ringed our walls. That was the [[evt:606|Second Siege of Zhinlampive]]. We ate the oxen. We ate the seed-grain. We ate what the storehouse of [[dei:36|Nidziktti]] held, and the priests did not forbid it. The [[cur:13|Bilu]] in the treasury bought nothing, for there was nothing to buy.

This year, in the sixth year of my seat, I went out against them. I did not wait behind the wall to starve. I put on my bronze. I took the spears that were left to me and I went out before the gate. Let no one say that Tsibulun hid. Let no one say it. That was the [[evt:612|Battle of Zhinlampive]]. My spearmen stood, and stood, and then they did not stand. [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm sent the dust into our eyes and not into theirs. I do not know why. I gave him his bull every spring. I gave him his bull.

Then the men of Egu came in by the broken gate.

My mother, I will tell you what was done, and I will not make it smaller.

They went into the house of Nabudzin by the water and they took the bronze from the god's table. They went into the storehouse and carried the jars out on their shoulders, laughing. They went into the street of the potters and set it burning. The smoke stood over the city for three days, black as the house of [[dei:38|Shedartsi]]. They led the young women away by the wrists. They led the boys away with ropes on their necks. The old men they left in the street, because the old men could not be sold. This is the [[evt:613|Second Sack of Zhinlampive]]. It is the second time. We saw the first in my father's days, when I was young. Now we have seen the second.

I lay that night in the court of my house with my face in the ash. My servants were gone. My hearth was cold.

In the morning the captains of Egu came for me. They did not kill me. They wanted my hand. They set a tablet before me and they said: "Put your hand to the clay." And I put my hand to the clay. What else could I do? The city was already theirs. That is the [[evt:614|Conquest of Tsinlampiba]]. Seventeen years ago, at the [[evt:557|Treaty of Zhinlampive]], my father put his hand to the clay, and our city bowed its neck once and rose again. Now it has bowed its neck to [[pol:14|Egu]], and I do not know if it will rise.

They say I am lord of nothing now. Let them say it. I am still the son of my father. I still know the names of the gods of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] and the order of their offerings. The wheel turns. What goes down comes up again; the priests have always said so. I hold to it.

My mother, send me grain if you can. Send me a cloak. Send word of the boys of our house, whether they live. Do not come here. The road is full of the men of Egu, and they are not done eating.

May Tselni watch over you by night. May Nabudzin carry this clay safely to your hand.

Written in the sixth year of Tsibulun, in the city of [[pol:23|Tsinlampiba]], which was mine.
