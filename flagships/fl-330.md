*[Obverse. Tablet from the house of the singers at Wal'a. Lines divided by double rulings after each refrain.]*

Sing to [[dei:11|Nizhashta]], lady of the spear-shaft, red is her hand!  
Sing to [[dei:10|Masto]], lord of the sun, who sees the field from the height!  
Sing to the king, sing to [[per:1053|Yalni Shimin]], king of [[pol:17|Wal'a]], the bull of the river!  
Sing, for the gate of Shiüzhi is open, and the road to [[set:22|Wal'a]] is full of gifts.

The men of [[pol:28|Tilcui]] sat on the land of [[reg:6|I'iti]].  
They sat on it like a hen on her eggs. They gave nothing; they bowed to no one.  
The king said: "Let the gift come to the house of Wal'a, as the gift is due."  
Tilcui said: "No." So the king took down the spear from the wall.  
Six summers the spear did not go back to the wall.  
That was the [[evt:818|First Wal'a–Tilcui War]]. Sing it, singers.

The first ring was the [[evt:834|First Siege of Shiüzhi]].  
[[per:1103|Tlin'ina]], the king's general, the long-armed, went out.  
He went out against [[per:1095|A'i Li]], the new lord of Tilcui, who had taken the seat that same year.  
That was the [[evt:833|Battle of Shiüzhi]], before the walls of [[set:131|Tilcui]].  
A'i Li ran into his gate like a rat into a wall. The field was Wal'a's.  
Nizhashta, red is your hand. The field was Wal'a's.

One summer Nizhashta hid her face.  
That summer the king's men lay down by the walls, and the river carried them.  
We do not sing that summer. Let the mothers sing it. We sing the summer after.

The second ring was the [[evt:846|Second Siege of Shiüzhi]].  
The king went out himself, Yalni Shimin went out, the bull of the river,  
he poured oil to [[dei:6|Nili]] at the ford, he poured beer to Nizhashta at dawn.  
He went against A'i Li a second time, and the king did not turn his back.  
That was the [[evt:845|Battle of Shiüzhi]]. A'i Li ran into his gate a second time.  
The field was Wal'a's. Nizhashta, red is your hand. The field was Wal'a's.

The third ring was the [[evt:851|Third Siege of Shiüzhi]].  
Tlin'ina went out a second time, the long-armed went out,  
he went against [[per:1128|Lampin Mish]], the general of Tilcui.  
That was the [[evt:850|Battle of Shiüzhi]]. Lampin Mish ran; the dust hid his back.  
The field was Wal'a's. Four times the spears met before Shiüzhi, and three times the field was Wal'a's,  
and the last time is the time that is remembered.  
Nizhashta, red is your hand. The field was Wal'a's.

*[Reverse.]*

Then A'i Li opened the gate. He came out to the king.  
He put dust on his head before the king, he set his hand in the king's hand.  
They swore by the sky, they swore by the river, and that was the [[evt:852|Treaty of Shiüzhi]].  
Hear the words of the oath, you who stand at the gate:  
The fields of I'iti that were Tilcui's, they are Wal'a's.  
The silver of Tilcui comes to Wal'a every year, six summers and thirty it comes.  
Ten years the spear sleeps on the wall.  
The daughter of the king goes to the house of A'i Li; she goes to his son as a bride.  
So the two houses are one blanket; so the gift is sure.

O Masto, lord of the sun, take the fat of the bull!  
O Nili, lord of the river, take the barley and the beer!  
O Nizhashta, lady of the spear-shaft, take the bronze we hang in your house,  
the bronze of Tilcui, the shields of Tilcui, hung in your house.

Who is like the king? He went out, and the gate opened.  
Who is like the king? He went out twice, and twice he came home.  
The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. Sing to the king.

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Sung in the 20th year of Yalni Shimin, king in Wal'a, at the feast of the bride's going-out.  
> The singer who drops a line of this hymn, may Nizhashta stand against him.
