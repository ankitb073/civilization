*[A small envelope-tablet; the envelope bears the seal of a woman of the royal house and the words "to the hand of the queen, in Navnge".]*

To the queen, my mother, say: thus says [[per:19173|Nurukyinmumath]], your daughter, who kisses the ground before your feet.

May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky keep you. May [[dei:15|Vinbe]] of the earth make your path soft. May my father the king, [[per:19084|Bunethin]], great king of [[pol:779|Yide]], live, and his house stand, and his enemies bow.

Mother, it is done. I am a wife. The tablet that the scribes write for the treasury will say: the [[evt:19801|Marriage of Nurukyinmumath and Mokhkon]], the house of Yide joined to the house of [[pol:802|Nele]], in the 49th year of Bunethin. That is what the treasury will say. This tablet is for you, and it will say what the treasury does not.

The road was eleven days. On the third day the mule that carried the bronze mirror went lame, and I said, this is an omen, and the women laughed at me. On the seventh day a hawk flew across the road from the left hand to the right, and the priest who rode with us said, this is a good omen, and I did not laugh at him. On the eleventh day we came to the gate of Nele, and the people were on the walls, and they threw barley.

He met me at the gate. Mother, he is young. You told me so, and still I was not ready: he is younger than I am by seven summers, and he has the beginning of a beard and not the end of one. But he got down from his chariot and came to me on foot, which they say the men of Nele do not do, and he took the veil from my face with his own hand and not with the priest's rod. He said: you have come a long way. That was all he said. I have thought about it every night since.

The rites were as ours, and not as ours. Their priests pour first at the hearth, and only after toward the sky; when I asked why, his mother said: because the hearth is nearer than the sky. I think she meant it for me. She is a hard woman, and she counts my dowry in her head when she looks at me, every [[cur:406|Ngevu]] of it. I am courteous to her. I give her the first cup. You taught me that.

Now I must tell you the thing I do not want to tell you.

The merchants who come by the king's road say strange things about the great house. They say the far provinces will not send the tribute this year. They say that in Mane men speak of a king of their own, and in Nebe, and in Mahne; they say the governors of the borders sit in their halls and do not answer the king's letters. My husband's father hears these things and is silent at dinner, and looks at me. Mother, is it true? Is my father well? Is [[set:10|Navnge]] safe? Send me a tablet in your own words, not the words of the scribes, and send it by your own man.

If the great house is shaken, I am glad I am here, in a house that is bound to ours by oath, and I will do what a daughter of Yide must. If I am needed to speak to my husband's father, I will speak.

Send me also, if you can: the blue linen from my chest, the small lamp of [[dei:19|Yenan]] that stood by my bed, and seed of the yellow melon, for they do not have it here.

Tell the women of the palace I am not unhappy. Tell them it is not as the songs say, and not as they feared. Pour for me to [[dei:17|Nithikhchi]] of the grain at the new moon, that I may bear a son for [[per:19671|Mokhkon]] before the next harvest, and that he be born under a good sign.

The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. I left one house; I have begun another.

*Written in the house of Nele, in the 49th year of Bunethin.*
