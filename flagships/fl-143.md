*To His Excellency the Governor of [[set:319|Nivinga]], holding that place for the Emperor [[per:43645|Pavbukhshifin]] and the Empire of [[pol:1785|Tallin]]: the humble complaint of the elders, householders and fishermen of the coast of [[reg:28|Shithyu]], set down in the Governor's own tongue by a scribe of the harbour, and marked by us.*

Excellency,

We do not write to quarrel. We write because we were told, by the priests who came with your ships, that the Emperor is a just man and that his governors hear petitions. We ask only that you read what follows to its end, and judge by the law of your own people, since we are not permitted to judge by ours.

**First.** When your ships came into our bay this year we brought them water and fish, as our fathers did for every stranger driven onto this coast. We did not know that we were welcoming owners. Within a month the ground above the harbour, where our boats have been drawn up since before memory, was marked out with stakes and cords, and we were told that this was the [[evt:44577|Founding of the Colony of Nivina]], as your clerks write it, that the ground now belonged to the colony, and that we might no longer beach our boats there without leave. We ask: by what instrument was this ground given? No treaty was made with us. No price was offered. If it was conquered, against whom was the war fought? We have not lifted a hand against any man of yours.

**Second.** The grove on the eastern hill, where our dead are laid and where we keep the feasts of our gods, has been cut for timber for the palisade. We are told that our gods are shadows, and that the One whom your priests serve has no need of groves. But your own priests of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] teach, as we have heard them preach in the square, that the good life is compassion and the release of suffering. We ask Your Excellency whether it is compassion to dig up a grandmother to make room for a gatepost.

**Third.** Your priests go from house to house and tell our children that their fathers' gods will not save them at the last judgement. We do not forbid any man to speak. But we ask that they speak to the grown, who can answer, and not to the children, who cannot; and that no man who keeps his fathers' gods be refused work at the harbour for it, as has happened to four of our young men since the new moon.

**Fourth.** For the fish and the timber we sell to the colony we are paid in [[cur:1028|nivbe]] of silver. We object to the scales on which it is weighed against our goods, which are the colony's scales, and which give, by our reckoning, a third less than the old weights of this coast. Let there be one set of weights, kept in the open, and inspected by an elder of ours and a clerk of yours together.

**Fifth, and last.** Your sailors drink in the harbour and boast. They tell us of the [[evt:44551|battle at Jerathnu]], of the [[evt:44552|sack of Jerathnu]] that followed it, and of the [[evt:44553|Conquest of Jerathnu]], and they laugh and say that what was done there can be done here. We do not believe that the Emperor sent you across the sea to make another Jerathnu of a people who gave his ships water. But we would be glad to hear Your Excellency say so, in writing, under seal, so that the sailors may hear it read.

These are our complaints. We have set them down plainly, as men who mean to live beside you and not against you. If Your Excellency will hear us, we will keep the peace of this coast as faithfully as we have kept it since before [[set:0|Tallin]] was a name to us. If not, we ask at least that this paper be sent to the Emperor, that he may know what is done in his name at Nivinga.

Given at the harbour of Nivinga, on 5 Anshe 2351 of the Vishithmikh, as Your Excellency's clerks reckon the year.
