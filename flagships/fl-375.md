*Spoken by [[per:23177|Ma'shi Nim]], king of [[pol:1017|Uem'o]], before the lords and the household in the great hall of [[set:205|Pivish]], on the feast of the turning, in the year 1529 from the founding of Pivish.*

Lords of Uem'o, friends of my house, servants of the hall: two years have passed since this kingdom set its feet upon the road of [[mov:69|Kievi]], and today the last of the household altars of the old rite is closed. I have asked the teachers to let me speak first, not because a king knows more of heaven than a teacher, but because a king who asks others to change ought to be seen to change himself.

Let no one say that the [[evt:23651|Conversion of Uem'o to Kievi]] was the whim of a king. I did not lead the people to this road; I followed them onto it. When I counted the households of the kingdom, I found that more than half had already turned. The potters had turned, and the boatmen, and the farmers of the river fields. They had found, by the light within them and by plain reason, what their fathers had not been taught. Was I to stand in the gate with my back to my own people and call it piety? A shepherd who will not walk where his flock has gone is no shepherd, but only a man standing in a field.

Nor do I say that our fathers were fools. They served the gods of [[mov:1|Ya'kam]] with right offerings, and the gods did not abandon them. But hear what the teachers of Kievi say, for it seems to me both just and gentle: there is one divine power, and the gods our fathers knew are its servants, or its shadows cast upon the wall. A man who has seen only the shadow of a lamp has not seen nothing; he has seen something true, but not the whole of it. We do not curse the shadows. We turn to the lamp.

And what does the lamp ask of us? Not a hundred bulls. Not gold upon the altars. It asks justice in dealings and moderation in all things. It asks that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. Lords, I see the rings upon your hands, and I see the ring upon mine. I do not ask you to throw them in the river. I ask you to wear them as a man wears a borrowed cloak, ready to give it back.

You will remember the troubles of these last years. You will remember the [[evt:23536|Revolt of Nishmam]], and the long [[evt:23537|First Nishmam War of Independence]] that followed it, when men of our own land took up arms against the crown. You will remember the [[evt:23578|Battle of Bau]], where our host prevailed, and the [[evt:23579|Suppression of the Nishmam Revolt]], to which I set my own hand. Some of you said then: "See, the gods have given us the victory." I say to you now: take care. The victory was ours, and the dead of Bau were ours too, on both sides of the field. The lamp does not rejoice in a heap of the slain. If there is any sign in it, it is this: that a kingdom divided against itself is a house with a fire in the cellar, and that only justice will put it out.

Therefore I say: let the light go to Nishmam, but let it go as light, not as a sword. Kievi is for all peoples. Its teachers seek those who will hear; they do not drive men in with sticks. Let the men of Nishmam see that the lords of Uem'o deal justly, take moderate dues, and keep their word. That is a sermon no tongue can preach so well as an honest scale.

There are some among you, I know, who closed their household altars only this morning, and who closed them with a heavy heart. I do not despise you. The road is long. All things turn upon the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The rite of [[mov:1|Ya'kam]] has ended in this hall, but the seeking of the good has not ended, and it will not end.

Go now, lords of [[pol:1017|Uem'o]], and let each of you light the lamp in his own house. And pray for your king, who is an old man and has more need of light than any of you.
