*[Obverse. The surface is worn at the upper left.]*

[…] I, a servant of the king, a bearer of the shield, a man of the river country, I set down these words. [[dei:37|Novku]], lord of the counted words, keep them straight. [[dei:38|Shedartsi]], lord of the house under the earth, keep my companions who went down to you. Let no one say I have lied on this tablet.

In the fortieth year of Patun the elder, king of [[pol:14|Egu]], the drum was beaten in the city. The king said: "The land of [[reg:8|Duvegupuinga]] is good land. The barley stands thick there. Let Egu take it from [[pol:7|Buneynga]]." The priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] turned the wheel. The spears went out. I went out with the spears.

This is the first thing I saw. At the [[evt:727|battle of Riniy]], in the forty-first year of Patun the elder, [[per:1005|Kuttnakbethun]] led us, Kuttnakbethun of the red cloak. Against us stood [[per:910|Etsdayin]], lord of Buneynga, a young man, a man of many spears. Their spears were more than our spears. We went forward; we did not go forward. The ground was soft; the ground drank us. The field was theirs. Of the men of my hearth, I came back, and one other came back. The rest went down to Shedartsi. Kuttnakbethun, who is now with Shedartsi, wept in his tent. I saw it.

Then Patun the elder went down under the earth, full of years. His son sat upon his seat, [[per:1026|Patun]], our king, the young bull, may he live. The king said: "My father's war is my war." He made the offering to [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm, a black ox, and the smoke went straight up.

This is the second thing I saw. At the [[evt:734|battle of Ture]], in the second year of our king, the king himself went down. Against him stood [[per:1039|Natuanan]], the captain of Buneynga. The king stood in the front; I stood behind the king. We broke them. We drove them into the reeds. The field was Egu's. That night we sang; that night the beer was poured for Dumnu. Let the scribe write it twice: the field was Egu's.

This is the third thing I saw. At the [[evt:736|battle of Ture]], in the third year of our king, [[per:1044|Shababan]] led us against Natuanan. They had come back many, many more than we. We were few as the fingers. We stood; we did not stand. The field was theirs.

This is the fourth thing I saw. At the [[evt:737|battle of Yishdhagshal]], in the fourth year of our king, the king went down against [[per:1045|Lungutran]], the captain of Buneynga. The sun was against our eyes. The dust was in our mouths. Of all the battles, that was the worst battle. Our dead lay in rows like sheaves after the sickle, and their dead were few. I do not say more of Yishdhagshal. I will not say more.

This is the fifth thing I saw. At the [[evt:739|battle of Riniy]], in the fifth year of our king, we went down again to Riniy where we had first gone down. Lungutran stood against the king a second time. Their spears were three for our one. The field was theirs.

*[Reverse.]*

Then the king sat down at Riniy. The men of Buneynga sat down across from him. This was the [[evt:740|treaty of Riniy]]. Egu gave silver to Buneynga, silver weighed out in talents, more silver than I have seen in my life, more silver than the storehouse of Egu could spare. Ten years of peace were sworn. Ten years the spears were to sleep.

Seven years I carried the shield. Five fields I stood on. One field was Egu's. This is the truth, and Novku sees it.

Do not say the gods turned from Egu. The wheel turns; what goes down comes up. [[dei:33|Nabudzin]] still carries the barley to the city; the king still sits upon his seat. If my sons go down to Riniy, O Dumnu, let the field be Egu's.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in the 13th year of Patun, king of Egu. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Dumnu break his roof over him.
