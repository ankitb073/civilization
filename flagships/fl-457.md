**CORRESPONDENCE**

**A COMPLAINT FROM TAALATHYI**

*The following letter reached this office by the morning post. We print it as received, without endorsing its opinions, in the conviction that a free press must hear from those whom the Government's policy has touched most nearly.*

Sir,

I write in your language because it is now the language in which my people must make their complaints. I was born in [[set:113|Taalathyi]], in the Yiwutu country, in the street of the bookbinders, and I have lived in [[set:10|Navnge]] for nine years, where I have paid my taxes, kept the law, and heard your newspapers explain my homeland to me.

Four years ago the Government of [[pol:1960|Navnge]] pressed what it was pleased to call a colonial claim against [[pol:1969|Taalathyi]]. Your leader-writers told their readers that the country of [[reg:5|Yiwutu]] was disordered and required the order which only Navnge could supply. I do not know what disorder they meant. Our city had a queen, a council, schools, a post office and a railway station. It had, until the summer before last, an archive.

You will recall the summer before last, for it was celebrated in your streets. General [[per:45908|Duhufnen]] marched against our city with an army which your own War Office gave at five hundred and twenty-seven thousand men. Our queen, [[per:45886|Khuyishiwituwiwe]], went out to meet him with twelve thousand eight hundred. I do not pretend the result was ever in doubt. But I ask your readers to consider the figures that followed, which I have from your own official returns. Navnge lost sixty-seven thousand five hundred and ninety-six men on that field. Taalathyi lost four thousand two hundred and seven. Sixteen of your sons died for every one of ours, so that a claim might be pressed upon a city of booksellers. I grieve for them. Their mothers did not choose the war any more than ours did.

Then your army entered the city. It was not the first time Taalathyi had been sacked; our old men count ten such occasions before this one, and we are an old city. But this time the archive was burned. The registers of our births and marriages, the title-deeds of every house, the letters of our poets and the accounts of our guilds, centuries of them, went up together in an afternoon. When I wrote to the Ministry to ask by whose order the fire was set, I received a printed card thanking me for my interest.

And after all this, what? No treaty. No settlement. No governor, no schools, none of the order that was promised. Your army went home. The claim, we are told, has lapsed. Within the year a kingdom came against us from another quarter and took what you had left. We have changed masters, and you have changed the subject.

The summer before, the King, [[per:45991|Dimintun]], was cheered through your capital for the [[evt:46893|battle of Yuftuy]], at which, I read, three hundred and seventy-five thousand of his soldiers overcame thirty-five. The [[evt:46894|Treaty of Yuftuy]] that followed was printed in gold on your front pages. Taalathyi did not merit a paragraph.

I am told that since the [[evt:46911|founding of the Inithnòthtunism movement]], nine years ago, it has become the fashion in this city to say that history marches toward a better order, that the party knows its direction, and that the cause of the nation comes before the opinion of the individual. I have no quarrel with any nation's love of itself. I only observe that the march, when it passes through other people's countries, leaves footprints.

Your churches of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] teach that the good life is one of compassion and release from suffering, and that their message is for all peoples. I believe your priests are sincere. I would ask them only to read that teaching aloud in the War Office.

I ask three things: that the Government publish the order under which the archive of Taalathyi was burned; that any records carried away be returned to the people of Taalathyi, wherever they now live; and that those of us resident here, who have no country now to go back to, be allowed to remain without the annual humiliation of the police register.

I am, Sir, your obedient servant,

A NATIVE OF TAALATHYI, RESIDENT IN NAVNGE

*2 Baadhu 2596 of the Vishithmikh*
