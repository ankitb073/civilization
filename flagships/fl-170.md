I, [[per:16942|Navavun]], son of [[per:16820|Nalazhe]], king of kings in [[pol:637|Kazhe]], in the 67th year of my reign and the seventy-fifth of my life, being sound in mind though no longer in body, set down these words, that my son may read them, and the city hear them, and the gods judge them.

**Of my beginning.** My name means "flood," and the priests who named me did not know how well they named me. My father went to the gods when I was a small child, and I came to the seat as a boy of eight, with my mother Nashiyabu holding the reins for me until my hands could hold them. I honour her here before all men. Whatever I built, she laid the foundation.

**Of the war with Nilu.** Many wars I made in my long reign, and from every one of them Kazhe came home larger; but a wise king does not spend his people as a spendthrift spends his inheritance, and I will set down here only the two that were greatest. When Nilu would not be a good neighbour, I let the flood rise. At [[evt:17553|Shimbayi]], at [[evt:17564|Aguga]], and again at [[evt:17576|Aguga]], my armies met theirs, and every time the field was ours, and every time their dead outnumbered ours many times over. Then [[evt:17577|Nilu itself was opened]] to my soldiers. I forbade them to burn its records, holding that a city's memory is not plunder. By [[evt:17578|the peace of Nilu]], sworn in that city with my own hand, Nilu gave up two provinces and two towns, paid an indemnity in silver, and swore fifteen years of quiet. I have kept my side of that oath. Let my son keep it.

**Of my daughter's house.** My daughter [[per:17036|Nakhamale]] I gave in marriage to the king of Tadudüvi, so that the two houses should be one. When her husband went to the gods, she governed for their young son, and governed well. But a woman and a boy cannot hold a frontier against the world. When the men of that country would not accept my counsel, I sent my general, who bears my own name, and at [[evt:17665|Divnüzith]] and at [[evt:17668|Laavi]] their small forces were scattered; at [[evt:17669|Alunzhi]] the gates were opened, and nothing was burned. By [[evt:17670|the settlement at Alunzhi]], which my grandson signed with me, the lands of Tadudüvi were gathered under the roof of Kazhe. Let no one say I made war on my own blood. I brought my own blood home. That was the meaning of [[evt:17643|the last war of Tadudüvi]], whatever the songs of that country may say.

**My bequests.** To the house of [[dei:104|Shilimu]], lady of wisdom, I leave the library of my palace, that the teachers may use it; for the priests of [[mov:16|Ninzhe]] say that teachers are to be honoured, but no one owns the truth, and a king least of all. To [[dei:107|Nizhinande]], lord of war, I leave the standards taken at Aguga. To Bumbe of the sea and Khudhaze of the sky, a bull each year for ten years. To the poor of [[set:84|Kazhe]] I leave the grain of the royal granaries for one year, and to every soldier who served at Nilu, ten [[cur:335|Nzhibu]] of silver.

**To my son.** To my son [[per:17034|Nandudhu]] I leave the seat, if the gods and the great men confirm it. Rule as I ruled: slowly, then suddenly. Do not go to war for glory; go to war when the alternative is worse, and then go to win. Pay the soldiers before they ask. Honour your sister.

**A reflection.** All things turn on the great wheel. I received my father's realm small, and I leave it great; I do not imagine it will stay great because I wish it. Prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour, but favour is lent, not given. I have tried to deserve the loan.

Sealed in Kazhe, in the 67th year of Navavun. Let it be read in the hall of Kazhe when I am gone, and not before.
