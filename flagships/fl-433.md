*[Obverse. Letter tablet. The envelope is lost; the seal on the tablet itself is that of a woman of the royal house.]*

To the king of [[pol:188|Yisamashti]], my father, my lord, say:

Thus says [[per:4705|Tlamfen Lieze]], your daughter, your servant, whom you sent to [[pol:151|Khie'a]].

I fall at the feet of my father seven times and seven times. May [[dei:0|Atlie]] of the sky keep your city. May [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river fill your fields. May [[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, keep your enemies far from your walls.

My father said to me: "When the thing is done, write to me what was done, as an envoy writes, and leave nothing out." Now the thing is done, and more than the thing. I write.

**Of the coming in.** We came to [[set:83|Khie'a]] with the oxen and the carts. The carts were full of the gifts of Yisamashti: the dyed cloth, the oil in the sealed jars, the cups of silver, the bronze for the king. The men of Khie'a met us at the river. They put bread in our hands and salt in our mouths. They led us in by the great gate, and the women of the city stood on the roofs and cried out.

**Of the king.** [[per:4621|A'ininieme]], king of Khie'a, sat upon his seat. He was an old man; his beard was white. But his eyes were clear, and his voice was a king's voice. He rose for me. He said: "The daughter of my brother of Yisamashti is welcome in my house." He showed me the tablets of his first year, the [[evt:4265|Treaty of Lininadhe]], when he made the lord of Mikhushitsu bow under his hand. He said: "That was my first year. You are my last joy." So he said. I set down what he said.

**Of the king's sister.** At the right hand of the king stood his sister, [[per:4623|O'atl]]. She looked at the gifts; she looked at me. She said little. She counted the jars.

**Of the day itself.** On the day of the full moon, the priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] made the offering before all the gods, each in their place, each with their due. To Atlie a white bull; to [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth a black ewe; to [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon a jar of milk; to [[dei:1|Uelti]] of wisdom a tablet with my name and the king's name written on it. The priest broke the bread between us. The king put the band on my brow. The people cried: "The houses are one. Khie'a and Yisamashti are one house." Seven days they ate. Seven days they drank. This was the [[evt:4499|marriage of A'ininieme and Tlamfen Lieze]], and all of Khie'a saw it.

*[Reverse.]*

**Of what came after.** Before the year was out, the king went down to Muevas'e. He lay down, and he did not rise. I put on the dark cloth. I cut my hair. I wept for him; my weeping was not false. Then O'atl his sister sat upon his seat, and it is now the second year of O'atl.

**Of my place.** My father, hear me. I am the widow of the king, and I am a stranger in this house. O'atl gives me my bread and my oil; she gives me my women and my rooms. She does not give me her ear. The men of Waga have come out against Khie'a over the road of the traders, and the queen's captains go in and out of her house, and no one tells me what is said. I do not know how that war will end.

But this I say, and let my father weigh it: the oath was sworn before all the gods. The houses were made one. The king has gone down, but the oath has not gone down. Let my father send his envoy to O'atl and remind her of the oath. Let the road between Khie'a and Yisamashti stay open. And if my father wills that his daughter come home, let him send the carts; I will come.

> Written in the 2nd year of O'atl, queen of Khie'a, by my own hand, at Khie'a. Whoever breaks this tablet on the road, may Lashla'e hide his light from him.
