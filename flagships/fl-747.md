**I.**

We who came back sing this, in the year 2775 of the [[mov:24|Udunkala]],  
Not the poets of the hall, who were not here, but the bakers and the carters,  
The women of the washing-steps, the boys who ran with water,  
Those who came back to [[set:212|Tambüzhinu]] and found it twice a ruin.  

A city is not its walls, the teachers told us; a city is its citizens.  
We believed it in the schoolroom. We have tested it since.  
The walls are down, and we are still here, a few of us; judge for yourselves  
Whether that is a city, or only people standing in ash.  

**II. The first burning.**

In the summer two years gone the men of [[pol:967|Gishugizi]] came,  
More than the olive trees on the hill, more than the stones of the road;  
Our captain went out to meet them in the [[evt:25478|Battle of Dambüzhi]]  
And did not come back, and his shield came back without him.  

Then was the [[evt:25479|Third Sack of Dambüzhi]], which our grandfathers' grandfathers  
Would have told us was impossible, a third time, as if the city were a field  
That must be burned over before each sowing. They took the silver of the temple,  
They took the bronze from the fountains. They took my sister's husband to row.  

We hid in the quarries, in the cisterns, in the tombs of the old families,  
And when we came out the smoke stood over the harbour like a second hill.  
We said: it is over now. We said: the one Power has tested us and is satisfied.  
We were mistaken in the second part. The first part was the Power's business.  

**III. The second burning.**

The next summer they came again. Our army was broken at Vuzi;  
And because there was nothing left to take, they burned what we had rebuilt,  
The roofs of new timber, still pale; the shop with its door not yet painted;  
That was the [[evt:25493|Fourth Sack of Dambüzhi]]. I did not hide that time. I watched.  

**IV. The peace.**

Then the elders went out with olive branches, and our lord [[per:25097|Nalauts]]  
Put his seal to the [[evt:25494|Treaty of Dambüzhi]], that Gishugizi might have  
The lands they asked, and silver besides, and our bowed heads, and our oath.  
We are their vassals now. It was that or nothing. The elders chose the something.  

Do not ask the price of grain; it is cheap, under a [[cur:533|Lud]] the measure.  
Grain was never our trouble. Our trouble is that grain needs an oven,  
And an oven needs a roof, and a roof needs timber, and timber needs silver,  
And the silver goes out to the conqueror on carts, the silver of the peace.  

**V.**

The teachers say the world runs in a line toward a judgement,  
That nothing comes round again, that every deed is written once.  
If that is so, let it be written once, and plainly, here:  
That we came back. That we swept the steps. That we buried whom we found.  

Let it be written that the baker's widow lit her oven in the open air,  
On three stones, the first week, and gave the first loaves away.  
Let it be written that the boys pulled the nails from the burned beams to use again.  
Let the judge, at the end, weigh that against the fire.  

We who came back sing this. We are fewer each year.  
But a city is its citizens, the teachers said. Then here is the city. Count us.
