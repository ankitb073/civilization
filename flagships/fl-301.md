*[Obverse.]*

In the 1st year of [[per:115|Ngamultun]], king in [[set:0|Tallin]], I set down what my eyes saw. Let the scribe press it into the clay. Let [[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire harden it. Let no man scrape it away.

Hear it, [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear. Hear it, [[dei:16|Dumamnul]], father of the high sky. Hear it, [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the black earth, who drinks what is poured out. I was in the war. I carried the spear. I came home. I set it down.

**The going up.** The men of [[pol:11|Poyonga]] held the land of [[reg:9|Tupujinga]]. The barley of Tupujinga went into their jars. The sheep of Tupujinga went into their folds. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] opened the liver of a white ram. The priests said: "The land is for Tallin. Go up." The king said: "Go up." So we went up. So began the [[evt:155|First Tallin–Poyonga War]]. We sharpened the bronze. We gave the bull to Vulme. We gave the barley-cake to Nidhikhti, that the fields we took would bear.

**The first years.** Two summers we walked the borders. Two summers we burned their threshing floors and they burned ours. I do not count those days. The scribes of the king count them. I count my sandals, which I wore through three times.

**Nin.** In the third summer the general [[per:106|Jeytun]] led us. At [[set:1|Noni]], by the river crossing, we met the men of Poyonga. We were 1,630 spears. They were 852 spears. Their king came out before them, [[per:66|Tatureljifin]], lord of Poyonga, with the gold on his neck. He called on his gods. We called on ours. Vulme heard us.

Hard was the day at Noni. The dust hid the sun. 269 of ours lay down in the reeds. 295 of theirs lay down in the reeds. And Tatureljifin, king of Poyonga, lay down among them. His gold was in the dust. His gods did not lift him up. Let the singer say it twice: Tatureljifin lay down at Nin. So was the [[evt:156|Battle of Nin]], and the field was Tallin's.

**Poynge.** In the fourth summer Jeytun led us again. We were 2,816 spears; we were many as the stars of Dumamnul. We went to [[set:68|Poyonga]], to the walls of their city. [[per:111|Badominin]], the general of Poyonga, came out with 932 spears. His beard was grey. He was brave. I say it: he was brave. He did not run.

257 of ours lay down before the walls. 404 of theirs lay down before the walls. Badominin lay down with them. So was the [[evt:158|Battle of Poynge]], and the field was Tallin's.

**The burning.** Then we went into the city. This was the [[evt:159|First Sack of Poynge]]. We took the bronze. We took the oil. We drove out the oxen. The tablet-house of Poyonga burned; its tablets cracked in the fire like the voices of old men. Their words are gone. Ours remain. Eva ate their words and spared ours. Is this not a sign?

**The oath.** Then the men of Poyonga bowed their necks. In Poynge the oath was sworn before the gods; this was the [[evt:160|Treaty of Poynge]]. Poyonga gave Tupujinga to [[pol:1|Tallin]]. Ten years the spear would sleep. We came home singing. The women threw barley over us at the gate.

**The count.** Four summers of war. Two battles. One city burned. The scribes say: 526 of Tallin went to Vinba. 699 of Poyonga went to Vinba. More of theirs than ours. So the gods judged.

*[Reverse.]*

Now the old king has gone to the gods, and Ngamultun sits on the seat. The priests say: all things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. I say it after them. Vulme, keep the spear sleeping the ten years. Nidhikhti, fill the jars of Tupujinga for Tallin. Yenan of the night, watch over the men who lay down at Noni, ours and theirs.

> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Vulme break his spear; may Vinba refuse his bones.
