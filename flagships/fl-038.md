[[per:38489|Bilkulufath]], by the grace of the Most High sovereign of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]], keeper of the faith, to the lords of our council, to the bishops of the faith, and to the good people of our city of [[set:0|Tallin]]: greeting, and the peace that comes from above.

Know that on this day, by the favour of the One who weighs all things, our host has met the army of [[pol:1565|Binge]] before [[set:161|Linidhagushal]] and has driven it from the field. Let the bells be rung in every church of the realm, and let the bishops sing the hymn of thanksgiving, for the Most High has remembered His servants.

You will recall, my lords, how matters stood. Seventeen years ago, in [[evt:39168|the first war against Binge]], we came to this same place, and at [[evt:39183|Linidhagshal]] the enemy had the better of us; and at [[evt:39185|Nudhuruigirimu]] in the following year, by the treachery of the season and the sickness in our camp, we were again turned back. At [[evt:39186|the peace of Nudhuruigirimu]] I set my own seal to the terms, and our treasury paid out silver to Binge which our fathers had laid up rimi upon [[cur:897|rimi]]. I do not forget it. Neither does the Most High forget the humbling of the righteous.

Now the term of that peace is run out, and [[evt:39320|this second war against Binge]] has been taken up in justice. For the land of [[reg:8|Diviguvivinge]] was ours by right of old, and those who hold Binge hold it by no right at all, since [[evt:39247|the usurpation of Vaayushin]] put a thief upon the seat of their kings, and what was stolen is held still. Those who serve a stolen throne serve a lie, and the Most High does not give the victory to a lie.

The enemy came out under [[per:38764|Bumetaalin]], a captain of some skill, I will grant him that. He held the ground by the river and the old walls. At dawn our lords of the right hand went forward with their banners, each with the men he owes us by his oath; and I rode with the centre, as is fitting, for a sovereign who asks her vassals to bleed must show them that her own blood is not too precious for the field. Three times they stood. Three times we broke them. At the ninth hour their line gave way and they fled down the river road, and our horsemen followed them until the light failed.

The heralds have walked the field. They count above twenty thousand of the men of Binge dead before Linidhagushal, and the river is full of their arms. Our own loss is not light; many good knights are fallen, and I commend them to your prayers by name when the lists are sent. But it is far less than the enemy's, and the host is whole and in good heart.

As for myself, I took a blow in the press near the end of the day. The physicians have bound it and say I must lie still. Let no rumour run through the city that the sovereign is struck down: I write, or rather I speak and the clerk writes, with a clear mind and a grateful heart.

Therefore, my lords, I command you: let the levies of the coming season be raised without delay, and let no lord excuse himself from his service, for Binge is broken in the field but not yet humbled at the peace. Let the bishops keep the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] in every pulpit, and preach that the Judgement comes for kings and usurpers alike. Let the widows of the fallen be fed from our granaries. And let the people of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]] give thanks.

Given in our camp before Linidhagushal, in the year 4953 from the founding of Tallin, in haste, by the hand of our clerk, and sealed with our seal.
