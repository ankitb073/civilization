*[Obverse.]*

Say to my mother, in the street of the tanners in [[set:17|Peme]]: thus says your son, of the spearmen of the fourth hundred.

May [[dei:14|Volme]], lady of war, keep you. May [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]], lady of grain, fill your jar. May Yenan of the moon watch your door. I am well. I am alive. I say it first so you will not weep before you hear the rest. The scribe of the hundred writes this for me, and I give him a half-[[cur:166|Rengel]] for it, so read it twice.

Hear what was. Three summers we have been in this land. The first summer we fought at Nuvun. The second summer we fought before the walls of [[set:222|Nenshenuethnon]]. This summer we fought there again, and then we sat down before the walls, in the [[evt:9980|First Siege of Ninshinuesnun]], until they opened.

Mother, they were few. Their men on the wall were few; you could count them on your hands and your feet. Yet they were not cowards. They threw down stones. They shot from the gate-tower. They poured hot sand. Many of ours lay down under the wall who never saw it open. The son of the potter from our street lay down. Tell his mother he was brave. He was brave. Tell her it was quick. I do not know if it was quick.

More of ours died of the flux than of stones. The water of this land is bad. Boil it, the old men say. With what fire? There is no wood. We burned the doors of the farms. Volme forgive us, we burned the doors.

*[Reverse.]*

Now it is ended. The gate was opened. The king [[per:9905|Befpan]] came up from the camp in his chariot with the gold on the horses. Their lord, [[per:10173|Najema]], came out of the gate on foot with the elders, and bowed down, and they put their seals on the clay in the gate itself. The heralds cried it in the camp: the [[evt:9981|Conquest of Ninshinwesnon]]. This land is Peme's now. [[pol:433|Nenshenuethnon]] is ours. We shouted. I shouted too. I did not feel like shouting. I shouted.

We did not burn the town. The king forbade it, for it is ours now, and a man does not burn his own house. But the soldiers took what they could carry. I took nothing but a cup of copper, which I bought from a man of the town for a Rengel, honestly. I bring it to you. It has a fish on it.

The barley here is cheap, a Rengel and a little more the measure, but there is little of it, for we ate the fields. The people of the town look at us with hard eyes. A woman spat at my feet at the well. I did not strike her. What would you have done, mother? I think you would have spat too.

They say we go home when the barley is cut, all but the hundred who stay to hold the gate. Pray to Yenan that the fourth hundred is not that hundred.

Tell my father I have kept the spear he gave me, and the edge is good. Tell my sister I have a cup with a fish on it. Tell the potter's wife I am sorry. Give her the half of my pay when I come home. I swear it before Volme.

Your son, of the fourth hundred, alive.
