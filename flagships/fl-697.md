Five typed peace treaties of AY 4743–4779, now bound in the state archive of [[pol:1656|Ane]], end with the same line: "Drafted by Onivish." That line is nearly all that survives of [[per:49134|Onivish]]. The record gives her birth in 4714, her people, the Lizo, her appointment as envoy of Ane in 4734, at twenty, and her natural death in 4786, at seventy-two, still in office. It names no parents, husband or children; no letter, speech or likeness survives. An archaeologist knows people by what they left. Onivish left paper.

## A drafter among presidents

The record calls Ane a kingdom, but from 4702 it was a federation, led by presidents chosen for five-year terms; her treaties style it "the Republic of Ane". Onivish served under at least eleven, five of whom signed her treaties for Ane: Isyi Tlyi, [[per:45207|Tokh]], Sauae, Poashimin and Ie Mish. In her time Ane grew from 34,458,304 people in the 4710s survey to 51,200,538 by the end of the 4780s, and from 371 districts to 471. It dated its instruments by the era of its state cult, [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]], which asks justice in dealings and moderation in all things.

Every surviving peace treaty of Ane from 4743 until her death is hers, and the form never varies: a preamble, an article ending the state of war, the terms, a promise "to refrain from any act of force against one another for a period of 20 years", and the closing words "in two copies". Whether she sat at the tables or only wrote down what others agreed, the record does not say.

## One text, two stories

Each war she closed left a chronicle on each side, rarely agreeing; hers was the one page both sides signed.

At Likhtli in 4743 she ended the [[evt:45793|Tsey–Ane War]]: Tsey was to pay 49,605,487 Ta in ten annual instalments, some 356 tonnes of silver at the Ta's standard of the day (PAL·TR·02825). Tsey's Assembly rejected it, 128 to 213, one deputy calling it "not peace but surrender"; the government proceeded (PAL·PD·00015). Tsey's annalist recorded only that it "was sworn, and the land had rest" (PAL·AN·00794). Eighteen years later Tsey broke apart; the record counts her treaty, with two lost battles and two revolts, among the causes.

In 4753 she ended the [[evt:45860|First Wemu–Ane War]]. Ane's chronicler, Oi Lyizi, wrote that the enemy "came to beg for peace … Their pride was broken" (PAL·CH·08042); Wemu's, that "at the urging of the priests, the king agreed to terms" (PAL·CH·08041). At Tiyinmiw in 4769 Fyinan "renounces in favour of Ane all rights and title" to 49 districts and six towns (PAL·TR·02833). Oi Lyizi made it "116 districts in all"; Fyinan's historian had written, "Nothing of worth was lost." At Omatse in 4779 Nemeth ceded 19 districts (PAL·TR·02839), which Ane's next chronicler made "30 districts in all", while Nemeth's said only: "The border was settled."

Nor was her work always welcome at home, even in victory. Ane's own Assembly voted Tiyinmiw down, 204 to 221. Poashimin, its signatory, called it "the best that could be had"; Yi Shimin said: "Our sons come home. That is enough." But Minasathe asked, "49 districts? The army gave us the means to ask for more," and Ie Foyatl warned that Fyinan "will rebuild, and our sons will fight this war again" (PAL·PD·00021). Tsey's Assembly had heard her peace called surrender; Ane's, too small. Again the government proceeded.

## The people the articles left out

Nearest to me is the peace of 4758, which ended the [[evt:45867|Ane Colonial War in Daadüdhih]] against my own country. Our historian Nadhash, writing thirty years on, has Ane, "having gained nothing", suing for peace (PAL·CH·08046); Oi Lyizi has Khishkhis, "having gained nothing, withdrew to its own land" (PAL·CH·08045). Onivish settled the quarrel in one sentence: "The frontiers shall remain as they were before the outbreak of hostilities" (PAL·TR·02829). The record counts Ane's losses at 218,602 and ours at 215,809.

A year later an open letter printed at Ane spoke for the Ane-speaking people of Tatuthivi, still under Khishkhis governors. Of her treaty they wrote: "We have read it. Every one of us has read it, for it is short." They miscounted its articles, remembering two where the surviving text has three, but not its silence: "There is no mention of Tatuthivi at all" (PAL·LT·01767).

Tiyinmiw, too, had people outside its articles. In 4774, in the court of Ölye, one of the six towns her treaty transferred, Yi Shimam, formerly a subject of Fyinan, said his father's fields, "255 acres, were taken from us when the land was given to Ane by the Treaty of Tiyinmiw." The Crown replied: "The fields were found empty and were given to settlers." He might keep them as a tenant of the crown, at 49 Muie a year (PAL·LS·00765). Of the [[evt:46153|Ane–Nemeth War]], a Tule millhands' song from Nis observed: "the conscription board in Nis does not ask whose grandfather walked" (PAL·PO·00111).

## Twenty years at a time

No party to her five treaties fought Ane again within the twenty years she wrote into each. Tsey did not outlive its truce; Khishkhis never fought Ane again; Wemu returned only in 4790, thirty-seven years after Itlishithyi; Ane and Fyinan fought again, as Ie Foyatl had warned, only in 4828. Fyinan and Nemeth, both born of Tsey's collapse, paid in Tsey's paper Ta; they had kept it as their own, and it stayed in use until 4855.

She died in 4786, in the second year of the [[evt:46247|Ane–Mana War]], fought against another of Tsey's heirs. Its peace, at Lishimti in 4788, was drafted by Shivashfoyatl, envoy beside her since 4765 (PAL·TR·02844). Five years after her death Wemu sacked [[set:7|Ane]]; forty-eight years after it, the state she served collapsed.

What she thought of it, the record does not say. She left a form of words two enemies could each sign and each read differently at home. The chroniclers of six states swelled their victories and shrank their dead; the plainest documents of Ane's wars are hers.
