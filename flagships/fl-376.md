*Spoken by [[per:23850|Nagyeme]], queen of [[pol:1030|Khanji]], before the assembled court in the great hall at [[set:71|Lamlivje]], in the year 2703 from the founding of the city, when the lords and ladies of her household were received into the teaching.*

Lords of Khanji, ladies of my household, keepers of my gates and my granaries: hear me, not as your queen only, but as one who has sought and has found.

A year has turned since I put my seal to the decree, and the kingdom of Khanji laid down the rites of [[mov:11|Kyef]] and took up the teaching of [[mov:61|Langikaf]]. There were those among you who said the queen was young, and that the young are easily moved. There were those who said it in my hearing, and more who said it out of it. I do not reproach them. I ask only this: that a thing be judged by whether it is true, and not by the age of the one who says it.

Consider what we were taught before. We were taught that the world is full of gods, each with a house and a price; that one must be fed with smoke, and another with silver, and another with fear. We went from altar to altar like a debtor going from creditor to creditor, never free of any. And when the harvest failed, the priests said a god had been slighted; and when it prospered, they said a god had been paid. Is this knowledge, or is it bookkeeping?

The teaching I have embraced says otherwise. It says there is one power behind all things, and that the old gods, whom our fathers honoured in good faith, are its servants or its shadows. I do not say our fathers were fools. A man who sees the moon reflected in a pool is not a fool for loving the reflection. But he is wiser when he lifts his eyes.

And how shall a man lift his eyes? Not by a priest's leave alone. The teaching holds that each seeker may find the truth by the light within and by the labour of reason. I have found it so. I did not come to this faith because a stranger commanded me, but because I sat with the question through many nights and could not, in honesty, answer it any other way.

Do not suppose, however, that the new teaching makes the life of a court easier. It does not. It asks of us what the old rites asked: duty, order, and right sacrifice. But it asks more. It asks that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. Look about this hall. Look at the gold on your sleeves and the rings on your hands. I do not command you to cast them into the river; I am not a tyrant, and a gift wrung from a man is no gift. But I tell you that I have given from my own treasury [[cur:519|Yivi]] by the chestful to the houses where the poor of Lamlivje are fed, and that I mean to give more. Let those who would stand near the queen stand near her in this also.

One thing more. This teaching is not for Khanji alone. Its message is for every people under the sky, and those who hold it are bidden to bring others to it, by persuasion and by example, never by the sword. If our neighbours see a kingdom more just, more sober and more open-handed than before, they will ask why. Let us be ready with the answer.

I know that some of the old priests have gone back to their villages with sour faces. Let them go in peace. Let no one break their shrines or mock them in the street. A truth that must be defended with stones is not yet believed.

Lords and ladies, the [[evt:24107|Conversion of Khanji to Langikath]] was written by my hand in a single day. Its fulfilment will be written by yours, over all the days that remain to us. Rise now, and come forward one by one, and be received.
