*Persons of the dialogue: [[per:22240|Kithilianwu]]; a priest of Hath; a young man of the city. The scene is the grain market of [[set:52|Ihaemiw]], on the day the news came from Ashmath.*

**Young man.** You have heard, Kithilianwu? The host is beaten again at [[evt:22424|Ashmath]]. Two summers ago we lost the field there and our general with it, and now we have lost it a second time. The criers are saying it in every street.

**Kithilianwu.** I have heard. And I see that the price of grain has heard it too, for it stands at seven if the measure, and the poor are counting their [[cur:491|if]] twice before they buy.

**Priest.** It is a judgement upon the city. We have been slack at the altars. When the city was pious, the gods gave it Lakhitli; you remember, young man, how [[evt:22226|Lakhitli was taken]] and its land joined to ours, when you were a child. Prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour, and its loss is the sign of their anger. Every child of [[pol:911|Ihaemiw]] knows it.

**Kithilianwu.** Then tell me, priest, since you are learned in these things: does a man of Movosh sacrifice to our gods?

**Priest.** Certainly not. They have their own.

**Kithilianwu.** And yet they win. If prosperity is the sign of favour, then the gods of Movosh love Movosh more than [[dei:83|Hath]] loves Ihaemiw; or else Hath is weaker than they; or else Hath has given our fields to strangers who never burned a grain of incense to him. Which of these will you have?

**Priest.** None of them. The gods of each people have their place and their due.

**Kithilianwu.** Then their place is a market stall, and their due is a price, and the people with the deeper purse buy the victory. I do not think so meanly of the divine. Consider, young man: is a father's love shown by the coins he gives his son?

**Young man.** Not always. A foolish father spoils his son with coins.

**Kithilianwu.** And a wise father?

**Young man.** He teaches him to do without, so that he is not a slave to his belly.

**Kithilianwu.** You have answered better than the priest. Hear, then, what I teach, and judge it as a citizen judges a law. There is one Power, and Hath of the sky and [[dei:88|Mahtu]] of the sun and [[dei:86|Shu]] of the grain are its servants, as magistrates are the servants of the law. This Power does not deal in favours, but in justice. It does not measure a city by its granaries, but by its duty: whether the soldier holds his place, whether the magistrate gives true weight, whether the priest offers rightly and in order.

**Priest.** You keep the sacrifices, then?

**Kithilianwu.** I keep them. Order is not my enemy, priest, nor the priesthood; I would have teachers ordained and the teaching handed down as carefully as yours. But I say the wise man withdraws from wealth and appetite, because appetite makes him a coward in war and a cheat in peace. And I say more than [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]] says. Your teaching is silent about the beginning and the end. Mine is not. The world was made once, and it will be judged once, and at that judgement neither the victor of Ashmath nor the vanquished will be asked how much grain he held.

**Young man.** And will the men of Movosh be judged also?

**Kithilianwu.** Also. This teaching is not the property of Ihaemiw. It is for every people, the enemy too. That is the hardest part, and I do not expect the criers to shout it today.

**Priest.** The queen will not thank you for preaching to the enemy while [[evt:22376|the war with Movosh]] goes against us.

**Kithilianwu.** Queen [[per:22196|Yawni Nim]] is new to her throne and wiser than her counsellors. Let her hear it. A city whose citizens fear nothing but the loss of their goods will be beaten at every Ashmath. A city whose citizens fear only injustice cannot be beaten, even when it loses.

*Here the priest went away. But the young man stayed, and others with him; and from that day those who held to these words called themselves [[mov:77|Ashahatl]]. So was [[evt:22462|the teaching of Ashahatl founded]] in Ihaemiw, in the year 2586 from the founding of the city.*
