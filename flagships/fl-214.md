title: A Civil War in the Language of Foreign Wars: The Two Khovave Accounts of AY 660–664

The record calls the [[evt:2915|First Kadhdazhile Civil War]] (AY 660–664) a war "between rival claimants". It grew from the [[evt:2914|Secession of Luluye]], led by the claimant Nanizhi, and ended in a suppression with an amnesty. Neither surviving chronicle uses any of that vocabulary. Both are written in Khovave, and both describe the war as though it had been fought between two foreign states. This borrowed language is the chief distortion of both texts, more consequential than any of their numbers, and that the record lets us see past it.

## The tablets

The Kadhdazhile account (PAL·CH·00432, excavated at Adhizhili in AY 4889, fire-damaged at the edges) was set down by Navavun "in the 2nd year of Nanduyadhu", AY 674, ten years after the war. Navavun was sixty when the fighting began. The Luluye account (PAL·CH·00431, excavated at Luluyi in AY 4888) is Nagubu's, dated "in the 65th year of Luluye", AY 724. That count belongs to a self-styled empire that lasted four years, and its author was born thirty-two years after the war ended. Both were translated by Nalilimi.

A note on names. The Luluye scribe Nagubu shares his name with two Kadhdazhile commanders of the war: the Nagubu who held Luluyi in AY 661, and a different Nagubu who fought at Luyügih in AY 662. Navavun's text calls both men simply "Nagubu". In a civil war, names cross the lines because the population does.

## Field by field

*Who began it.* In the record, [[pol:122|Luluye]] began the war by seceding. Navavun puts it in the terms of a foreign invasion: "Nanizhi of Luluye, swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Kadhazhile." Nagubu reverses the blame and uses the same register: "there came word that Nakhuye of Kadhazhile had gathered an army against Luluye. Our king could not suffer the insult." Neither text admits that the two parties had been one state until AY 660.

*The battles.* The record has three, all in [[reg:20|Khishkhih]] and all won by [[pol:18|Kadhdazhile]]: the [[evt:2928|Battle of Luluyi (AY 661)]], the [[evt:2935|Battle of Luyügih (AY 662)]] and the [[evt:2945|Battle of Luluyi (AY 664)]]. Both scribes have all three with the right victor. Nagubu excuses the defeats, once with neglected omens and twice with allies who "failed to come". In a secession, that excuse may mean provinces that did not join.

*The dead.* The record gives Luluye 23 dead (7, 7 and 9), from forces of 22 to 32 men against Kadhdazhile armies of 7,597 to 10,057. Kadhdazhile lost 2,958 (982, 741 and 1,235). Navavun reports 82 rebel dead (25, 25 and 32), about three and a half times the record. His rebel musters of 39, 57 and 41 are inflated by about 1.8. His own side's losses are discounted. Nagubu gives his side 7 dead in all and Kadhdazhile 7,332 (2,434, 1,837 and 3,061), each about two and a half times the record's figure.

*Commanders.* None was killed, and neither tablet says otherwise.

*Outcome and land.* Kadhdazhile won. The [[evt:2946|Suppression of the Luluye Revolt]] annexed all forty-six districts of the seceding state and granted an amnesty. Navavun has the last stronghold opened to the king ("Luluye is no more, and its people serve Kadhazhile") and counts 110 districts won, well over twice the truth. Nagubu calls the war a stalemate that cost a few villages, and he closes with "the gates were opened to the enemy. Let this record keep what the sword has taken."

## What the formulas hide

The foreign-war vocabulary hides one fact in particular, and the record supplies it. Nanizhi, the defeated claimant, was not executed. She signed the suppression in person and lived until AY 686. Rebel leaders against foreign overlords were often executed at the peace. This war was settled more like a quarrel within one house, and both scribes had reasons to disguise that.

Navavun needed rebellion to look like invasion, because an invader can be crushed without anyone asking whether he had a claim. Nagubu, writing two generations later for the memory of a four-year empire, needed Luluye to have been a state that was attacked, not a province that had rebelled. Each scribe's numbers can be corrected from the record. Their shared framing can only be corrected by noticing that both of them adopted it.
