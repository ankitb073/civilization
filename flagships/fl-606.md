title: Navuvage of Puvu: the usurper's son and his sister's war

The first coin ever struck in Puvu shows an old man with his head veiled, facing right. The legend reads *Navuvaġə kambə*, "Navuvage, king". On the reverse is a temple front and the mint mark of Nilu. It is a heavy piece of good silver, about nine grams and better than nine parts in ten pure. It was issued in AY 2553, a year before the king's death. [[per:21046|Navuvage]] had then ruled [[pol:826|Puvu]] for fifty-eight years, and later ages called him the Conqueror. The portrait is the only likeness of him that survives. The veil may be a mark of piety, of mourning or of age. It suits a ruler whose public life is well recorded and whose private life the record shows only in outline, and that outline is hard.

## Born in the year of the usurpation

He was born in 2475, the year his father seized the throne. [[per:20816|Nakathash I]], a Khufiènji man with no recorded parents and remembered as the Usurper, founded a house that would rule Puvu until its fall. Navuvage was the youngest surviving child. Two elder sisters had been born before the seizure, and a baby brother died in 2473. His father reigned for twenty years. In his last years the Kumbumbi rose in what the record counts as their fifth war of independence.

The [[evt:21179|Accession of Navuvage]] came in 2495, when he was twenty. His eldest sister died that same year. His first act as king was to finish his father's war. In the [[evt:21128|Fifth Kumbumbi War of Independence]] the rebel king [[per:21006|Nabyiludu II]] could field only twenty-one men. At the [[evt:21189|Battle of Luyügih (AY 2495)]] the young king brought more than fifty thousand against them and won, but the record counts nearly 9,500 Puvu dead against nine of the rebels. Whatever killed those soldiers, it was not the enemy. His victory stele, which survives complete, claims a hundred enemy dead and offers the battle to his god. Nabyiludu survived the surrender and lived another thirty-six years.

## A war between siblings

The next decade brought the two wars that define the reign. In the [[evt:21281|Sixth Puvu–Khanji War]] Navuvage beat [[per:21020|Nambavul]], later called the Unfortunate, at the [[evt:21290|Battle of Hil (AY 2504)]]. The [[evt:21307|Treaty of Lamlivzhe (AY 2506)]] took seven districts. Lamlivzhe was besieged the same year, and the king's stele for Hil claims 6,900 enemy dead where the record gives 925.

Meanwhile his family divided. In 2505 his surviving sister, [[per:21044|Naguvu]], eight years older than he, took fifty-one districts into secession as the [[pol:916|Kajini]] empire. The [[evt:21293|Fifth Puvu Civil War]] was a war between the children of the Usurper. The record makes it painfully exact. At the [[evt:21309|Battle of Dambüzhi (AY 2506)]] Naguvu led nineteen men against her brother's 28,925. At Hil in 2509 she led thirty-six against more than twenty-seven thousand. Both times she lost a handful, and both times he lost thousands. Over the whole war Puvu lost more than eighteen thousand men and Kajini twenty-five.

The Kajini account of the war, written in 2541 by a palace scribe of the defeated state, is unusually honest about its own losses. It counts two or three men lost in each battle, which is close to the truth. It explains each defeat by a river that "rose and cut the army in two" or by neglected omens. It inflates the Puvu dead to seven, ten or twelve thousand a battle. It ends: "the royal city was taken. Let this record keep what the sword has taken." The [[evt:21324|Suppression of the Kajini Revolt]] in 2509 was signed at Hil by brother and sister, and its terms included an amnesty. Naguvu was executed that same year, aged forty-two. The record does not say whether Navuvage ordered it. It does show that the amnesty did not cover her. He was thirty-four, and he had now outlived every sibling.

## Conquests and granaries

The middle of the reign belongs to conquest. The war against [[pol:917|Luthe]] ran from 2515 to 2520. Its battles were won by royal armies of thirty to seventy thousand against a few dozen men, until the [[evt:21427|Battle of Khadambe (AY 2520)]], where the king faced a real army of thirteen thousand and beat it. A stele of 2519 credits him with a victory at Khanindu that the record assigns to one of his commanders. The [[evt:21428|Treaty of Khadambe (AY 2520)]] was granted, in its own words, "by Navuvage, emperor of Puvu, to Navande of Luthe". The Luthe queen [[per:21226|Navande]] ceded fifteen districts. The bronze tablet asks that it "be read aloud in the markets of both lands".

In the year before Khadambe he restored the granary at Nilu, his capital, and the slab recording it survives in good condition: "May Pugazhu bless the city." It is a small text, and a useful corrective. A king who spent so much of his reign at war also stored grain for his capital.

In 2529 he went to war with Tagath. He won at Dadifi in 2531, and in 2532 one of his generals destroyed a Tagath army and killed its commander. The [[evt:21549|Treaty of Dagafu (AY 2532)]] took nine districts and made Tagath a vassal. Dagafu was sacked. Its queen, [[per:21324|Natili]], was twenty-seven. Eight years later Tagath fell to Luthe, the kingdom Navuvage had humbled, and Natili was executed. Vassalage to Puvu had not protected her.

## The old king and the silver

The private record of his last decades is a list of losses. His queen died in 2536. A school exercise from Nilu of 2538 shows a pupil copying a model letter addressed "To Navuvage, my lord", announcing that "the boat of my lord has arrived at the quay". The pupil was told to write it again, ten times. In 2544 Nambe invaded, and the [[evt:21663|Nambe–Puvu War]] ended with the [[evt:21682|Treaty of Luyululi (AY 2546)]]. Nambe paid 1,238 talents of silver, and Puvu's merchants were to trade in its markets free of tolls. That year his eldest son died at forty-one.

Puvu had worked out the striking of coins for itself in 2533. For centuries it had reckoned in the Mathe, a weight of silver. The [[evt:21699|First Coinage of Puvu]] in 2553 replaced it with the struck [[cur:501|Shizhi]], and it is that coin that carries the veiled head. Navuvage died a natural death in 2554, aged seventy-nine, and his surviving son [[per:21502|Nakathash II]], thirty-two, succeeded him.

The house of Nakathash held Puvu for another fifty-eight years. They were not easy years. There were civil wars in the 2570s and a plague in 2578, and in 2612 the empire broke into six states. Navuvage's own reign had added some thirty districts, more than a million subjects and a coinage. It had also cost the lives of tens of thousands of soldiers in wars against enemies who barely existed, and the life of his sister. The coin is the record's most generous witness to him, and like all coins it shows only what the king wished to be seen.
