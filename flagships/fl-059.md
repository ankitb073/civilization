To the priests of the house of the gods in [[set:16|Turamtu]], who keep the truth in its order, and to the elders who sit in the gate, say: thus says [[per:11840|Ukhtsekhtsen]], queen of [[pol:466|Turamtu]], the victorious, beloved of [[dei:136|Dueminu]] the storm. In the 7th year of Ukhtsekhtsen I command this tablet to be written, that the words spoken at Bamiu be not forgotten. Men forget. The clay does not forget.

Hear how it began. The men of [[pol:529|Bamiu]] did not give the gods their due. They did not pour water to [[dei:141|Nabüdin]] the river. They did not burn first-fruits to [[dei:139|Nidikhti]] the grain. They mocked the priests of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]]. So I went out against them, and [[evt:11705|the holy war]] was mine.

At [[evt:11747|Diviminnge]] they came out against me. I struck them. Their dead lay on the bank like fish when the river falls. At [[evt:11758|Viririnu]] [[per:11813|Magshi Mish]], their king, came out against me himself. I struck him. A spear went into his side. His men took him up and carried him into Bamiu, and his blood marked the road. Then I sat down before [[set:26|Bamiu]], the city. I cut its fields. I stopped its water. That was [[evt:11759|the siege of Bamiu]], the third that city has suffered, and the hardest.

On the seventh day the gate opened. Three envoys came out with dust on their heads. Their chief envoy said: The king is wounded. The king asks for peace. I said: Let the king come out to me himself. He said: The king cannot walk. I said: Then I will go in to him.

I went into Bamiu with ten men. My captains said: do not go. I went. Magshi Mish lay on a bed in his house. His face was grey like ash. The physicians stood by him and burned herbs.

He said: What do you want of me?

I said: The land along the river, from the place where we fought first down to the water. That land is mine.

He said: The land I will give.

I said: Silver, a great weight, weighed on the balance of Turamtu, not on yours.

He said: The silver I will give.

I said: The gods of Turamtu will sit in your house. Your gods may sit where they sit, but the gods of Khunewmu will sit beside them, and you will pour to them first.

He was silent a long time. His women wept behind the curtain. Then he said: Let it be so.

I said: For ten years no spear of Bamiu will be raised against Turamtu.

He said: For ten years.

Then the scribes wrote [[evt:11760|the tablet of Bamiu]]. He reached for his seal, and his hand would not close on it. I set my seal on it with a hand that did not shake. We swore before Dueminu, before [[dei:138|Tuel]] the moon, before Vinba the earth, before Nidikhti and Nabüdin. And we swore before [[dei:137|Shyedartsi]], lord of those below, and Shyedartsi heard the oath.

Before the moon was new Magshi Mish went down to Shyedartsi. The wound of Viririnu killed him. His seal is not on the tablet; his oath is. The tablet stands.

Now two years have passed. I hear that some in Bamiu say: the king who swore is dead; the tablet died with him. Let the priests answer them. Say: a tablet sworn before the gods does not die when a man dies. Say: Shyedartsi holds the dead king to his oath, and Dueminu holds the living.

See how the gods favour Turamtu. The granaries are full. Grain is cheap in the market, a measure for little more than one [[cur:249|idin]]. The river rises in its season. The enemy bows. This is the sign of the gods' favour, and I am the hand that received it.

Let this tablet be set in the house of the gods, beside the tablet of Bamiu. Let it be read aloud at the new year, so that the young men know what their mothers' queen did. Thus says Ukhtsekhtsen.
