*A song of praise for our Lady [[per:31931|Lamfin Li]], Empress of [[pol:1177|Iethithi]], daughter of Nusefaya, of the house of Im'amnim, of the people of the Nishmam; sung before her throne in [[set:104|Iethithi]] in the twenty-eighth year of her reign, and in the year 3343 from the founding of the city.*

**I.**

Glory to the One Power, before whom the old gods kneel as stewards kneel,  
who turns the great wheel of the years, and what ends begins again;  
who gave to Iethithi, in the turning of the wheel, a Lady  
whose name is Nine House, for nine houses are not enough to hold her praise.  

**II.**

In the sixth year of her reign the stranger came against the realm.  
He came against Nyethatse with spears; he thought to find a woman weeping.  
He found the hosts of Iethithi like a forest that walks.  
At the [[evt:32307|Battle of Nyethatse]] he broke upon them as a wave upon the mole,  
and at the [[evt:32308|Treaty of Nyethatse]] he paid the silver of the beaten  
into the treasury of our Lady, and went home with his head bowed.  

**III.**

Then in the seventeenth year began the [[evt:32399|Iethithi–Zhidhzhinga War]],  
that the lands beyond the river might come under her just hand.  
At the [[evt:32412|Battle of Nidhinge]] her hosts prevailed, and the year was good.  
But in the next year, at [[evt:32416|Nidhinge]] again, the captain she had trusted  
went proudly where he should have gone humbly, and the field was lost,  
and many sons of Iethithi lay in the river reeds.  
Let no one blame the Lady for it. Let them hear what the Lady did.  

**IV.**

She did not weep in the palace. She did not send another captain.  
She put on the mail of her fathers, and she went out herself,  
as the sun goes out in the morning and does not ask leave of the clouds.  
At the [[evt:32421|Battle of Taalinnuy]] she rode before the host,  
and the enemy, who had laughed at Nidhinge, did not laugh.  
At [[evt:32428|Nidhinge]] she came again to the river where her sons had fallen,  
and there she paid the debt of the reeds in the enemy's own coin.  
At the [[evt:32436|Battle of Diviminnge]] she finished what she had begun,  
and the lords of Zhidhzhinga came out to her with their hands open.  

**V.**

By the [[evt:32437|Treaty of Diviminnge]] they gave her the land beyond the river,  
and the town that stands by the reeds of Nidhinge, and the town by the field of Diviminnge,  
and the river itself, with its fish and its ferries and its tolls;  
and they weighed out silver to her, and swore to keep the peace for fifteen years.  
The Lady took the land and the silver. She did not take their temples.  
She did not burn their roofs. She said: "The wheel turns; I may need them for friends."  

**VI.**

Praise her for her justice, for the teachers of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] ask justice in dealings,  
and in her markets the weights are true and the judges cannot be bought.  
Praise her for her moderation, for the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite,  
and she, who could eat from gold, eats from the same dish as her captains.  
Praise her for her bounty, for in her days a measure of grain  
has been sold in the market for less than one [[cur:675|isa]], and no one in the city goes hungry.  

**VII.**

Lords of the realm, who hold your lands of her and owe her your swords,  
bend the knee, and keep the faith you swore.  
Commons of Iethithi, who owe her your labour and your prayers,  
lift your voices. Merchants, lift your cups. Priests, lift your lamps.  
Let the wheel turn as it will; while it turns, let it turn under her hand.  
Long live our Lady Lamfin Li, the Victorious, Empress of Iethithi!
