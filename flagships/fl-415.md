*[Obverse.]*

To [[per:54|Totatpen]], my son, lord of [[set:68|Poyonga]] after me, say:  
thus says [[per:46|Kutberuyin]], your father, who lies on his bed and cannot rise.

May [[dei:55|Dumamnul]] of the high sky keep you.  
May [[dei:56|Nabuthin]] of the river water your fields.  
May [[dei:59|Winba]] of the earth hold up your house.  
May [[dei:57|Bubin]] of the moon light your road at night.

My son, hear your father. The scribe writes my words as I speak them. My voice is weak, so he leans close. When this tablet is put into your hand, I will have gone down to the earth, and you will sit in the high seat. Hear me, and do not weep too long.

Three years ago there was no city here. There was the bend of the river, and the reeds, and the hill where the herdsmen camped. I said: "Here." I set the first stone. I poured the first oil. That was the [[evt:110|Founding of Poyonga]]. You were a young man; you carried the baskets of earth beside me. Do you remember? Your hands bled, and you did not complain.

Now the city stands. It is small. Its wall is low. Its granary is not full. A small city is like a young tree: one hard wind, and it falls; ten good years, and no wind can move it. Give it ten good years, my son. Give it twenty.

Hear what you must do.

Pour to the gods each in their place and with their due. Pour to Dumamnul at the new year. Pour to Nabuthin at the flood. Pour to Bubin at the full moon. Pour to [[dei:58|Chithal]] of the war, but do not ask Chithal for war. Ask only that war stay far from Poyonga.

Keep the priests of [[mov:8|Ligay]] near you. They know the order of things. They know what was done before. A young king who does not listen to the priests is a boat without an oar.

*[Reverse.]*

Keep the herdsmen of the hills as your friends. Give them salt. Give them a place at the feast. When they are your friends, your flocks are safe. When they are your enemies, no wall is high enough.

Do not take what belongs to the gods. Do not take what belongs to the widow. Do not move the boundary stone of a field.

Be kind to your sister. She is wise, and she sees what men do not see. When you do not know what to do, go to her house and eat with her, and listen.

If ever you go down to the sea, pour to [[dei:60|Shineyta]] before you put the boat in the water. Do not forget. My father forgot once, and the boat was lost.

The wheel turns, my son. What ends will begin again. I go down to the earth, and you go up to the seat. That is the order of things. Do not fear it.

I have no gold to leave you. I leave you a city. I leave you a wall, and a granary, and a house for the gods. I leave you the name of [[pol:11|Poyonga]]. Make it great.

I am tired now. The scribe says the tablet is full. Let him seal it.

*[Colophon, in the scribe's hand.]*  
These are the words of Kutberuyin, lord of Poyonga, spoken from his bed in the house of the king.  
The tablet was sealed while he lived. It was given into the hand of his son after he went down to the earth,  
in the 1st year of Totatpen, in the days of the [[evt:114|Accession of Totatpen]],  
when [[per:54|Totatpen]] sat down in the high seat of [[set:68|Poyonga]].  
Let the gods receive Kutberuyin. Let the gods keep his son.
