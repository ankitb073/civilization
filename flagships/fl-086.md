title: Najiga and the Priest at the Gate of Khudhas

In the 7th year of [[per:14697|Navandi]], king of [[pol:579|Kashunji]], in the month of the first rains, [[per:15111|Najiga]] sat at the gate of the house of [[dei:28|Khudhas]] in [[set:46|Kashunji]]. A priest of the house came out to him, with the ash of the morning sacrifice still on his hands. These are the words they spoke, as Najiga set them down.

**The priest said:** You sit at the gate of the sky-god, and you do not bring an offering. The people see it and they talk. What is your teaching?

**Najiga said:** I bring an offering. I bring my question. Tell me: when the sun of Ugash goes down, does it go down for ever?

**The priest said:** No. It comes up again. So the moon of [[dei:31|Kumbi]] is eaten and is whole again; so the barley is cut and springs again. All things turn on the great wheel. This the fathers taught, and the priests have kept it in order since the beginning.

**Najiga said:** And the man who dies, does he come up again like the barley?

**The priest said:** His name comes up again in his sons. His house comes up again. The wheel turns.

**Najiga said:** Then tell me of the men who went down to Adhgumgi in the days of our fathers. The priests read the liver for them. The king poured beer to [[dei:32|Nijinandi]] of the battle. Fat oxen were slaughtered. And what came of it?

**The priest said:** [[evt:14751|At Adhgumgi]] the host was turned back. Many died. It was not the will of the gods that day.

**Najiga said:** Many died, and many had no sons. Where is the wheel for them? Their widows went to the doors of strangers.

**The priest said:** The gods are many. Each has a place and a due. Perhaps the due of one was not paid. Perhaps Bumbi of the sea was angry. Perhaps Khudhas was not honoured with the first fruits.

**Najiga said:** So a man must pay five lords, and if he forgets one he dies? That is how the tax-gatherer speaks, not how a father speaks. I tell you there is one Power above the sky and the sea and the sun and the moon. Khudhas is its servant, as the governor is the servant of the king. Bumbi is its servant. Nijinandi of the battle is its servant, or its shadow on the wall.

**The priest said:** Do you say the gods of Kashunji are nothing?

**Najiga said:** I say they are not first. The house has many rooms, but one master.

**The priest said:** And the wheel? Do you take the wheel from us also?

**Najiga said:** I say the road runs straight. It begins once, and it ends once, and at its end every man is weighed, the king with the herdsman. That is why the dead of Adhgumgi are not lost: they wait to be weighed.

**The priest said:** And what will the Power weigh? The fat of the ox? The silver in the bowl?

**Najiga said:** It will weigh what you did for the widow at your door. It will weigh whether you gave bread to the man of another people. The priests say the prosperous man is loved by the gods; I have seen prosperous men beat their servants. Mercy is the offering. To loose a man from his suffering is the sacrifice.

**The priest said:** The gods of Kashunji are for the people of Kashunji. Would you give them to strangers?

**Najiga said:** The Power is not for one people. It made the people of Kashunji and the people beyond the hills. I will send teachers to them. They will be ordained in order, as you were ordained, and they will keep the teaching as you keep yours.

**The priest said:** The king will hear of this.

**Najiga said:** Let the king hear. [[per:14697|Navandi]] is a just king. I have taken no silver, not one nil, from any man. I ask only to speak at the gate.

Then the priest went back into the house of the god, and he did not answer. But three men who had stood by and listened came and sat down beside Najiga. These were the first of [[mov:49|Buzhja]].

So was [[evt:15187|the teaching of Buzhja founded]] in Kashunji, at the gate of the sky-god, in the seventh year of the king.
