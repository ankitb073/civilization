**Preface.** I set these things down in my old age, at [[set:156|Fel]], in the thirty-first year of Yef. I count the years from the rising, as we all did then; that the kingdom of [[pol:708|Yef]] has not stood these four-and-twenty years does not seem to me a reason to forget how to count. A people that loses its kings may keep its calendar. It is the last thing a conqueror thinks to take.

**I. The rising.** In the first year, [[per:16282|Nafüs]] raised the standard against [[pol:702|Nginakhzi]]. The young will ask why. I answer: because we were taxed by a court that scorned our tongue, judged by magistrates who did not know our customs, and governed, that very year, by a man who had no better right to the seat than we had. [[per:16281|Nakidhuvu]] had seized the throne of Nginakhzi by force; we asked why we should obey by law a king who ruled by none. That was the [[evt:16370|Revolt of Yef]], and it began the [[evt:16371|Yef War of Independence]].

**II. The forty.** In the second year, [[per:16283|Nage]] took forty of us to Khambizhinu. Forty. I was among them. Against us [[per:16113|Nanüdhunan]], an old soldier of Nginakhzi, brought more than eight hundred. We lost the field, as anyone could have foretold; but we left a hundred and two of theirs on it for eight of ours. That was our first [[evt:16373|Battle of Khambizhinu]], and from it I date my belief that numbers are not the whole of war, though they are the greater part.

**III. The year of hope.** In the fourth year Nage went down again with six hundred, and this time Nakidhuvu himself came out to meet him with a thousand. I have heard men say since that a usurper fights badly because he fights for himself alone. I do not know if that is philosophy or merely spite; but at our second [[evt:16391|Battle of Khambizhinu]] the usurper's men broke, and we shut the city in. The [[evt:16392|Siege of Khambizhinu]] was the first time I saw a city from the outside and wished it ill. I am not proud of the wish.

**IV. The fall of Nafüs.** In the sixth year Nafüs led us in person against [[per:16321|Nadidhumi]] at our third [[evt:16409|Battle of Khambizhinu]]. We were the more numerous and we were beaten, and two hundred and fifty-seven of ours did not come home. Before that year was out Nafüs himself had fallen in battle. Let those who say a king should not risk his person remember that ours did, and that it was the risking, and not the falling, that made him a king.

**V. Victory, and what it cost.** In the seventh year [[per:16333|Najekh]] led us against Nadidhumi once more, fewer against more, and won. That was our last [[evt:16420|Battle of Khambizhinu]]. Then the city was opened to us, and I will not pretend the [[evt:16421|Fourth Sack of Khambizhinu]] was anything but what the name says. I took nothing from it but a book, and I have kept it. The [[evt:16422|Treaty of Khambizhinu]] followed: Nginakhzi acknowledged our independence and swore a truce of fifteen years. Four hundred and sixteen of ours had died for it in seven years, and nine hundred and forty-two of theirs.

**VI. The same summer.** Before the ink of that treaty was dry, the army of Kashunji came against us with ten thousand men. We met them at Hil with fewer than seven hundred. Of the [[evt:16423|Battle of Hil]] I will say only that it was short. Hil was sacked, and soon after came the [[evt:16425|Conquest of Yef]]. Nage did not see the end of that year. We had been free, by the treaty's reckoning, for a matter of months.

**VII. Reflection.** I am asked whether it was worth it, to throw off one master and receive another within the season. The teachers of our faith say that all things turn on a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. I do not claim to know the truth of it; no one owns the truth. But I observe that we were once forty, and became a kingdom, however briefly; and a thing that has been done once is a thing men know can be done. Najekh is an old man now, and tends beans. When the young men visit him, he does not tell them to rise. He tells them how we counted the years.
