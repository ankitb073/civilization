*[Stele text, copied on clay for the house of the gods.]*

I am [[per:450|Yidi'ash]], lord of [[pol:17|Wal'a]], strong of arm, beloved of the gods.  
In the 2nd year of my lordship I set up these words before the gods of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]].

Hear, O [[dei:8|Nish'il]], lord of the high sky.  
Hear, O [[dei:10|Masto]], the sun, who sees all that is done.  
Hear, O [[dei:9|Shimintlo]] of the storm.  
Hear, O [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river.  
Hear, O [[dei:7|Yub]] of the fire.  
Hear, O [[dei:11|Nizhashta]] of the war.  
Hear, and remember, and do.

There was a man. His name was [[per:386|Piscua'agw]].  
He was not born to the high seat. He was not chosen by the gods for the high seat.  
In the [[evt:326|Usurpation of Piscua'agw]] he reached out his hand and took it.  
He took the staff from the rightful lord. He took the seat of the rightful lord.  
Twenty-three years he sat in it, a thief in the house of the gods.  
Twenty-three years the gods watched him, and they were silent.

But the gods are not silent forever.  
Masto saw him. Nish'il saw him. The wheel turned.  
In the last year the breath went out of him. He lay down and did not rise.  
No spear struck him. No man struck him. The gods struck him.  
They took his breath as a man takes back a stolen ox.

Then the house was empty, and the city was afraid.  
Who will hold the staff? Who will pour to the gods?  
The thief left no son of his body to sit in the seat.  
I stood up. I, Yidi'ash, stood up, and the men of the city stood with me.  
I took the staff, not from a lord, but from the dust where the thief let it fall.  
That is the [[evt:375|Usurpation of Yidi'ash]], as the scribes call it.  
Let them call it what they will. The gods know it was the setting-right of the house.

*[The curse.]*

Now hear my curse upon the name of Piscua'agw.  
May Nish'il shut the gate of the sky against him, so that he does not go up.  
May Masto turn away the face of the sun from his grave.  
May Shimintlo scatter the dust of his tomb.  
May Nili carry his name down the river and drown it in the reeds.  
May Yub burn the tablets that bear his name, and may they crack in the fire.  
May Nizhashta deny him a place among the brave.  
May no one pour water for him at the new moon.  
May no one say his name at the feast.  
May he be hungry in the house below, and may he be thirsty.

Whoever sets up the name of Piscua'agw again on any wall,  
whoever pours for him, whoever calls him lord:  
may the same curse fall on him, on his house, on his sons, and on his flocks.

*[The blessing.]*

But whoever keeps these words,  
whoever pours to the gods in their order and with their due,  
whoever honours the lord of Wal'a who set the house right,  
may Nili water his field, may Masto ripen his barley,  
may his sons be many and his cattle fat.

My sons stand beside me. My daughter stands beside me.  
My house is a true house. The gods have seen it.  
Let this stone stand in the gate of [[set:22|Wal'a]], where every man who goes in and out may read it.  
Let the priests read it aloud at the turning of the year.  
Let the name of Piscua'agw be cursed. Let the name of Yidi'ash be remembered.

The wheel turns. What was stolen is given back.  
So says Yidi'ash, lord of Wal'a. So say the gods.
