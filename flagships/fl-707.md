*[Obverse.]*

Sing it low, for the granary hears.  
Sing it low, for the gods are near, each in their place.  

In the third year of [[per:20655|Sha Yuf]], the queen who sits in [[set:248|Tivinnu]],  
in the month when the barley should stand to the knee,  
the barley stood to the ankle, and was yellow, and bent down.  
This is the song of the [[evt:20730|Famine in Mikhukhizhi]].  
This is the song of the granary that answers nothing.  

There was an omen. The moon rose red over [[reg:10|Miuhitl]].  
The old woman by the well said: red moon, thin bread.  
We laughed at her. We do not laugh now.  
There was an omen. The storks did not come back to the roof.  
There was an omen. A ewe dropped a lamb with no eyes.  
We gave it to the priest. The priest said: it is nothing. Eat.  

The granary of our village is round, of mud and straw,  
with a door of cedar and a seal of the headman.  
In the good years we struck it with a stick and it said *thud*,  
like a full belly, like a fat man sitting down.  
Now we strike it and it says *dom, dom*, like a drum.  
Like the drum of the soldiers going out.  

*[Reverse.]*

Where did the grain go? Hear me, I will tell you.  
The tribute-men came after the war, with their scales and their tally-sticks.  
They said: the army ate at the [[evt:20789|Battle of Chezhietle]].  
They said: the army ate at the [[evt:20830|Battle of Mane]].  
The queen's men won there, and the drums came home,  
and the men came home, some of them, and the grain did not.  
I do not blame the queen. Who blames the queen?  
I blame the man with the scales. His thumb was on the pan.  

In the market at the river the merchant sits with his sacks shut.  
He says: six [[cur:436|Ninkil]] the measure, and a little more.  
Six Ninkil! I have one Ninkil, and it is my mother's, sewn in her hem.  
For a Ninkil I have bought chaff, and lentils with stones in them,  
and a cake of pressed dates that the mice had found first.  

The priests of [[mov:25|Miyituyishiu]] say: be moderate.  
They say: the wise turn from appetite.  
We are moderate. We are very moderate.  
My children are wise. They have turned from appetite.  
They lie in the shade of the wall and do not ask.  
That is the worst thing. That they do not ask.  

Gods of the field, each in your place,  
gods of the threshing floor, gods of the rain-cloud,  
I do not know your secret names, the priests keep them.  
Hear me with the names you have.  
Send rain to the barley. Send the storks back to the roof.  
Let the granary say *thud* again, like a fat man sitting down.  
Let the tribute-men lose their way on the road.  

Sing it low, for the granary hears.  
Sing it low. Strike the door once. Listen.  
*Dom.*  
Sing it again tomorrow, if there is a tomorrow with bread in it.
