*I. Invocation*

Not to the many I sing, whose altars stood in every lane,  
not to the hungry gods who asked for smoke and asked again;  
I sing to the One that turns the wheel, that neither eats nor sleeps,  
whose light is lit in every breast, and every breast it keeps.  

Hear me, citizens of [[set:104|Iethithi]], gathered in the square:  
the lamp you sought in the temple-dark was burning in you there.  

*II. The Old Way*

Our fathers kept the rites of the [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] with care,  
they poured the wine, they cut the throat, they read the liver's ware;  
and who will mock our fathers? Not I. They did what they could see.  
The old gods were not liars; they were shadows on the tree,  
the servants at the gate of the house, who took the guest's cloak in;  
we thanked the servant, and went no farther. That was all our sin.  

*III. Bathshi*

Recall the year of bitterness, when the spears of [[pol:1116|Iethithi]]  
went down upon the plain of Bathshi to bring the foe to knee,  
and at the [[evt:26003|Battle of Bathshi]] the plain was not our own;  
our king himself, [[per:25533|Isakha]], stood, and stood, and stood alone,  
and the dead lay thick as autumn leaves along the river's bed.  
Then the citizens asked the priests: "Why?" And the priests poured wine instead.  

But certain ones among us, who had heard the new word told  
by teachers come from far away, who carried neither gold  
nor sword, nor any wand of power, but only reason's light,  
said: "Look within. Be just. Be moderate. Put down your appetite."  

*IV. The Turning*

In the next year, on that same plain, the spears went down once more,  
and at the [[evt:26011|Battle of Bathshi]] fortune turned, as it had turned before;  
and in the third year, on that plain, they met the foe again,  
and at the [[evt:26024|Battle of Bathshi]] the field was ours; and then  
we knew the wheel had turned, as the teachers said it would:  
not by the smoke of slaughtered beasts, but by the mind made good.  

So in that year the city spoke, the greater part, aloud:  
"We will be [[mov:69|Kievi]]." And the king bowed his head before the crowd,  
and said: "Then I am with you." This is the [[evt:26058|Conversion of Iethithi to Kievi]],  
and I who sing it saw it, and I wrote it, and I believe.  

*V. Precepts*

Deal justly with the stranger, as you deal with your own kin.  
Take less than you are owed; the wise man asks for less, and wins.  
Leave wealth before it leaves you; leave appetite to the beast.  
Seek, every seeker, with your reason; the truth is not a feast  
laid out by priests at a fixed hour, for a fee, behind a door:  
it is a lamp, and it is yours, and it was always yours before.  

And go, and tell it to the nations; let no people be denied.  
The One that turns the wheel for us turns it on every side.  

*VI. Prayer for the King*

Keep, O Turner of the Wheel, our king [[per:25533|Isakha]] in his eleventh year;  
give him the moderate heart, give him the reasoning ear;  
and keep the spears of [[pol:1116|Iethithi]] that still stand in the field,  
till justice, and not appetite, shall teach the foe to yield.  

Sung at Iethithi, in the 11th year of Isakha.
