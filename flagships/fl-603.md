In AY 972 a pupil at Ane named Min'asilie'an copied out the kings of Mii for his teacher. The seventh line reads simply "Mishfa'i, reigned 41 years." The teacher's note at the bottom says "Write it again, ten times." Of all the documents that mention [[per:5249|Mishfa'i]], this exercise may be the most honest. It records what a schoolboy seventeen years after her death was expected to know, which was the length of her reign and nothing else. Tradition calls her "the Conqueror". Her own monuments and letters speak in the stock formulas of kingship, changed only to make her a queen. Her life has to be read through those formulas and against them.

## The daughter of a fallen queen

She was born in 894, a Lizo princess of the house of Ma'she Movi, which had held the throne of [[pol:93|Mii]] for eighty years. Her mother, [[per:4758|Ba'ashim'a]], called the Brave, had reigned since 888. Her father, who has no name in the record, was a consort. She had one younger brother. Two disasters shaped her youth. In 911 the River Fever swept through Mii and killed about a quarter of a million people, one in five of those it reached. In the same year the court declared war on the small city-state of [[pol:188|Yisamashti]]. In 913 Ba'ashim'a led an army of more than twenty thousand against five hundred defenders at Faamni and won the field, but she was killed there.

The [[evt:4927|Accession of Mishfa'i]] followed in 914, when she was twenty. Within the year she led the army back to the place where her mother had died. At the [[evt:4932|Battle of Faamni (AY 914)]] she had about twenty thousand men against 379. The court account of the war, written more than forty years later, says she "drew them into the hills" and lost twenty-one men. The record says Mii lost 1,793, almost five times the whole enemy army. Two years later the [[evt:4944|Conquest of Yisamashti]] was signed at Itlishithyi, and the ageing queen of Yisamashti, [[per:5076|O'e Luesh'a]], called the Pious, set her seal to the end of her city. She had commanded at Faamni on the day Ba'ashim'a fell. The record states that she was executed in the same year. It does not say who ordered the execution, or why. It is hard not to read it as a daughter's settling of accounts, and harder still to prove it.

## The making of a conqueror

The wars of her middle years follow a pattern. Mii's armies were large and its enemies small, and the court chronicles leave out the defeats. In the [[evt:4986|Mii–Val'a War]] of 922–925 she beat the Val'a general [[per:5317|Sha Mü'a]] at Namosash in 923. The Mii account of the war, written in her twenty-second year, goes straight from that victory to a second one at Namosash in 925, where Sha Mü'a was killed and "his head was brought to the queen". Between the two lies the [[evt:5000|Battle of Itlishithyi (AY 924)]]. There an army of nearly thirty thousand under the queen herself was routed by fewer than two thousand men of [[pol:205|Val'a]], and more than eleven thousand Mii soldiers died. It was the worst day of her life as a commander, and no document of her court mentions it. The [[evt:5014|Treaty of Bau (AY 925)]] gave Mii ten districts and a large indemnity. It was signed for Val'a by [[per:5243|A'i Tlü III]], a boy of fourteen. Bau was sacked that year. Its treaty tablet is lost except for a quoted curse on anyone who changes its words.

One document from these years shows how most of her subjects lived. In 929 a merchant of Namosash named Milamovi, sick and expecting to die, sealed his testament "in the 16th year of Mishfa'i". He left his house and its debts to his son, cloth and copper to his agent and his partner, and money to the temple for feeding the poor on the feast day. He also freed his servants. Namosash had seen two battles within six years. Milamovi's will gives no sign of them.

The [[evt:5061|First Tamthal Trade War]] against [[pol:185|Tomözel]] in 931–933 brought two districts, an indemnity and vassalage, under the [[evt:5075|Treaty of Ishwyithie (AY 933)]]. The Tomözel account says that "some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor". Both chroniclers were consoling someone. Then the record falls silent on public affairs, and the private record is grim. Her husband died in 935, her brother in 937, and her only son in 938, at the age of nine. Her daughter, born in 924, was now her sole heir.

In 942 the king of Khie'a, [[per:5473|Eshueshiluesh'a]], later called the Cruel, wrote to her in the standard form: "To Mishfa'i, queen of Mii, my sister," with good wishes for "your house, your sons". The [[evt:5179|Second Mii–Khie'a War]] followed, and two victories led to the [[evt:5194|Treaty of Kheye (AY 944)]], with six districts and eleven years of tribute in silver. On its silver tablet she speaks in the treaty formula: "Thus says Mishfa'i, great queen, queen of Mii." Her victory stele for Tliyiya, set up the same year, claims that "she killed 2,400 soldiers". The record gives 1,025.

## The revolts of the last decade

Mishfa'i's conquests did not hold. In 946 Yisamashti, conquered thirty years before, rose under [[per:5503|Yiedi'ada]], and the [[evt:5205|Yisamashti War of Independence]] began. In 947 a famine killed some sixty-four thousand, and the province of Miezanitie seceded, opening the [[evt:5220|First Mii Civil War]]. Her letter that year to Shimenshimen I of Miezanitie is the only letter in her name that survives: "You have harboured my enemies. Hand them over, or I will come and fetch them myself."

The fighting came to the capital. At the [[evt:5232|Battle of Ane (AY 947)]] and twice more in 948 and 949, Yisamashti rebels numbering twenty or thirty men faced royal armies of fourteen to twenty-six thousand under the queen's own command. They lost a handful each time, and Mii lost several thousand in all, more to sickness and hunger in the camps, surely, than to the enemy. Her stele for a victory at Yiutl in 949 claims a hundred enemy dead. The record counts ten, and the commander that day was not the queen. In 951, at the [[evt:5286|Battle of Onithie (AY 951)]], [[per:5572|Shimenshimen II]] defeated her outright with an army half the size of hers.

The endings were milder than the beginning of her reign. Yiedi'ada surrendered at Ane in 953 under terms of amnesty and lived another twenty-four years. The [[evt:5307|Suppression of the Miezanitie Revolt]] followed in 954, again with amnesty. The queen who may have executed O'e Luesh'a in her twenties pardoned her rebels at sixty.

## Death, and the silence after

Mishfa'i died a natural death in 955, aged sixty-one. The [[evt:5308|Accession of Eshueshilie'an]] made her daughter queen. [[per:5386|Eshueshilie'an]] reigned eighteen years, put down another revolt in the 960s, and is remembered as the Last. In 973 Mii broke into six successor states. Among them were a Miezanitia, an Ana seated at the old capital, and kingdoms seated at Namosash and at Faamni, where her mother died and where her own wars began.

The Annals of Mii, compiled by the scribe Nitllo To'a in 972, carry her reign as far as its thirty-third year: "The lords of the border rose in revolt. … Yizamashti crossed the frontier in arms." There they stop. The annalist chose not to record the defeats, the famine, the pardons or her death. What he left out tells us almost as much as what he kept. She was a ruler whose victories were real and whose losses were concealed, and who learned late in life that a rebel could be pardoned. The schoolboy at Ane was told to write her name ten times. He was told nothing else about her.
