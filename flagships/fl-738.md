To my cousin, son of my mother's sister, say: thus says your cousin, a woman of [[set:113|Tlalesyi]], who sits by the road. May [[dei:0|Etlie]] the sky keep you. May [[dei:3|Niele'i]] the river keep you. May your house be whole. My house is not whole.

Hear what has happened. The men of [[pol:409|Yi'otl]] have come again. Again, cousin. When I was a girl with my hair in one braid they came and broke the wall and took the grain. You remember it: it was the [[evt:10736|Fifth Sack of Tlilisyi]], and your father's roof burned, and afterwards our lord paid them silver for the peace. Now I am a woman with three children, and they have come again.

Our lord [[per:10866|O'e Ash]] went out against them before the walls. Men say the [[evt:10862|Battle of Tlilisyi]] was lost at the river gate. Men say the men of Yi'otl were four for each one of ours. I do not know. I know that our men came running back through the gate, and the gate did not shut.

They burned the city. This was the [[evt:10863|Sixth Sack of Tlilisyi]]. They burned the house of the tablets, where the scribes kept the tablets of the fields and the tablets of the debts. Cousin, the tablet of our grandfather's field was in that house. The tablet of my husband's debt to the oil-seller was in that house. Both are dust. Who now will say which field is ours? Who now will say what my husband owes? The oil-seller is dead also. Lashla'e the moon knows. Nobody else knows.

My husband is with me. He has a cut on the arm; it is closing. The children are with me. The middle one coughs at night. We took the goat, the grinding stone, the blanket and the small jar of barley. The jar is empty now. The goat gives a little milk. The children drink it. We do not.

Here by the road a measure of barley is near one [[cur:242|Esued]], and we have no esued. My husband carries water for a farmer for half a loaf. I grind for the farmer's wife for the other half.

Cousin, send to me. Send barley, one measure, two measures, as your hand is able. Send it with the potter's boy who goes on the road. If your hand is not able, send me word that you are well. That is also bread.

Men say the war is not finished. Men say the men of Yi'otl will sit down before the other towns. I do not know where to go. If your village is quiet, may I come with the children and sit at the edge of your field? I will grind for your wife. I will spin. My husband will dig. I swear by [[dei:2|Muevas'e]], the earth, that I will repay you when there is a field again.

May Etlie keep you. May Niele'i keep you. Kiss your mother for me; she is my mother's sister. Your cousin, who sits by the road.
