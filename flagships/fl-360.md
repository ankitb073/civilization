To my son, who is outside the wall with the herds of our house, say:  
Thus says [[per:633|Natpimttumin]], your father, who sits in the seat of [[set:61|Tsinlampiba]] in the third year.

May [[dei:33|Nabudzin]] of the river carry this clay to you. It goes out in the bottom of a fish-basket, under the reeds, on the head of a woman who is not afraid. Give her bread. Give her a [[cur:13|Bilu]] of silver. Do not ask her name.

Hear me, my son. With your mother it is well. With me it is well. With the city it is not well.

The men of [[pol:22|Birnadu]] are around the wall. They are on the north side and on the river side. Their fires are many at night; I have stood on the roof of the house of [[dei:34|Tselni]] and counted them until my eyes ached. This is the [[evt:556|First Siege of Zhinlampive]]. Our fathers never saw a ring of spears around this wall. Now I see it.

You know how it began. Five summers ago the men of Birnadu came down into our fields and said: "Give us silver. Give us barley. Give it every year." The elders of the city said no, and when I took the seat, I said no. That was the beginning of the [[evt:532|Virnado–Tsinlampiba War]]. I do not say it was wrong to say no. I say only that it has been long.

Last year they came in. You were there; you saw it. That was the [[evt:551|First Sack of Zhinlampive]]. They burned the house of tablets. The lists of the kings, the songs of the founding, the reckonings of the storehouse, all went into the fire, and the clay cracked in the heat like river ice. I wept for the tablets more than for the jars. Who will remember us now, if not these words I press into this clay?

This year I sent out the spears before the gate. My captain led them; he is a good man and did what a man can do. But the men of Birnadu were more than three for one of ours. Our men stood a little and then went back behind the wall, and one hundred and twenty-eight of them did not come back. That was the [[evt:555|First Battle of Zhinlampive]]. Since that day the gate has been shut.

Now hear how it is inside.

The barley in the storehouse of [[dei:36|Nidziktti]] is low. We give a half measure to each house, then a quarter. The children cry at night. The priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] keep the offerings in order, each god in the god's own place, but the offerings grow thin. The priests say the wheel turns, and what goes down comes up again. I believe them. I also count the jars.

My son, hear what I ask of you.

Do not try to come in. The river gate is watched. Keep the herds in the hills, far from the road. If the men of the hill villages will give you barley, take it and hide it, against the day the gate opens. Keep silver by you, in Bilu, in a pot under the floor; if they want silver, it may be that silver will buy them away. I have not said yes to them. I have not said no. I sit in the seat and I think.

If [[dei:38|Shedartsi]] takes me before this is ended, you are the son of the house. Hold to the gods. Hold to the city. Do not let the men of [[pol:22|Birnadu]] tell you that [[pol:23|Tsinlampiba]] is nothing. It is not nothing.

May [[dei:37|Novku]] of wisdom open your eyes. May [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm blow in the faces of our enemies.

Written in the third year of Natpimttumin, inside the wall, by lamplight, in haste.
