*A Panegyric upon the Most Serene and Victorious Empress, Khuluftungath of the House of Shidhilath, pronounced before the Court at Tallin on the thirtieth anniversary of her accession, the 13th of Ushuth, 2102 of the Vishithmikh, and printed at the desire of many who heard it.*

It is the custom of orators, Most Serene Lady, to begin by protesting that their subject exceeds their powers. I shall not trouble you with that protest, for in this case it is merely true, and the truth needs no ornament. I shall instead do what the learned do with any great matter: set out the evidence in order, and let the reader draw the conclusion.

Consider first the name. It is well known that the name of our Empress, in the old tongue of the Imu, signifies *the green lady*; and I ask whether any name was ever better chosen by parents ignorant of what it would come to mean. For what is green but the colour of growth, of increase, of the field that was barren and is now in crop? And what has the reign of [[per:40936|Khuluftungath]] been, these thirty years, but the greening of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]]?

Consider next the inheritance. When her father, [[per:40567|Lotafbafin]] of glorious memory, departed this life in the year 2072, he left to his daughter a realm at peace but not secure, rich but not feared, faithful but not yet zealous. The frontier was a line on a map, and the map was disputed. The Empress was then a young woman of twenty-seven years. There were those at court, I do not name them, they are dust, who whispered that the Shidhilath had produced a daughter where a son was needed. Let us see what the daughter did.

In the year 2084 she unsheathed the sword of the faith against the unbelievers beyond the frontier, and in 2086 her armies, at the [[evt:41665|battle of Yirantudhu]], broke the first resistance. At the [[evt:41669|first battle of Tungaruikhrimu]] and again at the [[evt:41678|second]], her generals carried the cross-roads of the march, and the enemy, who had trusted to his fastnesses, found that no fastness avails against a host whose cause is just. By the [[evt:41679|treaty of Tungaruikhrimu]] in 2089, which she signed with her own hand, the province was joined to the Empire, and its prince bowed as a vassal of the throne of [[set:0|Tallin]]. And here observe what the moralists among us too often forget: the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]], whose message is for all peoples, was carried not by preaching alone but under the protection of arms, and the churches now stand where the groves of the old gods stood.

Was the Empress then content? She was not; for the One does not give victories that they may be hoarded. In the [[evt:41728|Second War against Ashash]], begun in 2095, her armies went out in numbers such as no living man had seen. At the [[evt:41743|fourth battle of Linririnu]] the enemy was overthrown; at the [[evt:41753|battle of Khash]], the most complete victory of the age, he was annihilated; and at the [[evt:41761|battle of Lininadhe]] he made his last stand and lost it. By the [[evt:41762|treaty of Lininadhe]], in 2098, Ashash ceded its border districts, paid an indemnity to the treasury, opened its markets, and became, like the march before it, a vassal of the Empire.

I will be told by the sceptics, for there are always sceptics, that these victories were won by generals. I answer: were the generals then self-appointed? Did they raise their own armies, pay them from their own purses, and sign their own treaties? The general wins the field; the sovereign wins the war. And it is the war, not the field, that history remembers.

Consider last the fruits. The [[cur:897|Rimi]] rings true in every market from the new march to the frontier of Ashash. The granaries are full. The churches are full. And at her side stands her son, the Prince [[per:41599|Taanalin]], in the full vigour of his years, the living assurance that the house of Shidhilath will not fail.

Thirty years, Most Serene Lady. The barren field is green. May the One, who orders time from the Creation to the Judgement, grant you many more; and when at last He calls you to account, may He find, as we have found, that the talent He lent was returned a hundredfold.
