*Persons of the dialogue: [[per:24921|Nashinjè]]; an Elder of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]]; a Young Man lately returned from the wars. The scene is the threshing floor below the town of [[set:150|Kavazhu]], in the 13th year of [[per:24706|Nangashu]], king of [[pol:1079|Kambive]].*

**ELDER.** They tell me, Nashinjè, that you have been teaching the farmers here that the god does not love the rich. Is that so?

**NASHINJÈ.** It is not quite so, Elder. I have been asking them whether the god loves the rich *because* they are rich. That is a different question, and a better one.

**ELDER.** But our teaching is plain. Prosperity is a sign of divine favour. A man who prospers has dealt justly and lived with moderation, and the One rewards him.

**NASHINJÈ.** Then let us test it, as reason bids us; for you and I agree, at least, that each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason, and need not take it on any man's word. Young man, you were at Kabaf?

**YOUNG MAN.** I was. I carried a spear in the [[evt:25094|Battle of Kabaf]].

**NASHINJÈ.** And how did it go?

**YOUNG MAN.** Badly. We were beaten, and the king set his seal to the [[evt:25095|Treaty of Kabaf]] on terms the merchants of the city still curse at their tables. It was the end of the [[evt:25049|Fourth Fuvu Trade War]], and we lost it.

**NASHINJÈ.** Tell me, Elder: were the men who beat us more just than we? More moderate? Did the One look upon their counting-houses and say, "Here are the righteous"?

**ELDER.** I cannot say that.

**NASHINJÈ.** Nor can I. Then either the god's favour has been withdrawn from Kambive for some sin no one can name, or else prosperity and the god's favour are not the same thing at all. Which is the more reasonable?

**ELDER.** You go too fast. A single defeat proves nothing.

**NASHINJÈ.** Then consider a longer reckoning. Twelve years ago the old state fell apart in the [[evt:25002|Collapse of the Kaavu State]], and from its pieces arose this kingdom of ours. Were the men of the old state all wicked, that it fell? Were we all good, that we rose? I think the wheel of fortune, if I may borrow the phrase of our neighbours, turns without consulting the moral law.

**ELDER.** Our teaching does not speak of wheels. It says that time runs in a line, from the creation to the final judgement.

**NASHINJÈ.** Perhaps it does. I confess I do not know how the world began, nor how it will end, and I distrust those who say they know. What I do know is nearer to hand: this young man's limp, that widow's empty bowl, the child coughing in the house behind us. Suffering is not at the end of the line or at the beginning. It is here.

**YOUNG MAN.** Then what is the good life, if not prospering?

**NASHINJÈ.** Compassion, and the release of others and of ourselves from suffering. The Elder's teaching asks for justice in dealings, and I honour it; justice is a fine scale. But a scale only weighs. Compassion lifts. When you saw a wounded man at Kabaf, did you ask whether his accounts were in order?

**YOUNG MAN.** No. I carried him.

**NASHINJÈ.** Then you already know my whole teaching, and I have wasted the afternoon.

**ELDER.** And the One? Do you deny the One?

**NASHINJÈ.** Not at all. There is one divine power, and the old gods are its servants or its shadows; in this we stand together. I add only that the One does not belong to Kambive. If what I say is true, it is true for the men who beat us at Kabaf, and for the men beyond them, and for every people under the sky. A truth one keeps for one's own city is not a truth but a possession.

**ELDER.** You will make enemies, speaking so.

**NASHINJÈ.** I have made some already. I hope to make more friends.

*[Here the young man asked to be taught, and others with him; and that evening, on the threshing floor, they agreed to call themselves the [[mov:92|Lave]]. This was the [[evt:25220|Founding of the Lave Faith]].]*
