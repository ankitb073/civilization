To the king [[per:17807|Jeybalothen]], my lord, son of Nofdon, son's son of Nofdon, and to those who hold the seal at his right hand in [[set:0|Tallen]], say: thus says your servant, the scribe of the camp, who writes at the mouth of [[per:17812|Bolon]] your general.

May [[dei:14|Volme]] guard the king. May Domamnol lengthen his days. May Nedhekhte fill his storehouses. With the camp all is well. With the horses all is well. With the king's army all is well.

**Of the battle.** In the 2nd year of Jeybalothen, in the month when the barley stands green, Bolon went out against the field below [[set:157|Peyekhjeda]]. Before the battle the priests opened a ram. The liver was whole and bright, without spot. At night [[dei:19|Yenan]] rose red over the hills of Yeda, and the old men said: that is the blood of the enemy. So it was.

[[per:17703|Lewokalen]], who calls himself king of [[pol:779|Yeda]], came out with his own body. He stood in the chariot with his standard. Bolon set the men of Tallen in three bands, as the king's father set them. At the third hour Bolon sent the left band around the stream. The men of Yeda turned to face it, and Bolon struck their heart. They broke. They ran to the hills. Lewokalen ran with them.

We counted the dead of Yeda by the right hands: three thousand and one hundred and more. Of the men of Tallen, six hundred went down to Venba. Their names are written; the tablet goes with this one. Let their fathers be given barley from the king's store.

**Of what has gone before.** Let the king hear, though he is young, and let it be read to him. When [[evt:17813|the war with Yeda]] was young, the king's grandfather, [[per:17701|Nofdon]] the elder, went out to this same field, and Lewokalen stood against him, and Volme turned her face from Tallen. In the next year [[per:17739|Donon]] the general fought [[evt:17839|the second battle below Peyekhjeda]], and the men of Yeda were cut down like reeds; near three thousand of them lay in the furrows, and the stream ran thick. Three years after, Donon came again, and [[per:17772|Panolon]] of Yeda held, and many of ours went down.

In the year after that the king's father, [[per:17786|Nofdon]], went out against Panolon at [[set:135|Verathkano]]. Hear it, my lord: the men of Yeda fled before your father, and in the hour of their flight your father fell. He died with his face to the enemy and the field in his hand. The priests have said it: such a death is the gift of Volme.

Last year Bolon met Panolon below Peyekhjeda, and the gods tested us, and we drew back. This year the gods have given it back to us twice over.

**Of what the camp asks.** The war is not finished. Lewokalen lives. Panolon lives. Yeda still holds its hills. Let the king send to the camp: men from the river towns, two thousand at least, with spears; barley, a hundred ass-loads; oil; sandals. Let the king send silver, [[cur:364|Kerel]] by weight, for the men have not been paid since the barley harvest, and a soldier unpaid is a soldier who looks toward home.

Let the priests of Tallen give a white bull to Volme, and a black ewe to Venba for the dead, and let them watch the moon in the next month and send word of what they see.

**Of the omen.** On the night after the battle a hawk came down on the standard of Tallen and did not fly away until the morning. The priests of the camp say: this is the king's grandfather and father watching. I write what they say.

Your servant kisses the ground seven times and seven times before the king, my lord.
