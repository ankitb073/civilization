*To my mother, say: thus says your son, in the garrison at [[set:185|Tinlambuknir]] in the land of [[reg:15|Bene]]. May Nidikti of the grain fill your bins, may Winba of the earth keep the field. In the 42nd year of [[per:4537|Nyetamat]], the queen.*

Mother, I am well. I am fed. My feet are hard now, and my shoulders. The march was long, but I am here.

The garrison is in the old fort on the hill above the crossroads. Below us is the field. The old spearmen say: here, on this field, the queen beat the men of [[pol:119|Tonnga]] two years running, when they were young men, in the [[evt:4390|First Baan Trade War]]. They say the queen stood in the first rank herself, in the second year, at the [[evt:4446|battle of Tinlamvukhnir]]. They say the Tonnga captain, [[per:4775|Libunen]], fell there by the crossroads, and his men ran into the hills. When the plough turns the field below the fort, it turns up bones, and arrowheads, and once a bronze ring.

There is an old spearman of our hundred with one ear. He tells the story every night at the fire. He says: the war did not begin well. He says: at the [[evt:4410|battle of Linidhagshal]] we were beaten, and the men of Tonnga laughed at us. He says: then we came again, and again, and they did not laugh. He says the war was for the road and the market. Before the war the caravans of Bene went to Tonnga. Since the [[evt:4447|Treaty of Tinlamvukhnir]], they come to [[pol:172|Duegowinga]]. That is why we sit on this hill: to watch the road.

I watch the road. Every day the donkeys come, salt and cloth and copper going down, oil and barley coming up. The traders pay a toll at the crossroads, and the toll goes to the queen. I count the donkeys for the scribe. I can count to a hundred now and make the marks.

Barley here costs a [[cur:48|Menmu]] and a little more the measure, as at home. The people of the villages are not unkind. A woman by the crossroads gave me curds for mending her fence. Her father fought for Tonnga in the war. She says it without anger. She says: that was before.

The old spearman with one ear says he was also at the [[evt:4539|Third Siege of Riniy]], in the queen's other war, and the men of Riniy now send silver to the queen every year. He says: the queen is lucky. He says: we are lucky to have a lucky queen. He says it every night.

Mother, I ask: is my sister married yet? Did the brown cow calve? Has the east ditch been cleaned? I dream of the east ditch. I dream of the mud of it, and your bread.

They say we stay on this hill until the next levy comes in the spring. Then I come home. Tell my father to keep my sickle sharp. I will come home for the harvest, if Duemino of the storm is kind to the road.

Your son, who thinks of you at the evening fire.
