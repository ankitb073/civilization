**1 Nizö 4157 of the Myitiyishiu, before dawn.** I write this by one candle in my chamber in the palace of [[set:7|Ane]], so that there may be one account set down while the ink of events is still wet. I have served the Crown of [[pol:1656|Ane]] twenty years in council. I have served [[per:44383|Ie Nyime]] the Second since he came to the throne a boy. If I am a traitor by nightfall, let it be said I was a punctual one.

The causes are not of today, and I put them down in order, as a clerk should. In the year 4114, when I was in the cradle, the [[evt:44777|famine in Nis]] emptied the villages of my father's country. In 4127 came the [[evt:44957|famine in Yiutl]], which carried off, by the registers, above a million and a half souls in the stricken provinces; I was fifteen, and I counted carts. In 4149 the [[evt:45185|famine in Nis]] returned, as if to make sure of us. Three times in one life the granaries of the realm were found empty, and three times the Crown ordered prayers and taxes in that order.

**The same day, the third hour.** The King has been told that the guild-masters and the deputies of the quarters are assembled in the hall below. He asked me what they wanted. I told him: bread at a price a carter can pay, which today is above two [[cur:1038|withe]] the measure; an account of the treasury; and an end of wars that are begun for glory and ended for nothing. He said that the [[evt:45164|Treaty of Namosash]] had brought the realm land and silver. I said that was so, and that it was signed when he was sixteen, and that the realm had since spent the silver and more on the [[evt:45236|Third Ane–Zhukhi War]], which was not won, and was not lost, but was merely stopped.

**The same day, noon.** He spoke of [[evt:45243|Lininadhe]]. It is true that we won the field. It is also true, and the Ministry of War knows it though it did not print it, that we marched and paid near two hundred thousand men to take a field the enemy held with scarcely seven thousand. I asked the King whether that was a victory or an invoice. He did not answer, which I took for an answer.

**The same day, the sixth hour.** I went down to the hall. I proposed only what I had proposed in council a dozen times: that the realm be governed by those who pay for it, and that the Crown, which had been unable to govern the granaries, cease to govern at all. The hall rose. I was named to carry the resolution upstairs. I did not ask to be named.

**The same night.** The King received the resolution in his dressing-gown. He read it twice. He asked whether his person would be safe. I gave him my word, and I give it again here, on paper, where it can be held against me: no hand shall be laid on him. He is in the east apartments with his own servants and a guard of honour. He asked for his confessor of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]], and the man was sent for. I am told he quoted at me afterward that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I hope he did. It is the first time he has.

**2 Nizö, dawn.** The crowd in the square is calling it the [[evt:45287|revolution]]. I do not like the word. It suggests a wheel, and the teachers say little of wheels; I would rather it suggested a ledger, balanced at last. But a crowd does not choose its words to please a clerk.

**2 Nizö, the fourth hour.** The deputies have voted the Crown into abeyance and named me first officer of the Republic. I, [[per:44632|Isuilyian]], who was yesterday a councillor of the King, am today asked to be what the King was not: answerable. I have ordered the granaries opened at the old price for ten days, and an inventory of the treasury to be printed, every figure, by the end of the month. If the figures are bad, let them be bad in public.

I came back to this chamber to write, and found that someone had already taken down the royal arms from over my door. I did not order it. I have not ordered it put back.
