**Early spring, 3rd day.** Measured the well this morning with the knotted cord: nineteen cubits to water. Last spring it was sixteen. My mother wrote fourteen in this book in the year the rains first failed. That was the year the [[evt:25355|Great Drought in Khishkhih]] began, as the caravan people call it, twenty summers ago. I was a girl then. I have never known a wet year as a woman.

**Spring, 9th.** Sowed the east strip with barley at half the usual seed to the row, as the council advised. Thick sowing only feeds the dust. Kept back one jar of seed under the floor of the storeroom. Told no one but my husband.

**Spring, 20th.** The council met at the threshing floor. The tally of household grain was read out, house by house. Eleven households have less than forty days' bread. We have sixty if we eat once a day. The council voted to close the common store until the harvest is counted. My husband voted against. He says a store you cannot eat from is a grave with a lid on it.

**Late spring, 2nd.** The barley came up thin and yellow at the tips. Wind every afternoon from the south, hot, carrying grit. Covered the seedlings with straw mats. By evening the mats were in the ditch.

**Late spring, 14th.** Market at the crossroads. Grain dealers have come up from the river towns, but they want twice what they asked at the new year, and three times what they asked when the children were small. They take no promises. They will take wool, cloth, copper. I sold my mother's copper pot. I came home with a sack the size of a child.

**Early summer, 5th.** People from the hill farms are coming down. Four families camped at the spring last night. One of the women has a baby that will not take the breast. I took her a bowl of barley gruel. My husband said nothing, which is how he says yes.

**Early summer, 11th.** The council has sent two men to the river towns to ask for grain on credit against next year's wool. Everyone knows what the river towns will say. Nobody says it out loud.

**Early summer, 19th.** Harvested the east strip and weighed it on the council scale: a little more than the seed we put in. Less than a fifth of an ordinary year. In the ordinary years of my mother's book, one measure sown gave six or seven.

The old woman next door says it is the [[evt:25580|famine in Shithiu]] her grandmother told of, come back again. The council clerk wrote "hunger in all of [[reg:28|Shithuyu]]" in the record and read it out. I did not need a clerk to tell me.

**Midsummer, 1st.** The children ate the last of the dried apricots today. My youngest asked if there would be more next year. I said yes.

**Midsummer, 8th.** The hill families have moved on towards the river. One grave was left at the spring, a small one. Someone put a ring of stones around it and a cup of water beside it, which is the most anyone here can give.

**Midsummer, 15th.** Well at twenty-one cubits. My husband and his brother will dig two more this week, if they can still lift the spade. The [[cli:145|Great Drought of Khishkhih]] has been with us longer than my marriage. I do not write that it will end soon. I write what I can see: the sky white, the barley gone, the jar under the floor, the children asleep.
