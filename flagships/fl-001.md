*[Obverse. The first lines of the column are broken away.]*

[…] the river […]  
[…] and the reeds bowed down.

Sing, O [[dei:1|Uelti]], keeper of words, who sets each word in its place,  
sing, O [[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, whose feet are red,  
sing of the spears of [[pol:93|Mii]] that went down into Yiutl,  
twelve summers they went down, twelve harvests they went down.

The men of [[pol:30|Yi'otl]] shut the road of the traders,  
the road of the donkeys they shut, the road of the boats they shut,  
the silver did not come to [[set:7|Ana]], the grain did not go out from Ana.  
Then the king of Mii lifted his hand to [[dei:0|Atlie]] of the high sky:  
"Atlie, father, see the shut road. Open it with the spear."  
And Atlie heard, and the spears went down.

At [[evt:3491|Onithie]], by the walls of Onetie,  
[[per:3820|Lo'öbi]] of the bronze helm stood against [[per:3787|Ienomamshe]],  
Ienomamshe, king of the Yi'otl, who wore the gold on his arm.  
Lo'öbi broke them. Lo'öbi drove them. The field was Mii's.

At [[evt:3494|Myizinithie]], by the walls of Miezanitie,  
[[per:3826|Yi Ibe]] the swift stood against Ienomamshe a second time.  
Three thousand Yi'otl lay down in the barley.  
Three thousand Yi'otl did not rise. The field was Mii's.

At Onithie a second time the king himself went down,  
[[per:3470|A'insimen]], lord of Ana, the elder of that name.  
Hard was the day. The dust hid the sun. Nieshashta turned her face.  
But there fell Ienomamshe, king of the Yi'otl,  
there fell the gold-armed one, and his arm-gold was in the dust.  
Let the singer say it twice: there fell Ienomamshe.

*[Some twenty lines lost. Traces of the words "bitter", "the ford" and "[…] did not return".]*

[…] at Myizinithie, the third time, [[per:3854|Tiebashpo]] the steadfast  
stood against [[per:3851|Mi'itha]], the stubborn one, the general of the Yi'otl.  
Tiebashpo made the morning offering to Muevas'e of the black earth,  
and Muevas'e opened her mouth and drank the Yi'otl.  
Six hundred of ours fell; for each of ours, six of theirs.

At [[evt:3548|Myishlithie]], by the walls of Mieshlothie,  
Tiebashpo stood against [[per:3833|Mila'ash]], king of the Yi'otl.  
Mila'ash had many spears, Tiebashpo had few spears.  
Few were the spears of Mii; the field was Mii's.

At [[evt:3556|Fiukh]], by the walls of Fil'i,  
[[per:3853|E'a Mue'a]], king of Mii, stood against Mi'itha a second time.  
He poured beer to Niele'i of the river at the crossing,  
he poured oil to Nieshashta at the place of the spears.  
Mi'itha broke. Mi'itha ran. The field was Mii's.

Then the kings sat down at Fiukh, the two kings sat down.  
E'a Mue'a spoke, and Mila'ash spoke.  
They swore by the gods of the sky, they swore by the gods of the earth.  
The spears were laid down. The donkeys went out on the road.  
Twelve summers ended; the wheel turned.

What ends will begin again, says the priest.  
What is laid down will be taken up, says the priest.  
Remember Onithie. Remember Fiukh. Remember the shut road.

*[Reverse, lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Written at the word of [[per:4052|A'insimen]], king in Ana, in the 33rd year of A'insimen.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Lashla'e the moon withhold his light from him.
