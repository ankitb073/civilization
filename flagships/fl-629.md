In the temple precinct of the old capital, among the bricks of a building put up in AY 3246, excavators found one inscribed with two short sentences: [[per:29004|Nambuvethi]], great king, built this temple for his lord the god, and may the god protect the king. It is a modest text for a man later generations called "the Great". It is also one of the few things he says to us directly, and it asks for protection rather than claiming glory. This chapter tries to take him at that word, and to separate the king who needed protecting from the conqueror the epithet describes.

## A crown won by a father

He was born in 3199, a Kubis, into a war. Four years earlier his father, [[per:28912|Natatha]], had seized the throne of [[pol:1206|Kambambül]] in a moment the record marks as a crisis, and two years after that he had opened the [[evt:29327|First Kambambül–Kaazhe War]]. The record names the usurpation as its cause. A usurper needs victories, and Natatha bought his at a terrible price. The opening battle in 3198 was lost, and its commander killed. Natatha led the army himself in the year his son was born. By the war's end Kambambül had lost 84,079 men against 28,739 for [[pol:1164|Kaazhe]]. The borderland they fought over had been emptied once already: a plague of 3182–3186 had killed about three in ten of its people.

In 3205 both of the boy's parents died, of natural causes, in the same year. The record says nothing more, and I will not invent a fever for it. The [[evt:29408|Accession of Nambuvethi]] made a six-year-old emperor, with [[per:29006|Natithmi]] as regent. That same year the war ended in the [[evt:29414|Treaty of Bandidadhu (AY 3205)]]. Its bronze text survives almost whole. "Peace is granted by Nambuvethi, emperor of Kambambül," it says after the opening invocation, to [[per:28221|Nadaazum]] of Kaazhe. Kaazhe gave up twelve districts and three towns, among them [[set:261|Bandidadhu]] where the oath was sworn. It owed tribute for thirty-four years and agreed to a truce of fifteen, and raiders of either side were to be "handed over for judgement". The two royal names on the tablet belong to a child of six and a queen of seventy-five who had reigned since 3134 and would die the next year. Neither wrote it: the scribe Navizavu signs it, and the record has no names for the soldiers who won it.

## The regent's decade

[[per:29006|Natithmi]] governed for ten years, until 3215. We know almost nothing of him beyond his dates. He was thirty-eight when he took power and he lived until 3248, so he stood near the throne for nearly the whole reign. No record of quarrel survives, and none of gratitude. The empire he held for the boy was large, about ten and a half million people, and unsettled: its internal order stood near the lowest point of the century when the regency began and barely moved while it lasted. The year it ended, Kambambül opened a holy war against a breakaway state on its frontier and gave it up within the year. It looks like the first act of a young man eager to rule, and it came to nothing.

He married a woman a year older than himself, and in 3216 she bore a daughter, [[per:29504|Nakagum]]. She was his only child. The law of Kambambül gave the crown to sons, and no son came. Everything later in the reign should be read with that in mind.

## The holy war and the executed king

At twenty-three he went to war in his own person. The [[evt:29647|Holy War of Kambambül against Tyimbi]] (3222–3225) was fought against [[pol:1158|Tyimbi]], a kingdom that could put perhaps six thousand men in the field. At the [[evt:29662|Battle of Dangafu (AY 3223)]] he led 121,137 against 6,332 under [[per:29254|Nangatsu]], and lost 8,715 men to the enemy's 2,282. [[set:234|Dangafu]] was sacked that year, and again in 3225 after the [[evt:29680|Battle of Danuthu (AY 3225)]], where 73,016 Kambambül troops broke 5,189 led by the king of Tyimbi himself, [[per:29230|Natili]]. The [[evt:29682|Conquest of Tyimbi]] that followed annexed ten districts and ended the kingdom. It was settled at Dangafu, and the record lists Natili, the last king of Tyimbi, as executed in its course.

That execution is the hardest fact in Nambuvethi's life, and no surviving inscription of his mentions it. A holy war could justify much, and the record does not say who gave the order. But the conquest bears his name as signatory, and the defeated king did not leave Dangafu alive.

## Kaazhe undone

The Treaty of Bandidadhu had not settled Kaazhe; it had begun to break it. The record ties that treaty, the losses of the first war and the old plague to a civil war in Kaazhe in 3212 and another in 3236. The second of these is listed among the causes of the [[evt:29783|Second Kambambül–Kaazhe War]] (3236–3240). Nambuvethi struck while his neighbour was divided. At the [[evt:29810|Battle of Mungu (AY 3238)]] he led 99,959 men against 18,709 and won decisively. Kaazhe's commander, [[per:29374|Nambuvu]], a man born in the same year as the emperor, died on the field. Kambambül lost 16,115 men that day and the enemy 7,858. The victory relief cut at Mungu claims "19,900 soldiers" killed, two and a half times the true figure. Two more battles near the town followed under other commanders and cost Kambambül nearly 37,000 more.

The [[evt:29847|Treaty of Mungu (AY 3240)]] was sworn at [[set:255|Mungu]] with [[per:29113|Nanizhi]], king of Kaazhe since about the age of one. So both kings at the table had come to the throne as small children; one had grown into a conqueror and the other into the last of his line. Kaazhe surrendered twenty-eight districts and four towns, Mungu among them. In 3242 it collapsed into two successor states. Over the two wars against it Kambambül had lost some 145,000 men.

## The daughter's reign, and after

The last war of the reign, against a further neighbour from 3246, was fought by his generals. A second relief, damaged, claims for him 24,600 enemy dead at Lamligul in 3248; the record gives 5,940, and another man led the army. In 3250 Nakagum, then thirty-four, commanded the decisive battle. That year her father died, aged fifty-one, of natural causes. The [[evt:29950|Accession of Nakagum]] put a woman on a throne whose law favoured sons, and she signed the peace that made the defeated kingdom a vassal. She reigned thirty-one years.

Over his reign the empire grew from about 277 districts to 336 and from about ten million people to twelve and a half million. Its internal order, never strong, was better at his death than at his accession. That is the substance behind the epithet. The cost was paid by the conscripts at Mungu and Dangafu, the people of Tyimbi and a king who was not allowed to live. Kambambül outlasted him by half a century and broke into six successor states in 3300. One of them was ruled from Dangafu, the town he had sacked twice.
