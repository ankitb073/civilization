*[Obverse. A small tablet, worn at the corners, as if carried against the body.]*

To the headmen of the villages of the hills, to every man of [[pol:9|Pumi]] who still holds a spear outside the wall, say: thus says [[per:352|Tavin]], your chief, who sits on the seat of Pumi in the fiftieth year.

With me it is well. My body is whole. My beard is white, but my arm holds the spear. May it be well with you, with your flocks, with your hidden grain.

This tablet goes out in the night. It goes down from the wall on a rope. It goes in the basket of one who knows the path through the reeds. If the men of [[pol:1|Tallin]] find it, let them read it, and let them know that the chief of Pumi is not afraid.

Hear what has been, and hear it twice.

Two summers ago the men of Tallin came down into our land. Last year I went out against them at [[evt:399|Nilu]] with the spears of Pumi. [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the bronze did not go before me that day. The field was Tallin's. I came back to the city with fewer men than I went out with, and the women of [[set:17|Pumi]] cut their hair.

Then the men of Tallin came to our wall. They sat down before it. They went away, and they came again. This year our spearmen went out once more before the gate, and once more the field was Tallin's. I stood on the wall and I saw it. Now they sit around the city like dogs around a pen. No donkey goes out. No donkey comes in.

Hear how it is inside the wall. The granaries are low. Before the war a measure of barley cost less than one [[cur:5|Ineth]]; now a man gives an ineth for a handful, and is glad to get it. The children cry at night. The old men eat once in a day. We have killed the oxen of the plough; when the war is over, who will pull the plough? The water of the well is low and tastes of mud. The smoke of the dead goes up every evening.

But the wall stands. Hear this: the wall stands. The gate is shut. The priests give what they can. To [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] of the grain they give the last sheaf of the storehouse, so that she will remember us at the harvest. To [[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire they give the fat of the last ox. To [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon they keep the lamp, though the oil is little; they burn it low, but it does not go out.

*[Reverse.]*

Now hear what I ask, and do it quickly.

Gather the young men of the villages. Gather the shepherds with their slings. Gather the hunters with their bows. Do not come against the camp of Tallin on the open ground; you are few and they are many. Come by night. Burn their fodder. Drive off their donkeys. Cut the road by which their grain comes. Let them be hungry as we are hungry.

Send barley if you can. Send it by the reed-path, in small baskets, by night. Let the one who carries this tablet show you the path.

And if the gods have turned their faces from Pumi, if [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky does not hear, if [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the earth opens her mouth for us, then hear my last word. Hide the gods' things in the hills. Hide the bronze of the house of Vulme. Hide the tablets of the priests. Let the men of Tallin take the city if they must; let them not take the gods. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] say: all things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. If Pumi ends, Pumi will begin again. The gods must be there when it begins.

I do not say Pumi will end. I say: be ready.

*[Left edge.]*

> Written inside the wall, in the 50th year of Tavin, chief of Pumi.
