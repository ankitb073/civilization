**THE ENGINE THAT BURNS WITHIN**

*The inventor of the new motor, whose trials in [[set:114|Thimisitsiy]] have been the talk of the capital, writes for our readers. — Ed.*

**By [[per:45386|Bailada]]**

Thimisitsiy, 12 Isyivish 2606 of the Khikhishiu.

I have been asked by the editor to explain, in plain words and without diagrams, what it is that I have built, why it matters, and why I believe it belongs to the people of [[pol:1922|Yiutl]] before it belongs to anyone else. I will do my best on the first two counts. On the third, I confess, I am not impartial.

**What it is.** Every reader knows the steam engine. A fire burns under a boiler; the boiler makes steam; the steam pushes a piston. Between the fire and the piston stand a furnace, a boiler, a chimney, a stoker with a shovel and a great deal of iron, and between the lighting of the fire and the first turn of the wheel stands an hour of waiting.

My engine does away with all of that. It burns its fuel inside the cylinder itself. A small charge of oil spirit, mixed with air, is drawn into the cylinder and squeezed by the piston. A spark lights it. The burning gas drives the piston down. The piston turns the shaft. The waste gas is pushed out, and the cycle begins again, many times each second. There is no boiler to burst and no stoker to feed it. The engine starts within a minute of being cranked, and stops when the fuel is shut off.

The motor now running in my workshop weighs about as much as two grown men. It does the work of four good horses, and it does it without hay, without rest, and without complaint. At present prices it runs a working day on fuel that costs less than a single [[cur:1083|Fyishi]].

**Why it matters.** Consider what a steam engine cannot do because it is too heavy. It cannot pump water on a hill farm where no rail runs. It cannot drive a small boat on a river without a barge's worth of coal behind it. It cannot be carried in a cart to a mill that a flood has stopped. My engine can do all of these things, and I have no doubt that cleverer men than I will find uses for it that I cannot imagine.

The teaching of [[mov:57|Khikhishiu]] bids us seek release from suffering, our own and others'. I have watched women carry water up the hillside villages of my boyhood, two buckets at a time, from dawn until noon. If this engine ends that labour in a single village, I shall count my twenty years at the bench well spent.

**Why it belongs to Yiutl.** There are gentlemen, I am told, who now claim to have sketched such an engine in their notebooks long ago. I do not doubt that many men have dreamed of it. But the [[evt:46105|Invention of the combustion engine in Yiutl]] was not a dream in a notebook. It was a machine that ran, in a workshop in this city, in the year just past, before witnesses, and it has run every day since.

This Republic has grown great of late. Since the [[evt:46104|Conversion of Yiutl to I'ishiu]] it has found its conscience; since the [[evt:46087|First Battle of Fyitlivul]] and the [[evt:46097|Battle of Onithie]], and the [[evt:46098|Treaty of Onithie]] that President [[per:45372|Luimtli]] signed, it has found new lands and new markets. What it lacks is power, not in the sense of armies, but in the sense of horses that never tire. I offer it that power.

I ask the Assembly for three things: a patent that will protect the design in this Republic; a modest sum to build a second and larger engine for public trials at the river docks; and an assurance that the workshops of Yiutl, and not foreign ones, shall have the first right to manufacture it.

I ask the readers of this newspaper for something simpler. Come and see it run. The workshop door in the tanners' quarter is open on the first day of each week. Bring your doubts; I will bring the engine.
