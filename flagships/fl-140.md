*Full text of the address delivered by Chairman [[per:45428|Eshuesh]] to the crowd assembled in the great square of [[set:131|Shiüzhi]] on the anniversary of the [[evt:46163|revolution]], 24 Ta'a 2398 of the Kievi, as taken down by the official stenographers.*

---

Citizens of [[pol:1773|Iith]]! Workers of the mills and the yards! Mothers! Soldiers! *(Prolonged cheering.)*

One year ago you stood in this square, and you did not ask permission. You did not petition. You did not send a delegation with its hat in its hand to the old Assembly, which would have received it, thanked it, and filed it. You came here, two hundred thousand of you by the police count, and the police were counting from behind their doors! *(Laughter.)* And you said: enough. *(Cries of "Enough!")*

They ask me abroad, in the newspapers of countries that have never been sacked, why Iith has put aside the old republic, its forty parties and its forty newspapers and its forty ways of doing nothing. I will tell them. I will tell them here, so the mails can carry it.

I am an old man. I was not yet thirty when the invader came to the walls of this city. I was here. I stood on the wall by the river gate. Some of you were not born. Let me tell you what your fathers saw.

They came against us at the [[evt:45787|Battle of Shiüzhi]] with nearly eighty thousand men. We had not five thousand. Not five thousand! And I tell you that those few thousand made the enemy pay for every street, and that one man in five upon these walls died on them. *(Cheers.)* But courage is not a cannon. The next year came the [[evt:45799|Tenth Sack of Shiüzhi]]. The tenth! Ten times in our history this city has been opened like a tin. I saw the warehouses burn on the quay. I saw the doors of this very building broken in.

And then the gentlemen of the republic went to sign the [[evt:45800|Treaty of Shiüzhi]], in our own city, on the enemy's terms, and came home and told us we should be grateful, because the paper promised us twenty years of quiet. Twenty years! *(Cries of "Shame!")* A truce is not a victory, citizens. A truce is the time the enemy gives you to grow fat for the next sack.

And what did the republic do with its twenty years, and the twenty after them? It talked. It argued over tariffs while the grain rose. It argued over the budget of the army while the army had boots for one man in three. A measure of grain costs more than one and a half [[cur:1046|il]] today, and that is after a year of our work; I will not tell you what it would have been without it.

That is why there is one Party now. Not because one voice is better than many, but because a house on fire needs one bucket chain, not forty committees on the pattern of the bucket! *(Long applause.)*

They say we have betrayed the teachings of the [[mov:69|Kievi]], which tell every seeker to find the truth by his own inner light. I honour that teaching. Let every man find the truth by his own light, by all means, and let him find it at his workbench, and at his post, and in the ranks. The Kievi also teach justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and that the wise withdraw from appetite. Where was the moderation of the speculators? Where was the justice of the old Assembly to the widows of the tenth sack?

And the Kievi teach that all things turn on a great wheel, and what ends will begin again. Citizens, Shiüzhi has been sacked ten times, and ten times Shiüzhi has risen. *(Cheers.)* The wheel has turned. Now it is our turn to keep it turning the right way.

Long live Iith! Long live Shiüzhi, the city that will not stay fallen! *(The crowd sang the national hymn. The Chairman withdrew at a quarter past the hour.)*
