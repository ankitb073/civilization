For a scholar of Khishkhis, [[per:38174|Nashathe]] of [[pol:1540|Fièvi]] is not a neutral subject. In AY 4062 and again in 4063 his armies besieged [[set:85|Nambi]], and peace was dictated there, in the city that is now our capital. Four years later the old Khishkhis empire collapsed, and from its ruin rose the state whose citizens we still are. The record does not make one event the cause of the other, and an archaeologist should not either. But it would be dishonest to write his life without saying where it touches ours. Tradition calls him the Victorious. The evidence I trust most is not a stele but a line scratched into temple plaster at Athnambi in 4082: "Namuvthe, son of Nazhige, of Fièvi, was here when Athnambi fell." Namuvthe was one of the hundreds of thousands who marched for Nashathe. Unlike most of them, he left his name.

## A child among the unburied

Nashathe was born in 4043, a Khumbivi prince of the Nakumbèn house, in the year his mother, [[per:38097|Navande]], took the throne. A sister born the same year, probably his twin, did not survive it. His mother reigned eight years and died in 4051, aged thirty-eight. The [[evt:38705|Accession of Nashathe]] made an eight-year-old emperor of nearly thirteen million people, with [[per:38176|Nashinzhè]] as regent until 4059.

His first public act was a treaty he cannot have understood. The [[evt:38709|Treaty of Shudhunki (AY 4051)]], on a bronze tablet that survives complete, has peace "granted by Nashathe, emperor of Fièvi" to the Khanji queen [[per:37789|Navashadhe]], a woman of sixty-two. Khanji ceded eight districts and accepted Fièvi's overlordship. It "shall send its levies when summoned". Two years later came the Grey Sickness of 4053, a plague of terrible reach. It killed some 4.4 million people, nearly a third of those it reached. The Annals of Fièvi record the year in a single line: "The dead lay unburied." The boy emperor was ten.

## Nambi

The regency ended in 4059, and in 4061 the eighteen-year-old ruler began the [[evt:38804|Second Fièvi–Khishkhis War]] against the [[pol:1454|Khishkhis]] empire. His generals won two great battles, at Jyijinjivji in 4062 and on another field in 4063, each with about 140,000 men against 80,000. His rock relief for the first claims 206,000 enemy dead. The record gives about 31,000. Even so, it was the only war of his reign in which the enemy lost more men than Fièvi did, some 62,000 against 33,000. Nambi was besieged in 4062 and again in 4063. The [[evt:38824|Treaty of Nambi (AY 4063)]], signed in the city with [[per:38162|Namuth]], remembered as the Last, took forty-nine districts and four towns, Shyih and Mbambanuduyih among them, and imposed an indemnity of more than a hundred thousand.

What followed belongs to our own history more than to his. Within four years the old Khishkhis state had collapsed and our own had risen, with Nambi as its capital. Whatever weakened the older empire, the treaty of 4063 cannot have helped it.

## Conquering the small

After Nambi, Nashathe's wars took a pattern. Very large armies were sent against small and often new neighbours, and they won at great cost. In the [[evt:38860|Second Fièvi–Puvu War]] of 4069–4073 he commanded once, at the [[evt:38915|Battle of Shyithe (AY 4071)]], with 95,000 men against 21,000. He lost more than sixteen thousand. That same year famine killed some 780,000 of his subjects. The [[evt:38928|Conquest of Puvu]] was signed at Khayagu after the town's seventh siege, and its king, [[per:38297|Nanjijim]], lived another eighteen years. A stele of 4075 claims fourteen thousand enemy dead at Nilu.

The [[evt:38957|Fièvi–Adunambi War]] of 4079–4082 is the clearest case. Adunambi was a city-state of a single district, and its forces in the field never reached eight hundred men. Fièvi sent armies of 140,000 against it and lost more than sixteen thousand soldiers to take it. The enemy lost 239. [[set:140|Athnambi]] was sacked in 4081 and again in 4082, and it was then that Namuvthe cut his line into the temple wall. In those same years two of the emperor's young sons died, at ten and at three.

The [[evt:39079|Fièvi–Kabaf War]] of 4091–4094 was fought against the city-state of Kabaf, a royal city of Puvu kings in ages long past and independent again since 4063. At the [[evt:39100|Battle of Kabaf (AY 4092)]] Nashathe brought 145,000 men against 9,000 under the city's ruler, [[per:38468|Namyitsüdi]]. The war cost Fièvi nearly 54,000 dead to the city's 7,000. After the [[evt:39113|Conquest of Kabaf]] in 4094, Namyitsüdi was executed. A scribe's word list from these years, made in the emperor's thirty-eighth year for clerks dealing with a neighbouring people, sets out the words for law, war, fire, enemy, god, river, king, bread, road and city. As the vocabulary of an expanding empire, it hardly needs comment.

## The last campaign

His empress died in 4098. In 4100, at fifty-seven, he went to war once more, against the city-state of Damvizhi. At the [[evt:39173|Battle of Dambüzhi (AY 4101)]] he led nearly 235,000 men, the largest army of his life, against 135 under [[per:38627|Nakhijvu]]. He won. The enemy lost fifty-four men and Fièvi more than forty thousand. No record tells us what killed them. Disease in a vast camp before a small walled town is the likeliest explanation.

Nashathe died a natural death in 4102, aged fifty-nine, before Damvizhi fell. His eldest surviving son, [[per:38631|Nadave]], finished the conquest the next year. Under the law of seniority three of his sons ruled in turn: Nadave until 4131, then the twins [[per:38632|Naguvu]] and [[per:38633|Nagième]] until 4147. Fièvi itself lasted until 4255.

The record lets us put two figures side by side. Under Nashathe the empire grew from some 216 districts to more than 320, and from thirteen to over twenty-one million people. In wars against enemies who were usually tiny, it lost well over two hundred thousand soldiers. The epithet records the first figure. Namuvthe's graffito, cut by a man who survived the second, is a better memorial than any stele.
