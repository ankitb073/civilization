*Taken before the reverend fathers and the aldermen sitting for the restoring of the rolls of the city, which perished in the fire.*

In the name of the gods of this city, and by the fire of [[dei:18|Iva]], which I saw do its work: I, sometime porter and lamp-keeper in the house of the master of rolls, being asked what I know, say as follows.

I was a lad of fifteen when the [[pol:1139|Linunushal]] came against [[set:160|Nunulnga]]. That is three-and-twenty years past. The year before, they had beaten our men below the walls. That summer they beat them again at the [[evt:30342|Battle of Poynge]], and the host that came on afterwards was as the stars of heaven, as the sand that is upon the shore. We on the walls could not see the end of their fires.

My master kept the rolls of the city: the rolls of fields and vineyards, of rents and dues owed to the lords and to the temples, of who was born and who was wed. He was a careful man and a just one, and he kept the keys on his own belt. When the gate fell, he sent his household down into the cellar under the hall, and he stayed above with the rolls. He said a steward does not leave his lord's goods. We did not see him again.

Through the grating of the cellar I watched the hall burn. Parchment does not burn as wood burns. It curls, and blackens at the edge, and the writing stands out on it brown for a moment before it goes, as though each roll were trying to be read one last time. That is my memory of the [[evt:30343|Fourth Sack of Nunuunge]]. I have not been able to sit near a hearth with an easy heart since.

In the year following they came again, and the city was sacked a second time, the [[evt:30390|Fifth Sack of Nunuunge]], and after it the [[evt:30391|Conquest of Tubizhinga]], and [[pol:1267|Tuvizhinga]] was ours no longer. The lords who burned us are now themselves broken into pieces, men say. The wheel turns, as the priests have always taught us it would.

Now to what the reverend fathers ask. Of the lands, I can swear to these things only:

That the vineyard below the east wall paid each autumn to the temple of [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] a measure of wine for every row, for I carried the tally-sticks myself.

That the mill by the ford belonged to the widow's family and not to the man who now claims it, for I saw her husband pay the dues on it at my master's table, three years running.

That the long field beyond the cemetery was common pasture, and was never written to any one man in my master's time.

Of the rest I cannot swear, and I will not, for it is written that a false witness shall not go unpunished, and I am too old to begin lying now.

I am asked also of my condition. I live by the charity of the temple and by mending nets. Grain is half a [[cur:691|Badhu]] the measure, and men complain of it, but I remember worse. I ask the reverend fathers, when the new rolls are written, to write my master's name at the head of them, for he died beside the old ones.

May [[dei:19|Yinan]] give light to the scribes. This is my testimony.
