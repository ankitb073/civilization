In AY 4640 the Houses of Prayer of [[pol:1785|Tallin]] were issued a printed service of thanksgiving. It begins: "it is now two years since the sickness left our streets, and twelve since it first came among us." The pestilence was the Marsh Cough. By the count of the registrars it had passed along the trade roads into fifty-four cities and killed two in every five people in many of them. The merchants, the preface says, had noticed that it first appeared at a port "within a few years of the First Contact ..., when ships came to us from the farther shore". I cannot translate that sentence without a pause. The record names my own country as Tallin's partner in that contact of 4623. The ships were ours. The ruler in whose first full year this prayer was printed was [[per:44127|Yivith]], later called the Great. She inherited a country in mourning, and this chapter asks what she made of it.

## A daughter of the plague years

She was born in 4608, a woman of the Jego. Her father, [[per:44064|Nivlalin II]], came to the throne when she was seven. Her mother died when she was thirteen, and one of her two younger sisters died four years later, also at thirteen. She was fifteen when the ships arrived and twenty when the sickness began. It lasted ten years and killed nearly thirty million people across the lands it reached. She married a man four years younger. Their only child, a daughter, was born in 4641.

The [[evt:44859|Accession of Yivith]] came in 4639, when she was thirty-one, a year after the plague lifted. Tallin then held about 500 districts and over thirty-seven million survivors. It kept more than 1.3 million men under arms, which was its own kind of wound.

## The trade war

Within four years she was at war. The [[evt:44908|24th Tunnge Trade War]] (4643–4646) was fought against [[pol:1877|Kunnga]], a city-state; the record traces it to Tallin's own expansion. In 4644, with the war under way, a usurper, [[per:44297|Rigathan]], seized Kunnga's throne. That year a Tallin general won a battle at the cost of 85,314 of his men.

In 4645 the queen took command herself. At the [[evt:44921|Battle of Nunnga (AY 4645)]] she led 877,514 men against 10,958 under [[per:44299|Khetsin]]. She won. Tallin lost 107,120 men and Kunnga 1,554. Both sides' printed histories survive, and between them they show how the age wrote about war. Tallin's, published in 4689, says she "held the heights", that the enemy had 30,912 men, and that Tallin lost "only 763". Kunnga's, published in 4653, blames the failure of its supply trains and claims 330,543 enemy dead against 339 of its own. Each multiplies the other side's losses and hides its own. The truth is worse than either: a hundred thousand of her soldiers died to defeat a force about one eightieth the size of her own.

The [[evt:44924|Treaty of Nuwkennga (AY 4646)]], sworn at [[set:129|Nuwkennga]], gave Tallin five districts and a town, an indemnity, trading rights and a twenty-year truce. Kunnga's historian calls the cession "some villages of no account ... which had always been poor". The war had cost Tallin 242,266 men and Kunnga 8,830. Seven years later a former subject of Kunnga went to court for his father's 170 acres in the ceded land. The Crown answered that the fields "were found empty and were given to settlers". The judge let him keep them "as a tenant of the crown, paying each year 34.8" in silver money.

## Three cities

The rest of her wars followed the same pattern on a smaller scale. Her generals fought them all. The [[evt:45003|Tallin–Ivun War]] (4652–4654) sent armies of 740,887 and 500,443 men against [[pol:1899|Ivun]], which fielded about two and three thousand. Tallin lost 92,888 men and Ivun 2,010. The [[evt:45029|Conquest of Ivun]], signed by [[per:44230|Baaluthshifin]] at [[set:197|Ivun]], annexed three districts. Each side's chronicle uses the stock charges: he had forgotten "the oaths his fathers swore"; she was "swollen with pride". In 4653, between the two battles, her twelve-year-old daughter died.

The [[evt:45093|Third Tallin–Binge War]] (4660–4662) took one district from [[pol:1908|Binge]]. It was an ancient town at [[set:147|Binge]], independent only since 4650, and it cost Tallin 89,520 men to the defenders' 928. The [[evt:45105|Conquest of Binge]] was signed with its ruler, [[per:44327|Nathbihimtin]]. A merchant house's ledger for that year notes in its margin: "The roads are unsafe because of" the war, and "Losses written off after" the conquest. The [[evt:45160|Tallin–Hiyikhzhidhe War]] (4668–4670) did the same to [[pol:1909|Hiyikhzhidhe]], whose town of [[set:157|Piyikhzhidhe]] had been independent for twenty years. Tallin lost 124,353 men against 1,396 for one more district. The defenders' chronicle remembers "a night of rain that bogged the guns". The [[evt:45213|Conquest of Hiyikhzhidhe]] was signed in 4670 by [[per:44328|Nginin]].

## The usurper and the verdict

Yivith died in 4671, aged sixty-three, of natural causes. She left no living child, and the house that had ruled Tallin since the empire's foundation in 4387 ended with her. The record marks a crisis, and the [[evt:45216|Usurpation of Tukhulven]] followed. [[per:44533|Tukhulven]] reigned barely two years. The record counts his seizure of power among the causes of two wars in 4676 and 4687 and a civil war in 4728. Kunnga came back in 4676 to fight Tallin again, and the record traces that war to hers. Tallin itself lasted until 4831.

In her thirty-two years the empire grew from about 501 districts to 506. Its population rose from thirty-seven million to over forty as the plague generation was replaced. Its internal order was slightly firmer until her death unsettled it. Her four wars cost Tallin about 549,000 soldiers. Their enemies lost about 13,000, and Tallin gained ten districts. She commanded in one battle, the costliest of all. If "Great" describes the size of her armies, the epithet is accurate. As a judgement of her reign it does not survive the arithmetic. The tenant farmer at Nuwkennga, paying rent to the crown for his father's land, is a better witness to what she left.
