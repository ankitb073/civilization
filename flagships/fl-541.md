title: Nitllu Ada to His Son, on the Founding of the House
---
*[Obverse. Envelope inscription:]* To my son. Let the scribe keep it until the boy can read it himself.

To my son, speak. Thus says [[per:413|Nitllu Ada]], your father, a merchant of [[set:13|Tivintusa]], servant of [[dei:83|Sada]] of the high sky and of [[dei:85|Nil'i]] of the river, who carries the boats.

My son, you are small. You sit in the courtyard and play with the knucklebones. You cannot read this. I write it now, so that when you are grown you will read it and know how the house began. May [[dei:84|Lashli'i]] of the night count your years long.

Hear. In the 8th year of [[per:419|Cui'dilli'an]], king in [[pol:13|Tivintusa]], I went up to the house of the gods. I gave a ram to Sada. I gave barley to [[dei:86|Shu'i]] of the grain. I gave oil to Nil'i at the river steps. Then I called the scribes. Then I called the elders of the gate. I said: "From this day there is a house. Its name is [[hou:3|Shi'ash'u]]. Its seat is in Tivintusa. Its trade is copper and barley. Let the scribes write it. Let the elders witness it." The scribes wrote it. The elders witnessed it. This was the [[evt:356|Founding of the House of Shi'ash'u]].

My son, hear how it was before. My father carried copper on his back. He carried it from the hills to the gate of Tivintusa, one load, two loads. I carried copper on an ass. Then on two asses. Then I had ten asses and three men. Then I had a boat on the river. Now there is a house. Your hand will hold what my father's back carried.

Hear the rules of the house. I set them in the clay. Do not break them.

One. Weigh with the king's stone. Do not keep a light stone for buying and a heavy stone for selling. The gods see the stone. The elders see the stone. Your father was never caught with a false stone, because your father never had one.

Two. Pay the tax at the gate. Pay it in full, in [[cur:3|Awa]] of full weight. The king keeps the road; the road carries the copper. Who cheats the king cheats his own road.

Three. Lend barley in the hungry months. Lend it at the king's rate and not above. A man who borrowed barley from you in the hungry month will buy copper from you in the fat month. A man you squeezed will buy from another.

Four. Pour to the gods before you count the silver. Prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour. When the gods favour the house, give them their due first. When the gods turn their face, give them their due first also.

Five. Do not quarrel with your brothers over the house. The house is one. If you divide it, you have two small houses, and small houses are eaten.

Six. Keep the tablets. Every load, every loan, every debt, write it. What is not written is a quarrel waiting to be born.

Seven. Honour the elders of the gate. Honour the priests of [[mov:13|Unsatlmum]]. Honour the king. Do not stand too near any of them. A merchant who stands too near the great is burned like a moth at the lamp.

My son, these are the rules. Your father learned them with his feet and his back. You will learn them with your eyes, on the clay. That is the gift of a house.

When I go down to [[dei:87|Sutlpi]] in the house below the earth, pour beer for me at the river steps. Say: "Nitllu Ada carried copper. Nitllu Ada made the house." That is enough.

May [[dei:88|Mastu]] of the sun shine on your road. May Shu'i fill your granary. May the copper come down from the hills to you as it came down to me.

*[Edge.]*

> In the 8th year of Cui'dilli'an, king in Tivintusa. Sealed with the seal of the house of Shi'ash'u, its first seal.
