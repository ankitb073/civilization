**AN OPEN LETTER TO THE PRESIDENT AND THE ASSEMBLY OF MBILU**

*From the annexed districts of Mane. Printed at the expense of the undersigned, 11 Khièthan 4771 of the Uthungala, and offered to any newspaper of the capital that has the courage to set it in type.*

President [[per:45979|Nazhizhive]], and Members of the Assembly:

We write to you in your own language, because two years of experience have taught us that you do not read ours.

We are the people of the districts that the [[evt:46870|Treaty of Ene]] transferred to the Republic of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]]. We were not consulted on that transfer. We were not represented at the table where it was signed. We learned the terms from a printed notice nailed to the door of the telegraph office in [[set:64|Ini]], three days after the ink was dry. If free republics dispose of a people as of a parcel of land, we should like to be told so plainly.

First, the war, of which your newspapers have said so much and we so little.

The [[evt:46819|First Mbilu Colonial War in Mana]] arose, your ministers tell us, from the [[evt:46818|Mbilu Claim against Mana]]. We have read that claim. It is eleven pages long. Not one of those pages mentions the wishes of the people who lived on the land claimed.

Now consider the figures, which are not ours but yours, taken from the War Ministry's own published returns. At the [[evt:46848|Battle of Ometh]], near [[set:72|Umifu]], the Republic sent upward of five hundred and thirty thousand men against a defending force that the same returns put at sixty thousand. At the [[evt:46855|Battle of Tivintukhe]], near [[set:13|Shivindua]], it was nearly five hundred thousand against sixty thousand. At the [[evt:46869|Battle of Ene]], four hundred and fifty thousand against fifty-four thousand. Over the whole war the Ministry admits to fourteen thousand, seven hundred and forty-five Mbilu dead and wounded. It records forty-nine thousand, eight hundred and sixty-six losses on our side.

We know what to make of these figures. Eight men against one is not a war but a beating, and for every soldier of the Republic who fell, more than three of ours were carried home. Either the Republic's generals feared us more than they admit, or they meant to make an example of us. We are content to let the Assembly choose. We say only this: the women of our villages counted their own dead, and they did not need the Ministry to tell them the number.

We have no quarrel with the soldiers of Mbilu who died. They did not choose the war any more than we did. Their mothers weep as ours do. We have a quarrel with the men who sent them, and with the men who now send us the bill.

For there is a bill. The treaty imposes an indemnity upon the vanquished, and the vanquished, it turns out, are us. It is collected in [[cur:1094|Dathu]] by agents who arrived on the first train after the treaty and have not left since. A measure of grain in the market of Ini now costs above four Dathu, and the grain itself goes out by the same railway, to your ports, while our children eat millet.

The religion of your Republic, [[mov:24|Uthungala]], teaches that there is one divine power, that every seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason, and that justice in dealings is required of all. We do not share your creed, and we keep the gods of our fathers. But we ask you, by your own creed: is this justice in dealings? Your preachers say that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. Then by your own reckoning, look at our districts, and ask what the divine power thinks of what you have done in them.

We ask for three things:

1. That the indemnity be suspended until an inquiry, sitting in the districts and hearing their people, has reported to the Assembly.
2. That the annexed districts be given delegates in the Assembly, elected by their own people, in proportion to their numbers.
3. That the schools and courts of the districts be permitted to use the language of Mane alongside your own.

These are not the demands of rebels. They are the requests of people who have been told that they are now citizens of a republic, and who would like to be treated as such.

*Signed on behalf of the district committees. The names are withheld, for reasons the Assembly will understand.*
