In AY 3745 a declaration was read at [[set:140|Athnambi]], which its documents spell Adunambi. It announced that the realm of [[pol:1496|Tadudüvi]] "owes neither homage nor fealty" to [[pol:1451|Kabaf]]. Such texts usually boast. This one, catalogued as PAL·DE·00954, pauses after its list of battles to say: "We confess that the price was dear. Our dead outnumber theirs more than twice over." The record bears it out. In the [[evt:35268|Third Tadudüvi War of Independence]] the rebels lost 3,505 men and Kabaf 1,561. The king in whose name the declaration was read was [[per:34777|Nadigi]], whose name means "rain". I write as a citizen of Khishkhis, the country whose queen broke his army at Dyizhih, and I have tried not to read him through her stones.

## A rebel from nowhere

The record is silent about Nadigi's first thirty-eight years. It gives his birth in 3700 and his people, the Kumbumbi: no parents, no office, no deed before the rising. Another Nadigi of those years, a Kabaf general born in 3680, lost to Puvu at Khathekhizi in 3727; the record keeps the two men apart.

It does show the kingdom he rose against. Kabaf's queen, [[per:34645|Natidmi]] the Convert, was beaten by Puvu at Khambe in 3726. Her capital was sacked the next year, and the Treaty of Kabaf of 3727 gave away sixteen districts. In 3733, in her reign, Kabaf adopted the Sinimu faith. The record names the sack, Khambe and that treaty, but not the conversion, as causes of the [[evt:35267|Revolt of Tadudüvi]] in 3738. Yet the new state took as its faith the Cult of [[mov:24|Uthungala]], which Kabaf had abandoned. It was the faith of 35 of the 84 districts that rose with him: more than any other, but not a majority.

Those 84 districts made the rebels the larger state from the first day. By the end of the 3730s Kabaf held eight districts and about 415,000 people. By the end of the 3740s Tadudüvi counted 3,567,253 people and 70,875 men under arms. The declaration says that "for many generations" Kabaf had taken this land's sons and grain. That was true, at most, of part of it. Thirty-four of the districts had been Kabaf's since the kingdom's founding in 3665 and twenty-four since a treaty of 3689. Twenty-six had come to it only in 3725, by the Treaty of Nzhinduh. "Our fathers rose against that yoke twice before," it adds, "and twice it was laid on them again." The record knows both risings, in 2013 and 3627, and both were crushed. Neither was against Kabaf, which did not exist before 3665. The declaration has turned every former master into one yoke.

## Twenty-nine to one

Nadigi commanded in person only once in this war. At the [[evt:35332|Battle of Nzhivi (AY 3740)]] he led 41,220 men against 1,439 under [[per:34605|Natadtsoth]], nearly twenty-nine to one. He won, losing 140 men to Kabaf's 410. His captain Navande fought the rest of the war: at Nzhinduh in 3741 and three times before Athnambi. In the first of those battles, in 3742, Navande's 21,715 men were thrown back by 1,659 and lost 3,105 against the defenders' 415. That one day accounts for nearly nine-tenths of the rebels' dead. Natadtsoth fell before Athnambi in 3743. The record counts Tadudüvi as the attacker in all three, outside its own capital, and does not explain why.

Of that defeat the declaration says, "We do not hide it." The king's own victory stele, PAL·IN·02695, set up in 3746, is less candid:

> Nadigi, king of Tadudüvi, defeated Kabaf in Adunambi. He killed 2,000 soldiers. He erected this stele for Kudze, his lord. [...]

The battle of 3744 was Navande's, and Kabaf lost 329 men. The basalt is damaged and its final lines are lost. Note also whom it honours: Kudze, the old sky god, "his lord". The declaration of the year before speaks only of a One Power "before whom the old gods are servants and shadows". A brick from the city gate, PAL·IN·06023 of 3751, asks Nüdinande, god of war, to "bless the city". The state's faith spoke of one power; the king's own inscriptions still addressed the old gods.

The [[evt:35378|Treaty of Athnambi (AY 3744)]], signed by Nadigi and Natidmi, granted independence and a truce of fifteen years. Natidmi died the next year, and Kabaf collapsed in 3758. In 3742, mid-war, Nadigi and his wife Nafüvel had a daughter. Her name, Namumbi, means "peace".

## The queen at Dyizhih

In 3749 [[pol:1454|Khishkhis]], whose faith was the Vimekhulu, began the [[evt:35428|Holy War of Khishkhis against Tadudüvi]]. It lasted ten years, and Tadudüvi lost 18,112 men to Khishkhis's 10,016. In 3750 Namumbi died, aged eight. The record calls her death natural and says nothing more, of her or of her father's grief.

Tadudüvi won only two battles in this war, both under Navande at Dyizhih. Nadigi commanded in three and lost them all, each differently. At the [[evt:35493|Battle of Shyih (AY 3754)]] [[per:34990|Nanizhi]] outnumbered him two to one, 13,453 to 6,606. Nadigi lost 526 men, fewer than the enemy's 587, but Nanizhi held the field. At the [[evt:35539|Battle of Shyih (AY 3758)]] Nadigi had the larger army, 8,850 to 7,239, and lost 480 men to Nanizhi's 160. At the [[evt:35548|Battle of Dyizhih (AY 3759)]] the queen of Khishkhis, [[per:34720|Nandundath]], came herself with 8,500 men against his 12,504. He lost 4,020, about a third of his army; she lost 512. Her stele for that day (PAL·IN·02711) says "She killed 17,200 soldiers", more men than he had brought to the field. Her stele for Shyih claims 2,200 dead where the record has 480. These are my own country's monuments; they deserve the same doubt as his.

The [[evt:35549|Treaty of Dyizhih (AY 3759)]], signed at [[set:146|Dyizhih]] by Nandundath and Nadigi, ceded 31 districts, with Nzhivi, Shyih, Yaadhi and Gandagi. Nzhivi, the field of his only victory, was among them. It also imposed an indemnity of 37,311 kilograms of silver and a truce of fifteen years. Its terms say nothing about faith, and Tadudüvi kept Uthungala. Nandundath set up a boundary stone in Daadüdhih with the words "May Shilim curse the enemy."

In the same year came the [[evt:35438|First Coinage of Tadudüvi]], the silver [[cur:828|Vevi]]. The coin is about 92 per cent fine: 13.8 grams, with 12.7 of silver. On the specimen PAL·NU·00303 his head is veiled and faces left, with the legend NADIĠI TAMBƏ, and the reverse shows a temple façade. Many specimens survive. The record does not say whether it was struck to pay Khishkhis. At 12.7 grams the indemnity came to nearly three million Vevi. When Tadudüvi next paid Khishkhis, the treaty text of 3778 named the sum in Vevi: 1,797,874.

## The quiet years

After Dyizhih the record falls almost silent for sixteen years: no battle, and no inscription of his. The kingdom gained ten districts between 3750 and 3774, and a merchant house was founded at Dyizhih in 3768. By the end of the 3760s Tadudüvi held 58 districts and 2,253,803 people, about two-thirds of the population it had counted twenty years before. Its army numbered 17,983.

The truce ran out in 3774, and in the same year Khishkhis began the [[evt:35738|Sixth Khishkhis–Tadudüvi War]]. Nafüvel died that year at seventy-one. Nadigi died in 3775, of natural causes, at seventy-five, before any battle of the new war had been fought. He left no son; the only child the record knows had been dead twenty-five years. That same year [[per:35242|Nalivi]], a man of thirty from another line, seized the throne: the [[evt:35744|Usurpation of Nalivi]]. Navande fell at Dyizhih in 3777, aged sixty-three, and in 3778 Nalivi signed away Dyizhih itself. The kingdom collapsed in 3792, fifty-four years after the rising.

As a general, Nadigi was poor. His one victory came at odds of nearly twenty-nine to one, and his stele claimed another man's. But he held the kingdom for thirty-seven years, through a holy war that took 31 of its 84 districts, and it outlasted him by only seventeen. A declaration issued under the great seal of his realm set it a standard: "so that it may never be said of Tadudüvi what we have said of Kabaf." We cannot know what his people thought of him. What survives of him in the largest numbers is his coin: a veiled king, in silver that many hands once held. My own name means "drought". I have tried to be fair to the rain.
