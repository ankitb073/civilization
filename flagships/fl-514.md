*[Parchment roll, the hymn written in alternating strophes for two choirs; the rubrics are in red. The roll was kept in the treasury of the river-temple.]*

*First choir:*

[[dei:6|Nili]], father of the river, who bore our fathers' boats  
to the bend where they laid the first stone of [[set:222|Ninxinwisnon]],  
twelve hundred years ago and one; receive this hymn.  
[[dei:8|Nixil]] of the open sky, who keeps the hours in order,  
[[dei:10|Masto]] of the sun, who weighs the harvest in his light,  
[[dei:7|Yüp]] of the hearth, whose flame is the city's oldest citizen:  
receive this hymn, each in your place, each with your due.

*Second choir:*

For it is not with one voice that a city is ruled,  
nor with one hand that a field is ploughed;  
the river does not do the work of the storm, nor the sun the work of the hearth.  
So say the priests of [[mov:1|Yacuam]], who learned it from their teachers,  
who learned it from theirs, in order, down the long stair of years.  
Many gods, each in their place: that is the city's wisdom, and the city stands.

*First choir:*

Yet we will not pretend the years were kind.  
[[dei:11|Nizhaxta]] of the spear, you turned your face from us at the [[evt:22442|Battle of Tuzhi]];  
you let the strangers come up to our own walls  
at the [[evt:22447|Battle of Ninshinuesnun]], where the smoke went up from the gardens;  
you let them take Shish, and at the [[evt:22452|Treaty of Shish]]  
our queen [[per:22078|Nixi]] set her seal to the loss of fields our grandfathers cleared.  
We do not hide this from you. You know it; you were there.

*Second choir:*

And yet the wheel turns, and what ends begins again.  
The queen bought peace at Tungaruikhrimu with no field given;  
fifteen years of quiet on that border, written at the [[evt:22456|Treaty of Tungaruikhrimu]].  
The granaries have filled since; the [[cur:512|Ilxezh]] has kept its full weight.  
Is that not the favour of the gods returning, slow as the river rises?

*First choir (softly):*

Now in the market, by the fountain of the potters,  
a young man of our own city stands and speaks.  
His name is [[per:22521|Ia Ximam]]; he was born among us; we do not curse him.  
He says that time runs not round but straight, like a road to a judge.  
He says the gods are servants, or shadows, of a single power.  
He says his word is for all peoples, and the young men listen.  
The scribes have entered it in the city's book: the [[evt:22743|Founding of the Ia Faith]].

*Second choir:*

Hear us, Ia Ximam, son of the city; we answer you in measure, not in anger.  
If the gods are shadows, who cast them? If the river is a servant, whose boats does it carry?  
A city that has one master has one door, and one door is soon shut.  
We were not lost at Ninshinuesnun for want of one power, nor shall we be saved by one;  
it was many hands at the walls, and many gods above them.  
Teach, then, if you must; the teachers of [[mov:80|Ia]] say no one owns the truth, so let them not claim to own ours.  
But let the city keep its altars, for the city has kept them twelve centuries.

*Both choirs:*

[[dei:9|Ximintlo]] of the storm, pass over our roofs.  
Nili, rise in your season. Masto, ripen the barley.  
Yüp, keep the hearth of [[pol:954|Ninxinwisnon]], as you have kept it, burning.  
Each in your place, each with your due: receive this hymn.

*[Rubric:]* Sung at the river-temple at the new year, in the year 1202 from the founding of Ninxinwisnon.
