title: Sermon of Nabandangi at the Gate of Adhgomgi

Hear me, people of [[pol:806|Adhgomgi]]. Hear me, potters and porters, widows and soldiers, you who sell oil and you who buy it. Thus speaks [[per:18640|Nabandangi]], a man of no great house, at the gate of [[set:49|Adhgomgi]], in the 14th year of [[per:18424|Nadazom]] the queen. I do not ask you for silver. Keep your silver. I ask you for your ears.

Two years have turned since the word was first spoken among you, since [[evt:18747|the Way was opened here in Adhgomgi]]. In those two years the priests have called me a madman, and a liar, and a thief of sacrifices. I am none of these. I am a man who looked inward, as any of you may look, and found a lamp burning there that no priest had lit.

Hear the first word. There is one power above all things. The gods you know are its servants. [[dei:28|Khodhaze]] is the roof of the house, but he is not the master of the house. [[dei:30|Pogazho]] is the lamp of the day, and [[dei:31|Khombe]] the lamp of the night, but a lamp does not light itself. [[dei:29|Bombe]] keeps the sea as a steward keeps a storehouse, for another. Honour them as you honour the steward. Do not mistake them for the lord.

Hear the second word. No man owns the truth. The priests of [[mov:4|Khopande]] say: the truth is handed down from priest to priest, as a jar is handed from the potter to the buyer, sealed. I say: break the seal and look. The lamp is in your own breast. Seek, and reason, and you will find. A potter may find it at his wheel. A widow may find it at her loom. It does not come only to those who have been anointed with oil.

Hear the third word. They tell you that prosperity is the sign of heaven's favour, that a full storehouse is the smile of the gods. Then look at the storehouses. Look at Shuthunile.

Nineteen years ago the drums beat against Lamlivtse. The young men marched out, and the priests read the livers and said: the omens are good. And [[evt:18461|that war]] came to nothing, and the drums fell silent, and nothing was won. Then the drums beat again, against the Fili. Three thousand of our sons went out to [[evt:18576|Shuthunile]] against two dozen men. Two dozen! And nearly five hundred of our sons came home on biers. The priests said: [[dei:32|Nizhinande]] is pleased. The priests said: the gods have given us land. By [[evt:18582|the tablet of Shuthunile]] the queen received the fields of the Fili, and I do not speak against the queen, who did what queens do. But I ask you: is bread cheaper in Adhgomgi? Still a [[cur:382|lash]] for a measure, as before. Is the widow's jar fuller? Is the soldier's mother comforted? If this is favour, what is wrath?

So I tell you: the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I do not say: run to the hills and eat roots. Keep your house. Keep your duty. Keep order in your street and honesty in your scales. Make right sacrifice. But the right sacrifice is not the fat of a bull burned on a stone while your neighbour's children go hungry. The right sacrifice is your appetite. Burn that. The smoke of it goes higher than any bull.

Hear the fourth word, and it is the hardest. This Way is not for Adhgomgi alone. It is not for our people alone. The priests say: our gods are for our people, and the stranger may keep his own. I say: the one power has no favourite people. The Fili whose fields we took have the same lamp in their breasts. The men of Lamlivtse have it. The merchant from over the sea has it. Go to them. Tell them. Do not burn their houses to teach them; [[evt:18554|the war against the Fili]] taught them nothing but grief. Sit at their fires and speak.

These are the four words of [[mov:61|Langikaf]]. I did not invent them. I found them, as you will find them, if you look.

The priests are coming now; I see their staffs above the crowd. Do not fight them. Go home in peace. Look inward tonight, before you sleep. That is all I ask, and it costs you nothing but your pride.
