*[Obverse.]*

To the overseer of the merchants of [[set:0|Tallin]], to the heads of the caravan-houses, to every man who weighs silver at the gate, say: thus says [[per:4011|Evenin]], king of [[pol:61|Tallin]], your lord.

With me it is not well. With my house it is not well. With the city it is not well. I will not write the words of greeting that the scribes write. Let the scribes write them in a better year.

Hear what has come upon us.

The sickness came up out of the marsh at the edge of the city. It came in the hot month, when the water stands green and the flies are thick. First the reed-cutters lay down. Then the potters by the low gate. Then the men of the market. They burn with fever, then they shake with cold, then they burn again. On the third day they do not know their wives. On the fifth day the wailing goes up from the house. That is the [[evt:3665|Marsh Pestilence]]. Your own eyes have seen it. Your own houses have heard the wailing.

Now hear what the runners tell me. They say it has gone out along the roads, along your roads, merchants, along the roads of the donkeys. It is in [[set:17|Pumi]]. It is in [[set:18|Lenunushal]]. It is in [[set:67|Duninga]] and in [[set:75|Sheney]] and in [[set:68|Poyonga]]. It has gone where the caravans went. It went in the bales of wool. It went in the jars of oil. It went on the backs of the drivers.

Was it the gods? I do not know. I am young; I have sat on the seat two years only. Last year at [[evt:3696|Shivange]] [[dei:14|Vulme]] went before me, and the field was mine, and I said: the gods love Tallin. This year at [[evt:3709|Shivange]] Vulme turned her face, and the field was the enemy's, and in the same year the marsh began to breathe death. Did we neglect the gods? I gave the bull. I gave the ram. I gave the first sheaf to [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]]. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] say: prosperity is a sign of the gods' favour. Then this is a sign also. I do not know what it signifies.

Some say the men of [[pol:40|Navnga]] did it. They say the enemy threw dead men into the marsh by night. I have heard it said in the market. I do not say it is true. But I say this: the runners say the sickness has gone into Navnga too, and that their villages wail as ours wail. If they sent it, it has gone home to them. So the gods answer those who poison water.

*[Reverse.]*

Now hear my word, and do it.

Shut the market. Shut it from this day until the new moon, and from the new moon until I send again. Let no caravan go out from the city. Let no caravan come in. If a caravan comes to the gate, let it camp outside, beyond the ditch, and let water be carried out to it, and let no one touch the bales.

Do not hide your sick. A house that hides its sick kills its neighbour. Bring them to the house of [[dei:18|Eva]], where the fire is kept; let the priests tend them. Burn the bedding of the dead. Burn it, though it is good wool. Wool can be bought again.

Do not raise the price of grain against the poor. I know your trade. I know that when the roads are shut, the merchant says, "Grain is scarce; the price is two [[cur:48|Menmu]]." I say: grain is not scarce in the storehouses of the king. I will open them. If any man sells grain above its price in this year, let his name be brought to me.

May [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the earth close her mouth. May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky send the cold wind that clears the marsh. May [[dei:19|Yenan]] keep the count of the months until this ends.

What ends will begin again, the priests say. I pray that this sickness is the thing that ends.

> Written in the 2nd year of Evenin, king in Tallin, in the year of the sickness.
