When the annalist of Myizinithie reached the peace of AY 4892, she gave it one line: "Envoys came and went; the Treaty of Myizinithie was sealed" (PAL·AN·00870). One of those envoys was [[per:49174|Tafununin]] of [[pol:1959|Yidhe]]; no writer of his age came nearer to noticing him. Outside the four treaties he drafted, his name survives only in the three words at the foot of each: "Drafted by Tafununin." A translator learns to read the foot of a page; this chapter reads his.

## What the record keeps

The record gives him a birth in 4854, a people, the Jego, a name meaning *bull fame*, and a natural death in 4922, at sixty-eight. It names no parent, wife or child, and says nothing of his schooling, face or place of death. He became envoy of Yidhe in 4874, aged twenty, under Alintin II the Just, and held it for forty-eight years, through the reigns of Khukhaalith and [[per:46334|Shifin]], fourteen years his junior. Seven years separate his appointment from his first surviving text; what he did in them, the record does not say.

His was an empire growing fast, governed from [[set:0|Taulin]] and dating its instruments by the era of the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] faith. Between the survey of the 4870s and that of the 4910s it grew from 182 districts and 46.1 million people to 256 districts and 81.3 million. Fifty-six of those districts came by his four treaties, every peace Yidhe made while he held office. He did not invent their form: his predecessor Baaraamadhin's Treaty of Luunge of 4861 (PAL·TR·02880) has the same first article, the same twenty-year truce, the same closing line naming its drafter. What was his is what went inside the frame.

## Riniy and Myizinithie

The [[evt:47225|Second Yidhe–Riniy War]] was no contest. At Thinirnge in 4881 Yidhe fielded 543,798 men against 19,299 and lost 378 to Riniy's 6,514; Riniy itself was then sacked. In the ruined city his text (PAL·TR·02896) took three districts and the town of Luluunul, an indemnity of 2,374,333 Zhidhul "in ten annual instalments", and a twenty-year truce. Khukhaalith signed for Yidhe; for Riniy, King Munin, who had signed Baaraamadhin's peace thirty-six years before.

Then the historians went to work. Khuthununin, writing in Taulin in 4904, made the cession "7 districts in all" (PAL·CH·08247). Bipavbukhin of Riniy wrote that "at the urging of the priests, the king agreed to terms", and that "Nothing of worth was lost" (PAL·CH·08248). All three give the same day, 24 Baadhu 2626; they agree on little else. Riniy collapsed in 4883 with its treasury in deficit; whether a single instalment was paid, the record does not say.

The [[evt:47340|Yidhe–Myizinithie War]] ended in 4892 with a harder instrument (PAL·TR·02906): nineteen districts and three towns, and an article by which Myizinithie "places its foreign relations under the protection of Yidhe." Khuthununin counted "35 districts in all" (PAL·CH·08269). Nitllu Ash of Myizinithie, writing in 4931, said that "wearied of bloodshed, the president granted peace", as if the beaten side had something to give, and that "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor" (PAL·CH·08270). That president, Iizhe, had signed away nineteen districts and the republic's foreign relations. Its surveyed stability never recovered; it collapsed in 4915.

## The emperor's pen

The [[evt:47536|Yidhe–Navnge War]] closed at Piyikhzhidhe in 4910 (PAL·TR·02915): eighteen districts with Binge and Rurin, treatment for Yidhe's goods and shipping "no less favourable than that accorded to any other nation", and the usual truce. Navnge's chronicler wrote only "The border was settled" (PAL·CH·08296). The Ode for the Fortieth Year of Shifin, printed in 4927 for schools, barracks and railway stations, gave the deed to the emperor: "at the Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe he set down his pen" (PAL·PO·00039). The pen was Tafununin's. The ode names battles, a colony and a currency; it does not name him.

What a cession meant on the ground survives in one lawsuit (PAL·LS·00784). In 4919, at Binge, Khufununin, son of Lonathbin, "formerly a subject of Navnge", asked for his father's 240 acres back. Counsel for the Crown answered that "The plaintiff did not swear allegiance within the year", a condition found nowhere in the treaty's four articles. The court let him keep the fields as a tenant of the crown at 28.3 [[cur:1121|Tafbaf]] a year, some twenty-eight measures of grain at the price the Taulin papers quoted in 4917.

## Two brothers at Himi

The last treaty was the barest. The [[evt:47633|Yidhe–Divigvivinge War]] brought the sack of Himi in 4918, its archives burned; when the city was dug in 4925 that year showed as "a thick layer of ash over collapsed walls" (PAL·EX·00002). The Treaty of Himi (PAL·TR·02918) took sixteen districts with Zhidhzhinge and Luunge, and a truce: no indemnity, no trade clause. Shifin signed for Yidhe; for Divigvivinge signed King Dulomin, who had commanded against Yidhe at Luunge the year before. The record shows what neither chronicle says: the two signatories were brothers, sons of Khukhaalith and Nathulnun II, whose marriage had itself been an article of the Treaty of Luunge. Khuthununin gave the cession as "32 districts in all" (PAL·CH·08305); Tunin of Divigvivinge, in 4944, used Myizinithie's stock phrase: "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor" (PAL·CH·08306). Eighteen years after Himi, on Dulomin's death, Shifin took his brother's crown as well.

Tafununin died in 4922; the next year Taalivaanavin held his office. None of his truces was broken within its twenty years, and Myizinithie and Navnge each collapsed within three years of theirs running out. I do not credit that quiet to his prose. In every war he closed, Yidhe lost far fewer than its enemy (1,724 against 8,595 in the first, 8,188 against 29,332 in the last); he wrote for the stronger side. Yet where the chroniclers' numbers fail, his hold. The court historian doubled his districts, the defeated waved them away, the poet gave his pen to the emperor; only the frontier agrees with the man who signed himself "Drafted by Tafununin" and nothing more.
