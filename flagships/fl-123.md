title: The Priest of Masto on the Years Without a Sun

I, a priest of the house of [[dei:10|Masto]] in [[set:26|Vami]], who have kept the morning fire for thirty years, set down what I saw of the darkened sky, in the 25th year of [[per:18453|Swilcuo]], queen of [[pol:717|Iti]], may Masto shine upon her. I set it down so that the priests who come after me will know the signs if the signs come again. For all things turn upon the great wheel, and what has been will be.

**The year of the veil.** In the 10th year of Swilcuo the queen, in the month of first barley, Masto rose red as a coal in the morning. At noon he was the colour of brass. At evening the whole west burned like a forge, and the people came out on the roofs to look, and the children were afraid. We thought it a sign of war, for in the year before our soldiers had won the field at [[evt:18660|Yyixi]], and men said the sky was rejoicing. It was not rejoicing.

Then a fine grey dust fell on the altar. I swept it with my own hand at the morning office, and the next morning it had fallen again. Travellers came from the side of Yimla. They said: a mountain has split open and burned; [[evt:18603|Fimiy has burst]], and the smoke of it stands up to heaven like a pillar, and then spreads out like a roof. So [[dei:8|Nixil]] the sky drew a veil across his face, and Masto behind the veil was a lamp seen through a curtain.

**The cold.** That summer the barley did not fill. The ears stood white and empty in the fields. Frost came in the month of threshing, which none of the old men had seen. [[dei:6|Nili]] the river ran low and grey. The next year it was the same, and the year after it was the same. The priests of [[mov:1|Yacuam]] gathered in the house of Masto and made sacrifice, a bull at the new moon and a ram at the full, and still the veil did not lift.

**The omens of war.** In the second year of the veil the army went out again, and at [[evt:18680|Tiaiosh]] it was turned back. Let no one blame the soldiers. They marched under a sky without shadows, and the omens at the gate were dark, for the liver of the ram was pale as clay, and [[dei:11|Nizhaxta]] would not show which way he faced. In the fourth year of the veil the queen herself went out. Before she marched she came to the house of Masto in the dark of the morning and poured oil on the fire with her own hands, and I stood beside her. The flame leapt up white, and I said: it is a good sign. At [[evt:18706|Yyixi]] she broke the enemy, and by [[evt:18707|the treaty of Yyixi]] they gave up land and silver to Iti. Thus the gods showed that they had not forgotten us, though they hid their faces.

**The hunger.** Yet the land they gave was grey with the ash, and the silver could not buy what the fields would not grow. In those years I saw a measure of grain sold for five [[cur:397|witlsa]] and more, where in my youth it cost one. Mothers in the lower city sold their bracelets for bread. The queen opened the granaries of the temple, and we gave what we had, and it was not enough. I buried many of my own congregation. I do not write their number; Masto knows it.

**This present year.** Fifteen summers now the veil has hung over us, through [[cli:113|the long winter]]. But this year, at midsummer, the shadow of the pillar in the court of Masto was sharp-edged for the first time since I was young, and the old priests say the veil is thinning. I do not say it is lifted. Grain is still dear. And the army has gone out again, [[evt:18831|against the Shetsi]], and we do not yet know how the gods will judge it.

Hear, you priests who come after. If Masto rises red, and the dust falls on the altar, do not say it is the sky rejoicing. Fill the granaries. Pour oil to [[dei:7|Yüp]] that the hearths may not go out, and pray to [[dei:9|Ximintlo]] that he may blow the veil away. And keep the morning fire, whatever the sky does. That is our duty. The wheel will turn.
