**LETTER FROM THE FRONT**  
**The President to the People of Mbilu**

*Received by wire at the Ministry of Information and printed by order in all the morning papers. Field headquarters, before Shivindua, 10 Mbive 4795 of the Uthungala.*

Fellow citizens,

I write to you not as your President but as one soldier among many, with mud on my boots and the smoke of Shivindua still in my coat. Let the clerks of the capital write the communiqués. You are owed a letter.

The war is won.

You will remember how it began. In the first days of the campaign your Government laid before the world the [[evt:47188|Mbilu Claim against Mana]], a claim moderate in its terms and just in its foundations, for the lands and markets that the authorities of [[pol:1974|Mane]] had left to rot under their neglect. They answered with insult. They answered with the rifle. So the [[evt:47189|Second Mbilu Colonial War in Mana]] was forced upon us, as every war has been forced upon a peaceful republic by those who mistake patience for weakness.

Twice I led our armies against them at [[set:64|Ini]].

At the first [[evt:47195|Battle of Ene]], last year, the enemy dug himself into the river line and fought, I will grant him this, with the courage of desperation. He was thrown out of every trench he held. His losses, in killed, wounded, captured and deserted, have never been honestly reported by his own ministers, and I do not believe they ever will be; but our officers who walked that ground after the battle did not think a single regiment of his came away whole.

We then sat down before Shivindua, in the [[evt:47196|Siege of Tivintukhe]], and through the winter we held him there like a fox in a hole.

This spring their general, [[per:46204|Vaèlumuga]], came out to break the ring. He came out to the same ground at Ini, as if he wished to die where his army had already died once. At the second [[evt:47202|Battle of Ene]] he had his wish. He was killed on the field among his staff, and his army ceased to exist as an army. I record that he did not run. It is the only good thing I can record of the cause he served.

Then the city fell, and you have read of the [[evt:47203|Eleventh Sack of Tivintukhe]]. You will read more, in the foreign papers, about the fire in the archives. I tell you plainly: the city was full of their soldiers in civilian dress, and their incendiaries were in the record office before ours were in the street. Men who burn their own history to deny it to the victor have told you everything about themselves.

The [[evt:47204|Treaty of Tivintukhe]] was signed in the ruins, and I signed it with my own hand. Mane cedes to the Republic the districts in dispute. Mane pays to the Republic an indemnity of 27,346, to be lodged with the Treasury. Mane binds itself to twenty years of peace. These are not the terms of revenge. They are the terms of order.

I will not pretend that victory was cheap. Our own casualty lists are long, and the Ministry will publish them in full, as a republic must. Every name on them is a name the nation owes. But I ask you to weigh them against what we have bought: new soil under the flag of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]], new trade for every factory between here and the capital, and an enemy who will not soon trouble our children.

The teachers of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] say that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. Then let us be prosperous, and let us be just in our dealings with the people we now govern, so that the favour is deserved.

I will be home before the harvest. Keep the price of bread down while I am gone; I am told it is near six [[cur:1094|Dathu]] the measure, and that is a scandal in a victorious country.

Your servant and fellow soldier,

[[per:46223|Nakhumbèn]]  
*President of the Republic, Commander in the Field*
