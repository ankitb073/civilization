title: What the Spearman Saw in the Navnga War
---
I am a man of [[pol:61|Tallin]], a spearman of the old host, the son of a potter of the lower town. Now I am old. Now my knees are bad and my hand shakes on the stylus. Hear what I saw, you who come after. Hear it before [[dei:19|Yenan]] the moon takes my eyes.

In the days of our fathers the men of [[pol:40|Navnga]] came against us. That was [[evt:2074|the war of Navnga against Tallin]]. They took tribute from us. They took our barley, they took our silver, they took our oxen. Our fathers bowed their heads. The old men told it at the gate, and we children heard it.

Then the queen [[per:3573|Shijilialath]], the great lady, said: "The land of [[reg:16|Yida]] is ours. Its fields are ours, its waters are ours. Go down and take it." So began [[evt:3676|the war of Tallin against Navnga]]. We gave a bull to [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear. We poured beer to Vinba of the earth. The priests read the liver, and the liver was good.

At [[evt:3691|Rile]] the queen went down with us. The men of Navnga were as many as locusts; they were two for each one of us. [[per:4008|Rakathren]] led them. We were pressed back to the river. I do not say we were beaten. I say the locusts were many. In the next year the queen went to her mothers, and the king [[per:4011|Evenin]] sat on the high seat.

At [[evt:3696|Shivange]] the king led us. Dulomin, king of Navnga, led them, and again they were more than we. But Vulme stood at our right hand. We broke their line at midday. The dead of Navnga lay in the field like sheaves after the sickle. Seven thousand, the scribes said. I believe it. I walked among them.

In the next year at Shivange we went again, under [[per:3889|Pavbuman]] the general. We were few; the ground was soft; a man of the host had stolen from the shrine of Eva, and the fire god turned away. We went back to the hills. It was the will of the gods, not the strength of Navnga.

At [[evt:3713|Taulin]], before the walls of [[set:0|Tallin]], Pavbuman stood. [[per:3868|Dulomin]] came with a great host to look upon our city. Our women stood on the walls; our children stood on the walls. We came down on him like a storm out of the sky. Of my company one man in four died. Of his, a field full. Dumamnul saw it from heaven.

At Nilinge Pavbuman stood against Rakathren, and Navnga fled. Then at Rile again, where the queen had been pressed, the king Evenin stood, and Navnga had the more men, and Navnga held the field at evening. I do not call it a defeat. We were not broken.

At [[evt:3760|Vuvu]] the last fight was fought. The host of Navnga was very great. Pavbuman held the line until the sun went down, and then we drew back. In that same year the two kings met at Vuvu. Evenin and Dulomin ate the salt; they swore by their gods. So was [[evt:3761|the peace of Vuvu]] sworn: each kept what he held. Nine years I had carried the spear.

Hear me. Navnga took tribute from our fathers, and in my war Navnga took no tribute from us. Their dead were more than our dead. Their king did not see the inside of Tallin. Is that not a victory? The young men laugh. Let them laugh.

After those days the king Evenin went out again, and Linnshal fell, and [[evt:3871|Lenunushal was taken]]. I was old then; my son went. He came home with a bronze cup. Then the king Evenin went to his fathers.

Now [[per:4163|Pimkultuth]] sits on the high seat; this is her second year. May Nidhikhti fill her granaries. May Vulme guard her spear. May Yenan watch over the old men who sit by the gate and tell what they saw.

I have written it. I have set it down in clay. Let no man say it was not so.
