*A song made by [[per:24312|Alinurin]], daughter of the queen, for the second turning of the year since her marriage, and sung in the hall of [[set:45|Nunnga]] in the 9th year of [[per:24246|Teyimith]].*

**I.**

Sing, maidens of [[pol:1054|Tunnga]], sing as you sang two summers past,  
when the torches went down to the river and the river gave back their light;  
sing, young men of [[pol:1057|Virrinu]], who came with your lord across the ford,  
and let the old men listen, for the old men remember why we sing.

Once there was a single realm between the hills and the water,  
and when it broke, as the potter's jar breaks, into many shards,  
each shard said to the others, "I alone am the jar."  
This is the way of shards. It is not the way of the wise.

In the [[evt:24349|Collapse of the Turamtu State]] our grandfathers wept,  
and in the [[evt:24352|Rise of Tunnga]] our mothers took heart again;  
but what is broken asunder may yet be bound with a cord,  
and the cord that binds two houses is not iron, but a vow.

**II.**

O [[dei:141|Navthin]], river that runs between our lands and belongs to neither,  
you carried his boat to our shore on the morning of the vow.  
O [[dei:139|Nithikhti]], keeper of the sheaf — forgive me, I do not know your heart, only your harvest —  
you heaped the wedding table with bread that the poor shared too.  
O [[dei:138|Tuel]], moon of that night, you were full, and the priests said it was well;  
O [[dei:136|Dueminu]], lord of storms, you held your breath, and the sky was clear.

**III.**

My mother the queen, Teyimith, stood at the head of the hall.  
She did not weep, for a queen does not weep before her lords;  
but afterward, in the inner room, she held my face in her hands  
and said, "You are a bridge now. Do not let them walk on you."

I was fifteen summers old. I did not understand her.  
I understand her a little better now.

**IV.**

And you, [[per:24317|Vuitathvathen]], son of the house across the river,  
you who were shy at the feast and bold in the morning,  
you who sent back the dowry-cattle saying, "I did not come for oxen" —  
was that wisdom, or pride? I have decided it was wisdom.

They say a marriage between houses is a treaty written in flesh.  
Let it be so. But a treaty is kept by fear of breaking it,  
and a marriage is kept by the wish to keep it. In this,  
O lords of both banks, a marriage is the better kind of law.

**V.**

Two summers now. The fields of both banks have been sown and reaped.  
No spear has crossed the ford. The traders cross it freely.  
Is that my doing? No. But I will not pretend it is no one's.

So I give thanks in the right order, as the priests of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]] have taught:  
first to the gods, each in their place and with their due;  
then to my mother, who made the match with a steady hand;  
then to the house of Virrinu, which sent us its best;  
and last to [[per:24317|Vuitathvathen]], my husband, who is not a god,  
and who will laugh when he hears this song, and then ask to hear it again.

**VI.**

Sing, maidens. Sing, young men. Let the cup go round once more.  
Let [[dei:140|Vinba]] of the earth keep the roots of both our houses deep;  
let [[dei:137|Shyethartsi]], who keeps the house below, wait long for us both.  
Let the river run between us, and the bridge run over it,  
and let our children, if the gods send children, not know which bank is theirs.

*Here ends the song. The lords of both banks answered: "So be it."*
