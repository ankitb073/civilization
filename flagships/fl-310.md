*[Obverse. To be sung at the house of Vulme, with drum and reed-pipe.]*

Praise [[dei:14|Vulme]], lord of the bronze, whose spear is never dull,  
praise Vulme, who walks before the king, whose shield is the morning.  
Praise [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the high sky, who sees the field from end to end.  
Praise [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the black earth, who drinks what the spear pours out.  
Praise them, O [[pol:10|Lenunushal]], praise them, city of the wheel.  

The men of [[pol:12|Jinontonu]] held the land of [[reg:9|Tupujinga]],  
they held the pasture, they held the water, they held the barley-ground.  
They did not give the gods of Lenunushal their due.  
They did not give the king of Lenunushal his due.  
Then the king lifted his hand to Vulme. Then the spears went down.  

**First field.**  
At [[evt:537|Dininge]], by the walls of [[set:67|Duninga]],  
[[per:712|Baluthmanen]] of the long arm stood against [[per:504|Dolulrinin]].  
Baluthmanen gave the bull at dawn; Vulme took the bull.  
Dolulrinin gave nothing; Vulme turned his face from him.  
More than four hundred of Jinontonu lay down in the barley.  
The field was Lenunushal's. Say it, singer: the field was Lenunushal's.  

**Second field.**  
At [[evt:543|Dininge]] a second time, in the next year, at the cutting of the grain,  
Baluthmanen stood again, and against him [[per:718|Lomidominin]].  
Lomidominin was proud; his spears were fewer than ours.  
Fewer were his spears, and fewer when the sun went down.  
More than five hundred of Jinontonu lay down by the ditch.  
The field was Lenunushal's. Say it, singer: the field was Lenunushal's.  

**Third field.**  
At [[evt:552|Dininge]] the third time, in the third summer,  
the king himself went down, [[per:704|Bonafin]], shepherd of Lenunushal.  
Bonafin girded the bronze; Bonafin took the spear in his hand.  
Lomidominin stood against him a second time; Lomidominin did not stand long.  
Hard was the day; the dust went up to Dumamnul; our dead were not few.  
But the men of Jinontonu broke, the men of Jinontonu ran,  
their dead were more than ours, their wailing was louder than ours.  
The field was Lenunushal's. Say it three times: the field was Lenunushal's.  

Three fields, three summers, three times Dininge.  
Three times Vulme went before us. Three times Vinba drank.  
Fifteen hundred of Jinontonu went down into the earth;  
for each of ours that fell, two of theirs and more than two.  

Then Bonafin sat down at Duninga, and Dolulrinin sat down before him.  
Dolulrinin bowed his head; Dolulrinin set his seal for Jinontonu.  
They swore by the gods of the sky, they swore by the gods of the earth.  
That is the oath of [[evt:553|Dininge]]. Let no man break it.  
The barley-ground of Tupujinga is ours; its water is ours.  
The spears were laid down. The ox went back to the plough.  

**Offerings.**  
For Vulme, a white bull. For Vinba, a black ewe.  
For [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] of the grain, the first sheaf of the conquered field.  
For Eva of the fire, oil and fat upon the altar.  
For [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, a lamp that does not go out.  
For the house of the gods, silver: let it be weighed in [[cur:9|Nivba]],  
let it be weighed before the priests, let it be weighed in order.  

What ends will begin again; the wheel turns, say the priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]].  
The wheel turned for Lenunushal. The wheel turned in its season.  
Prosperity is the sign of the gods; see, the gods have given it.  
Remember Dininge. Remember Duninga. Remember the three fields.  

*[Reverse, lower edge.]*

> Written in the 4th year of Bonafin, king of Lenunushal.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Vulme break his spear.
