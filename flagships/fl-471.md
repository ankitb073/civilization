*Extracts from the private diary of [[per:45504|Nazhe]], kept during his service at the court of [[pol:1826|Luthe]], as afterwards printed by his own direction.*

**16 Guvu 4703.** Rain since dawn. The post from the river provinces has not come in since midnight; the clerk says the riders are stopped on the road, and the clerk is not a man given to drama. At the morning levee there were eleven ministers present out of twenty-two. The others sent notes pleading illness.

The King is seven years old. He sat on the great chair with his feet not touching the step, and turned the pages of a picture-book while the Chamberlain read out the day's petitions. I do not blame the boy. It is not his fault that he was crowned in his cradle, nor that the men who govern in his name have governed so badly. But I find I can no longer pretend that a child with a picture-book is an absolute monarch, and I suspect the city has stopped pretending too.

**17 Guvu 4703.** Bread riots in the lower town. The price of a measure of grain has passed three [[cur:1078|Khimbe]], which for a labourer is a day's wage and more. The garrison was ordered out at noon. By two o'clock half of it had gone over to the crowd; I watched from the palace roof as a company of infantry laid down its muskets in the great square and was cheered for it.

Old soldiers in the crowd still wear the ribbons of the [[evt:46042|Battle of Kavu]] and the [[evt:46055|Battle of Kavu]] the year after, and of the [[evt:46056|Treaty of Kavu]], sixteen years ago, when we were proud of ourselves. I saw one of them, a grey-headed sergeant, standing on a cart with a bundle of handbills. They said: THE CROWN IS STOLEN GOODS.

That is the heart of it, and every courtier knows it, though none will say it at table. The house that sits on the throne of Luthe sits there by the [[evt:45859|Usurpation of Nalale]], and it has never been able to make men forget it. A crown obtained by force can be held by force; but the force has just laid down its muskets in the square.

In the evening a delegation came to my rooms: three members of the old Assembly, two officers, and a printer. They asked whether I would lead them. I asked for the night to think.

**18 Guvu 4703.** I have not slept. I will set down what was done today, plainly, so that no one may say afterwards that I hid it.

At eight I went to the Council Chamber with the delegation and some forty officers. The ministers who were present did not resist. The Chamberlain wept. I told them that the absolute power of the crown was ended, that a republic would be proclaimed at noon, and that no man would be harmed who did not take up arms against it. Not one of them took up arms.

At ten I went to the nursery. [[per:45439|Nashinzhè]] was having his breakfast. He asked me whether the soldiers were going to shoot him. I told him no, and I gave him my word, and I mean to keep it. He will leave [[set:25|Khavu]] tomorrow by the morning coach, with his household and his tutors, for a house in the country. He will grow up a private citizen, and perhaps a happier one than he would have been as king.

At noon, from the balcony of the palace, the Republic was proclaimed. I read the declaration myself. The crowd in the square was larger than any I have seen. When I reached the words "the people of Luthe are sovereign," the noise was such that I could not go on for some minutes.

This evening the provisional council named me its president. I did not refuse. I do not know whether that was duty or vanity. The faith of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] teaches that every seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason, and that justice in dealings is required of all men; I pray that it is the first and not the second that guided me, and I know that the final judgement will tell me which.

The [[evt:46212|Luthe Revolution]] is one day old. Bread must be in the bakeries of [[set:25|Khavu]] within two days, or it will not live to be three. I go now to see the grain merchants.
