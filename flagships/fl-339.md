*[Obverse]*

O [[dei:15|Winba]], mother of the ground, who keeps the dead. O [[dei:16|Dumamnul]], father of the sky, who saw it all from above. O [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, who saw it by night. You saw it. I saw it. Let the tablet see it also, and keep it.

In the third year of [[per:268|Lathilat]], queen of [[pol:11|Poyonga]], the lady of the high gate, I write what my eyes saw in her second year, in the year that is past, when the men of [[pol:1|Tallin]] came into the city.

In that year the queen went out to Vuvu. She went out at the head of the host, with her bronze cap, with the standard of [[dei:14|Wulme]] before her. Fifteen hundred went out with her, and more. The men of Tallin were more than five thousand; they were like the reeds of the marsh. The queen fought. Nearly four hundred of ours lay down at Vuvu. The men of Tallin also lay down, but not many of them, far fewer than ours. And the field was theirs. That was the [[evt:264|Battle of Vuvu]]. The queen came back to the city with the remnant. Her face was dust. She did not weep before the people.

Then the men of Tallin came to the walls of [[set:68|Poyonga]]. They came by the barley road. They burned the barley. They cut the orchards. They filled the canal with earth. They sat before the gate, and they beat on the gate with a ram of cedar, and the gate groaned like an ox.

*[Reverse]*

On the day the gate fell, I was in the court of the house of Ewa, sweeping the ashes of the morning fire. I heard the gate fall. I heard a sound like the sea. Then the men of Tallin were in the streets.

I saw them take the silver from the house of weighing, the [[cur:1|Inet]] in its baskets, the rings, the bars. I saw them take the oil jars. I saw them take the oxen of the temple. I saw them drive the young men out of the gate with their hands bound. I saw an old man of the potters' street struck down at his own door, and his wife put dust on her head. I saw a child sit by a broken jar and not cry.

I hid in the dark of the storeroom with the grain of [[dei:17|Nithikhuti]]. Three days I hid. On the fourth day it was quiet. The house of the tablets was not burned; the scribes had shut its door and sat behind it, and the men of Tallin did not care for clay. The tablets of our fathers are whole. I give thanks to Ewa that the fire did not go there.

This was the [[evt:265|Second Sack of Poynge]]. Let it be counted. Let it be remembered. Let the sons of Poyonga say it at the threshold.

*[Lower edge]*

Now the queen sits in the gate again. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] have washed the altars. They say: all things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The walls are mended with new brick. The [[evt:259|Second Tallin–Poyonga War]] is not finished; the wheel has not stopped. May Wulme lift her spear for Poyonga. May Winba give back our barley. May Lathilat live.

I have written it. My hand is tired. My eyes saw it.
