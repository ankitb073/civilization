*[Obverse.]*

*One mother:* How many went out?  
*All the mothers:* Twenty and eight. Twenty and eight went out with the king.  

*One mother:* How many came back?  
*All the mothers:* Twenty and two. Twenty and two came back to us.  

*One mother:* And the six?  
*All the mothers:* The six lie by [[set:240|Tiaiosh]]. The six lie in the field.  

Hear us, gods of the field and the house, each in your place, each with your due.  
We do not know your secret names. The priests of [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]] keep them.  
Hear us by the names you have. Hear the mothers.  

In the year of the [[evt:15001|Revolt of Yimla]] the men of the town broke the tally-sticks.  
They threw the tribute-jars of [[pol:652|Yisamashtsi]] into the river.  
They said: [[reg:14|Yimla]] is for Yimla. They said: we will have our own king.  
They raised [[per:14919|Amötli]] on the shields. He was a man of our town.  
He drank at our wells. He knew our sons by their names.  
So began the [[evt:15002|First Yimla War of Independence]].  

*All the mothers:* We were glad. We hung flowers on the gate. We were glad.  

*[Reverse.]*

In the second year of Amötli the men of Yisamashtsi came.  
They came up the road to Tiaiosh like the river in the spring,  
many as the reeds, many as the sheep of the high pastures,  
with bronze on their heads and bronze on their breasts.  
Their captain was [[per:14934|Shakhi Shimkham]], a hard man, a grey man.  

And Amötli went out to meet them, and the men of the villages of Yimla with him, three hundred and more.  
Twenty and eight went out with him from our town.  
Twenty and eight, our sons, the young men of the town,  
with their fathers' spears and their mothers' bread in a bag.  
We said: do not go, they are like the reeds. They said: we go with the king.  

In the [[evt:15014|Battle of Tiaiosh]] they stood in the field below the town,  
and they fought, and men of Yisamashtsi lay down before them.  
But the reeds do not end. Cut one, there is another.  
And six of ours lay down in the field, and fifty more of the village men beside them, and the rest came back to the walls,  
and the king came back to the walls, and did not speak.  

The field is theirs. The war is not ended. We do not know how it ends.  
The priests say: be moderate in grief, as in all things. The wise do not hold too hard.  
We are mothers. We do not know how to hold less hard.  

*One mother:* What did my son take with him?  
*All the mothers:* A spear. A bag of bread. His father's cloak.  
*One mother:* What did he leave me?  
*All the mothers:* His sandals by the door. His bowl. His name.  

We wash the six. We cannot wash them; they are in the field.  
We wash their sandals, then. We wash their bowls. We say their names.  
Twenty and two are home. Twenty and two eat at our fires.  
We are glad of the twenty and two. We weep for the six.  

*One mother:* How many went out?  
*All the mothers:* Twenty and eight. Count them with us. Count them every year.
