No king chooses the epithet "the Last". It is given afterwards, by people who know how the story ended, and it tends to swallow everything else about a life. [[per:27078|Eshueshfoyatl]] reigned over [[pol:922|Ana]] for forty-two years, from AY 3044 to 3086. He commanded his armies in person and won every battle he led. He swore two victorious treaties, and he outlived his own empire by six years. This chapter tries to give him back the parts of his life that his epithet has hidden, without pretending that the ending did not happen.

## Heir to a long house

He was born in AY 3016, a Lizo of the house that the usurper A'initlue'e I had founded nearly ninety years before. His name means "swift reed". His father, [[per:26935|Ba'aliuye'an]], had come to the throne in 3021 and reigned for twenty-three years. His sarcophagus lid in the royal tombs, the Epitaph of Ba'aliuye'an (PAL·IN·05060), names him simply as "son of Ma'she Tlie". The grandfather was the great king of that line, and the family's sense of itself rested on him.

The [[evt:27573|Accession of Eshueshfoyatl]] in 3044, after his father's natural death, gave a man of twenty-eight an empire of some 380 districts and twenty-two million people. Its army numbered over four hundred thousand. The record also allows us to see something that the court did not advertise: the measure of order within the realm had stood low for a generation, through the whole of his father's reign. He inherited an empire that was large, rich and already loosely held together.

## The war of equals

His first war was his own, and he fought it himself. The [[evt:27600|First Ana–Eshueethie War]] (3048–3051) was an attack on the [[pol:1122|Eshueethie]] empire, a power large enough to field armies of sixty and seventy thousand. Eshueshfoyatl commanded in all three of its battles. At the [[evt:27608|Battle of Tunavükhini (AY 3049)]] his 248,298 men beat 71,284 under [[per:27209|Ipil Toa]], and the losses were nearly equal, 22,002 against 21,452. At the [[evt:27627|Battle of Shidyun (AY 3050)]] he beat the same general again and lost fewer men than the enemy. At the [[evt:27631|Battle of Tiaiosh (AY 3051)]] his 294,704 broke Ipil Toa's 56,552 at a cost of 31,891 dead. His army then besieged the enemy capital.

The [[evt:27633|Treaty of Ishwyithie (AY 3051)]] was sworn in that capital, [[set:3|Ishwyithie]], with King [[per:26829|Pitselitluee]]. It gave Ana thirty-six districts and five towns, with ten years of tribute. No treaty of his house had taken so much at once. By the record's count the empire held some three million more people in 3059 than it had in 3049. The war had been a hard one, 67,548 dead for Ana and 60,733 for Eshueethie, but it was a contest between equals, won in the field by the king himself.

His basalt stele at Tiaiosh, the Victory stele of Eshueshfoyatl: Battle of Tiaiosh (PAL·IN·02046), claims 52,300 enemy dead against the record's 21,776. By the standards of royal monuments that is almost modest.

## The trade war and the stone at Ove

The next war came to him. In 3056 the kingdom of [[pol:1110|Tsiy]] attacked over the markets of Mii, and the [[evt:27676|Third Mii Trade War]] was fought by a general of the king. At Ove in 3057 an Ana army of 279,254 defeated an invader of 72,764, but lost 45,448 men to the enemy's 20,547. The king raised a diorite stele there, the Victory stele of Eshueshfoyatl: Battle of Ove (PAL·IN·02053), claiming 161,600 enemy dead, eight times the truth, for a battle he had not fought. The [[evt:27699|Treaty of Ove (AY 3059)]], sworn at [[set:260|Ove]] with [[per:27114|Ha]] of Tsiy, brought an indemnity and trading rights. Ana had won, but it had lost more men than the invader, 72,505 against 57,034.

The two steles divide his reign. The first exaggerates a real victory of his own. The second turns a costly defence led by someone else into a royal triumph. After Tiaiosh in 3051 the king never again appears in the record as a commander.

## The long unravelling

From 3059 to 3086 Ana swore no more treaties. A holy war in 3065 against a short-lived empire that rose out of Tsiy's civil war ended in nothing. So did a war in 3075–3076 against Shetsi, a state carved out of Eshueethie. Its king, Esie Ibe, wrote to Eshueshfoyatl in the formal language of royal brotherhood: "With you, your wives, your sons, your chariots and your land, may all go very well." Then he came to the point: "Your herdsmen graze their flocks on my side of the boundary stones… If you will not answer, let the sword answer." The letter (PAL·LT·01325) is a reminder of how formula can mislead. The record knows of no wife and no son of Eshueshfoyatl. The wishes were addressed to a household that did not exist, and the absence of an heir was itself a political fact.

The figures for these decades are stark. Over the 3060s and 3070s the army grew by half, to more than half a million men. Between 3050 and the collapse the record's measure of the realm's cohesion fell from weak to almost nothing. A state was paying more soldiers and commanding fewer loyalties. In 3083 Ana went to war with Eshueethie once more. At Edeye'a in 3084 a royal general led 361,382 men against 53,029 and lost. More than a hundred thousand of Ana's soldiers died in that one battle. A victory the next year changed nothing. In AY 3086 the empire of [[pol:922|Ana]] dissolved into six successor states, each with a capital of its own.

## Six years after

Eshueshfoyatl did not die with his state. He lived until AY 3092 and died of natural causes at seventy-six. The record gives him no office after the collapse and no place of death. He had no heir, and the house of the sword raven, which had held Ana for a hundred and fifty-eight years, ended with him. His old enemy Eshueethie outlasted him, but only until 3109.

"The Last" is an accurate word and an unfair one. The record shows a capable young commander who won the hardest war of his house in person and then, for reasons it does not explain, left the field to others. It shows a king who took credit he had not earned, as nearly every king did, and an old man presiding over a realm whose bonds were dissolving faster than any army could hold them. The collapse had causes larger than [[per:27078|Eshueshfoyatl]]. But for thirty years he did nothing in the record to slow it.
