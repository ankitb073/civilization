**A DEFENCE OF THE TEACHING CALLED BANGEKHULU**

*Against the Censure lately published by the Elders of the Vimekhulu; humbly addressed to His Imperial Majesty's Council at Dagafu, and to all readers of good faith. By [[per:43655|Navande]], minister of the word at [[set:163|Mbilu]]. Copied at Mbilu, 12 Nigu 4483 of the Uthungala.*

**To the Reader.**

A year ago, in the meeting-house at Mbilu, I said aloud what I had for many years said only to my own congregation; and before the month was out the Elders of the [[mov:70|Vimekhulu]] had declared me severed from their communion, and the gazettes had given the affair a name: the [[evt:44194|Bangekhulu Schism]]. I did not choose the word *schism*, nor do I accept it. But since it has been set down in writing, I must answer it in writing, and I do so here in seven articles, as briefly as a contested matter permits.

**Article I. That we have not departed from the One.** The Elders accuse us of novelty. I reply that the [[mov:157|Bangekhulu]] confess, as the Vimekhulu confess, one divine Power over all things, of whom the old gods are at best servants. On this, the chief matter, there is no quarrel at all. Let the Censure say so plainly, or let it show the line in our confession where we have said otherwise.

**Article II. Wherein we differ.** We differ in this only: the Elders teach that the good life is the life of duty, order and right sacrifice, and they have built upon that teaching a calendar of offerings so crowded that a labourer cannot keep it without neglecting his trade. We teach that what the One requires of us is justice in our dealings and moderation in all things. A false weight in the market is a greater offence than a missed offering at the altar; a debt honestly paid is a better sacrifice than a bullock. If this is heresy, it is a heresy that every honest merchant in Mbilu already practises.

**Article III. That we retain the ministry.** It is said that we have thrown off the ordained ministers. We have not. Our ministers are ordained by the laying on of hands, as before; we have only forbidden them to sell what they are given freely to dispense.

**Article IV. Of riches.** Here the Elders and I agree, and I wish they would remember it: the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I have taken nothing from my congregation but my bread. The Censure was copied, I am told, on fine paper and at no small cost in [[cur:923|Ngathe]]. I leave the reader to compare.

**Article V. That the teaching is for all peoples.** We send our word to every nation, as the Vimekhulu have always done. We do not withdraw from the world to make a sect; we go out into it, to the docks and the barracks, to the new provinces annexed since the [[evt:44028|Conquest of Dambulu]], to anyone who will listen.

**Article VI. Of our loyalty to the State.** I am aware that this Defence will be read by His Majesty's Council, and that in a time of war any quarrel among believers may be taken for disorder. Let me therefore state without reserve: the Bangekhulu are loyal subjects of [[pol:1670|Dagafu]] and of His Imperial Majesty [[per:43607|Nangandumbe]]. We honour the [[mov:24|Uthungala]], which is the faith of the Empire, and we have never sought to trouble it; we are a dispute within a household, not an assault upon the street. In the [[evt:44126|Holy War of Dagafu against Kankush]] our young men serve in the ranks with the rest, and our congregations pray for the Empire's arms every seventh day. If the Elders wish to call that sedition, let them say so to the recruiting officer.

**Article VII. Conclusion.** I ask nothing of the Elders but that they read what we teach and not what they fear we teach. I ask nothing of the Council but that it permit us to meet, to preach and to pray, as the Vimekhulu do. And I ask of every reader only that he ask himself, before he condemns us, which pleases the One more: a full altar, or an honest scale.

*Given at Mbilu, in the year 4483 of the Uthungala.*

**NAVANDE**
