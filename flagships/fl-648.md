In AY 4944 the excavator Nambili cut trenches across the palace quarter of Nisshudi and found three victory steles, set up by rulers who had beaten the city within a single decade. I translated two of them, and only afterwards noticed that neither names the woman it was raised against. The queen of Pviyo's stele says she "defeated Nithtsokuti in Nithtsokuti", as though nobody had ruled there.

That is the condition of [[per:10658|Sim'atlue'e]], whom the record calls the Unfortunate. The record knows a historian of [[pol:461|Nistlo'ti]], Mi'sönieme (AY 1480–1537), but not one tablet of his survives. Everything written of her within living memory came from the enemies who destroyed her. In their tablets she is Thimkatsveke. Her own name meant *eagle raven*.

## A daughter of a shrinking house

She was born in AY 1460 into the royal house of Waga, whose capital was Bakh. Her grandmother Nueza'obi reigned from 1413 to 1476. Her mother, [[per:10037|Ma'she Sim'a II]], later called the Brave, followed her on the throne; the mother's name meant *Ten Eagle*. Her father, Mi'i'obi, died when she was thirteen. She had two elder brothers, Pi'efoyatl and [[per:10657|Ueda]]. Waga held 62 districts in the survey of the 1460s and 45 in that of the 1470s, and the whole loss was one rebellion: in 1477, when she was seventeen, seventeen districts broke away as Nistlo'ti, with Nisshudi as their capital.

A letter from her mother to the rebel king Tli'uammo survives. It calls him "my brother", the form that crowns used with one another; the record knows of no kinship between them. Its business is blunt: "Your herdsmen graze their flocks on my side of the boundary stones. Keep them on your side, or I will drive them off with spears."

In 1480, at the First Battle of Bakh, her mother stood with 2,378 men against 13,298 from Tantethi, and she was killed. Waga lost 615 men, Tantethi 118. The Tantethi chronicler adds that "her head was brought to the queen". Waga's own chronicle, written at Bakh in 1490, omits the battle; in it the dead queen lives on to make the peace: "at the urging of the priests, the queen granted peace". Of the terms it says: "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor." The Treaty of Bakh in fact gave up nine districts, two towns and thirty-seven years of tribute. Her eldest brother died that year of natural causes. In 1481 Ueda took the throne and put down the first Nistlo'ti, with an amnesty.

By then Sim'atlue'e had a husband, I'pil Foyatl, two years her elder, and a daughter and a son born in 1479. The record keeps nothing more of the marriage.

## The second Nistlo'ti

In 1483, at twenty-three, she claimed her brother's throne and split the realm. The [[evt:10461|Secession of Nistlo'ti]] took the same province again. Sixteen of the seventeen districts that Ueda had recovered two years earlier went with her, and she ruled them from [[set:225|Nisshudi]]. The town had been founded by Waga in 1444 and became a rebel capital that same year. In 1447 its second king, the infant Tlamfin Mish II, was executed at the age of two. His damaged epitaph came out of the same excavation as the steles.

The record does not say why she rose, though among its causes it lists the sack of Bakh in 1480 and the rising of 1477. Her faith, [[mov:0|La'im]], held that prosperity was a sign of divine favour. A house that had lost a war, a queen and a queen's head could be read as a house the gods had abandoned. I offer that as a reading, not a finding. Her actions are clearer: she founded a dynasty in her own name rather than her mother's, and marched against her brother.

At the [[evt:10494|Battle of Tunavükhini (AY 1485)]] she attacked Ueda in person. She had 1,615 men to his 2,875, and she lost 280 to his 213. In 1486 she went for Bakh itself, where her mother had fallen; the veteran general [[per:10598|Yam'i]] met her 1,059 men with 2,015. She lost 72, he 26.

## Three wars at once

In 1485 two neighbours also went to war with her: [[pol:449|Kvile]] over trade, and [[pol:429|Pviyo]] to exact tribute. Each chronicle reverses the facts. Kvile's, written in 1498, says she, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Kvile". Pviyo's, from 1499, reports "word that Thimkatsveke of Nithtsokuti had gathered an army against Pviyo". In the record, Kvile and Pviyo were the attackers.

They inflate the numbers too. At the [[evt:10505|Battle of Tunavükhini (AY 1486)]], the young king [[per:10561|Valuthlulrinen]] brought 4,589 men against her 1,127. She lost 348, he 95. His chronicle gives her 1,965 men and 590 dead, and his rock relief raises the dead to 1,300. At the [[evt:10513|Battle of Nisshudi (AY 1487)]] she had 702 men against 799 under [[per:10734|Dingovoyen]]. It was her most even fight, and she lost 166 men to his 49. The stele of Pviyo's queen [[per:10341|Dodshoyath]], which I translated, says: "She killed 900 soldiers." Pviyo's chronicle says 617. The town was sacked. Nambili's Stratum V, "burnt timbers and scattered arrowheads", belongs to that year. Two queens then sealed the [[evt:10515|Treaty of Nisshudi (AY 1487)]]. It took six districts and ninety kilograms of silver a year for thirty-nine years, with trading rights and a ten-year truce. The chronicle says Nistlo'ti "bowed to the feet of the queen".

Her best day came later that year, at the [[evt:10516|Battle of Tunavükhini (AY 1487)]]. Outnumbered nearly four to one, she lost 70 men to Kvile's 39. Kvile's court annals leave the battle out.

That same year the [[evt:10462|Sixth Waga Civil War]] ended without a settlement, because [[pol:407|Waga]] itself had ceased to exist. Ueda, "the Last", lost his throne, and a new king, [[per:10728|Ina]], founded [[pol:462|Yisamashtsi]] at Bakh. Sim'atlue'e had outlasted her brother's state without winning its throne. After her general I'pil Tlie lost before Nisshudi in 1489, the [[evt:10533|Treaty of Nisshudi (AY 1489)]] took four more districts, Tunavükhini among them, and ninety kilograms a year for seventeen years. One clause survives, quoted half a century later: "The merchants of Kvile shall pass through the land of Nithtsokuti unhindered; no toll shall be taken of them and no man shall seize their goods." Kvile's chronicle puts the cession at "9 districts in all". Her daughter Nitllo Ash died that year, aged ten. The record calls it a natural death and says no more.

## The end at Nisshudi

Three more children followed in the 1490s. The youngest, born in 1495, was named Sim'ayueve, *eagle fire*, carrying the eagle of her mother's name and her own. The survey of the 1490s finds Nistlo'ti with six districts, 46,871 people and 606 men under arms, 922 kilograms of silver in debt and paying tribute to two crowns.

In 1492 Ina attacked to take territory. At the [[evt:10591|Battle of Tunavükhini (AY 1494)]], near a town Kvile had held since 1489, his general [[per:10788|Min'asmamshe]] brought 957 men against her 210. She lost 74, he six. In 1496 Ina himself came to Nisshudi with 1,021 men against her 185; twenty-seven of hers died, and eight of his. Ina's stele is dedicated to Muevas'e, a goddess of her own people. It claims: "He killed 200 soldiers." It is more men than she had. Nambili's Stratum IV, the town's third sack, is "ash and fallen brick".

Two names stand on the [[evt:10607|Conquest of Nistlo'ti]], signed at Nisshudi: Ina's and hers. She died the same year, aged thirty-six, executed after thirteen years on a throne of her own making. The record gives neither the manner nor the order; that the conqueror wanted her dead is a likely inference, no more. Ueda, the brother she had challenged, died a natural death in 1504. Her husband lived until 1531.

"Unfortunate" is a fair epithet, but not the whole story. Nistlo'ti fought ten battles and lost all ten; the seven under her own command cost her 1,037 men. Her enemies' steles multiplied those dead roughly four, five and seven times over, and their chronicles made her the aggressor in wars they had started themselves. The record shows a young woman who raised a province her brother had crushed only two years before and held it for thirteen years against four states. In the end she signed away her own kingdom in her own city. Nothing she said or wrote survives; we know her only through those who beat her.
