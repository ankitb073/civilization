title: May the Goddess Protect the King: Rumudan of Tonnga and the Nine-Year Kingdom
---
Of the forty-two inscribed finds from Nambili's excavation of Anavun in AY 4919, the earliest is a bronze plaque, weathered but legible, its text complete. In Navaavun's translation it reads:

> Rumudan, king of Tonnga, built this temple for Nishashutha, his lady. May Nishashutha protect the king.

It is the only document [[per:9175|Rumudan]] left us, dated to the eighth year of his reign, AY 1337. The goddess it names, spelled Nizhashta in the commentary, was the war goddess of the [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]] gods. The prayer is ordinary. His record in war is not. Rumudan commanded eight battles and lost all eight. His kingdom fought eleven and won none. It lasted nine years, and he outlived it by ten.

## A crown in decline

The record knows nothing of Rumudan before AY 1330: no father, mother, wife, child or birthplace. He was born in AY 1301, a man of the Mienibe people, and was twenty-nine when he led the [[evt:8851|Revolt of Tonnga]] against [[pol:339|Viririnu]].

It does show the kingdom he grew up in, and it was in decline. Rumudan was six when the Blue Fever of AY 1307 struck Viririnu among many states; the record counts more than a million dead. A priest at Biyitl wrote: "Those who nursed the sick fell sick in turn." Rumudan was eight when King Mavaluthin I died in battle at Viradhuthpi. He was ten when the Treaty of Tungaruikhrimu gave thirteen districts to Bani, with tribute for fourteen years. In 1327 the throne passed to an infant, Mavaluthin II, with a regent, Talepimtüth, a woman of thirty-one. The record lists three causes of the revolt of 1330: the fever, the death at Viradhuthpi and the treaty.

His state, [[pol:392|Tonnga]], took its name from the region of Tunnge. It was not the first Tonnga: one stood there from 1229 to 1321, another from 1321 until 1329, when Ulokutnakhen of Virnatho conquered it. Rumudan's fifteen districts had belonged to neither; ten had been Viririnu's since 1259. Still, the record numbers his wars as though his kingdom continued theirs.

It was a mixed country. The record gives five districts to the Mienibe, Rumudan's own people and also the people of the crown he rose against; four were Lona, three Lelmi, two Jinru, one Tueng. Nine kept the Lona gods, three the Ya'cuam. Its capital was [[set:213|Anavun]], which the record says Viririnu founded in 1330, the year of the revolt itself; Nambili dates his lowest stratum, "the foundation of the city", to about then. Did the rebels take a town the crown had just built, or does the record credit the kingdom whose land it stood on? I cannot decide.

## Independence by default

The war went badly from the start. In 1331 Tonnga's general Tovunin attacked near Nuvun with 1,649 men against 852 and lost 671 to the enemy's 179; the Viririnu commander fell, but his side held the field. In 1332 Rumudan marched on Viririnu itself with 1,505 men against 871 under [[per:9206|Nathvipavukhin]], a Mienibe general a year his senior. He lost 130 men to the enemy's 27 at the [[evt:8873|Battle of Viririnu (AY 1332)]], the first of two battles he lost despite outnumbering the enemy.

Then, in 1333, Viririnu broke apart into a city-state and a new kingdom, Tungarvikhrem. The [[evt:8852|Tonnga War of Independence]] ended without a settlement, and Tonnga was free. The record does not give Rumudan the credit: the causes it lists for the collapse are the fever, the treaty of 1311 and three battles lost to Bani in 1304, 1309 and 1311. Tonnga survived because no one was left to put it down.

## Four enemies

Freedom lasted two years. In 1335 three neighbours went to war against Tonnga, and in 1336 Virnatho made a fourth. The record names one cause common to all four: the defeat at Nuvun in 1331. [[pol:342|Ayot]], under the usurper [[per:9119|Shim'am'agw]], waged the [[evt:8898|Second Holy War of Ayot against Tonnga]]. [[pol:372|Yimla]] began the [[evt:8899|Yimla–Tonnga War]] and [[pol:373|Buneynga]] the [[evt:8900|Second Buneynga–Tonnga War]], both to take land.

In 1336 the Ayot general [[per:8835|Cui'a'igw'a]], then sixty, brought 1,857 men against Rumudan's 1,298 and won the [[evt:8916|Battle of Anavun (AY 1336)]], losing 42 men to Tonnga's 98. Anavun was sacked that year; Nambili's Stratum IV is a "burnt destruction layer, ash and fallen brick", laid over a city only six years old. At the [[evt:8918|Battle of Nuvun (AY 1336)]] Rumudan led 2,509 men, the largest army he ever commanded, against 2,074 under [[per:9247|Shosh'atlü]] of Yimla. He lost 199 to the enemy's 44.

The year 1337 was the worst. Cui'a'igw'a returned with 2,780 men against 1,431 and won the [[evt:8929|First Battle of Anavun (AY 1337)]]; Tonnga lost 354, Ayot 136. Ayot besieged the city, and Rumudan made peace. The [[evt:8931|Treaty of Anavun (AY 1337)]], sworn with Shim'am'agw at Rumudan's own capital, gave Ayot four districts and a ten-year truce, bound Tonnga to the Ya'cuam gods, and imposed a tribute of 123 kg of silver a year for thirty-four years. No text survives. At its end the kingdom's whole treasury stood at 410 kg, and the tribute ran to 1371, three decades after Tonnga fell.

Peace with Ayot brought none with the others. That year Rumudan lost the [[evt:8932|Second Battle of Anavun (AY 1337)]] to Yimla, 1,953 men against his 1,611, losing 227 to Yimla's 71. At the [[evt:8933|Battle of Nuvun (AY 1337)]] [[per:9086|Ebulngen]] of Buneynga brought 7,588 men against his 1,657. Virnatho beat Tovunin near Viririnu and besieged Anavun.

The plaque belongs to this year. Whether the temple went up before the treaty or after, the record does not say. But a conversion clause is written only for a kingdom that does not already keep the victor's gods. The Mienibe had a war goddess of their own, Nidi. Rumudan, a Mienibe king, built a house for the war goddess of the people who had just beaten him and asked her protection. Piety, policy, or the prayer of a man fighting three wars at once? I think all three.

## What the conqueror wrote

In 1338 the Buneynga emperor [[per:8925|Vananen]] the Devout took the field himself at the [[evt:8946|Battle of Nuvun (AY 1338)]], with 7,510 men against 1,173. Tonnga lost 185 men, Buneynga 39. The Annals of Buneynga (PAL·AN·00187), compiled in 1393, note one battle of the war, in Vananen's thirtieth year: "The armies met at Ruvon; the enemy fled." In 1339 Tovunin fell at Nuvun, and [[per:9126|Ulokutnakhen]] of [[pol:325|Virnatho]], aged twenty-six, brought 2,194 men against Rumudan's 1,659 at the [[evt:8960|Battle of Anavun (AY 1339)]]; Tonnga lost 379, Virnatho 133. After a third siege both kings signed the [[evt:8962|Conquest of Tonnga]] at Anavun, and eleven districts passed to Virnatho.

Virnatho's account of the [[evt:8912|Third Virnatho–Tonnga War]] (PAL·CH·01575), a fire-damaged clay copy excavated at Virnadhu in 4931, was written in 1387 by the palace scribe Pabukhdomnon, born three years after the conquest. He begins: "Rumuthan of Tonnga, forgetting the oaths his fathers swore, set his hand against Virnatho." The record makes Virnatho the attacker, fighting for land, and the phrase is a formula: a Nonenga scribe used it in 1337 of Ulokutnakhen himself, who, "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore, set his hand against Nonenga" (PAL·CH·01547). At Anavun, a damaged line credits Rumudan with 3,118 men; the record gives 1,659. "The scribes counted 696 enemy dead; we lost 33." The record says 379 and 133: the enemy's losses nearly doubled, his own cut to a quarter. He has twenty districts taken; the record, eleven. His last word on the kingdom: "Tonnga is no more, and its people serve Virnatho."

## Ten silent years

Rumudan was thirty-eight. He died a natural death in 1349, at forty-eight. The record does not say where, or whether he lived those years as a captive or a private man. His dynasty bore his name and had one king.

For the people of Tunnge the kingdom's years were years of war; only 1334 passed without one. Tonnga lost 3,257 men in eleven battles, more than the 2,389 its last survey counted under arms, so the ranks were refilled from a country of about 205,000. Their districts passed from a Mienibe crown to a Mienibe rebel, then to Ayot and Virnatho. The town outlasted it all: Nambili's top stratum is "the modern town". The goddess did not save the kingdom. She may be said to have kept the king, which is more than anyone did for Tovunin, or for the more than three thousand men Tonnga lost.
