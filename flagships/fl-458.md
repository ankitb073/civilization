**THE KING TO HIS SOLDIERS AND HIS PEOPLE**

*A LETTER FROM THE FRONT IN YIWUTU, PRINTED BY COMMAND*

*The following letter, written by His Majesty's own hand at headquarters before Taalathyi, was received by wire at the Ministry of War on the evening of the 23rd and is published by command in all the newspapers of the Kingdom this morning, 24 Pivu 2594 of the Vishithmikh. Copies are to be posted in every railway station, post office and house of prayer.*

---

Soldiers of [[pol:1962|Yaaril]], and all my people at home,

I write to you from the heights above [[set:113|Taalathyi]], where I have spent the last week among you, in the trenches and the field hospitals and the railheads, and I write so that every family in the Kingdom may know from their King, and not from rumour, what their sons have done.

You know why we came. The [[evt:46865|Yaaril Claim against Tlilisyi]] was put forward openly, before the world, and the government of [[pol:1969|Taalathyi]] answered it not with argument but with rifles. They chose war. They have had it.

Last year, at the [[evt:46897|Battle of Tlilisyi]], our army under General [[per:46028|Nathtaalin]] met the enemy before this city and broke him. The enemy's commander, [[per:46029|Tamfin Imin]], fell on that field. I do not rejoice at the death of any man; our faith forbids it. But I will say that he chose his cause, and his cause failed him. The city was entered in the days that followed. Let those abroad who call it the [[evt:46898|Tenth Sack of Tlilisyi]], as though we were the tenth set of brigands in its history, remember that it was the enemy who fortified a market town and made it a battlefield.

This year, at the [[evt:46910|Second Battle of Tlilisyi]], they came again, under [[per:45985|Iminishyiwa]], and again they were broken. I watched the last of it from the observation post on the ridge. Their line did not retire; it dissolved. Their dead lay thick along the railway cutting and in the orchards below the old wall. The foreign correspondents will publish their own figures. Believe me rather than them: the enemy's losses have been heavier than his government dares admit, and his army, as an army, has ceased to exist on this front.

I will not hide from you that our own losses have been grievous. When more than eight hundred thousand of our men take the field, the lists of the fallen are long, and I have read every page of them. The enemy's agents in the neutral press have inflated them further, for their own purposes. I say this: every name on those lists is a name of Yaaril, and every family shall have from the Treasury a pension in [[cur:1092|Ir]] for life, by decree signed this day.

Some at home ask why we persist. I answer in the words of our own faith. The teachers of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] tell us that the one divine power intends its message for all peoples, and that the good life is compassion and release from suffering. There is little compassion in a country whose rulers send boys against our guns rather than treat; there will be more when the people of [[reg:5|Yiwutu]] are governed justly, fed regularly, and taught. That is what our soldiers are building here, with the spade as much as with the rifle.

The [[evt:46866|Yaaril Colonial War in Yiutl]] is not yet finished. I make no prophecies; time runs in a line toward a judgement that is not ours to pronounce. But I tell you what I have seen: a Yaaril army that does not falter, officers who sleep in the mud beside their men, and a railway already running where there were only mule-tracks two years ago.

To the mothers and wives of [[set:245|Yaaril]]: your men are well fed, well shod, and proud. Write to them. The field post is regular, and I have ordered it to be free.

To my soldiers: I am proud of you. The whole Kingdom is proud of you. Hold what you have won.

**[[per:45871|Bathnun]], King.**  
*Headquarters before Taalathyi.*
