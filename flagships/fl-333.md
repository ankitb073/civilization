*[Obverse.]*

To the chief priest of the house of [[dei:33|Nabudzin]] in [[set:63|Egu]], speak. Thus says [[per:1116|Ttumpithat]], queen of [[pol:14|Egu]], beloved of Nabudzin of the river, beloved of [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm, lady of the ford and of the market road.

With me all is well. With my soldiers all is well. With my baggage and my oxen all is well. Pour out for me before the river every morning, as I have commanded, and may it be well with you.

Hear now, and let it be read aloud in the courtyard, that the people of Egu may hear what the gods have given them.

The men of [[pol:34|Yeshtakshal]] are thieves. They sat upon the road of [[reg:8|Duvegupuinga]] like a toll-gatherer upon a bridge. Of every ass-load that went down from Egu they took a share. Of every boat they took a share. They raised the share each year, and when the merchants of Egu cried out, they laughed at them. Twice before, in the days of the old kings, men went to war over that road, and still the road was not clean. I did not begin this war. Yeshtakshal began it with greedy hands. This is the [[evt:872|Third Diebeguvebenga Trade War]], and may the gods make it the last.

**Runiy.** In the second year I went out before my spears. At [[set:121|Runiy]] [[per:1158|Varaman]], the general of Yeshtakshal, stood across the field with his shields locked. He was cunning; he held the high ground and the gully. Dumnu sent rain on us both. But I went forward, and Varaman went back. Their dead lay in the gully like reeds after a flood. This was the [[evt:879|Battle of Riniy]]. The field is Egu's.

**Before their walls.** In the third year I went out again. Before the walls of [[set:80|Yeshtakshal]] Varaman came out a second time. Again he went back. Again his men lay down. This was the [[evt:886|Battle of Yishdhagshal]]. The field is Egu's.

**Zhivampive.** In this year I went out a third time. Near [[set:24|Dzebampiba]] Varaman came out a third time; he is stubborn as an old ox. A third time he went back. Who now in Yeshtakshal will follow him? This was the [[evt:892|Battle of Zhivampive]]. The field is Egu's.

Of our dead I will speak when I come home. I have given their names to the scribes. Let each widow receive her measure of barley from the queen's granary, and a jar of oil, and two [[cur:14|Niba]] of silver. I have sworn it before Nabudzin.

**The gate.** Then I went up to Yeshtakshal and the gate was opened. This was the [[evt:893|Sack of Yishdhagshal]]. Their bronze is mine. Their tolls are mine. The tablets of their tolls I have broken with my own hand.

**The oath.** Then the elders of Yeshtakshal came out with dust on their heads. At Yeshtakshal they swore before the gods, and I swore. This is the [[evt:894|Treaty of Yishdhagshal]]. Yeshtakshal gives the land along the road to Egu. The road is open to the merchants of Egu, and no share is taken. Ten years the spear sleeps.

My father, when he swore the [[evt:844|Treaty of Linnshal]], came home with what he had taken out and nothing more; may he rest beside Shedartsi. I come home with a road. Let the priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] say which is the greater gift to Egu. All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. I have turned it.

Therefore: let the white bull be made ready for Dumnu. Let the barley-cake be made ready for Nidziktti. Let the singers learn the names Runiy, Yishdhagshal, Zhivampive. Let the road be swept from the gate of Egu to the river. I am coming home.

*[Edge.]*

> In the 7th year of Ttumpithat, queen in Egu. Written in the camp at Yeshtakshal on the day the oath was sworn.
