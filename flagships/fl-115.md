[[per:34426|Tukulven]], by the grace of the One emperor of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], to the masters of the merchant houses of [[set:0|Tallin]], who hold their charters of our house and their warehouses of our peace: grace, and the patience to read this to the end.

We write from the camp, with our own hand, because our secretary is dead. He was well at the morning office and dead before the lamps were lit, and the red marks were on his neck as they are on all of them. We do not tell you this to move you. We tell you so that you will know we are not writing of a thing we have only heard of.

You know already how [[evt:35300|the red sickness]] began. It came out of [[set:77|Nodhinge]] in the spring, and men say it came in the bales of the cloth merchants, and men say it came in the water, and some say it was carried to us on purpose by those who knew their bales were foul. If that is so, the One will find them. Word comes to us that it is in [[set:147|Binge]] and in [[set:76|Nimiti]], and in [[set:135|Virathkanu]] and [[set:10|Navnge]] they are burning the bedding in the streets. Now your own letters tell us it is in Tallin, in the lower town and by the river gate, and that the rich are leaving by the upper gate with their plate in carts.

Hear us, then, and hear us as your lord.

First. Let no man say that our war brought this upon you. We did not go into the field against the [[pol:1373|Ishuikithyi]] to bring sickness home, and the sickness is among them also, as our scouts report; their villages lie open and unwatched. If the One has sent it as a judgement, He has sent it on all the realms together, and not on Yidhe for [[evt:35384|this war]]. At [[evt:35396|Lininadhe]] we won the field, and the One gave it to us. Does the One give victory with one hand and plague with the other for the same offence? We think not. Let the priests of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] dispute it if they will; and let them dispute it more quietly than the sectaries of Kivunge, whom we hear have [[evt:35435|gone out from their own brethren]] in this very season, as if the One were served by more division.

Second. You hold your charters of our house. A charter is not a gift; it is an obligation. In fair seasons you owed us your taxes and your loyalty. In this season you owe us your warehouses. Let no house close its doors. Let no house hoard. Grain is cheap now, less than a [[cur:778|zhiflun]] the measure, because the mouths that should eat it are being carried out on the carts; but do not imagine it will stay cheap when the reapers die in the fields. Buy now, at the fair price, and store in the imperial granaries, and you will be paid from our treasury when the One lifts His hand. The house that sells dear in this season, we will remember. The house that sells fair, we will remember also.

Third. The teaching of our faith is compassion and the release of suffering. Let every house forgive the debts of widows whose husbands have died of the sickness this year, and we will take the loss upon our own treasury, as far as it will bear. Let no orphan be turned out of a merchant's door for a debt his father owed.

Fourth. Keep your clerks at their books. When this has passed, and it will pass, for time runs forward to the judgement and does not stand still even for plague, we will want to know who did his duty and who ran.

We are young, as you know, and we have been called the Conqueror, and we confess that this is an enemy we do not know how to fight. Pray for us, and for the army, and for Tallin. Read this letter aloud in the exchange. Given in the camp, in the year 4579 from the founding of Tallin.
