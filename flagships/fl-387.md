[[per:26252|Ashni]], by the favour of the One and the consent of the priesthood king of [[pol:1098|Mane]], to the teacher of the Kankhu who lies in the lower prison of [[set:13|Tsivintuha]]: I do not wish you health, for you have refused it; I wish you understanding, which you may still accept.

You asked the gaoler for ink, saying that you wished to write to your brethren a letter of a martyr, so that they might read it after your death and be strengthened. I have permitted you the ink. But before you write your letter, read mine; for it is not just that the condemned should have the last word and the king none.

You think me cruel. Consider first what I am charged to keep. The faith of this realm, the [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], teaches that all things turn upon a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again: the harvest, the dynasty, the soul itself. Upon that teaching rest the calendar of the temples, the order of the festivals, and the ordination of every priest from the high altar to the smallest shrine in the hill country. It is not my opinion. It is the frame of the house in which my people live.

You and your brethren of the [[mov:65|Kankhu]] go into the markets of Mana and say that the wheel is a lie; that time runs in a straight line to a final judgement, and that the world, once ended, will not begin again. You have your own priests, ordained by your own hands, who claim to keep the truth in order as ours do. You say you preach compassion. So do we. You say your message is for all peoples. So is ours. The difference between us is therefore not mercy, nor zeal, but the shape of time itself; and on that question a kingdom cannot have two calendars.

I will be candid with you, as one man who must die to another. I take no pleasure in this. When I was a young king I proceeded in the same manner against the Udushmamash, in the [[evt:26787|Persecution of the Udushmamash in Mane]], and I believed then that firmness was the whole of government. I am older now. I have learned that firmness is only the larger part of it.

But I have also learned what disorder costs. Even now my soldiers are in the field in the [[evt:26937|Second Mane–Navnge War]]. I saw the lists after the [[evt:26948|Battle of Ontiedzey]], where we held the field but buried four thousand five hundred of our own. Bread stands above two [[cur:592|Midnatl]] the measure in the markets of the capital. In such a season, a teacher who tells the soldier that the world is ending, and the farmer that the festivals are vain, is not a gentle philosopher. He is a second enemy within the walls.

That is why the [[evt:27018|Persecution of the Kankhu in Mane]] was ordered, and why I do not repent of it.

And yet our own teaching commands compassion and release from suffering, and I would be a poor servant of it if I did not offer you what it offers. Say before the priests that the wheel turns. You need not say it with your heart; I do not ask for your heart, only for your silence in the market. Say it, and you will go out of the gate with a cloak and a purse, and no one will follow you.

If you will not, then write your letter. I will not burn it. Let your brethren read it, and let them read this also, bound with it, so that whoever comes after may judge between us fairly: a teacher who would not bend, and a king who would not let the house fall.

Written with my own hand in the palace of Tsivintuha, in the year 3781 from the founding of the city.
