**THE PRESIDENT TO A SOLDIER OF THE ARMY OF SHYIYU**

*Published by order of the Office of the President, [[set:136|Mbilu]], 18 Guvu 4714 of the Uthungala. To be posted in every barracks, depot and posting-house of the Republic, and read aloud at evening roll-call in the field.*

---

Some weeks ago a private soldier of the colonial army, serving in the lines before Ometh, wrote to me. He wrote on a page torn from a field notebook, in pencil, and the letter reached my desk with mud still on the envelope. With his permission, which I have obtained by the field post, I print the heart of it here:

> "Mr President, I have been at Ometh twice. I was there last year and I am there again. I have seen the stakes and what they do. My friends from the depot are mostly gone. I am not a coward and I will go forward when the whistle blows. But the men ask me, because I can write, to ask you: what are we doing here, and is it worth it?"

I answer him here, publicly, because every man in the army of [[reg:11|Shyiyu]] is entitled to hear it.

You are doing, soldier, what the Republic of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]] asked you to do, and what it would not have asked if there had been another way. When the [[evt:46307|claim of Mbilu]] upon that country was laid, lawfully and in good form, before the government of [[pol:1941|Nimifu]], that government answered with insolence, and then with muskets. It is not Mbilu that chose the trench. It is Nimifu that dug it.

Consider what you and your comrades have done. At [[evt:46315|Ometh]], last year, under [[per:45521|Navièvi]], an army of more than one million men of the Republic met the forces of Nimifu under [[per:45567|Yuifiminu]] and drove them from the field. This year, at [[evt:46319|Ometh]] again, under the same commander, an army larger still met the forces of Nimifu under [[per:45604|Mèzhi Muga]], and again drove them from the field. Twice they came out to stop you. Twice they failed. Their line before [[set:72|Umifu]] is broken.

You ask whether it is worth it. I will not insult you by pretending that the cost has been light. The Republic's losses are heavy, and every one of them is a name read out in some village square. But understand why. The enemy did not stand and fight in the open. He hid behind stakes, behind earthworks, behind the walls of villages he had emptied of their own people. A small army behind stakes and ditches can kill a great many brave men in a morning; that is not a sign of its virtue, but of its methods. And understand also this: the enemy's losses are far greater than his government admits. Nimifu does not publish its dead. We have counted what we found on the field, and we have not found it possible to believe their bulletins.

Now our guns stand before the capital of Nimifu itself. I will not tell you what the next weeks will bring; no honest man can. I will tell you that the [[evt:46320|siege of Nimid]] is pressed with every resource the Republic has. The supply wagons carry forward shells and bread by day and night. The factories of the capital are working three shifts. The bakers of Mbilu have agreed to hold the price of the soldier's loaf, though grain stands near three [[cur:926|Dathu]] the measure.

The teachers of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] tell us that each of us must find the truth by his own inner light, and that every deed will be weighed at the final judgement, with justice and with moderation. I do not ask you to put out your inner light. I ask you to look by it at the men beside you, at the order of the Republic that sends you bread across the sea of mud, and at the country beyond the stakes that will be opened to the commerce of the world when this war is done. Then judge whether it is worth it.

I believe it is. I believe you believe it, or you would not have written to me rather than to a newspaper of the opposition.

Go forward when the whistle blows. The Republic is behind you.

[[per:45596|NAGIMI]],  
*President of the Republic.*
