*Copy retained in the chancery register, with the note: "Delivered by the Chamberlain into the hand of the young gentleman of Zhinontunu lodged in the west wing."*

[[per:41722|Balshiymaanin]], by the providence of the One Power sovereign of [[pol:1785|Tallin]] and its dominions, to the young gentleman of [[pol:1786|Zhinontunu]] lately committed to our keeping as a pledge of his king's good faith: greeting, and a welcome which we hope he will in time believe to be sincere.

You have been at our court now some days, and our Chamberlain reports that you eat little, speak less, and look out of the window toward home. We do not reproach you for it. A young man taken from his father's house to live among the people who have lately defeated his country would be a poor sort of young man if he were cheerful. But we write to tell you plainly why you are here and what we expect of you, since we would rather you heard it from us than from servants.

Consider first how the [[evt:42347|Tallin–Zhinontunu War]] began. Five years ago your king, [[per:41723|Bizhiynilin]], wrote to us, calling us his cousin in the salutation and threatening us with spears in the body of the letter, over some herdsmen and some boundary stones. That letter is in our archive; you may read it if you wish. It was followed by raiders across our border. We are not a prince who seeks quarrels; but we are not a prince who leaves them unanswered. Our armies went out to punish an injury, not to commit one.

You know what followed, though perhaps not as it truly was, for the bulletins of a defeated court are written to console rather than to inform. Our general met your hosts again and again before Puthnimdu, and the last of those encounters, the [[evt:42397|Battle of Puthnimdu]] of last year, broke the strength of your field army. This year he met your king's general Bivshen before your capital itself, at the [[evt:42403|Battle of Zhinontunu]]. Bivshen fell there, bravely, we are told, and his army with him; by the reckoning of our officers your dead outnumbered ours many times over. Then came the [[evt:42404|Siege of Zhinontunu]], and your king, wisely, sent envoys rather than more soldiers.

By the [[evt:42405|Treaty of Zhinontunu]], sworn at [[set:6|Zhinontunu]] in our name, your king has yielded to us twenty-seven districts, among them Uthtafpu; has engaged to pay into our treasury silver to the value of some nine million [[cur:897|rimi]]; and has sworn with us a truce of twenty years. Those are hard terms. They are less hard than the terms the field would have written.

You are the pledge of that oath. Understand what this means and what it does not. It does not mean that you are a prisoner. You will be lodged as a gentleman, attended, clothed and taught. Our tutors will instruct you in letters, in mathematics, in the law of nations, and in the doctrine of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]], which we profess and which our priests will expound to you without compulsion. You will ride in our parks and sit at our table on feast days.

It means only this: that if your king keeps faith, you will go home a man who knows [[set:0|Tallin]] as no one in Zhinontunu knows it, and who may serve as a bridge between our houses; and if he does not keep faith, you will have the misfortune of being the visible sign of his breach.

Our faith teaches that time runs in a line toward a final judgement, and that the good life is one of compassion and the relief of suffering. We expect to give account at that judgement for this war, and we do not fear the account. We expect also to give account for you. We intend that it shall be a good one.

Eat your dinner. The Chamberlain will bring you books.

*Given at our palace in Tallin, 28 Nurnin 2151 of the Vishithmikh.*
