title: Prayer of a Captive Weaver of Tunavukhnidi in the House of Her Masters

Gods of my mothers, gods of the hearth and the loom, you whom the priests of the [[mov:25|Miyituyishiu]] name in the temple and we named in the kitchen, hear me.  
The same gods sit in the temples of [[pol:995|Tlu]]. You are their gods also. Why do you hear them and not me?

Hear what was done, so that you cannot say you did not know.  
There were signs. In the month before the war came to our walls, a calf was born with two heads in the queen's herd, and the old men said, Two kings over one land.  
We did not want to understand them.

Five years ago the men of Tlu beat our men at the [[evt:23158|Battle of Khash]].  
Last year they beat them again at the [[evt:23197|Battle of Ashash]].  
Then they came to [[set:186|Tunavukhnidi]] and sat down before the walls.  
That was the [[evt:23198|Second Siege of Tunavükhini]]. We ate the oxen. We ate the dogs.  
We boiled the leather of the shields.

Then our queen, [[per:22818|Mashe Feyatl]], went out of the gate to their king, [[per:22995|Minage]].  
She set her seal beside his seal. The land was given. Our land is their land.  
The priests of Tlu read it out as the [[evt:23199|Conquest of Tonavekhinizi]].  
And when her seal was dry they put her to death.  
They did not give her to us to wash. They did not give her to us to weep over.  
O gods of the hearth, she was a hard queen, but she was ours. Who washed her?

I was taken with the women of the weavers' street.  
My daughter was taken to another house. I do not know which house.  
Now I weave in a house of Tlu, at a loom that is not my loom.  
My master sells the cloth in the market, and pays me with barley.  
The cook says a measure of barley costs less than one [[cur:480|Ishuiya]].  
I have not touched an ishuiya since the siege.

The priests of Tlu teach justice in dealings and moderation in all things.  
I have seen their moderation. They took only what they could carry,  
and they could carry everything.

Gods of the hearth, I do not curse them. You hear them; perhaps you would not hear a curse.  
I ask small things, things a weaver may ask.

Let my daughter be fed in the house where she is.  
Let me see her at the festival, if only across the square.  
Let my eyes keep, so that my hands keep, so that I am not sold on.  
Let the barley come in, for when the barley fails the captive eats last.  
And let our queen, who went out of the gate for us, find her way down to you  
without the washing and without the weeping.  
Take her in as if we had washed her. Take her in as if we had wept.

This I ask at the loom, with the shuttle in my hand, where I am allowed to stand.
