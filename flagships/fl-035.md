title: The Remembrance of a Lance of Iethithi in the War for Bani
---
In the name of the One, before whom the old gods are servants and shadows, I, an unworthy servant, once a lance in the host of [[pol:1177|Iethithi]] and now an old man who eats the bread of his lord's hall, set down what I saw in the war for [[reg:15|Bani]]. I write in the year 3396 from the founding of Iethithi, not for vainglory, which the Teaching forbids, but that those who come after may know what was given and what was paid.

**Of the cause of the war.** The kingdom of [[pol:1324|Yit]] had been brought low at [[evt:32633|Rurin]] by other hands, and its strength lay broken like a reed. The lords of the council said that the One delivers the proud into the hands of the just, and that Bani lay open like a field after harvest. So [[evt:32746|the war against Yit]] was proclaimed, and every lord was summoned to bring the lances he owed. My lord held his land of the crown by the service of thirty lances, and I was the least of the thirty.

**Of the first field.** We went down to [[evt:32760|Tyiyishikhishi]] under [[per:32345|Ka'a'ak]], a good captain and a just man. But our hearts were not just. We divided the cattle of Bani at the fire before we had seen them. Against us stood [[per:32459|Odilin]], captain of Yit, and he broke us. I will not write the number of our dead; it is written in the book of the One. We were scourged, as the proud are scourged.

**Of the second field.** The next year we fasted three days before we marched, and the teachers taught us moderation and the inner light. At [[evt:32773|Muethta]] Ka'a'ak met Odilin again, and this time the One was with us. The men of Yit fell before us as wheat before the sickle; their dead were four for every one of ours. There I took a wound in the thigh that aches yet.

**Of the third and fourth fields.** At [[evt:32783|Nevinatiyenshi]] we were five to their one, and still they nearly held the ford against us; I set it down lest anyone call the men of Yit cowards. The year after, at [[evt:32816|Vinaduthfi]], [[per:32518|Basiluesh'a]], who still lives, led us, and when the day was over the dead of Yit were twice our own, though the crows could not tell victor from vanquished.

**Of the last field.** In the seventh year of the war our lord the emperor [[per:32415|Ish'o Maf]] came himself to Nevinatiyenshi. Of how he came to the throne let others write; on that field he was a king. He rode along the lines bareheaded and said: you owe me your service, and I owe the One an account of you. Then we went forward, and [[evt:32833|the second day of Nevinatiyenshi]] was ours beyond all doubt. More than sixteen thousand of Yit lay dead, and Odilin led away what remained. Our own losses were some two thousand, and the Teaching says that a debt paid in full is no shame.

At that same place the envoys of Yit came with their heads bowed, and by [[evt:32834|the peace of Nevinatiyenshi]] they yielded Bani and the lands about it, and the town of Tyiyishikhishi, where we had been shamed, and they swore fifteen years of peace. My lord gave to each of his thirty lances twelve [[cur:675|isa]] of silver, and to me a horse besides.

**Of what came after.** Ka'a'ak is gone to the One these fifteen years. The emperor Ish'o Maf died in the year before the crown was divided, when the realm was broken among many heads, and [[evt:32891|Iethithi stood alone]] under its own lords, as Ayshi and Imshi and the others stood under theirs. I have heard young men in the hall say we gained a province and lost an empire. I do not answer them. The wise, says the Teaching, withdraw from wealth and appetite, and what is the hunger for land but appetite?

Yet I do not repent of my service. I went where my lord sent me, as he went where the crown sent him. If we erred, we erred in obedience, which is not the worst of sins.

All things turn upon the great wheel; what ends will begin again. May the faithful of [[mov:69|Kievi]] pray for the men who lie at Tyiyishikhishi and at Muethta and at Nevinatiyenshi, for those of Yit as for our own. Here ends the remembrance.
