To [[dei:49|Tuve]], lord of the land below, keeper of the gate, who eats the dead.  
To [[dei:53|Bine]], fire of the hearth.  
To [[dei:48|Kudze]], sky over all.  
I, a widow of [[set:169|Kagüne]], wife of a potter, daughter of a potter, speak. Hear me.

In the second year of [[per:13134|Nakagum]], king of [[pol:506|Puvu]], in the month of reaping, the [[evt:13436|Silent Ague]] came into Kagüne. It came first into the street of the potters. It does not shout. It does not burn. The sick lie down and grow cold, and they do not cry out, and on the third day they are quiet for ever.

My husband went to the war at Shuthunile for the king. He went with the levy of the first year, he came home. He went with the levy of the second year, he came home. At the [[evt:13426|Battle of Shuthunile]] he took no wound. Nüdinande spared him. Tuve did not spare him. He lay down on the mat by the kiln. He did not cry out. On the third day he was quiet.

I washed him. I put a jar of beer and a loaf in his hand. I put his potter's knife in his hand. I gave him to the earth of [[dei:51|Talu]].

Hear my vow, Tuve, hear it.

My son is seven years old. My daughter is at the breast. If you pass by my door, if you do not lie down on my mat, if you leave my son and my daughter in the light of Kudze until the barley is cut again:

I will give you a black kid at the new moon.  
I will give you a jar of oil at the full moon.  
I will give you one [[cur:269|Mbumbi]] of silver, the price of a measure of barley, each year at the reaping, as long as I live.  
I will give you the first pot from my husband's kiln, unglazed, and I will break it at your door.

If you take my son, I will give you nothing. If you take my daughter, I will give you nothing. If you take me and leave them, let the wife of my brother give the kid and the oil, and let her not forget.

Hear my vow, Bine, fire of the hearth. Keep the fire in my house. Do not let it go cold, as the houses of my street have gone cold. Of the five houses from the well to the shrine, two have no fire.

Hear my vow, Kudze, sky over all. Send wind. Blow the ague away to the mountains where no one lives.

The priests say the gods give fortune to those they favour. I have given what I had. I have given the beer of my wedding. I have given my husband's sandals to the lame man at the gate. Look at me. I am a small woman. Do not trouble yourselves with me.

This vow I have spoken at the shrine of Tuve in Kagüne. The scribe has written it on the clay. I paid him a loaf; he did not ask for more.
