*Spoken before the throne in [[set:264|Ölie]] on the feast of the crowning, in the 29th year of [[per:35994|Sitlyitlathe]], by a poet of the household.*

**Invocation**

Gods who keep each your place and your due,  
priests who keep the truth in order and hand it down,  
lords who hold your lands of the throne as the vine holds of the wall:  
hear me praise the master of us all.

**The Name**

They named him Bright Deer in the hour of his birth,  
and the midwives wondered, and the priests were silent;  
for what is a deer, they said, to rule an empire?  
O foolish priests! The deer outruns the hound,  
the deer drinks only at the clean spring,  
the deer is seen at dawn, and the hunter is still asleep.

**The Crown**

His father [[per:35675|Oi Toe]] went to the gods in the fifth year of his reign,  
and the boy was fourteen, and the crown was heavy.  
The lords of [[pol:1497|Ölie]] came and knelt, and some knelt slowly;  
he marked who knelt slowly, and he forgave them,  
and they knelt quickly ever after.  
As it is written: the young vine that is pruned bears most.

**The First Chase**

When the merchants of Ölie were barred from the markets of their neighbours,  
he sent his captains to [[evt:36566|Onithie]], and the gate of trade was opened;  
he sent them to [[evt:36583|Onithie]] again, and it was opened wider;  
at [[evt:36589|Nyinsitlasa]] the last bar was broken.  
By [[evt:36590|the peace sworn at Nyinsitlasa]], sealed with his own hand,  
the city itself was given into his keeping, with lands and a second town,  
and the roads were opened to the merchants of Ölie,  
and fifteen years of quiet were sworn before the gods.

**The Second Chase**

Then the men of Yi'otl stood stubborn in their places,  
few, but proud as rams upon a rock.  
So began [[evt:36662|the second war of Yi'otl]].  
At [[evt:36689|Sase]] the host of Ölie broke them;  
and at [[evt:36692|Myizinithie]] the Bright Deer himself went before the standard,  
as the scripture says a good lord goes before his men and not behind them;  
and when they stood again [[evt:36702|in the year after]], they fell again.  
By [[evt:36703|the treaty of Myizinithie]] the city of Myizinithie bowed its head,  
the indemnity was paid, and the truce was sworn.  
Let no one ask the cost of that war. A king counts the end of a road, not its stones.

**The Virtues**

Yet hear, lords, what is greater than these chases.  
The teachers of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] say: ask justice in dealings, and moderation in all things;  
the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite.  
Who has seen the emperor drunk? No one.  
Who has seen him fat with feasting? No one.  
His table is the table of a captain on the march;  
the [[cur:823|Myiu]] of his treasury go to the granaries and the roads,  
and the poor of the capital eat in the lean months from his barns.  
He judges the great lord and the small tenant by one measure,  
and the great lord, who would rather be judged by a different one, does not dare to say so.

**The Heir**

And the gods have given him a fawn, [[per:36355|Yithifoyatl]], now fourteen,  
the very age at which his father took the crown.  
Let him learn the ways of the bright deer:  
to run swiftly, to drink cleanly, to rise early.

**Envoi**

Seven years now the spears have rested in their racks.  
Seven years the merchants have gone and come in safety.  
Twenty-nine years the Bright Deer has kept the forest of Ölie.  
Gods of every place and due, give him twenty-nine more;  
and we, the lords and the poets of his household,  
will kneel quickly, as is fitting, to the end of our days.
