title: A Family of One: What Leehi Can and Cannot Tell Us About Proto-Haalood
---
The comparative method needs at least two witnesses. The [[lan:5|Haalood]] family has one. [[lan:35|Leehi]], attested from c. AY -643 to the present, is its only known daughter, and every asterisked Proto-Haalood form is therefore a product of internal reconstruction: an inference about Leehi's past drawn from Leehi alone. My argument is that the eight sound laws the record dates between c. AY -2816 and -1412 account fully for two of the five basic words usually cited, for part of two more and for none of the fifth, and that the gaps are an honest measure of what a single daughter can tell us.

## Where the laws work

The cleanest case is *god*, proto *matanna, Leehi madhana. Three laws apply in order. Degemination (c. -2436) gives *matana. Intervocalic voicing of *t *k *s (c. -1910) gives *madana. Postvocalic spirantization of *d *g *t *k (c. -1412) gives *maðana, which Leehi writes madhana. The voiced spirant is itself evidence for the order of events. Had spirantization come before voicing, *t would have become θ and stayed voiceless.

*Mother*, proto *ḏoddidda, Leehi dhödhidha, is almost as clean. Setting the vowel aside for a moment, degemination gives *ḏodida, and postvocalic spirantization turns both remaining stops into ð. The front rounded vowel is the interesting part, because two laws could have made it. The vowel fronting of c. -2816 (o > ø before a consonant and a front vowel) would already have done so if the geminate *dd counted as one consonant, as geminates often do. If it did not, the umlaut of c. -2196 (a o u > e ø y before a consonant and *i or *j) would have done so after degemination had removed the obstacle. The word alone cannot decide between them; the record's dated history credits the umlaut, so the geminate did not count as one.

*Silver*, proto *midača, Leehi midhazha, shows postvocalic spirantization in its first consonant: *miðača. But the affricate is a problem. The voicing law of c. -1910 covers *t *k *s and not *č, and no law among the eight spirantizes an affricate. The zh of midhazha must therefore come from a later change, after c. -1412 and quite possibly within Leehi's own history.

## Where they do not

*King*, proto *sällā, Leehi shala, shows degemination and nothing more that the eight laws can explain. The shift of *s to sh, the backing of *ä and the loss of length all lie outside them. *Water*, proto *kavunso, Leehi khavunhu, is untouched by any of the eight. The *s follows *n, so the voicing law passes it by. The apocope of c. -1562 removed only final y, u and ø, and only after a single consonant, so the final *o survived it. The spirant kh-, the weakening of *s to h and the closing of final *o to u all belong to some later stage.

Four laws leave no trace at all in these five words: the vowel fronting of c. -2816, the spirantization of *p to h (c. -2216), the lowering of e before a final consonant (c. -1641) and the apocope just mentioned. That is no reason to doubt them. It is a reminder that five words are a sample, not a grammar.

## What one daughter cannot tell us

Two limits deserve stating plainly. First, internal reconstruction can recover a distinction only where Leehi still shows a trace of it, in an alternation or an irregularity. A contrast that Leehi merged everywhere is gone, and no asterisk can bring it back. The vowels *ä and *ā in *sällā, for example, are hypotheses tested only against the language that lost them. Second, every law listed here predates the conventional start of the history of [[lan:35|Leehi]] at c. AY -643. What we call Proto-Haalood and what we call Leehi are a single line of descent, and the boundary between them is a convention of naming, not an event. In larger families a law's date can be checked against the point at which sister languages diverge. Here there is no sister, and I would read the dates as an order of events more than as a chronology.

None of this makes [[lan:5|Haalood]] less interesting. It makes the family a lineage rather than a family, and its asterisks deserve a lighter type than those of families with many witnesses.
