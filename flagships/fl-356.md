*[A small tablet, no larger than a palm, of the kind that can be hidden in a sandal or a water-skin. The signs are cramped.]*

To the captains of the hill villages, to the herdsmen of the high pastures, to every man of [[pol:11|Poyonga]] who is outside the walls and still holds a spear, say:  
thus says [[per:268|Lathilat]], your queen, daughter of the city, servant of [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky.

With me it is not well. With the city it is not well. With the granary it is not well.  
I will not write that it is well. A queen who lies to her captains is a queen of straw.

Hear me. The men of [[pol:1|Tallin]] are around the city. They are on the east side, they are on the west side, they are on the river side. Their fires are many at night; I count them from the roof of the house of [[dei:14|Wulme]], and I stop counting. This is the [[evt:287|First Siege of Poynge]], and it is the eleventh summer of this war, the [[evt:259|Second Tallin–Poyonga War]], which they began and never finished.

You know what happened before the walls. I went out. I went out myself, with the spears of the city, at the [[evt:286|Battle of Poynge]]. They were two to our one. We stood until the sun was high. Their dead were more than ours; tell this to everyone who says we ran. We did not run. We came back through the gate in order, carrying our wounded, and we shut the gate behind us.

Remember also Nin. Remember the [[evt:279|Battle of Nin]], five summers ago, when the men of Tallin came against us with nearly three spears to our one, and they died in the barley, so many that the vultures could not eat them all, and few of ours lay down. The gods were with us at Nin. [[dei:14|Wulme]], lady of the spear, stood in the ford with her feet in the water and turned them back.

Remember also the first summers of my reign, when they took the city and broke the houses: the [[evt:265|Second Sack of Poynge]]. I was young then, my captains. I built the walls again; my own voice called every course of brick. The walls are good walls. They will hold if there is bread.

Now hear what I need.

There is barley for one moon, if the children eat half. There is oil for less. The cistern is low; the water-gate is watched, and the boy who carries this tablet will go out through the water-gate under the reeds, if [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon hides his face tonight.

Gather the hill villages. Gather the herdsmen. Bring every spear and every sling. Come down by the old goat path behind the burial hill, where Tallin has set no fire. Come on the night of the new moon. When you come, burn a fire on the burial hill, and I will open the east gate and come out to meet you. Between us we will break them as we broke them at Nin.

Bring barley if you can. Pay the carriers in [[cur:1|Inet]] from the shrine of the grain; I give you leave, and [[dei:17|Nithikhuti]] of the grain will forgive it, for it is to feed her own people.

If you do not come, I will not open the gate to Tallin. Let the scribes of Tallin write that.

To the priests outside the walls, say: pour to Dumamnul of the sky, pour to [[dei:15|Winba]] of the earth, pour to [[dei:18|Ewa]] of the fire, that he burn their siege-sheds and not our roofs. Let no god be forgotten, each in their place, each with their due. All things turn on the wheel; it turned against us before, and it turned back.

*[Reverse, along the edge, in a different, hasty hand.]*

> Written in the house of Wulme, in the 11th year of Lathilat. The boy's name is not written, so that it cannot be found.
