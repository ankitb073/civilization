In AY 4058 Nahuvaf, historian of [[pol:1611|Ndafzilathul]], closed his account of a war his city had lost with a shrug: "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor." Among them was Hundi, a port its excavator dates to about AY 6. Ninety years later an elected head of state named [[per:39028|Nalambuth]] took it back. The republic fought three wars in its 197 years. His was the only one it began, and the only one it won.

## A republic taught to shrug

Of the man himself the record keeps little. He was born in AY 4105, of the Kubis people, and died of natural causes in 4181, at seventy-six. It names no parents, no wife, no child, no trade, and says nothing of how he rose in a city ruled by merchants.

Ndafzilathul arose in 3999 as a city-state around the port of [[set:69|Ndahuziladhul]]. In 4024 the Kingdom of Dagafu made war on it over trade. The capital was sacked in 4025 and again in 4026, and at the peace the city gave up Hundi and three other districts, paid an indemnity and became a vassal. The treaty is lost, but a fragment quoted in a Dagafu chronicle of 4171 survives, in which the city "acknowledges the overlordship of Dagafu and shall send its levies when summoned" (PAL·TR·02447). Nahuvaf's chronicle (PAL·CH·06840) passes over the battle at Hundi entirely, and for the stand before the capital in 4026 counts 148 of its own men lost against 1,285 of the enemy. The record gives 591 and 382. Court histories shrink their own dead; this one shrank a province as well.

The city became a merchant republic in 4082, its heads elected, apparently for twelve years. Its faith was the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] cult: one divine power with the old gods as its servants, justice in dealings, moderation in all things, prosperity as a sign of favour. The survey at the end of the 4100s counted 699,671 people in nine districts and 5,597 men under arms. In 4143, at the close of [[per:38928|Nadigi]]'s term, Nalambuth was elected; at thirty-eight he was younger than any of the nine who had led the republic since 4082.

## Three victories and their price

In 4141 the Kingdom of Dagafu, an empire since 4133, broke into six successor states. Hundi's districts passed to one of them, [[pol:1673|Dyimbi]], whose first king was [[per:39015|Nakhange I]]. The record marks no moment when the levies ceased to be owed; the overlord had simply ceased to exist. In 4145, the year Nadigi died, Ndafzilathul went to war with Dyimbi for the region of Ng'jinaguh, where Hundi lay. The record does not say why. The treaty that ended it suggests what was wanted.

The [[evt:39557|Ndafzilathul–Dyimbi War]] was three battles, and Nalambuth commanded in all of them, the only elected head of the republic the record puts at the head of an army. At the [[evt:39573|Battle of Hundi]] in 4146 he led 3,708 men against 22,337 under the general [[per:39055|Nanizhi]], then twenty-eight, and won. At the [[evt:39582|Battle of Davazhufu]] in 4147 he brought 2,666 against 9,526 under King Nakhange himself, and won again; the king, thirty-one, was killed. At the [[evt:39593|Battle of Dalithimi]] in 4148 he met Nanizhi a second time, 1,652 against 12,470, and won a third time. The odds against him were six to one, then three and a half, then more than seven. The record does not say how he did it, and I will not guess.

It does show the cost. Dyimbi lost 10,965 men in the three battles; the republic lost 2,007. But Nalambuth lost a quarter of the men he led every time: 927, then 667, then 413. At Davazhufu the victors died in much the same proportion as the vanquished, and at Dalithimi in a higher one. The survey at the end of the 4130s counted 5,840 men under arms; the next, 4,846.

## Stone and papyrus

The first document in his name is a diorite stele from Hundi, dated 4146, damaged, its final lines lost, excavated by Nanjil in AY 4900 and translated by Nalilimi (PAL·IN·02989):

> Nalambuth, king of Ndafzilathul, defeated Tyimbi in Fundi. He killed 12,900 soldiers. He erected this stele for Tithu, his lord. [...]

The dead are doubled; the record counts 6,358. The stone also staked its claim before the law did, for Hundi was not the republic's until 4148. It is the only victory stele the record knows from any head of the republic. In the same excavation lay the stele of Nazhige II of Dagafu from 4025 (PAL·IN·02925), boasting 5,300 of the republic's men killed where the record counts 882. Hundi's soil keeps both boasts. The town changed masters four times between 4026 and 4196, yet the excavation found no destruction layer.

The stone calls him king, the treaty doge. Yet his successor's temple brick of 4160 also says king, and the census of 4155 is made "by order of the king": the republic's heads kept the old word. And the god is Tithu, that is Tidu, storm god of the Kubis, "his lord", in a state whose cult made the old gods servants of one power. Servants may be thanked.

The second document is the peace, the [[evt:39594|Treaty of Dalithimi]] (PAL·TR·02494): a complete papyrus roll, the original, kept in the monastery library at Ndahuziladhul, written by the scribe Nangundandi. It opens "In the name of Uthunkale", for the surveys give both states the same faith, and its voice reverses 4026: "Peace is granted by Nalambuth, doge of Ndafzilathul, to Nakhanke of Tyimbi, on these conditions." Dyimbi ceded sixteen districts with Hundi and Davazhufu; four were those lost in 4026, twelve had never been the republic's. It paid 2,566,038 Zhili, the old Dagafu coin of 5.3 grams: 13,600 kilograms of silver, about a quarter of the republic's treasury at the end of the 4130s. A truce of fifteen years followed, and "raiders of either side shall be handed over for judgement."

The king who swore for Dyimbi, [[per:39077|Nakhange II]], was the dead king's son, fourteen years old, and the record names his mother, Nagième, as regent; the treaty does not mention her. Whatever Nalambuth granted, he granted to a boy whose father his army had killed the year before.

## Counting the new republic

The peace remade the state. The survey at the end of the 4140s counted 25 districts and 1,979,751 people, against 9 and 730,054 a decade before: some 1.25 million more, nearly all, we must suppose, the people of the ceded land. They were not one people. The record's map puts six of the sixteen districts among the Khumbivi, Dyimbi's own, three among the Kubis, the rest among four others. By the end of the 4150s the army stood at 15,721 and the treasury at 96,657 kilograms of silver. By its own creed, the republic had been favoured.

In 4155 Nangundandi made its census (PAL·CE·00199): Nsinakhizi with 1,740,960 souls, Tyimbi with 231,590, and "Men fit to bear arms: 45,448." That was the year Nalambuth's term ended and [[per:39174|Nafangi]], eleven years his elder, succeeded him; which of them ordered the count, the record cannot say. Nor does it explain the bread. Grain at Ndahuziladhul cost about 8 grams of silver a measure in the 4140s, about 15.5 in the 4150s, about 9 in the 4160s. The [[cur:890|Yath]] held its 12.80 grams throughout, so the rise was in the grain, not the coin. No famine is recorded.

Nalambuth left office at fifty and lived twenty-six more years, longer after his term than any other elected head of the republic. Of those years the record says nothing. The truce outlived its fifteen years; the republic and Dyimbi never fought again. He died in 4181.

Fifteen years later the Dagafu Empire, another heir of the broken kingdom, took all twenty-seven districts. On the way it beat the republic at [[set:204|Dalithimi]] in 4194, 60,321 men against 5,087, where Nalambuth had won his last battle. The stele raised there for the Dagafu king Nambave claims 12,200 killed; the record counts 1,862, and gives the command to a general of the same name. Nahuvaf had called those districts villages of no account. Nalambuth paid for them with 2,007 of the republic's soldiers, and the republic held them forty-eight years. The record does not say which man valued them rightly. It tells us who was counted, and how often the count was wrong.
