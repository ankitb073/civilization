title: Tablet of an Old Soldier of Kabave on the War for the Pievi Roads

I speak to you, all you people, you sons of the levy, that you may know the things that came to me. I am a man of Kabave, a son of the levy, a servant of Kudze who holds up the sky. I have grown old. My hand shakes and the scribe of the house of Bine sets my words in the clay. I speak in the 19th year of Natumbambu, king of [[pol:178|Puvu]], may Kudze keep him.

Hear how the war began. Long ago, in the days of the old kings, the men of [[pol:237|Tagavu]] came into our land, and they took land that was ours, and [[evt:5990|that war]] was a shame upon Puvu. Then in Tagavu a man [[evt:7448|seized a seat that was not his]]: [[per:7822|Navavunu]], the usurper, the son of no king. He shut the roads of [[reg:24|Pievi]] to the merchants of Puvu. He took a toll on the salt. He took a toll on the copper. Then our lady [[per:7819|Nakagum]], queen of Puvu, beloved of Nüdinande, said: the roads of Pievi shall be opened. So began [[evt:7662|the war for the Pievi roads]].

I went out with the queen to [[evt:7680|Shudunnile]]. We were twenty thousand. The men of Tagavu were half of us. [[per:8034|Naguvu]] led them. They saw our spears and they ran. I took a captive with my own hand. The queen gave me a ring of silver.

The next year I went out with the general [[per:8049|Natidme]] to [[evt:7690|File]]. Naguvu stood against us again, and he had more men than we. Natidme was a lion. Natidme was a bull of Nüdinande. He broke them. The dead of Tagavu lay in the fields of File, near four thousand. Of ours, not half so many.

The year after, the queen went out to [[evt:7699|Tadavli]], and the usurper himself came against her. Navavunu came out, and Navavunu ran. Three times we went out, three times the god of war stood with Puvu.

Then the god turned his face. At [[evt:7712|Kambizhinu]] the rain fell for nine days. The ground became mud. The carts sank to the axle. Naguvu came upon us in the mud, and we turned back. They did not follow us far.

Two years later, at [[evt:7736|Kambizhinu]] again, a sickness walked in the camp. The omens were bad. The liver was black. The men of the usurper came in the night, as thieves come, not as men of war. Many of my brothers went down to Tuve that night. I do not say their number. Let Tuve count them.

The next year at [[evt:7755|File]] we did not hold the field. But it was a small thing. The usurper did not hold much of it either.

Then the general Natidme went out a second time, to [[evt:7788|Kaguga]], and he struck the usurper. On that field, for each man of Puvu who went down, near three men of Tagavu went down, as if Tuve had weighed them in a single scale and pressed his thumb on it. Then the queen and the usurper sat down at Kaguga. They set their seals on [[evt:7789|the tablet of Kaguga]]. Each kept what he held. For ten years the spears slept. Puvu did not lose. Let no man say Puvu lost.

The next year our lady Nakagum went to her fathers. [[per:8112|Natumbambu]] sat on the seat. Before the walls of Kabave he crushed the rebels of Kagüne, as a man crushes a scorpion with his sandal.

Now the young men go out again into Pievi, and my grandsons with them. Two years past, at Kaguga, they won, as we won. Last year at Shimbayi the god turned his face again. I will not speak of Shimbayi. Let the mothers speak of it.

These are the gifts I had: a ring of silver from the queen, a captive, a field by the canal, and ten ngüzhin of silver from the general Natidme on the day of File. I was a man of the levy and I ate the bread of the queen. I did not turn my back.

You who come after: pour water for the men of Kambizhinu. Pour water for the men of Shudunnile. Pour water for the men of Kaguga. May Kudze keep the sky. May Talu keep the field. May Nüdinande go before the king. This I set on the clay, that my sons may know.
