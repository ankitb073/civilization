*[Obverse. The tablet is cracked through the middle and has been rejoined; a few signs are lost along the break.]*

Hear, O [[dei:37|Novku]], lord of the counted words, you who see what is true.  
Hear, O [[dei:34|Tsevni]], lamp of the night, you who saw it.  
Hear, O [[dei:38|Shedartsi]], lord of the house under the earth, you who received them.

I write what I saw with my eyes. I do not write what I heard.

In the first year of our lord [[per:1150|Pimttumkuttnakin]], the house of Buneynga broke in two, and [[set:80|Yeshtakshal]] stood up by herself. This was the [[evt:867|rise of Yeshtakshal]]. Our lord made the offering to [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm, and the priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] said: "The wheel has turned for Yeshtakshal." We were glad. We were a small city, but we were our own city. The traders came to our gate; we took the toll at our gate, and the [[cur:7|tuyettku]] came into our storehouse.

But [[pol:14|Egu]] wanted the road. Egu wanted the toll. Egu said: "The gate of Yeshtakshal is our gate." Three years the men of Egu came against us, every year in the season of the dry wind. Every year our young men went out, few against many, and every year more of theirs lay down than of ours. Let the scribe say it: more of theirs than of ours. But they had more to lose, and we had few to lose.

In the sixth year of our lord they came the last time. They came as many as the reeds of the river, as many as the locusts. At the [[evt:892|battle of Zhivampive]] our captain stood with thirty and eight shields. Thirty and eight against four thousand and more. They stood at the ford. They did not run. Fourteen of ours lay down at the ford; three hundred and forty of Egu lay down at the ford. Who has heard of such a thing? Let the singers sing it. But the ford was taken, and the road to the city was open.

*[Several signs lost along the break.]*

[…] in the morning the men of Egu came to the wall. There were not enough men on the wall. I was on the wall; I threw stones. The old men were on the wall; they threw stones. The women brought the stones up in baskets. But the ladders were many. They came over the wall at the corner by the potters' gate.

I saw this: the men of Egu in the street of the coppersmiths.  
I saw this: the men of Egu at the door of the house of [[dei:33|Nabudzin]], and they broke the door.  
I saw this: the toll-silver carried out in baskets, the copper carried out in baskets.  
I saw this: the oxen driven out, the asses driven out, the jars of oil carried out on poles.  
I saw this: an old man who would not give up his jar, and they struck him down at his door.  
I saw this: fire in the roofs of the reed houses, and the smoke over the city like the cloud of Dumnu.

*[Reverse.]*

Then our lord went out to them. Our lord, the devout one, who gives to the gods every new moon, went out with his hands empty. He sat down with the captain of Egu. He swore by the gods of the sky and the gods of the earth. Seven districts he gave to Egu, seven districts with their fields. The gate he gave to Egu: the men of Egu pass the gate and pay no toll. Ten years of peace he swore. Then the men of Egu went away, and they sang on the road.

Now the city is quiet. The reed houses are built again; reed is quick to build. The house of Nabudzin has a new door. But the toll does not come into our storehouse. The men of Egu pass the gate laughing.

Do not say Yeshtakshal was weak. Say: Yeshtakshal was small. Say: thirty and eight stood at the ford. [[dei:36|Nidziktti]] of the grain makes the barley stand up after the sickle; the wheel turns; what ends will begin again.

> Written in the 6th year of Pimttumkuttnakin, lord of Yeshtakshal. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Shedartsi open the earth under him.
