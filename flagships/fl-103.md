title: The Confession of Simanieme

To You, who are One, and before whom the many gods of my fathers stand as servants stand before the master of a house, I, [[per:22241|Simanieme]], make this confession: not because You need to hear it, but because the city does. A citizen who hides his error from his fellow citizens wrongs them twice, once by the error and again by the silence.

I was ordained in the order of the [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]], and I received the teaching in due succession, from the hands of men who had received it from others. I do not despise that succession; I confess only that I mistook the vessel for the wine. I served many gods, each in his place and with his due. I kept the measure in all things. I sought what our teachers call wisdom, which is to withdraw from wealth and appetite. I gave away the [[cur:480|eshueya]] my father left me. I ate one meal a day, and I thought myself better than the merchants of [[set:3|Eshueethie]] because I wanted less than they did.

Consider, reader, what came of this. In the year of [[evt:22423|Foyitlithyi]] our arms prevailed, and the priests of one god claimed the victory for their altar. In the next year our army was worsted at [[evt:22429|Fyinan]], and the priests of another god said that his portion had been neglected. In the year after, at [[evt:22434|Fyinan]] again, the young men of [[pol:955|Tomözel]] were cut down in such numbers that the mourners in the streets outnumbered the sellers in the market; and the priests of a third god called for a third sacrifice. I listened to them as one listens to courtiers who quarrel in the antechamber while the house burns. A city that obeys many masters obeys none. A heaven divided into portions is a heaven at war with itself.

And where was I, the wise man, in those years? I was fasting. I was withdrawn. I had made myself so free of appetite that I had become free also of my neighbour. The young men went to Fyinan and did their duty, and I, who had been taught everything, did nothing but keep my measure. This is the first thing I confess: that I called my idleness moderation, and my absence wisdom.

Then the truth came to me, not as a voice but as a conclusion, the way the sum comes at the end of a long reckoning. If there is order in the world, there is one who orders it. If there is one who orders it, then the gods are His stewards and not His rivals. And if He orders the world, then the good life is not to step out of the order, but to take one's post in it: to do one's duty, to keep the law of the city, to offer the right sacrifice at the right time, and to stand where one is set, as a soldier stands in the line.

In the year past I said these things aloud in Eshueethie, and some called me an apostate, and some called me mad, and a few called me teacher. That was the [[evt:22463|Founding of the Weitlegi Faith]], though I did not know then that it would be called so. I confess that I was afraid.

I am not afraid now. In this year our army has been worsted again, at [[evt:22439|Ishwyithie]], within sight of our own walls, and the city has more need than ever of men who will take their post. I do not withdraw. I ordain. I lay my hands on those who have learned, so that what I received will pass down in order; for truth that belongs to no one soon belongs to the loudest.

Nor is this teaching for Tomözel alone. The One did not stop at our borders, and neither shall the word of [[mov:78|Weitlegi]]. I would teach it to our enemies, so that they might at last have a reason to be at peace with us.

I pray for the queen, [[per:21706|Ashiyueve]], who has kept the city through forty years of fortune good and bad. May she find in the One a master worthy of her service, as I have.

Written in Eshueethie, in the year 2081 of the Mietseyeshio, for so the city still counts its years.
