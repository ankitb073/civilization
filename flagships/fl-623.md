title: Fame Bear: Bunethin of Yide and the Silence of the Annals

The court annalist of [[pol:779|Yide]] who wrote the Annals of Yide, AY 2300–2369 (PAL·AN·00364) announced that his record ran "to the 48th year of Bunethin". It does not. The last entry falls in the king's 33rd year, AY 2354: "There was a battle at Pyikhtside; the army of the king prevailed." After that, for fifteen years, the annalist wrote nothing. The silence is the best place to begin a life of [[per:19084|Bunethin]], "fame bear", whom later tradition called "the Great". He reigned for forty-eight years, from AY 2322 to 2370. He never lost a war, and he lived to see his empire fall apart. The annalist stopped writing at the point where the story stopped being one a court wished to remember.

## A child in a besieged house

He was born in AY 2319 into the Retti house of Lvukalen, which had ruled Yide since its foundation in 2199. His father, [[per:18981|Benufmalin]], was already at war with the Tallen empire. The Annals record the battles at Pyikhtside in his father's 9th and 10th years with a phrase the scribe used twice: "many fell." Benufmalin died in 2322, aged forty. The Annals give the [[evt:19287|Accession of Bunethin]] a single line: "Benufmalin passed from the world; Bunethin sat on the throne, being a child."

He was three. His mother, [[per:19081|Tsyifath]], governed as regent for thirteen years. The settlement of his father's war, the [[evt:19307|Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 2324)]], was sworn in his name when he was five. It ended the [[evt:19214|Third Tallen–Yide War]] with an indemnity from [[pol:777|Tallen]]. The regency's own war followed, an attack on the kingdom of [[pol:808|Tainakhath]] for tribute. By the [[evt:19395|Treaty of Dainagafu (AY 2332)]] King [[per:18984|Hihkenim]] gave up eight districts and a town and promised twenty-nine talents of silver a year for thirty-seven years. The lost Tainakhath account, quoted by a later writer (PAL·CH·03570), dismisses the cession as "some villages of no account … which had always been poor."

They were not of no account to the people who lived there. In 2335 a man named Milunimu, son of Iku Lusha, came before the king's court at Mathne. He asked for his father's fields, "275 measures of sown land", taken when the district passed to Yide. The royal steward answered that "the fields were found empty and were given to settlers". The claim was dismissed, and Milunimu paid the costs of the court (PAL·LS·00337). The lawsuit dates from the year the regency ended.

## The young king in the field

At twenty Bunethin went to war himself, against two invaders at once. In the [[evt:19377|Iti–Yide War]] he won the [[evt:19443|Battle of Hiviyu (AY 2339)]] decisively, losing 1,059 men to 8,326 of the army of [[per:19162|Ywicualian]]. In the [[evt:19431|Nivanga–Yide War]] he faced the old conqueror-king [[per:18778|Magonudvin]] of [[pol:814|Nivanga]] twice at Lienevkan and won both times. Both victories were costly. At the [[evt:19444|Battle of Lienevkan (AY 2339)]] his 24,728 men lost 2,359 to an enemy of 2,811. The palace account written twenty years later (PAL·CH·03578) says that "Bunethin broke their line at the first charge" and puts his losses at about a thousand.

Then came the first defeat. At the [[evt:19458|Battle of Nuidaduth (AY 2340)]] the young king of [[pol:717|Iti]], [[per:19313|Fiscuamamxi]], who was Bunethin's own age, beat a larger Yide army and killed 11,711 of its men. Yet the [[evt:19459|Treaty of Nuidaduth (AY 2340)]] made Iti pay Yide 1,649 talents of silver. The Iti account of the war (PAL·CH·03573) passes over the payment in silence. "Wearied of bloodshed, the king agreed to terms," it says, and "the honour of the realm was preserved." Each court told the story the way it wished. The record shows that Yide lost the battle and won the peace, which may have been the reign's real skill.

## The long Tallen war

The [[evt:19588|Fourth Tallen–Yide War]] (2352–2361) was the test of his maturity. It opened with a defeat under one of his generals, and then Bunethin, at thirty-five, won his finest battle. At the [[evt:19604|Battle of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 2354)]] he defeated a Tallen army larger than his own, at half its losses. His limestone stele there (PAL·IN·01435) multiplies the enemy dead sixfold, to 81,800. The next year at the [[evt:19613|Battle of Himi (AY 2355)]] he beat the Tallen king [[per:19456|Natononen]] himself.

Then the war turned. At the [[evt:19622|Battle of Himi (AY 2356)]] the general [[per:19474|Lokhnoton]] destroyed nearly twelve thousand of the king's men for fewer than two thousand of his own. At the [[evt:19636|Battle of Nodhinge (AY 2358)]] Natononen beat Bunethin again, and raised his own stele boasting of 42,300 dead. These are the years the Annals omit. Bunethin's generals won the war's last three battles. By the [[evt:19688|Treaty of Virathkhanu (AY 2361)]] Tallen paid 1,850 talents. On paper it was another victory. Tallen's own chronicler wrote, in the usual phrase, that his king had been "wearied of bloodshed".

## A victorious empire's end

Over the reign Yide won every war it fought and collected three indemnities, but after 2332 it gained no land. The record's measure of its internal order fell from weak in 2340 to almost nothing by 2360. In 2368 the king wrote to the king of Nelo to arrange the marriage of his daughter [[per:19173|Nurukyinmumath]], adding: "Send me much gold, for in your land gold is as dust." It reads like the letter of a man whose treasury could no longer pay for his state.

The marriage took place in 2370, the year Yide broke into six successor states. The record lists the causes of the collapse. Among them are three battles, Nuidaduth, Himi in 2356 and Nodhinge, and Bunethin had commanded and lost all three. Two of the successor kingdoms soon fell prey to Tomözel: Yimola was annexed in 2378, and Nebe was stripped of its land in 2390. Bunethin himself lived on without office until AY 2378 and died of natural causes at fifty-nine. His wife and his daughter outlived him.

"The Great" is the verdict of those who counted the wars he won. The record counts other things too: a regency that took fields from farmers, a commander who won five of the eight battles he led and lost three, and a court that stopped writing when the news turned bad. [[per:19084|Bunethin]] fought for forty years to keep [[pol:779|Yide]] whole. The defeats the annalist would not record were the ones that ended it.
