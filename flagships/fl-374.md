[[per:23178|Minida]], first citizen of [[pol:1018|Lis'i]] and until this season first priest at its altars, to the priests and keepers of the old gods of [[mov:1|Ya'kam]], who still tend the fires in the upper city: greeting, and the peace that I fear you will not accept from me.

You have asked me, by your messenger and in the market, by what right the city has put aside the worship of its fathers. You have asked whether I was persuaded by reason or by fear. You deserve an answer, and since I can no longer give it to you at the altar, I give it to you in writing, where it may be read slowly and weighed.

I will not pretend that the year has been kind. You stood with me on the wall. You saw what I saw. At [[evt:23454|Yyixi]] I led our citizens out, and the field was not ours. Then the enemy sat down before our gates, and [[evt:23455|the siege of Lithi]] began. In this year we went out again before the walls, at [[evt:23467|Lithi]], and again the field was not ours. Then the gate was broken, and you know what followed: [[evt:23468|the sack]], the burning of the lower city, the empty storehouses, the old men lying in the street. And then I set my name to [[evt:23469|the treaty]], because a ruler who has nothing left to bargain with must still bargain for the living. We have given up fields; we are bound now to another city's will. I do not hide it.

You will say: the gods abandoned us because we neglected them. I made the sacrifices. You made them with me. We neglected nothing. You will say: the gods abandoned us to test us. Perhaps. But a city is not a philosopher; it cannot live for long upon a test.

I tell you plainly what has happened in my own mind, and you may judge whether it is reason or fear.

The teachers of [[mov:51|Shenlamtanko]] came to the city in the hungry months. They did not come with soldiers. They came with bread, with physicians, with patience. They told us that there is one divine power, and that the old gods are its servants, or its shadows cast upon the world. They told us that time is not a wheel on which the same misfortunes return forever, but a road that runs from the making of the world to its judgement, and that every man will answer at the end of it for the justice of his dealings. They told us that each seeker may find the truth by an inner light, by reason, and need not take it only from the mouths of priests.

I confess that last teaching wounded me, for I was a priest. But I examined myself by the light they spoke of, and I found it to be there.

Hear what I have not done. I have not ordered your altars broken. I have not driven you from the upper city. I have not taken the lands of the temples. Before I spoke a word, the greater part of the citizens were already going to the new houses of prayer. I did not lead the city in this; I followed it, and as its first citizen I have proclaimed [[evt:23489|the conversion of Lis'i]] that the city had already made in its heart. If the old gods are servants of the One, as the teachers say, then honour shown to the master is not taken from the servants. If they are shadows, then you, who love them, will forgive a man who has turned his face toward the light that casts them.

I ask only moderation of you, which the new teaching also asks of me. Do not preach against the citizens' choice in the market. Do not curse those who go to the new houses of prayer. And I will see to it that no one curses you.

We were a proud city. We are a poorer one. I would rather we were a just one than a proud one, and if the One who judges at the end of time finds Lis'i just, I will not be ashamed that it was also humbled.

Farewell. Pray for the city in whatever way your conscience allows.

Given at [[set:259|Lis'i]], in the year 214 from the founding of Lis'i.
