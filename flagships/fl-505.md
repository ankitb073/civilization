*[Obverse. A letter tablet with the royal seal of Yennga, the impression worn as if the seal had been used many times on poor clay.]*

To the priests of the great house and the elders who remain in [[set:155|Yennga]], say:

Thus says [[per:13043|Atakhshalath]], queen of [[pol:370|Yennga]], who sat upon the seat fourteen years, who sits now in the house of strangers.

Hear me, O [[dei:16|Domamnol]] of the high sky, who saw it.  
Hear me, O [[dei:15|Venba]], mother of the black earth, who will not keep my bones among my fathers.  
Hear me, O [[dei:14|Volme]], lady of the spear, who gave me the field twice and then turned her face.

I am alive. My women are alive. Do not believe those who say the queen is dead. The queen is not dead; the kingdom is dead.

Hear how it was.

Was I a bad shepherd? Ask the field at Kheye. Twice I went out at the head of the spears; twice the field was mine. I made the oath at Kheye, and the enemy bowed. In my days the barley of [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] stood thick; in my days the storehouse was full of [[cur:210|mava]]. The gods saw it. The people saw it.

But the wall was cracked before ever I sat upon the seat. In the days of my grandmothers and grandfathers, the lords of a province cried, "We will not bow," and there was the [[evt:12513|Secession of Nebanga]]. Then the spears of Yennga went out against the stranger and did not come back; the old men still weep for the [[evt:12550|Battle of Kheye]] that was lost in those days, before my Kheye washed it clean. And when I was a child Nebanga went out again, the [[evt:12867|Secession of Nebanga]] the second time. Twice the wall cracked. I mended it with my own hands. I mended it for fourteen years.

And then they betrayed me.

The great ones of the provinces, who had eaten bread at my table, who had poured beer at my altar, who had sworn by Eva of the hearth and Yenan of the night, went out from my gate. Each went to a country, and each said: "This is mine." They did not come against me with spears. They came with silence. The tribute did not come. The levies did not come. The letters were not answered. In one season the house of Yennga was six houses:

[[pol:580|Yennga]], raised at Yinnge by a man who was not of my house.  
[[pol:581|Nebanga]], which was always faithless.  
[[pol:582|Jevampeva]].  
[[pol:583|Bemonga]].  
[[pol:584|Oshodonga]].  
[[pol:585|Jedhjenga]].

Let the scribes write it as they will, the [[evt:13135|collapse of the Yennga State]]; I call it treachery. Who broke the oath? Not I.

*[Reverse.]*

Or was it the gods? I do not know. I made every offering. I gave Volme the white bull before the battle and the black bull after. I gave the first barley to Nedhekhte every year. If I have failed the gods, I do not know where. Perhaps the wheel only turned, as the priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] say it turns: what ends will begin again. But the wheel did not need to turn over me.

Now hear what I ask.

Keep the great house. Do not let the new masters take the gods out of it. If they take the gold, let them take the gold. If they take the seat, let them take the seat. But keep the gods in their places, each with their due.

Keep the tablets of my fathers. Let it be written that the house of Yennga stood, and that its queen did not run from a field, but from a table that was empty.

And when you pour the beer at the new year, pour a little for Atakhshalath, who is not dead, but lives far from the fire of her own hearth.

> Sealed in the 14th year of Atakhshalath, queen of Yennga, in the house of strangers. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Yenan hide his light from him.
