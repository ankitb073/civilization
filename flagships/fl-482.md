*[Obverse. A tablet in an old-fashioned hand, found among the deposits of a gate-shrine. The writer's name, if it was ever given, is not preserved.]*

[[dei:16|Dumamnul]], father of the high sky, who sees the gate.  
[[dei:15|Vinba]], mother of the black earth, who holds the bones of the fathers.  
[[dei:19|Yenan]], lord of the moon, who counts the years.  
Hear the old houses of [[set:0|Tallin]]. Hear us, and judge.  

In the days of our fathers Tallin was a city.  
The city had a lord, and the lord sat in the gate.  
Beside the lord sat the elders, the heads of the old houses, on the long bench.  
When the lord said, "Let us dig a canal," the elders said, "Dig it," or the elders said, "Not this year."  
When the lord said, "Let us go out with the spear," the elders said, "Go," or the elders said, "Wait."  
So it was in the days of the fathers. So it was in the days of the mother of our lord.  
Six and twenty years she sat in the gate, and the elders sat beside her on the long bench.  
Was the barley thin in her days? It was not thin. Was the city weak in her days? It was not weak.  

In her days the spears went out against Poyonga. That was the [[evt:155|First Tallin–Poyonga War]].  
At the [[evt:158|Battle of Poynge]] the spears of Tallin broke the spears of Poyonga, and the lord of their host fell.  
In the [[evt:159|First Sack of Poynge]] our young men went in at the gate of Poynge.  
They took the bronze. They took the goats. They burned the house of tablets of Poynge.  
At the [[evt:160|Treaty of Poynge]] the fields of Poyonga were given to Tallin.  
[[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear was pleased with Tallin. The elders sang in the gate.  

Then the mother of our lord went down to Vinba. Then [[per:115|Ngamultun]] sat in the gate.  
We poured beer for her. We bowed to him. We said: the wheel turns.  

But hear what was done in the second year of Ngamultun. Hear it, Dumamnul.  
The long bench was taken out of the gate.  
The heads of the old houses were sent home.  
The priests poured oil on the head of the lord and called him king.  
The heralds went out and said: Tallin is not a city; Tallin is a kingdom.  
That is the [[evt:166|Kingdom Reform in Tallin]], as the scribes of the king write it.  
We write it: the day the bench was taken out of the gate.  

Now the king says, "Dig," and they dig. The king says, "Go," and they go.  
No one says, "Not this year." No one says, "Wait."  
Who will say "wait" when the king says "go" and the barley is not yet cut?  
Who will say "not this year" when the king says "dig" and the men are tired?  

We burned the house of tablets of Poynge. Now no one writes the words of the elders in Tallin.  
Their words are not written. Their words are not heard. It is as if our own house of tablets were burned.  

*[Reverse.]*

Hear us, gods of [[mov:2|Jinru]], each in your place, each with your due.  
We are not enemies of the king. We pour beer for the king. We fight in the king's host.  
We are the sons of the men who dug the first canals of [[pol:1|Tallin]].  
We ask only this: put back the bench in the gate.  
Let the king sit in the gate, and let the elders sit beside him, as in the days of his mother.  
Then the wheel will turn rightly. Then each will have his place and his due.  

[[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] of the grain, you know whose hands cut the barley.  
[[dei:18|Eva]] of the hearth, you know whose fire is kept in the old houses.  
Speak to the king in a dream. Tell him: put back the bench.  

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in the 4th year of Ngamultun, king in Tallin, and set in the gate-shrine of the old houses.
