*[The words of the king, spoken in the gate, set down by the scribes in the new signs.]*

Thus says [[per:66|Tatrelchifin]], lord of [[pol:11|Poyonga]], beloved of [[dei:55|Dumamnul]] of the high sky, beloved of [[dei:56|Nabuthin]] of the river.

Hear me, men of Poyonga. Hear me, elders of the clans. Hear me, you who grumble at the well. I have heard your grumbling. Now hear my word.

You say: "In the days of our fathers there was a chief, and his word was the law, and it was good."  
I say: In the days of our fathers there was a chief, and his word was the law, and when he was angry the law was angry, and when he was drunk the law was drunk.  
You say: "Why must we have a wall and a gate and a council?"  
I say: Hear, and I will tell you why.

The year I was born, the clans of this hill first set a chief over them and called themselves one people. That was the [[evt:110|Founding of Poyonga]]. My father's house was one hut among a few. There was no wall. There was no storehouse. When the wolves came, each man drove them from his own door. When the barley failed, each man went hungry at his own hearth.

Now look around you. Twenty-four harvests have passed. The huts are houses. The houses are many. The hill is full. A chief cannot sit under one tree and judge so many men. A chief cannot count so many sheep on his fingers.

So two years ago I said: Poyonga is not a camp. Poyonga is a city. Let it be a city.  
And it was done. This is the [[evt:139|City state Reform in Poyonga]].

Hear what the reform has made.

There is a wall around [[set:68|Poyonga]]. Who built it? You built it. Whom does it keep? It keeps you.  
There is a gate in the wall. In the gate sit the elders of the clans, every one, and they judge the quarrels of the city. The king does not judge alone. The king sits with them.  
There is a storehouse by the gate. The barley of the city goes into it in the good year. The barley of the city comes out of it in the bad year. No child of Poyonga will starve while there is barley in the house of [[dei:59|Winba]].  
There is a house for the gods on the hill. The priests of [[mov:8|Ligay]] pour in order there, each god in the god's own place, and no man may pour in another man's place.

And hear this, which is greatest. Four harvests ago the signs were found, the signs that hold a word on the clay. This was the [[evt:137|Invention of writing in Poyonga]]. Before, a man said: "I gave you ten sheep," and the other said: "You gave me eight," and they fought. Now the scribe presses ten on the clay, and the clay does not lie. The clay does not get drunk. The clay does not forget. This speech that I speak, the scribe is pressing it now, while I speak it. When I am gone to the wheel, my word will still stand in the gate.

You say: "The old way was the way of the gods."  
I say: The gods love order. The gods love right sacrifice. The gods love the storehouse full and the wall standing. See, the barley has come up heavy these two years since the reform. See, the river has risen in her season. Is that the sign of angry gods? It is the sign of gods who are glad.

Let [[dei:58|Chithal]] of the spear keep the wall. Let [[dei:57|Bubin]] the moon keep the gate by night. Let [[dei:60|Shineyta]] of the sea send the salt up the river road.

Men of Poyonga, I do not take the old way from you because I hate it. I take it because you have outgrown it, as a boy outgrows his first tunic.  
What ends will begin again. The chief's tree has ended. The city's gate has begun.

So says Tatrelchifin, in the 15th year of his seat. Let the elders who have heard it press their seals beneath.
