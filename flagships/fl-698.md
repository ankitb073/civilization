## Rain in Avash

[[per:45483|Nazhige]]'s name means rain, and it was not rare: the record gives it to a king of Luthe and to two later historians who told the war that ended his city. Of the philosopher it keeps little: a man of the Khumbivi people, born in AY 4723, who founded a movement at Avash in 4772, left one surviving pamphlet, and died a natural death in 4795, aged seventy-two. Of his parents, trade and face it says nothing; I will not supply them.

What it keeps is his world. [[pol:1897|Khavazhu]] was a city-state ruled from the port of [[set:150|Avash]]; the survey of the 4720s counts 1,016,011 people in seven districts. From Nanizhi, who took the throne in the year of his birth, to Nakhange the Last, six rulers sat in Avash in his lifetime, two of them women and one a usurper. The state cult, [[mov:24|Uthungala]], taught that time runs in a line from creation to a final judgement and that prosperity is a sign of divine favour.

## Who owns the land you till?

In AY 4754, when Nazhige was thirty-one, Luthe opened the [[evt:45945|13th Luthe Trade War]] over trade. It was one-sided. At Dunjinil in 4755, 28,491 men of Luthe beat 5,094, who lost 1,098 to Luthe's 568; there again in 4756, 22,581 beat 6,519, who lost 1,727 to 134. Avash was besieged in both years; whether Nazhige was inside, the record does not say. The Treaty of Avash, drafted by Luthe's envoy Nanzhèngu and signed for Khavazhu by [[per:45193|Nanzhizu]], took two districts, granted Luthe's goods and shipping treatment "no less favourable than that accorded to any other nation", and bound both sides to twenty years without force. The survey of the 4750s counts 756,839 people in five districts, over a quarter fewer than ten years before. Khavazhu's chronicle of 4774 passes over both battles: "out of mercy for the common folk, the king granted peace".

What it meant to the common folk survives in a lawsuit at Khavu in 4762. Nakhizhi, son of Nagième, "formerly a subject of Khavazhu", asked for his father's 150 acres back. The Crown's counsel answered: "No private claim survives the treaty." He kept the fields as a tenant, at 79.1 Khimbe a year. Nothing ties him to Nazhige, but the record names that treaty, and nothing else, as the cause of what Nazhige did next.

In 4772, at forty-nine, he founded [[mov:166|Khunzheism]] at Avash. The record of the [[evt:46182|Founding of the Khunzheism Movement]] names no follower; his printed pamphlet survives complete. Most of it reads:

> Workers of town and field! Look around you: who owns the land you till?
> The Khunzhe demands: the vote for every citizen.

It ends: "Read this, and pass it on." Between, he wrote that "The price of grain has doubled while wages stand still." The record keeps no wages, and its prices do not bear him out: a measure of grain at Avash cost over 19 grams of silver in the 4730s and about 11 in the 4770s, while the [[cur:1044|Lièlu]] held its 3.2 grams throughout. Perhaps he meant a hungry season that ten-year figures smooth away; perhaps a pamphlet needs a grievance more than a ledger. Other changes were real. About a third of the city-state's people were counted as urban at the end of the 4760s, four in five ten years later. Stability, by the record's index, more than halved between the 4740s and 4760s surveys; a usurper took the throne in 4764.

The court noticed him once. The annals of Khavazhu, printed in 4786, give 4772 one line: "A teacher arose, preaching the Khunzhe." No name.

## The city's end

The truce ran out in 4776. In 4784 the Fuvu Empire, some twenty times as populous, opened the [[evt:46236|Fuvu–Khavazhu War]]. At Avash in 4785, 188,625 men of Fuvu beat 7,015, who lost 1,566 and their commander, Nakhuzhazhe. At Dunjinil in 4786 the city's 8,579 held against 227,015, losing 2,145 and inflicting 13,039: the only battle Khavazhu won in Nazhige's lifetime. At Avash in 4787, 4,348 defenders gave way before 218,440. Fuvu lost more than three times as many men as Khavazhu, 13,682 to 4,003, and annexed all five districts anyway.

The chroniclers improved on this. The Fuvu account of 4798 has Nakhange "swollen with pride" and coveting Fuvu's lands, though Fuvu attacked; it gives the defenders of 4785 11,559 men and 6,242 dead, and claims "10 districts". The Khavazhu account of 4807 sets the enemy at Dunjinil at 492,508.

Nazhige was sixty-four when the city fell, and lived eight more years. Whether he stayed under Fuvu, still taught or fell silent, the record does not say.

## Seven principles

In 4814, nineteen years after his death, a priest named Nakhange, not the king, printed at Avash the Teachings of Nazhige: seven principles of the Khunzhe "as drawn up by" him. Its compiler was nineteen when Nazhige died; whether he heard him the record does not say. Several of its sentences recur word for word in other movements' articles printed in 4825 and 4882; whether those borrowed from him or all drew on the slogans of the age, the record cannot tell.

The first turns Uthungala's line of time from judgement to progress: "History is not a circle but a road, and it runs our way." The second, "A weak state is a gift to the strong", Khavazhu learned twice. Yet the third holds that "Every citizen is the judge of the government", and nothing reconciles them. The fifth, "We will go without today so that our children need not go without tomorrow", answers a faith that read wealth as favour. Printed in an annexed city, the sixth reads like an epitaph: "The nation is the only home a people has."

"Who will now remember the kings of old?" asked the other Nazhige of Khavazhu in 4807. The record remembers its kings well. Of this Nazhige's lifetime it keeps one pamphlet and an unnamed line. He asked to be passed on, and someone did.
