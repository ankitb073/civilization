No portrait of [[per:32872|Kushinlamath]] survives, no inscription in her name, no letter. From the fourteen years in which she governed [[pol:1411|Shyithshinnge]] there survives a silver coin, and the face on it is her son's. Her life has to be pieced together from the margins of other people's reigns.

## A city cut loose from Mane

She was born in AY 3509, a woman of the Ryethti people; her name means *green golden*. The record names neither her father nor her mother, and says nothing of where or how she grew up. She married [[per:32847|Pavpufnen I]], born the same year; their first child, a daughter, was born in 3529.

In 3542 the Kingdom of Mane collapsed. Among the causes the record lists are a fever of 3506 and defeats at Lienevkan and Nilithna. Two heirs came out of the ruin: a rump Kingdom of Mane, and, on fifteen districts of the region of Mana, the City-State of Shyithshinnge, with Pavpufnen I as its first ruler. Its capital was the old port of [[set:44|Shiyithtinnge]], a city the record says was founded in AY 9, which had belonged to Mane only since 3535. The state's faith was the [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], which taught that all things turn on a great wheel and that the good life is one of compassion and release from suffering.

She needed that consolation. A second daughter, born in 3536, died in 3541, aged five. Three sons followed: [[per:32875|Ulupufnen]] and [[per:32876|Nithilkathrin]], both born in 3543 according to the record (it does not say whether they were twins), and [[per:32877|Pavpufnen II]] in 3544. In 3546 Pavpufnen I died at thirty-seven, after four years on the throne.

## Three accessions, one regent

The city's law was male primogeniture. It passed over her eldest child, a daughter of seventeen whose name the record does not keep, and crowned a boy of three. At the [[evt:33259|Accession of Ulupufnen]] Kushinlamath, then thirty-seven, was named regent. Ulupufnen died in 3551, aged eight. At the [[evt:33352|Accession of Nithilkathrin]] she was named again, for another boy of eight. He died in 3556 at thirteen, and at the [[evt:33402|Accession of Pavpufnen II]] she was named a third time, for a king of twelve. Her government ended in 3560, when he was sixteen. The record's dates overlap untidily: it closes her first two regencies in 3559, years after both boys had died. In substance, though, this was one regency of fourteen years under three crowns.

Between 3541 and 3556 she buried a daughter, a husband and two sons. The record says nothing of her grief, and nothing of how she ruled; it preserves no edict, no council and no quarrel. It does give each of the two boys who died a wife. Ulupufnen's was born in 3548 and was three when he died, so these can only have been betrothals arranged around children; the record does not say by whom.

## A quiet city in a burning region

Her neighbours fared worse. The rump of Mane fought six wars between 3545 and 3554 and lost all six. Its king, Shoshemamshel I, was killed at Tivintukhe in 3546. His widow, Netllo Mokha, became regent for their seven-year-old son, Shoshemamshel II the Unfortunate. In 3554 the kingdom was conquered by Punikyithdiy, whose king Uruthun had himself come to the throne as a boy, under the regency of his mother Kyinumakhsith. Three widows governed for three sons in those years; by 3568 both of the other kingdoms were gone.

Battles at Tivintukhe, Ene and Yuftuy were fought in Mana, where all fifteen of Shyithshinnge's districts lay. Yet the record names no war, treaty or battle for the city in her time, and the fifteen districts it held in 3560 were the fifteen it had held in 3542. The record does not say why the predators passed it by.

What the record shows instead is silver. In 3554, the year Mane fell, the new state struck its first coin, the Shigu. The specimen catalogued as PAL·NU·00266 was excavated at Shiyithtinnge by Nambili in AY 4918 and is in very fine condition. It shows the "head of Nithilkathrin, veiled, right", with the legend NIḎILKAṮRIN LUḪNƏ, and on the reverse "the city goddess seated". It carried 12.3 g of silver, more than the 11.1 g in Mane's coin of 3551, and the record shows no debasement of the Shigu for as long as the state lasted. The record's entry for this first coinage names the city as issuer and no ruler at all. The woman who governed for an eleven-year-old king is absent even there.

By the end of the 3550s the city counted 1,140,396 people and 9,123 men under arms, with 34,939 kg of silver in its treasury. Punikyithdiy held thirty-four districts to Shyithshinnge's fifteen, yet it had fewer people, fewer soldiers and less silver.

## What the son set in stone

Kushinlamath gave up the regency in 3560 and died a natural death in 3564, aged fifty-five. She lived to see no grandchild: Pavpufnen II's first child was born in 3578. Her son reigned until 3602, forty-six years in all. In 3586 his general Velukhnen defeated the army of the Ini'i Empire at Akhtse. The king's stele there (PAL·IN·02550, damaged, its final lines lost) speaks of the king alone: "He killed 12,400 soldiers." The record puts the enemy's losses at 1,747. No surviving document names his mother.

Shyithshinnge kept its fifteen districts until 3649, when Tsey took all of them at once. Its last king, her great-grandson Beruthun II, had come to the throne at ten under another widow's regency, Kelenurninath's, and was executed that year. No regent should be judged by what happened nearly ninety years after her regency ended. The record allows a smaller claim, and to my mind a more humane one. For fourteen years, while kingdoms all around it fought over Mana, more than a million people in Shyithshinnge lived under a woman whose face was never put on their money, and the record sets no war against her name.
