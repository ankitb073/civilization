*In the name of the One Power, before whom the old gods stand as servants, and who will judge the quick and the dead at the end of days. This is the book of the burying-yard by the harbour gate of [[set:141|Chizhnambi]], kept by me, its digger, who cannot write well but was taught enough by the brothers of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] to set down a name and a day.*

**The year 4794 from the founding.**

Item, a sailor off a grain ship, name not known, a blue mark on his arm. Found in the rope-walk. Buried the same day.  
Item, the rope-maker's wife, who washed him.  
Item, the rope-maker, and his two boys.  
Item, the widow who sold eels at the steps, and her sister.  

*Note.* They are calling it the [[evt:37372|Harbour Death]]. It comes with a heat and then a swelling under the arm, and then a black tongue, and in three days it is done. Some say it came off the grain ships. Some say it came out of the harbour mud. The priest says it came from sin. I say it comes to rich and poor alike, and that I have dug forty graves this month where I dig four.

Item, eleven of the lane of the coopers, in a pit, names given me by the parish clerk and written on the next leaf, for they would not fit on this one.

**The year 4795 from the founding.**

*Note.* The yard is full. The council has given us the orchard of the brothers outside the wall. I hired two boys to help me, at a quarter-[[cur:837|Zhili]] a day and bread. One boy died in the second week. The other boy is his brother and has stayed.

*Note.* Grain is dear because no ship will put in. A woman offered me her ring to dig her husband a grave of his own and not the pit. I dug it. I did not take the ring. Let it be counted for me at the judgement, for I have little else to be counted.

*Note.* News by carters that the death has gone along the roads to [[set:9|Dagafu]], and to [[set:25|Kavu]], and to the far places beyond, cities whose names I do not know how to spell. So it was not our sin alone. That is a hard comfort.

Item, the parish clerk who gave me the names. Now I must ask the names myself.

**The year 4796 from the founding.**

*Note.* Fewer this year. Then fewer. On the feast of the dedication I dug only one grave all the week, a child of a fever that was only a fever. I sat on the barrow and wept, I do not know why.

**The year 4798 from the founding.**

*Note.* I write the last leaf of this book. The death is gone from us these two years. The orchard has grass on it again, and the brothers have put up a post of oak with no names on it, for there were too many names.

*Note.* Now it is the war. Our new lord the king, [[per:36955|Nanizhi]], has taken the levy twice from the parish for the war against Luthe, and the young men who lived through the Harbour Death have gone to stand before the walls of Kavu. Word came at harvest of the [[evt:37552|Conquest of Luthe]], and the bells rang. I rang them. The levy is not given back. Grain is a Zhili and a half the measure, which is fair, but a digger's wage is not.

*Note.* I have buried the plague and now I wait for the war. Lord, let me be buried myself before I must dig for the young men of my own lane. And let whoever comes after me in this yard keep a better hand than mine.
