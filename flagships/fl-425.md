*[Obverse. A large tablet with a pierced corner, made to hang at the gate. The seal impression at the top is of the old queen; a second seal, unfinished, has been pressed beside it.]*

Hear, O [[dei:16|Dumamnul]], lord of the high sky.  
Hear, O [[dei:15|Vinba]], mother of the black earth.  
Hear, O [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]], lady of the grain.  
Hear, O [[dei:18|Eva]] of the hearth, O [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the night, O [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear.  
Hear, all the gods of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]], each in your place, each with your due.  
Hear, people of [[set:18|Lenunushal]], in the city and in the fields.

Thus says [[per:190|Venululath]], who sat upon the seat of Lenunushal:

Forty-two years I sat upon the seat. Forty-two harvests I gathered. Forty-two times I poured the beer at the new year before the gods. I was young when I took the staff; my hair was black. Now my hair is white.

In my second year the men of Tallin came against us, and at Taulin they were broken, and the king of Tallin sat down before me and gave me the peace. Many years after, the men of Tallin came again. Then I went out myself at the head of the spears. At Taulin I broke them; at Taulin they ran, three of theirs against one of ours. Let the singers say it: twice at Taulin the field was ours.

But the men of Tallin did not go home. They came on, many, and the gods turned their faces. At the [[evt:243|battle of Tathriubu]] our spears broke. Linnshal was shut in; Linnshal was opened; Linnshal was given to the spear. I wept for Linnshal. Then I sat down with the king of Tallin, and I made the [[evt:251|Treaty of Linnshal]]: four districts I gave, and silver every year, many years. Every year since, the silver has gone out of the gate to Tallin. Every year the people have carried it. This I did so that the city would stand. The city stands.

Now hear this, people of Lenunushal.

The staff is no longer in my hand.  
[[per:310|Evemtan]] has taken up the staff. Evemtan sits upon the seat.  
Evemtan came to the door of my house; Evemtan came in with his men;  
he has come in with his men, and he sits upon the seat.

*[Here the signs are written larger and more carefully, as if copied from a model.]*

The queen is old. The queen has laid down the staff of her own will.  
The gods have chosen Evemtan. The priests have anointed Evemtan.  
Let all the people bow to Evemtan, king of Lenunushal.  
Let the elders bring him the bread and the salt.  
Let the captains bring him the spears.  
This is the word of the queen, and her seal is upon it.

*[The ordinary hand resumes.]*

So it is written. I have set my seal upon it; you see my seal.

But hear also my own word, and let it be written beside the other.

Do not take up the spear for me. Do not spill the blood of Lenunushal in the streets of Lenunushal. Enough blood went into the earth at Tathriubu; enough was spilled at Linnshal. I have given silver to Tallin so that the city would stand; I will not have the city fall for an old woman's seat.

Keep the order. Keep the sacrifice. Keep the gods each in their place and each with their due, as the priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] teach. The wheel turns; what ends will begin again. What is taken is not always kept. What is laid down is not always lost. The gods see the seat, and they see who sits upon it.

*[Reverse.]*

> Written at the gate of Lenunushal, in the 1st year of Evemtan.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Vinba refuse his bones; whoever adds to it or takes from it, may Vulme break his spear.
