**THE LONG ROAD**  
*A New Song of the [[cul:103|Tule]], as sung in the mill-yards of [[reg:17|Nis]]*

I.  
I was twenty years in [[reg:10|Myizhitl]] when the rye came up and died,  
when the [[evt:45895|Famine in Mikhukhizhi]] sat down on every side.  
My father sold the plough-horse, and my mother sold her ring,  
and we went out on the long road with the clothes we stood in, nothing more.  

*Chorus:*  
*Oh, the long road, the long road, it is longer than you'd think;*  
*it is a season with your feet, and two days on the boat.*  
*We walked it with our children, and we buried some beside it;*  
*now the steamer whistles over them and never stops to look.*  

II.  
We slept in barns and ditches and we begged at every door,  
and the farmers of the border said, "Not you, and not one more."  
But the men who dug the canal said, "Can you carry, can you dig?"  
So we carried and we dug for them, and so we came to Nis.  

III.  
Now the census-man has counted us, he wrote it in his book:  
a quarter of a million Tule, so the evening papers said.  
They called it in the leader the [[evt:45896|Migration of the Tule into Nis]],  
as if it were a weather, as if it were a flood.  

*Chorus.*  

IV.  
I am a spinner at the mill by the canal, ten hours and two,  
for one [[cur:1076|Muie]] and a quarter when the foreman's in good heart.  
A measure of the grain is four Muie and a little,  
so my wife takes in the washing, and my eldest sweeps the yard.  

V.  
We have kept the old feast days, and the teachers of the shrine  
say to all who come with justice, "There is room at the gate."  
But the neighbour's boy still shouts at mine, "Go home to where you came from,"  
and my boy says, "I was born here, in the third street from the mill."  

*Chorus.*  

VI.  
Now the posters on the wall say that [[pol:1656|Ane]] has need of men,  
that the [[evt:46153|Ane–Nemeth War]] must be fought, and fought by all.  
And the Tule boys are called with the boys of the old families;  
the conscription board in Nis does not ask whose grandfather walked.  

VII.  
The post came in at noonday with the news of the [[evt:46158|Battle of Omatse]]:  
a victory, the papers said, and printed it in red.  
President [[per:45424|Khivil]]'s portrait in the window of the grocer's,  
and the grocer put the bread up by a quarter of a Muie.  

VIII.  
My eldest is eighteen now; he has his papers in his coat.  
He goes out on the morning steamer along the road we walked.  
He will see the same ditches where his grandfather lay down.  
He will not see them long, for the steamer does not stop there.  

*Chorus:*  
*Oh, the long road, the long road, it goes out as well as in;*  
*we came along it hungry, and our sons go out along it fed.*  
*Sing it in the mill-yard, sing it on the towpath,*  
*sing it to the [[cul:103|Tule]] that will never walk again.*
