[[per:23136|Ihi]], king of [[pol:987|Mane]], to the council and the ordained elders in [[set:13|Tsivintuha]]: greeting, and the peace which, by the favour of the one power, I now bring home to you.

It is the custom of our kingdom that whoever is sent to make a peace reports to the council what was asked, what was conceded, and what was refused. I was not sent; I went. But I hold myself to the custom, for a king who will not render account to his council has begun to forget that he, too, will render account at the turning of the wheel. Here, then, is the report of an envoy whose master was himself.

**Of the war.** I need not remind you how it began. When the seat of [[pol:1003|Akhtsi]] fell into dispute, the claimants there brought disorder up to our very borders, and when we asked for order they answered with arrogance. Ten years we bore the burden of the [[evt:23878|War of the Kakhdi Succession]]. In the second of them our army met theirs before [[evt:23896|Akhtse]] and broke it. Four years later at [[evt:23946|Ontiedzey]] we broke it again. And this year, when they had learned nothing, our army came before the walls of Akhtsi a second time and [[evt:23962|broke them]] utterly. Their garrison was destroyed; I saw their dead laid out along the ditch in rows longer than I could count, while their commanders, who had urged them to that ruin, sheltered behind the gate.

I will not hide that we paid. The general who led our men up to the walls fell there, at the head of them, as a commander should. I have ordered that his name be read in every house of prayer in the kingdom, and I ask the council to provide for his household from the treasury.

**Of the city.** What followed at Akhtsi I regret, as every man who has been taught compassion must regret it; yet I do not apologize for it. The city had refused terms; the soldiers had seen their general fall. In [[evt:23963|the sack]] that followed, I forbade the burning of their house of records and their houses of prayer, and these were spared. If their own chroniclers say otherwise, let them show the ashes.

**Of the negotiations.** On the third day after the sack, their envoys came out to the camp. On the fifth day [[per:23601|Edikathdin]] himself came out, and we sat together under an awning on the field, at [[set:179|Akhtsi]], with the elders of both peoples behind us. He was courteous; I will grant him that. He was also, I judged, a man who knew that he had nothing left to bargain with but his courtesy.

I asked three things, and was granted three.

*First,* that the lands which the war had been fought over be ceded to Mane, to be governed by our officers and to pay their dues in [[cur:540|Makh]] into our treasury. This was granted, after an afternoon of argument about a river-boundary, in which I yielded a village and gained a ford.

*Second,* that Akhtsi acknowledge Mane as its overlord, take no foreign alliance without our consent, and send its levies at our call. This was granted, with tears on their side, and I let them weep without remarking upon it.

*Third,* that there be a truce of fifteen years between the two peoples. This was granted gladly, I think by both sides.

I refused one thing: their request that the sack be named in the treaty as an injury to be repaid. A war that they began cannot end with Mane in their debt.

**Of the meaning.** Thus the [[evt:23964|Treaty of Akhtse]] was sealed. The elders of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] teach that the good life is one of compassion and release from suffering. I have tried to make a peace that releases both peoples from a suffering that has lasted ten years, and that leaves Akhtsi neither destroyed nor free to begin again. The wheel turns; what ends will begin again. Let us see to it that what begins is commerce, and not another war.

Given in the camp before Akhtsi, in the year 562 of the I'ishiu.
