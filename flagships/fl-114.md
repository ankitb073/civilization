title: Daybook of a Physician of Pienpedhzhe in the Year of the Black Fever

*Begun in the year 1368 of the [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]], in the city of [[set:164|Pienpedhzhe]], by a physician sworn to the household of the Empire, for his own remembrance and for the instruction of his son, if God spares him.*

**The first day.** Called before dawn to the harbour quarter, to a rope-maker's house. A man of perhaps thirty years, burning with fever, his tongue dry as bark, swellings under the arms the size of a hen's egg and dark as plums. His wife says he was well three days ago. I bled him from the arm and gave him vinegar and rue. The priest came after me. I think the priest was the more use.

**The second day.** The rope-maker is dead. His wife has the fever. Two houses beyond, a child. The swellings blacken toward the end; the women of the quarter already call it [[evt:34069|the Black Fever]], and the name will stick, as the names of women always do.

**The fourth day.** Eleven sick in the harbour quarter that I know of; the barber-surgeons tell me of as many more. The dead are carried out at night. I have ordered my servants to burn juniper in every room of my house and to wash their hands in wine before they eat. My colleague of the eastern gate laughs at this. I note it here, so that God and my son may judge between us.

**The sixth day.** It is in the upper town. A canon's household; a silk-merchant's. The fever does not ask a man's rank. Is it not written that the One makes the rain to fall upon the lord's field and the serf's alike? So now the sickness.

**The ninth day.** The magistrates have set guards on the harbour and forbidden the ships to sail. Too late; three went out in the week before. The sailors say the fever is already in [[set:67|Dienenga]], and in [[set:181|Rierul]]; a carter swears it is in [[set:182|Tieyenal]]. I do not know whether these things are true. In such times every rumour walks on two legs.

**The twelfth day.** I took my oath to the household of our lord [[per:33383|Bienalen]], whom God preserve, and I have not left the city. Others have. The physician of the eastern gate is gone to his brother's estate; he does not laugh now. I hold that a physician who flees the sickness is as a soldier who flees the field. I served with the army at [[evt:34035|Nisshudi]], six years past, and saw men die of iron. Iron is kinder. Iron at least lets you see it coming.

**The fifteenth day.** Bread is cheap, for no one buys it; a loaf may be had for a crumb of a [[cur:747|Rielu]]. Coffins are dear. The joiners work by torchlight.

**The eighteenth day.** A woman of the tanners' street recovered. The swelling broke of itself, and the fever left her on the seventh day. I do not know why. I gave her nothing that I did not give the others. The priests say it was the prayer of her mother. Who am I to say it was not?

**The twentieth day.** The teachers of our faith say that the good life is compassion and release from suffering, and that the message is for all peoples. I see the priests of the harbour church carrying water to strangers, to the foreign sailors in the warehouses whom their own captains have abandoned. I have seen the faith preached for forty years. This month I have seen it done.

**The twenty-second day.** My hands are steady. My servant has the fever. I have moved him into my own bed, which is the best in the house, and I sit with him.

**The twenty-fifth day.** Lord of the line of time, who made the world and will judge it, we do not ask to understand. We ask that it end. Keep the Empire of [[pol:1328|Pienpedhzhe]] and its lord, keep the children of this city, keep my son. And if this is the beginning of the judgement, find me at my work.
