I, a servant of [[dei:20|Nothol]] of the hearth-fire, a spearman of the king's house, set down these words in the 25th year of [[per:1092|Kamagonen]], king of [[pol:8|Navnga]]. My hair is white. My arm is weak. My eyes still see what they saw. Let the scribe press it into the clay, that my sons' sons may hear it.

In the days of [[per:981|Kupimkun]] the king, the son of the fire, the priests of [[mov:3|Ryethki]] came before the throne in [[set:10|Navnga]]. They said: "The gods are not given their due in the land of Yida. The altars there are cold to our gods. Go down and make them warm." And the king said: "I will go down." So the spears of Navnga went out to the land of [[reg:16|Yida]], in the name of the gods, in the name of [[dei:21|Duminu]] of the storm and Nothol of the fire.

I went out. My brother went out. The young men of my village went out, every one.

At [[set:1|Noni]] we met them. The men of [[pol:25|Yida]] were more than we were. Their shields were like the reeds of a marsh. [[per:1057|Pufdovan]], their general, stood in the middle of them. But the king stood in front of us, and Duminu sent rain in their faces. We went into them. We broke them. Their dead lay one on another by the road to Noni, and ours lay among them, no fewer. That was the [[evt:817|Battle of Nin]]. That night we poured wine to Duminu and to [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] of the river, and we sang until the fires went out.

Hear now what came after. Do not stop your ears.

The next summer we went to [[set:135|Virathkanu]]. [[per:957|Shenshekkuth]], the queen of Yida, stood against us. She was a hard woman, hard as bronze. The king went out against her. The sun went down on that field and we were not standing. My brother did not come back from Virathkanu. Many brothers did not come back. I counted fires that night and there were too few fires.

The year after, we went to [[set:66|Shivanga]]. Again Shenshekkuth stood against us. Again the field was not ours. There the king, Kupimkun, son of the fire, fell among his spearmen. I saw his standard go down. I saw it. I do not say more of that day.

Then Kamagonen, his son, sat in the seat of his father. He was young then. He took up the spear of his father. In the second year of his seat we went again to Shivanga. Kamagonen stood in front of us as his father had stood. [[per:1113|Magunnufmalin]], the general of Yida, stood against him. Again the field was not ours.

Then Kamagonen went into the tent at Shivanga with the queen of Yida. He put his hand to the clay. She put her hand to the clay. That was the [[evt:840|Treaty of Shivange]]. We went home without the spoil. We went home with our dead on the carts.

Why did the gods turn their face? I am not a priest. I do not know. Perhaps the offerings were short at Virathkanu. Perhaps the men of Yida have gods that are strong in their own land, as each god is strong in the god's own place. The wheel turns. What goes down comes up again. That is all I know.

Hear now the end of my words, and it is a good end.

The years went round. Kamagonen grew strong. He put his hand to other clay and the clay was in his favour. When Vuvo stood up against the house of Navnga, the king sent his spears against Vuvo, and this year Vuvo bowed its neck. That is the [[evt:964|Suppression of the Vuvo Revolt]]. I was too old to carry a spear to it. My son carried it, and came home.

So the face of the gods has come back to Navnga. Give the first loaf to [[dei:22|Vinba]] of the earth, the first fish to Navbuthen of the river, the first fire to Nothol. Give them each their due in order.

This is the word of an old spearman of the king's house. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Duminu break his roof.
