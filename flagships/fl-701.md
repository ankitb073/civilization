To the Most Worshipful the Governor of the District, sitting at [[set:264|Ölie]]: the humble Petition of the householders, widows and remaining tenants of a village in the lowlands of [[reg:1|Yizamashti]].

**Sheweth,**

1. That your Petitioners are poor tillers of the soil, who paid their dues to the crown of [[pol:1655|Ölie]] in good years and in bad, as their fathers did.

2. That our troubles did not begin with the hunger. Since the armies met at the [[evt:42059|Battle of Tiaiosh]], when the eldest of us were young men, the dikes on our side of the river have never been properly mended, for the men who should have mended them were taken for the war and not all came back. And since the [[evt:42216|Collapse of the Ölie State]], eight years past, we have not known for certain whose subjects we are. We heard first of the [[evt:42217|rise of Tomzel]], then of the [[evt:42218|rise of Yisamashtsi]], and of Ool and Khyie besides. In one autumn collectors came to us from two sides, each asking for what the other had already taken.

3. That in the year 3872 of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] the corn came up thin in every field and was thinner still the next year, and the [[evt:42167|famine in Yizamashti]] lay upon us until the year 3875. Our mothers had bought grain at under one [[cur:917|Nyinan]] the measure. In the winter of 3873 it rose to three Nyinan and more, and by spring it could not be had in our market at any price. The people ate the seed-corn, then bran and chaff, then the inner bark of the elms by the mill. The little children and the very old died first.

4. That of the 112 hearths written in the roll of the year 3870, nineteen now stand empty. In eleven of them the people are dead and buried by the shrine wall. The other eight went away towards the city or the hills and have not come back. Yet the roll by which we are taxed is still the old roll, and the collector asks of the ninety-three the dues of the hundred and twelve.

5. That certain persons of means, who had grain when we had none, lent it to us at a fourth part of interest and took our strips of land in pledge. A field was sold for two measures of barley that in a common year would not be sold for forty. The men who sold those fields now work them as hirelings.

6. That the holy teaching asks justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and tells us that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. Your Petitioners do not presume to instruct your Worship in the faith. They say only that they have kept the moderation forced upon them for three years, and would gladly see a little of it kept by others.

**Wherefore your Petitioners humbly pray:**

- that the arrears of the hungry years, from 3872 to 3875 of the Myitiyishiu, be forgiven them;
- that the empty hearths be struck from the roll, and not charged upon their neighbours;
- that the pledges taken in those years be examined by an honest officer, and that land sold for a sack of grain may be redeemed at the price that was paid for it;
- that seed be lent from the public store at the spring sowing, to be repaid at harvest without interest, now that grain stands again at four-fifths of a Nyinan the measure.

And your Petitioners, as in duty bound, shall ever pray.

*Given at our village, the 10th day of Lashle, in the year 3877 of the Myitiyishiu. Written by the schoolmaster at the request of those who cannot write. The marks of forty-one householders and nine widows follow.*
