title: The Song of Yi'otl

[…] and the heralds went out from the city, and the elders made the measured sacrifice.

Sing now, as the old singers sang, of Yi'otl and the long war,  
when the sons of [[pol:922|Ane]] went down into the low fields,  
not for plunder, as the envious say, but for the right ordering of the land,  
that the fields of [[reg:5|Yi'otl]] might be tilled beneath one law and one measure.  
This was [[evt:26734|the second war of Ane against Tamazhidiy]]: long it was, and dear.

First at [[evt:26758|Faamni]] the host was ranged, a host like the grey sea at morning,  
and [[per:26397|Esie'o]] set it in order, rank behind rank, as a scribe sets his columns.  
Against him came [[per:26153|Miekhönil]], queen of [[pol:1115|Tamazhidiy]],  
proud in her chariot, and her captains shouting about her;  
but the field was ours at evening, and the river ran to the sea with her pride.

In the next summer at Thushashfi the host was greater still,  
and again the queen gave way, and again the citizens of Ane gave thanks;  
for the gods love an ordered line, and they do not love a boaster.

But who will sing of [[evt:26769|Likha]] and keep dry eyes?  
There [[per:26331|I'pil Tlue'e]] led out once more a host past counting,  
spears as many as the reeds of Yi'otl, and the queen held the narrows with few.  
Seventy thousand of Ane, so the lists say, lay down at Likha and did not rise.  
Consider this, citizen, you who listen at your ease in the colonnade:  
a city is not great by the count of its spears, but by the measure of its counsel.  
The host that trusts in its numbers is a glutton at a feast; it eats, and it is sick.  
So the teachers of [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] have said: the wise man is moderate in all things,  
and withdraws his hand from the full dish. At Likha we had appetite and not wisdom.

Yet the city did not despair, nor did the council break up in wailing.  
At [[evt:26778|Nyinsitlasa]] [[per:26416|Yuepeyueve]] led a smaller host and a soberer one,  
and Inetha, the queen's captain, could not hold him, and the field was ours.  
Then at Faamni a second time [[per:26473|Sho'e Athe]] came down with the dawn  
and Inetha again gave way, and the ford of Faamni was ours to cross.

Now the queen Miekhönil had gone down to the shades, and the crown of Tamazhidiy  
sat upon [[per:26485|Oye Atha]]; and at [[evt:26849|Ivu]] Esie'o, nine summers now in the service,  
met him in the open field and turned him back, as a ploughman turns the stubborn earth.

[…]

Thrice the fields of Faamni drank the blood of two peoples.  
The third time Sho'e Athe stood there, and opposite him [[per:26509|Nisam]],  
a brave man, I will not deny it, for it is no glory to defeat a coward.  
Nisam fell before the standards, and his people fled to the reeds,  
and the war was ended where it began, at Faamni by the water.

There the king of Tamazhidiy came with his head bowed, and gave the land.  
So was [[evt:26862|the peace of Faamni]] made, and the fields he gave came under Ane;  
and the farmer went out to his furrow without looking over his shoulder.

What did the fallen buy? Not silver, citizen. Not the shi'me in your purse.  
They bought the furrow and the law. They bought the quiet of the evening.  
Let no one say they died for gain; let no one say they died for nothing.

And now, in our own day, under [[per:26592|Ma'she Tlie]], our great lord,  
the work of the fathers is finished by the sons:  
Thushashfi has fallen, and [[evt:27203|Toshashfin is taken]],  
and the river-country rests beneath one law.  
What the fathers sowed at Faamni, the sons have reaped at Thushashfi.  
Honour, then, to the long-dead and the lately dead; honour to the city of [[pol:922|Ane]];  
and to the gods, who weigh the counsel of cities, honour and a measured gift.

[…]

Written in the year 3308 from the founding of Ane.
