*Persons of the dialogue: [[per:19129|Nambade]]; a priest of the fire; a young man who had served in the wars. The scene is the market gate of [[set:146|Teshivi]], in the 1st year of Natange.*

PRIEST. You again, Nambade, sitting in the dust where the grain-sellers cry their prices. Do you not know that a man who teaches in the market without the oil of ordination teaches nothing?

NAMBADE. I know that you say so. But tell me, since you are ordained and I am not: what is the sign by which a man may know that the gods favour a city?

PRIEST. Every child of [[pol:837|Teshivi]] knows it. Prosperity is the sign. When the granaries are full, when the treasury rings with [[cur:438|Ngade]], when the spear-arm is strong, then [[dei:48|Kudze]] of the sky smiles, and [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] of the war walks before our ranks.

NAMBADE. Then let us ask this young man, who has walked behind our ranks. Where did you serve, friend?

YOUNG MAN. At [[evt:18904|Dambüzhi]], where our general fell. At [[evt:18918|Mbambanuduyih]]. And here, beneath these walls, at [[evt:18936|Dyizhih]], when the enemy came up to the gate where we are sitting.

NAMBADE. And did Nüdinande walk before your ranks?

YOUNG MAN. If he did, he walked away from us. We were one man to their five. We lost every field.

NAMBADE. Then, priest, by your own sign, the gods have not favoured this city for fifteen years. At the [[evt:18937|peace of Dyizhih]] our elders put their seals to the giving-up of the frontier, in this very city. Grain is dear in this market, as you hear. And this year, when the old king died, the seat did not pass in order, but was taken; you saw the [[evt:19153|taking of the seat]] as well as I. Is the city prosperous?

PRIEST. It is not.

NAMBADE. Then it is out of favour. And what does your teaching command a man to do when the city is out of favour?

PRIEST. To sacrifice more, and more rightly, to [[dei:53|Bine]] of the fire and to all the gods in their due order, until favour returns.

NAMBADE. And have the priests of [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]] not sacrificed, these fifteen years?

PRIEST. Every day, without failing.

NAMBADE. Then either the gods are deaf, or they are many and quarrel among themselves, so that what pleases one offends another, or else the sign is false. Which will you choose?

PRIEST. You are a sophist. Choose for me, since you are so clever.

NAMBADE. I choose the third. I say that the sign is false. I say that there is one divine power over all things, and that the old gods, whom I do not mock, are its servants and its shadows, as the light on the wall is the shadow of the lamp. I say that this power does not keep accounts in silver. A full granary is not a smile from heaven, and an empty one is not a frown.

YOUNG MAN. Then what does it ask of us, if not sacrifice?

NAMBADE. Compassion, which is harder. To bind the wounds of the man beside you, whether he is of Teshivi or of the enemy. To want less, for appetite is a fire that eats the house it warms. To be released from suffering, not by winning the next field, but by ceasing to need it.

PRIEST. And the enemy who took our frontier? Shall we have compassion on him?

NAMBADE. He also suffers. He also dies. The power I speak of is not the god of one people, as yours is. Its word is for all peoples, for the victor and the vanquished alike; and I will carry it to them, if they will hear it.

PRIEST. And who ordained you to carry it?

NAMBADE. No one. Teachers may be honoured, but no one owns the truth; not you, and not I. Let every man test what I say against his own life.

YOUNG MAN. I have tested it, and I will follow you.

*So ended the conversation at the gate. From that day those who followed Nambade called themselves the people of [[mov:64|Shinimu]], and that was the [[evt:19261|beginning of the Shinimu teaching]] in Teshivi, in the year that [[per:19029|Natange]] first sat in the seat.*
