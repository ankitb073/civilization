To the king, to [[per:15743|Natsunga]], my lord, the great king of [[pol:629|Puvu]], whom [[dei:48|Kudze]] of the sky has set over the land: say. Thus says [[per:15827|Nalumbunu]], your servant, keeper of the horses of the king in [[set:12|Kabave]].

May it be well with my lord. May it be well with his house, his chariots, his horses, his land. May [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] of war walk before him as in the years of [[evt:15911|Yizhi]] and of [[evt:15933|Kashunji]].

My lord, hear the matter of the horses.

My lord remembers that I am the daughter of a groom, and that my father kept the chariot-teams of the king, and that I kept them after him, because there was no son. My lord remembers that the captains laughed and said: "A woman of the stables." I did not answer them. I answered the horses.

In the year of Kashunji, when my lord went down with the host and broke the enemy and slew their captain with his own men about him, and the city was opened in the [[evt:15934|Second Sack of Kashunji]], I went with the baggage. I saw the chariots go down into the river mud and stick there, the horse pulling, the wheel sunk, the bowman standing like a man in a basket. I saw the horse-herders of the hills, the ones who come to trade, sit upon the backs of their beasts and cross the same mud as a duck crosses water. I said in my heart: why does the horse of the king pull a box, when it could carry a man?

Two years I have worked at it. I have set boys of the stables upon the horse's back, with a cloth and a girth, and they have fallen, and I have set them up again. I have taught the horse to stand when the rider throws the spear, and to turn at the knee and not at the rein. Many fell. One broke his neck, and I paid his mother in silver by weight from my own store. I made offering to [[dei:51|Talu]] of the earth that the earth be soft for them, and to [[dei:49|Tuvi]] of the underworld that Tuvi not take them.

Now, my lord, there are sixty of them. Sixty riders who throw the spear from the horse's back, at the gallop, and turn, and come again. Where the chariot cannot go, they go. Where the chariot is slow, they are swift. My lord, let my lord come out to the field below Kabave on the day of the new moon and see them ride. If my lord does not see what I see, let my lord take the horses back, and I will go back to the stable and say nothing.

The priests of [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]] have said to me: "This is not the order of things. The horse is for the chariot as the ox is for the plough." I answered them: the order of things is that the king's enemies fall, and the king's horses serve the king. Is it not written that prosperity is a sign of the gods' favour? Then let the gods judge by the field which way pleases them.

And now a request. By the [[evt:15935|Treaty of Kashunji]] the land of my lord's enemy sends tribute to my lord for many years. Let my lord command that a part of the tribute be paid in horses, young ones, not yet broken to the chariot, for they learn the back more easily than the yoke. With three hundred such horses I will give my lord three hundred riders, and with them the [[evt:15921|mounted cavalry of Puvu]] will be a thing that the kings of other lands hear of and fear.

Let my lord not forget the name of his servant. When men speak of this, let them say: Nalumbunu did it, in the twelfth year of Natsunga.

May Kudze keep my lord. May Nüdinande give him the field.
