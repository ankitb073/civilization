**The first song.**

I am [[set:155|Yennga]]. I am the city. Hear me weep.  
I am the house of the great wheel, the house of [[mov:2|Jenro]].  
My gate was the gate of kings. My storehouse was the storehouse of lands.  
The silver of every road came to me, the [[cur:96|Bado]] of every market.  
I was the mother. I had a great house, and my daughters sat in it.  
Now my daughters have gone out. Hear me weep.

*The wheel has turned. The wheel has turned against the city.*

**The second song.**

Who struck the first blow? The years struck it.  
In the 405th year of Yennga my young men went down to Bimnge.  
At the [[evt:7833|battle of Bimnge]] they fell like barley in the hail,  
three thousand of them and more, and of the enemy almost none.  
The land was won after; yes, the land was won.  
But the mothers of my young men did not stop weeping.  
[[dei:14|Volme]] of the spear took her due from me, and more than her due.

*The wheel has turned. The wheel has turned against the city.*

**The third song.**

In the 435th year the old king went down to his fathers.  
The seat stood empty in the morning; by evening it was not empty.  
A hand that was not given the seat took the seat.  
That was the [[evt:8115|taking of the seat by Ngothshen]].  
From that day the great houses looked at one another sideways.  
From that day the oath of the seat was a thin rope, and it frayed.

In the 452nd year Toramto lifted its hand,  
at the [[evt:8253|going-out of Toramto]], and set up its own lord.  
Six years it stood apart. Six years my spearmen went up against it,  
and in the sixth year it was brought back, and its lord was cast from his seat.  
But a cloak that is torn and sewn is not a whole cloak.

*The wheel has turned. The wheel has turned against the city.*

**The fourth song.**

In the 471st year came the great breaking, the [[evt:8442|breaking of Yennga]].  
The one cloak was torn into six pieces. The one land became six lands.  
[[pol:371|Oshodonga]] took her piece and went out of my gate.  
[[pol:372|Yemola]] took her piece and went out of my gate.  
[[pol:373|Böneynga]] took her piece and went out of my gate.  
[[pol:374|Toböjenga]] took her piece and went out of my gate.  
[[pol:375|Pekhtonga]] took her piece and went out of my gate.  
Each sits on her own seat. Each counts her own years.  
Only one daughter stays in the house of her mother:  
[[pol:370|Yennga]] the small, the last, who sits in my walls,  
at the [[evt:8443|rising of the new Yennga]]. She is my comfort. She is too small.

The name of [[pol:175|Yennga]] the great went down from the roll of kings.  
Who now says "Yennga, mother of six"? No one says it.

*The wheel has turned. The wheel has turned against the city.*

**The fifth song.**

[[dei:16|Domamnol]] of the sky, you saw it, and you did not stop it.  
[[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, you saw it by night, and you said nothing.  
[[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] of the grain, my threshing floors are half empty.  
Eva of the fire, the fire on my altar is low.  
Venba of the earth, hold my walls; do not let them fall.

But the wheel turns. That is the teaching. What ends will begin again.  
The daughter who goes out of the door may come back through the door.  
O gods of Yennga, turn the wheel. Turn it toward the city.  
Let the six pieces be one cloak. Let the daughters come home.

*Written in the 473rd year of Yennga, the second year after the breaking.*
