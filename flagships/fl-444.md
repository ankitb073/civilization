*In the name of the One Power, before whom all ages run in a line to the Judgement, and under whose shadow the lesser powers stand as servants.*

**Here begin the Acts of the [[evt:39906|Council of Pikhtunge]], set down in fair copy by the least of the secretaries, at the command of the Elders of the [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]], in the year 1921 of the Vieshethmekh.**

*Of the convening.* In the year last past the Elders of the Vieshethmekh were called together in the royal city of [[set:177|Piekhkunga]], under the protection of the most pious queen [[per:39286|Pielulath]], sovereign of [[pol:1645|Piekhkunga]], who gave the hall, the lamps and the bread, and who, as befits a daughter of the faith, sat not among the Elders but below them, at the foot of the dais, on the first day. The cause of the convening was the dissent of the Wemo, the brethren of the realm of [[pol:1461|Khuemkhukhu]], who had for some years withheld their obedience in the matter of ordinations.

*Present.* The Elders of the provinces, each with his clerk; the delegates of the Wemo, three in number, with their sovereign's letter under seal; the queen's chamberlain; and the secretaries, of whom I was the third.

*First session.* The presiding Elder opened with the reading appointed for days of division: *"The Power is one; let not its servants be many."* He then exhorted the brethren to compassion, reminding them that the faith is a road of release from suffering, and that no road is made shorter by quarrelling upon it. All present assented.

*Second session.* The delegates of the Wemo laid their case. In sum: that the elders of their land have for three generations been named by the sovereign of Khuemkhukhu and afterward ordained; that their people know no other custom; and that a sovereign who feeds the poor and keeps the shrines is a servant of the One Power, and may choose its servants. They produced the sovereign's letter, which was read. It was courteous.

*Third session.* The Elders answered. The truth of the faith is kept by an ordained priesthood and passed down in order, hand upon head, from elder to elder; it is not the sovereign's to give, any more than the rain is. A lord may hold the land, and his men owe him their service and their spears; but the altar is held of no lord. It was further observed that if a sovereign may name the elders, a sovereign may also unname them, and the faith would then be the creature of every border war.

*Fourth to sixth sessions.* Much argument, which the presiding Elder ordered not to be minuted, lest it give scandal. I record only that tempers were lost on both sides, and that on the sixth day the queen's chamberlain was obliged to separate two clerks in the lower hall.

*Seventh session.* A form of reconciliation was proposed: that the elders already serving in Khuemkhukhu be confirmed by laying-on of hands from the Council, and that hereafter the sovereign might put forward names, but the Elders alone ordain. The Elders accepted it. The delegates of the Wemo asked leave to send it to their sovereign. Leave was given.

*Eighth session.* The sovereign's answer came. It would not yield the naming of the elders. The delegates, weeping (I set this down because I saw it), declared that they could not go against their sovereign and still be sworn servants, and that they would hold to their custom. The presiding Elder declared that the Council had failed to reconcile the Wemo, and that the matter was committed to the Judgement at the end of days, where no sovereign will name anyone.

*Of the expenses.* For bread, oil, lamps, parchment and the lodging of the delegates, the queen's treasury disbursed a sum in [[cur:911|Muevshe]] which her chamberlain has the account of. The Elders gave thanks, and blessed her house.

*Here end the Acts.* Copied fair and compared with the wax notes by the third secretary, in the year 1921 of the Vieshethmekh, at Piekhkunga. *Pray for the Wemo; pray for the copyist.*
