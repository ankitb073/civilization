*[Obverse. The upper edge is chipped; the first lines are lost.]*

[…] the brown water […]  
[…] and the herons stood in the shallows.

Sing, O [[dei:141|Nabüdin]], mother of the river, who feeds the barley,  
sing, O [[dei:136|Duemino]], lord of the storm, whose voice is in the hills,  
sing of the spears of [[pol:19|Talenonoy]] that went down into Tonnga,  
six summers they went down, six harvests they went down.

Remember the men of [[pol:7|Buneynga]], the men of the crooked ford.  
In the days of our grandfathers they came against Talenonoy,  
in the [[evt:644|Buneynga–Talenonoy War]] they came with their oxen and their boasting,  
and our grandfathers broke them, and they went home with empty hands.  
Their hands were empty, but their mouths were full. They did not forget.

Then came the [[evt:734|Battle of Ture]], and strangers broke the Buneynga there.  
Their spears grew few, their storehouses grew few.  
The house of Talenonoy lifted its hand to Duemino:  
"Duemino, father of the hail, give us the land of [[reg:7|Tonnga]]."  
And Duemino heard, and the spears went down.

At [[set:16|Tturamtu]], by the walls of Tturamtu,  
[[per:1060|Tibukuttnakin]] of the long spear stood against [[per:1045|Lungotran]].  
Many were the spears of Lungotran; few were the spears of Talenonoy.  
Lungotran broke. Lungotran ran. The field was Talenonoy's.  
That was the first time; that was the [[evt:773|Battle of Turamtu]].

At Tturamtu a second time Tibukuttnakin stood,  
against [[per:1061|Hannuhmalin]] he stood, Hannuhmalin of the bronze belt.  
Many were the spears of Hannuhmalin, like reeds in the marsh.  
Hannuhmalin broke. Hannuhmalin ran. The field was Talenonoy's.

At Tturamtu a third time [[per:1029|Tuttamat]] the steadfast,  
Tuttamat the daughter of the spear, stood against [[per:1035|Tyettungat]],  
Tyettungat the proud one, the war-leader of Buneynga.  
Tuttamat poured beer to Nabüdin at the crossing,  
she poured oil to [[dei:140|Winba]] of the black earth,  
and Winba opened her mouth and drank the Buneynga.  
For each of ours that fell, four of theirs fell. The field was Talenonoy's.

*[Some lines lost. Traces of the words "the long winter", "the empty jars" and "[…] did not come home".]*

[…] at Tturamtu the fourth time, in the sixth summer,  
Tibukuttnakin stood against Tyettungat a second time.  
Hard was the day. The dust hid the sun. The river ran red at the ford.  
As many of ours went down as of theirs; let the singer not hide it.  
But Tyettungat broke at evening, and the field was Talenonoy's.  
That was the last time; that was the [[evt:795|Battle of Turamtu]].

Then the two women sat down at Tturamtu, Tuttamat and Tyettungat sat down.  
They swore by Duemino of the storm, they swore by [[dei:137|Shyedartsi]] below.  
Buneynga gave the fields of Tonnga. Buneynga gave the river-meadows.  
Buneynga gave silver every year, silver of good weight, to the house of Talenonoy.  
So was the [[evt:796|Treaty of Turamtu]] sworn, and the spears were laid down.

Of ours, fifteen hundred went down to Shyedartsi.  
Of theirs, more than three thousand went down to Shyedartsi.  
Weep for ours, O mothers. For theirs, let Buneynga weep.

Now hear, you who stand in the court of the queen:  
[[per:1302|Lorehediyat]] sits in Talenonoy, Lorehediyat the bright,  
in her days the spears went down again, in her days the field at Zhivampive was ours,  
the [[evt:1011|Battle of Zhivampive]] was ours, as Tturamtu was ours.  
What the grandfathers began, the grandchildren keep.  
Remember Tturamtu. Remember the four fields. Remember the crooked ford.

*[Reverse, lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Written for the house of the queen, in the 11th year of Lorehediyat.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Nabüdin dry up his well.
