**AN OPEN LETTER FROM THE PEOPLE OF TATUTHIVI**  
**To the President of the Federation and to the Federal Assembly of [[pol:1656|Ane]]**

*Printed at Ane, 2 Toe 4235 of the Myitiyishiu, at the expense of the Tatuthivi Relief Committee. Price one-quarter [[cur:1076|Muie]]; free to soldiers and widows.*

Madam President,

We address you in the first season of your office, because you are new to it, and because you, at least, did not sign what was signed at Tlitlintlivtli. We are the people of [[reg:21|Tatuthivi]] who speak the tongue of Ane, pray to the gods of Ane, and live, as our grandparents lived, under the governors of [[pol:1638|Khishkhizi]]. We have been told for twelve years that we were the reason for a war. We write to ask what the war was for.

In 4222 the Federation laid the [[evt:45866|Ane Claim against Khishkhis]] before the world. Our committees were told to keep quiet, to pay the Khishkhis tax, to send no man to the Khishkhis levies if it could be avoided, and to wait. We waited. We hid soldiers of Ane in our barns. We carried their wounded. Some of us were hanged for it in the squares of our own towns, by order of governors who did not trouble to learn our names.

Consider the account, Madam President, for the newspapers of the capital have stopped printing it.

At [[set:37|Ntlivi]], the armies of Ane won five times. They won in 4223, at the first [[evt:45882|Battle of Nzhivi]]. They won in 4228, at the [[evt:45934|Battle of Nzhivi]] that cost the enemy three hundred and eighty-one thousand men in a single season. They won in 4230, when the Khishkhis general [[per:45093|Nanzhizhim]] fell on the field, at the [[evt:45946|Battle of Nzhivi]] which our children were taught to sing about.

At [[set:38|Tlitlintlivtli]], the armies of Ane were thrown back three times: in 4225, at the [[evt:45892|Battle of Jyijinjivji]], where one hundred and eighty thousand of our liberators fell; in 4229; and last year, in 4234, at the [[evt:45985|Battle of Jyijinjivji]] that ended it. At Shavi, in 4232, they were thrown back again, at the [[evt:45966|Battle of Shyih]].

The Federation's own figures give the dead of Ane at 1,097,454, and the dead of Khishkhis at 1,116,853. More than two million two hundred thousand graves. We have no figures for our own dead, for no one counted them; we were not soldiers of either side, only the ground on which both sides fought.

And then the [[evt:45986|Treaty of Jyijinjivji]]. We have read it. Every one of us has read it, for it is short. Its first article restores everything as it was before the war. Its second article forbids either party to take up arms again for twenty years. There is no third article. There is no mention of Tatuthivi at all.

So the [[evt:45867|Ane Colonial War in Daadüdhih]] ends as it began: the Khishkhis flag over our post offices, the Khishkhis rate on our grain, the Khishkhis schoolmaster in our classrooms. Only now the governors know which of us sheltered your soldiers, and they have twenty years of peace in which to remember it.

We do not ask for another war. The teachers of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] tell us that the gods ask justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and we have had enough of immoderation. We ask for three things, and we ask them of you, Madam President [[per:45278|Shitlyitlilyian]], by name:

1. That the Federation open its borders to every family of Tatuthivi who wishes to leave, without fee or passport.
2. That the Federation pay pensions to the widows of Tatuthivi who were hanged as its friends, at the same rate as the widows of its own soldiers.
3. That the word *Tatuthivi* be spoken, once, in the Federal Assembly, by a minister who is not ashamed of it.

Grain stands at six Muie the measure in Ane this winter. It stands higher with us. We are your people. We are still waiting.

*For the Committee: the undersigned, whose names are withheld for their safety.*
