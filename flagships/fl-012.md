title: The Words of a Spearman of Ana, Set Down in His Old Age

I, a man of the spear, son of a man of the spear, of the people of [[pol:229|Ana]], set down these words in the fifteenth year of [[per:6067|Esiesh Atha]], the king, the beloved of Atlia of the sky. Hear them, sons of my sons. Hear them, and pour water for me when I go down.

In the first year of the war the king before the king said: "The land of [[reg:13|I'a'ie'i]] is the land of Ana. Let it come home." So began the [[evt:5735|Ana–Waga War]]. I was young. My beard was thin. I took up the spear. My mother wept at the door.

In the second year we went out to [[set:190|Tlomesh]]. [[per:5896|Yiethi'itlue'a]] the old lion led us. We were many; the men of [[pol:153|Waga]] were few. But the men of Waga stood on the high ground, and they did not break. At the [[evt:5752|Battle of Tlomesh]] our brothers fell, more than a thousand, and of Waga hardly any. That night no one sang.

In the third year we went to the field of To'i'io'a. [[per:6010|Sho'a Mue'a]] led us. Against us stood [[per:6002|I'pil Lieze]], the fox of Waga. Remember that name, sons of my sons. Year after year he stood against us. Year after year. At the [[evt:5759|Battle of Toiyoe]] the fox ran, and we sang.

In the fifth year we went again to Tlomesh. Again Yiethi'itlue'a led us. [[per:6022|E'a Ash]] of Waga had a small host, a handful. We laughed. We did not laugh long. At the [[evt:5774|Battle of Tlomesh]] three thousand of ours fell. I lay in a ditch with an arrow in my thigh. I prayed to Niezhashta of war: "Do not let me die in the mud of Tlomesh." Niezhashta heard me.

Then the years went round like the wheel. At Fyinan we won. At Toiyoe we won. At Toiyoe we lost. The fox stood against us every time. In the ninth year Sho'a Mue'a led us at [[evt:5831|Onithie]], and the field was the fox's, and many of ours went down to the dark. In that year Yiethi'itlue'a, the old lion, went down to the dark also, in his bed, and in that year Esiesh Atha took the seat.

In the tenth year [[per:6078|O'e Tlie]] led us into the land itself, at the [[evt:5839|First Battle of Iiyyi]]. There the men of Waga were more than we. The field was theirs. I will not lie to you: we came home with our shields on our backs.

Then the king said: "I will go out myself." In the eleventh year Esiesh Atha went out himself. At the [[evt:5844|Battle of Toiyoe]] he stood in the war-cart. He did not stand behind. The fox stood against him, and the fox was broken. Then the king went out again, and again. He went to Tlomesh, and Tlomesh was ours. He went to the city of the kings of Waga and sat down before its wall, at the [[evt:5857|Siege of Bakh]]. He sat there a second year. Then the elders of Waga came out with dust on their heads.

At Bakh the oath was sworn, the [[evt:5867|Treaty of Bakh]]. The fox himself, I'pil Lieze, put his hand to it with the king. Waga gave Ana the land of I'a'ie'i. Waga gave Ana Tlomesh, where our brothers lie. Waga gave Ana [[set:111|To'i'io'a]], where we fought five times. Waga sends tribute to Ana; the scribes say for thirty-nine years. The merchants of Ana go into Waga and are not stopped at the gate.

Sons of my sons, hear me. We lost more men than Waga lost. The scribes say twenty thousand of ours fell, from the second year to the end. I believe it. I have counted my friends on my fingers and run out of fingers. But the land is ours. Tlomesh is ours. The dead of Tlomesh lie in the land of Ana now.

Atlia of the sky, keep the king. Niezhashta, rest your spear. Lashla'a of the moon, count my days kindly. I have set down my words. Let them be read.
