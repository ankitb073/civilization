*To my mother in [[set:127|Los]], say: thus says your son, the spearman of the hundred of the north gate. May Nili of the river keep you, may [[dei:7|Yof]] of the fire warm your bones. In the 18th year of [[per:13711|Ueshsaypaya]], the king.*

Mother, I am well. I am well, and I am not dead, and I am not sick. I say this first because you will look for it first.

I am still in [[set:22|Wau'e]], in the garrison, in the house by the gate. Two years now. Two harvests I have seen here and not at home. They do not send us home. The captain says: when the land is quiet. The land is quiet. They do not send us home.

You asked in your letter what it was like. I will tell you once, and then I will not tell you again.

We went down against [[pol:551|Wau'e]] in the second year of the war. At Yyixi I did not fight; I carried water. At the [[evt:13829|battle of Ash]] the king himself was with us, the king Ueshsaypaya, in a chariot with red horses. I saw him. He was near as the well is to our door. The men of Wau'e were few, few against our many, and they ran, and those who did not run lay down in the field.

Then we came to the city. The [[evt:13836|Tenth Sack of Bau]], the scribes call it. Ten times, mother. The people of the city say: ten times our walls have been broken, and ten times we have built. They say it to us in the market, to our faces, and smile.

I took a bronze cup in the sack. I took it from a house where no one was. I have it still. I will not bring it home. I do not want it in your house.

Then the king of Wau'e, [[per:13718|Ka'a'ata]], came out and put his seal on the tablet before our king, and the [[evt:13837|Conquest of Val'a]] was written, and the kingdom of Wau'e was a kingdom no more. They did not kill him. I saw him go out of the gate on a mule, with his head down. He is a young man. I am sorry for him. Do not tell anyone I said so.

Now we keep the gate. I stand on the wall in the morning and in the evening. Barley here is an [[cur:274|Ikhal]] and a half the measure and more, as at home, and the bakers of Wau'e give us the small loaves. There is a girl who draws water at the well of the gate. Her brother died in the sack. She does not look at me. I do not blame her.

The priests of our gods have set up an altar of [[dei:11|Nizhashte]] of war in the square of the city. The people of Wau'e go round it the long way.

The king has taken many lands, the captains say; first the lands of Tanchethi, now Wau'e. [[pol:526|Los]] is great. I know Los is great. I would like to see Los again.

Mother, send me the honey cakes if the boatman will carry them. Send word of my sister's child. Tell my father the spear is sharp and the arm is strong, and the son misses the plough.
