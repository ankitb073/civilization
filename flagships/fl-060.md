To the son of [[per:11821|Nambundu]], who sits in [[set:9|Tagath]] as the pledge of his father, say: thus says [[per:11678|Nabul]], king of [[pol:414|Tagath]], the strong bull, the beloved of [[dei:73|Tithu]] who rides the storm, the one to whom [[dei:77|Pugazhu]] the sun gave the four corners.

With me it is well. With my house it is well. With my land it is well. With you, son of Nambundu, it is well, for you are in my house.

You have asked the keepers of the gate: "Why am I here? When do I go home?" They did not answer you. The king answers you. Listen, and do not turn your face to the wall.

Your father, Nambundu, is an old man. His head is grey. His beard is grey. He would not bow his head to Tagath. He would not send the gift of the sun to Tagath. Therefore in the twenty-first year of Nabul the king went out, and so began the [[evt:11805|Third Tagath–Tyimbi War]]. The fault was your father's. The fault was the fault of [[pol:531|Tyimbi]].

In the twenty-second year my host met your father at [[evt:11828|Dalithimi]]. Your father's men fell like grain before the sickle. The field was mine. In the twenty-third year my host went down to [[set:136|Mbil]], and I opened it like a jar of oil, the [[evt:11836|First Sack of Mbilu]]. Did your father bow then? He did not bow.

In this twenty-fifth year I went out myself. At the [[evt:11862|Battle of Mbilu]] I met your father face to face. He stood with his spearmen before the gate. Tithu thundered over my head. My chariots rolled over his line. His host was cut down; it was not a host any more. Again I opened Mbil, the [[evt:11863|Second Sack of Mbilu]], and its storehouses came to Tagath on the backs of asses.

Then your father came out to me. His elders came out with him. At Mbil, before the gods, we swore the oath, the [[evt:11864|Treaty of Mbilu]]. Tyimbi has given me the land of Dyimbi. Tyimbi will send the tribute to Tagath, year after year, for thirteen years. For ten years no spear of Tyimbi shall be lifted against me. And you, son of Nambundu, you are the knot of the oath. That is why you are here.

Now hear what the king commands for you.

You will eat from my granary. Here grain is dear; a measure costs near three [[cur:228|gathu]] at the gate of Tagath. Yet you will eat and not pay. You will wear linen. You will have a woman to grind your flour and a boy to carry your water.

You will go to the house of [[dei:76|Tsilmu]] of wisdom, to the teachers. In [[mov:11|Kyif]] we say: the teacher is honoured, but no man owns the truth. So argue with the teachers. Ask them hard things. The one who asks hard things becomes a king; the one who asks nothing becomes a herdsman.

At the feast of [[dei:74|Binge]], when the first sheaves come in, you will stand among the sons of my house. You will not stand behind them.

When the tribute of your father comes, every year, I will show it to you. I will say: "Look, your father keeps the oath." And you will be glad. But if the tribute does not come, if the spearmen of Tyimbi cross the river, then do not look for me in the morning. I will not come to you.

Write to your father. My scribe will write it for you. Tell him: "I eat. I wear linen. I learn the tablets. Keep the oath." Tell him that Nabul is a just king to those who keep the oath.

Let Pugazhu shine on you. Let Tithu hold back his hail from your head.

Written in Tagath, in the house of the king, in the twenty-fifth year of Nabul.
