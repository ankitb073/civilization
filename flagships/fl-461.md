*[Obverse. Words that the king spoke at the gate of Tashifi on the day of the oath, set in the clay at his command.]*

Hear me, men of [[pol:85|Kumbumbi]]! Hear me, women of Kumbumbi! Hear me, you who were slaves of Tagine and are slaves no more!

I am [[per:2347|Nakalgu]]. I am the one who stood up. Five summers ago I stood up in [[set:146|Tashifi]], and you stood up with me.

Remember the yoke. The lords of [[pol:21|Tagine]] sat on our necks. They took our barley and gave us chaff. They took our sons for their walls and gave us back their bones. They took the fish from our nets and the oil from our jars. They said: "Kumbumbi is a dog; let the dog eat what falls from the table." Was it so? Was it so?

*The people answer: It was so.*

Then I stood up. This was the [[evt:2026|Revolt of Kumbumbi]]. I broke the seal of Tagine on the gate of Tashifi. I poured the oil to [[dei:48|Kudze]] of the high sky. I poured the blood to [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] of the spear. I said: "From this day Tashifi bows to no one." And Kudze thundered, and Nüdinande lifted his spear.

Tagine sent its spears. We went out against them. Let this be named the [[evt:2027|First Kumbumbi War of Independence]]; and if Tagine comes again there will be a second, and we will win that also.

In the second year my general [[per:2354|Nabule]], the bull of Kumbumbi, went down to Dyigini, to the walls of [[set:41|Tagine]]. [[per:2355|Nakuvu]], the general of Tagine, came out against him. Nakuvu was proud; Nakuvu was a boaster. Nabule broke him. The dead of Tagine lay before their walls like straw after the threshing; who could count them? This was the [[evt:2046|Battle of Dyigini]]. The field was ours. Then we sat before their walls; we shut their gate from the outside. This was the [[evt:2047|First Siege of Dyigini]]. They ate their own oxen. They ate their own pride.

In the fourth year Nabule went down again. Again Nakuvu came out. Again Nakuvu ran. This was the [[evt:2052|Battle of Dyigini]] again. Then we went in at their gate. We took back the barley they took from us. We took back the oil they took from us. We took back the bronze of our fathers. This was the [[evt:2053|Sack of Dyigini]]. Let Tagine learn what Kumbumbi learned: how it is to have a full jar emptied.

Then the lords of Tagine came out with dust on their heads. At Tagine, in their own gate, I set my hand to the oath and their great one set his hand to the oath. This was the [[evt:2054|Treaty of Dyigini]]. Kumbumbi is free. Kumbumbi bows to no one. Ten years the spear sleeps between us. Let Tagine keep the ten years; Kumbumbi will keep them also, with the spear beside the bed.

Who freed Kumbumbi? Did Tagine free it? No. Did the gods of Tagine free it? No. The gods of Kumbumbi freed it, and the arms of Kumbumbi.

*The people answer: The gods of Kumbumbi; the arms of Kumbumbi.*

Therefore hear what I command. Let the land of [[reg:21|Tatudüfe]] be ploughed by the men of Kumbumbi and no other. Let the tax go to Tashifi and no other. Let the priests of [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]] pour to Kudze of the sky, to [[dei:51|Talu]] of the earth, to Bumbe of the sea, to Bine of the fire. And let them pour to [[dei:49|Tuve]] below the earth, for the men of Kumbumbi who lay down at Dyigini. I do not count them before you; their mothers count them. Let each mother have barley from the house of the king.

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The days of Tagine over us are ended. The days of Kumbumbi are begun. Let no hand turn the wheel back.

*The people answer: Nakalgu! Nakalgu! Let no hand turn the wheel back!*

> In the 6th year of Nakalgu, king in Tashifi.
