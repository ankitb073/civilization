*[Obverse]*

To [[dei:4|Lashla]], lord of the moon, lamp of the night, who sees the sick in their beds;  
to [[dei:3|Niyl'i]], the river, who washes the dead and carries them away;  
to [[dei:0|Atlyi]], the sky, father over the roof:  
a widow speaks. A widow of the lane of the dyers in [[set:78|Haich]] speaks.

In the 31st year of [[per:8615|Icuil Tli]], the great king, king of [[pol:351|Myii]],  
in the month of the hot wind,  
the sickness came into Haich. The sickness came into the lane of the dyers.  
First the lips go blue. Then the nails go blue.  
Then the breath is short, short as a bird's.  
Then the fever, three days. Then the end.  
Men call it the [[evt:8481|Blue Fever]]. It began here, they say. It began in Haich.  
Now the traders who flee carry it down every road.  
They say it is in Ana. They say it is in Ashash. They say it is everywhere the roads go.

My husband, the dyer, the strong man, the man of the blue vats,  
he lay down on the fourth day of the month. On the seventh day he did not rise.  
His father lay down. His father did not rise.  
The woman of the next door, her two sons, they did not rise.  
The priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] do not come into the lane. The priests stand at the end of the lane.  
The carts come at evening. The carts take the dead to the river.  
I washed my husband with my own hands. I gave him to the cart.  
I have no husband. I have a son of seven years and a daughter of four years.  
They are not blue. Lord, they are not blue.

*[Reverse]*

Hear the vow. Hear the vow of the widow.

If my son does not go blue, if my daughter does not go blue,  
if they see the new moon and the moon after it and the moon after that,  
then I, the widow, will give to the house of Lashla:  
one lamb without blemish, at the full moon;  
one jar of oil, good oil, for the lamp of the lord;  
and two [[cur:131|I'ash]] of silver, weighed at the gate before the priests.

Two I'ash are much. A measure of barley at the market is one I'ash and the most of another.  
My husband's tools I will sell. His vats I will sell. The blue cloth in the chest I will sell.  
The debt of my husband to the wool-merchant, I will pay it. I will pay it after the god.

The god is first. The lord of the moon sees the beds at night.  
Lashla, look in at my door. There are two beds. Pass over them.  
Niyl'i, river, do not take my children in the cart.  
Atlyi, sky, keep the hot wind from my roof.

My husband's brother went with the king's army against Mikhuthitsu,  
in the [[evt:8525|Myii–Mikhuthitsu War]], three years ago. He has not come home.  
I do not know if he lives. If he comes, let him not come into Haich now.  
Let him stay on the road until the sickness goes.

I set this tablet by the door of the shrine.  
I set it by the other tablets. There are many tablets. There are many widows.  
The lord can read them all. The lord sees.  
In the 31st year of Icuil Tli, the king. May the king live. May the city live.  
May my son live. May my daughter live.
