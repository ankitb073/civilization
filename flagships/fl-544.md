*Here begins the record of the disputation held in the hall of our lord the king, [[per:39187|Lamfin Mua]], at [[set:104|Yethithi]], in the year 1796 of the Kievi, between [[per:39422|Tadash]], least of the servants of the One, and a doctor sent by the masters of the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]]. Written by the same Tadash with his own hand, that what was truly said may not be twisted by those who were not present.*

In the name of the One, who is above all and beneath all, the old gods being but His servants and His shadows. Amen.

**Of the occasion.** It is known to all in the kingdom of [[pol:1461|Wemu]] that in the year past, at [[set:127|Lus]], certain brethren and I withdrew ourselves from the governance of the masters, and took the name of [[mov:142|Ishlish]]; which our adversaries call the [[evt:39907|Ishlish Schism]], as though we had torn the robe of the faith, when we had only taken off a weight that was never part of it. The masters, being grieved, sent one of their doctors to dispute with me; and our lord the king, though he holds himself to the faith of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] and is not of our communion, of his justice granted us his hall and his presence, that the matter might be heard in good order.

**Of the first question: whether time runs in a line.** The doctor began by setting forth the teaching of his masters: that time runs in a line from creation to a final judgement, as an arrow runs from the bow to the mark. I answered that I honour the arrow, but that I see the year turn round, and the moon turn round, and the seed fall and rise again; and that the One, who made all things, did not make them in a manner contrary to Himself. "All things turn on a great wheel," I said, "and what ends will begin again. Is the spring a lie because it came last year also?" The doctor said this was the teaching of the heathen. I said that the sun also shines on the heathen, and is not therefore a heathen sun. At this the king smiled, and I record it, though I do not boast of it.

**Of the second question: who keeps the truth.** The doctor then said that the truth is kept by an ordained priesthood and handed down in order, from master to disciple, and that whoever breaks the order breaks the truth. I asked him whether the truth was the masters' property, as a field is a lord's, to be let out by charter. He said it was not property but a trust. I said: "Then let the trustees be honoured, as teachers are honoured; but let no one say he owns it." For the One does not hold His fief of any priest. And I reminded him that his own masters, at the [[evt:39906|Council of Pikhtunge]], could not persuade our brethren of Wemu by their order alone, and the council broke up without agreement; which would not have happened if the order were the truth itself.

**Of what we hold in common.** Lest any think we came only to quarrel, I declared before the king what we and the masters hold together, and the doctor did not deny it: that there is one divine power; that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering; and that this message is for all peoples, and must be carried to them. On these the Ishlish have changed nothing. We have only opened the door wider.

**Of the end.** When the candles were half burned, the king raised his hand and said that he had matters of war to attend to, for his men were still in the field against Tungaruikhrimu, though they had won the [[evt:39886|Battle of Tungaruikhrimu]] the summer before; and that he would not judge between two servants of the One, but would let each teach in peace so long as each paid his dues and kept the king's peace. He gave the doctor a purse of [[cur:808|Mimi]] for his road, and to me the same. I gave mine to the poor of Yethithi at the gate, as is fitting.

So ended the disputation. Let the reader judge which of us spoke for the One. I have set down the doctor's words as fairly as my own, and if I have erred, may the One forgive me, and may the wheel bring me round to correct it.

*Here ends the record.*
