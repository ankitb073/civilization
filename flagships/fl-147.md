*To His Excellency the Governor of [[set:324|Niamush]], who holds that place for the President and the federated provinces of [[pol:1656|Ane]]: the humble complaint of the elders of the seven villages of the bay, in the land of [[reg:30|Tligim]], set down in the tongue of Ane by the sworn interpreter of the colony, at Niamush, 13 Nyime 4185 of the Myitiyishiu.*

Excellency,

We, the elders of the seven villages that stand around the bay, come before you with our hands open, as our fathers came before strangers who landed on this shore, and as we came before your own people when their ships first anchored in the bay a year ago. We beg you to hear us patiently, and to read what follows to the end, for we have been told that in Ane a petition is answered according to its reasons, and we have tried to give reasons.

**I. Of the landing.** When your ships came, in the season of the long rains of last year, your captain asked us for water, and we gave it. He asked us for fish, and we gave them. He asked us for a strip of beach where his men might put up their tents until the sickness of the voyage left them, and we showed him the beach below the headland. That was [[evt:45535|the planting of your colony]]. We did not refuse you. Let this be remembered when the rest is read.

**II. Of the land.** The strip of beach has become the whole of the bay. A wall has been built across the neck of the headland, and our people may not pass it without a paper. The fields of the two nearest villages have been measured with chains and given to men from [[set:7|Ane]] who did not plant them. When we asked by what right, we were told that [[evt:45536|the town of Niamush]] has been founded by law, and that the law of Ane runs to its boundary stones. We do not know where the boundary stones are. They move.

**III. Of the grove.** Behind the second village there is a grove where our dead are laid, and where the gods of this shore are honoured by us as our fathers honoured them. Your carpenters have cut eleven trees there for the wharf. We do not ask you to honour our gods. We ask only that you leave them their trees.

**IV. Of the market.** Your merchants buy our dried fish and our hides for a single [[cur:1038|withe]] the bundle, and they sell us back a measure of their grain for two and a half. We have learned to count in your coin. We see what the count says.

**V. Of the men.** Since the new year, the captain of the fort has taken young men from the villages to dig and to carry, and he pays them in grain, when he pays them. Some of them have not come home. We are told that your President [[per:44882|Yiueluisha]] has great need of men for his war, and that he has spent many thousands at [[evt:45537|Nivanga]] and [[evt:45542|Lininadhe]]. We are sorry for the mothers of Ane. But our sons are not his soldiers.

**VI. Of what we ask.** We have heard from the sailors that [[evt:45543|a peace was made at Lininadhe]] this year, and that your nation rejoices in it. We rejoice with you. We ask you to make a peace here also. Let the wall have a gate that is open by day. Let the grove be marked with stones that do not move. Let the price of fish and grain be set in the hearing of both peoples. Let no young man be taken without his father's word.

We have been told also that the priests of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]], whom your people honour, teach justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and that the wise among you withdraw from wealth and appetite. We have not met these wise men in the bay. Perhaps they have not yet arrived on the ships. When they come, we will gladly show them the beach below the headland, as we showed it to you.

The elders of the seven villages have set their marks below. The interpreter swears before his own gods and ours that he has added nothing and taken nothing away.

*[Seven marks follow, and the interpreter's signature.]*
