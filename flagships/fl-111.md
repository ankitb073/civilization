*Here is set down, by the hand of the least of the servants of the Light, the record of the disputation held at [[set:206|Iazhash]] in the 9th year of [[per:38585|Sitlyitliluisha]], king of [[pol:1497|Ölie]], between [[per:38661|Fispemamshi]] and the elders of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikhi]] of that city. The king's reeve sat in the chair, that the peace be kept; for the king holds to the gods of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]], and would not judge between us, but only keep order, as is the duty of a lord toward all who live under his hand.*

**The first question.** The eldest of the elders rose and said: "Brother, you have divided the flock. Since the [[evt:39203|Council of Pikhtunge]], where the brethren of Ölie would not consent and the council broke up in nothing, you have gone up and down this city teaching what the fathers never taught. Is it not written that the body is one, and that he who tears it wounds the Light?"

Fispemamshi answered: "Fathers, I did not tear the body. The council tore it, when it sent us home with nothing in our hands. I have not changed one word of what we hold together: that time runs in one line, from the first day to the last judgement; that there is one Light, and the old gods are its shadows; that the teaching passes from hand to hand in order; that the good life is compassion and the release of suffering; and that the word is for all peoples. All this I keep, as a vassal keeps the oath he swore at his lord's knee. I have added nothing. I have only said aloud what the fathers left unsaid."

**The second question.** The elders said: "What is it, then, that you say aloud?"

Fispemamshi answered: "That prosperity is a sign of the Light's favour. The fathers were silent on riches, and in their silence men have grown ashamed of the harvest the Light has given them. Look at this city. The ships come into the harbour of Iazhash full; the counting-houses ring with [[cur:823|myiu]]. It is written that the Light gives the rain to the field that is ready. Wealth is a lamp lit from above. But a lamp is lit to be carried into dark rooms. The rich man is favoured so that he may relieve the poor; the full granary is blessed so that it may be opened. Favour and compassion are not two roads but one."

The elders said: "The king's armies prosper in the [[evt:39190|Holy War of Ölie against Ayshi]]. They won the field at [[evt:39196|Iwathuidha]]. Are they too favoured, who serve other gods?"

Fispemamshi answered: "The Light sends rain on the just and on those who do not yet know its name. The king's men prevailed, and I give thanks; and I weep also for the many of our own who did not come home from that field. If the Light gives the king victory, then the king owes the Light compassion toward the widows, as a vassal owes his lord the fruits of the fief."

**The third question.** The elders said: "And the last judgement?"

Fispemamshi answered: "It is nearer than the fathers dared to say. Therefore let no rich man delay his mercy, and no poor man despair, for the line has an end, and at the end every hand will be opened and every granary counted."

**The end of the disputation.** The elders took counsel together, and the eldest said: "We cannot receive this. Go out from us." Then Fispemamshi rose, and bowed to the reeve and to the elders, and said: "I go out, as the first teachers went out from their fathers' houses, carrying nothing but the word," and went out. And there followed him out of the hall merchants of the harbour, and widows, and three who had been ordained in the old order and laid their hands on him there in the street, that the succession not be broken.

So began, at Iazhash, the teaching of the [[mov:141|Pyizhenatlshi]], which our adversaries already call [[evt:39204|The Pyizhenatlshi Schism]]; but we call it the keeping of the faith.

*Written in the 9th year of Sitlyitliluisha, king of Ölie, whose seat is at [[set:264|Ölie]]. May the Light keep the king, the reeve, the elders, and even me.*
