In AY 2070, three days after a battle she had lost, the queen of Nilu wrote home from her camp. Her army had been driven from the field by the king of [[pol:629|Puvu]], and she wanted her people to hear the truth before rumour did. "Let Puvu call that a victory," she wrote. "One more victory of that kind and Napiningi will be king over a country of widows." The king she meant was [[per:16411|Napüningi]], who ruled Puvu from 2050 to 2101. Tradition calls him the Last, and calls his son the Last as well. The empire of Puvu broke apart in the year of his death. A biography of him has to ask how far that ending was his doing and how far it was simply where he stood when it came.

## The regency child

He was born in 2045, a Kumbumbi prince of the house of Natsunga, which had ruled since 1981. His father, [[per:16306|Navande I]], came to the throne in 2040 and died in 2050, not yet forty. An elder brother had died at six, before Napüningi was born. The [[evt:16482|Accession of Napüningi]] made a boy of five king of some four million people, ruled from Kabaf, with his mother [[per:16408|Nande]] as regent. A younger sister died in 2052, and Nande herself in 2056, when he was eleven.

The first war of the reign belongs to those regency years. The [[evt:16508|Holy War of Puvu against Tadudüvi]] of 2055–2057 was fought against a city-state founded only five years before. Its armies hardly existed: over the whole war it lost nineteen men, and Puvu lost more than nine thousand. Yizhi was sacked and besieged, and in 2057 the [[evt:16533|Conquest of Tadudüvi]] was signed there. The city's ruler, [[per:16459|Natsunga]], remembered as the Brief, was twenty-one and had held his throne for less than a year. He was executed the same year. Napüningi was twelve. We should not hold the boy responsible. We should note, however, that his reign began with a religious war against a city that could not fight back and with the death of its young prince.

## Eleven years of holy war

In 2062 the war came to him. Under its queen [[per:16279|Nakathash]], who had reigned for a quarter of a century and would reign for thirty-two years more, Nilu began the [[evt:16569|Holy War of Nilu against Puvu]]. It lasted eleven years. Napüningi commanded the defence in seven of its nine battles. He won five and lost two, and in every one he had the larger army. At the [[evt:16628|Battle of Lamligul (AY 2070)]] he had almost forty-two thousand men against the thirteen and a half thousand of the Nilu general [[per:16564|Naluvu]]. His victory stele claims nine thousand enemy dead. The record gives 3,234, which is precisely the number the queen gives in her letter, adding "I have read every name." She claimed in turn that Puvu had lost "not fewer than eight thousand". The record says about six thousand four hundred.

Her letter, the Letter of Queen Nakathash from the Camp in Pièvi, is the sharpest portrait of Napüningi we have, and it is a hostile one. "Puvu does not fight with right," she wrote. "It fights with numbers." The record bears her out. Puvu won most of the battles and lost more men than Nilu did, some forty-five thousand against twenty-nine. In 2071 the queen commanded in person at the [[evt:16634|Battle of Khavazhu (AY 2071)]] and was beaten. The [[evt:16644|Treaty of Dambüzhi (AY 2073)]] ended the war with nothing but a truce. Eleven years and some seventy-five thousand dead on both sides had changed no frontier.

Somewhere in those years he married [[per:16586|Nachügü]], five years his junior. Their first son was born in 2067. The empire, meanwhile, stood still. In every decade of his reign it held the same 221 districts it had held since the conquest of 2057.

## His own holy war

In 2080 Napüningi began a holy war of his own. The [[evt:16686|Holy War of Puvu against Luthe]] opened well. At the [[evt:16694|Battle of Dadifi (AY 2081)]] he beat the Luthe general [[per:16635|Natili]], and his stele claims 30,700 dead where the record gives about 3,900. After that it went badly. In 2084 his eldest son died at seventeen. In 2087, at Dadunde, Napüningi lost to a larger army. In 2088 he lost there again to a much smaller one, and some ten thousand of his men were killed. In 2089 he was beaten at Dadifi. The [[evt:16735|Treaty of Dadifi (AY 2089)]] reverses the usual formula. Peace "is granted by Nandithim, king of Luthe, to Napüningi of Puvu", and Puvu was to pay 1,365 talents of silver. [[per:16647|Nandithim]], later called the Victorious, was fifteen.

At the same time he was fighting the [[evt:16727|Second Kashunji–Puvu War]], begun by [[pol:579|Kashunji]] in 2087. At the [[evt:16761|Battle of Ajü (AY 2090)]] its queen, [[per:16611|Naguf]], twenty-eight years old, led eight thousand men against twenty-seven thousand and was killed. Her general, also named Naguf, fought on and lost twice more. The [[evt:16781|Treaty of Shuthunile (AY 2093)]] was sworn with her son, [[per:16714|Nakadha]], thirteen years old and remembered as the Last. This time the formula ran the other way: "Peace is granted by Napüningi, emperor of Puvu." Kashunji paid 403 talents and opened its markets to Puvu's merchants free of tolls. It was the only peace of his adult reign that brought Puvu anything.

## The empty end

The record measures the order of a state as well as its armies. In Puvu that measure fell to almost nothing in the 2080s and to nothing by 2100. In that year Napüningi began yet another war, against Nkinakhizi. He died a natural death in 2101, aged fifty-six. The [[evt:16838|Accession of Navande II]] made his son, [[per:16591|Navande II]], thirteen, king under the regency of his mother, Nachügü.

The empire did not survive the year. In 2101 Puvu broke into five successor states. One was centred on Kabaf, the old capital. Another, at Alunzhi, took the name of the city-state Napüningi had destroyed as a boy. The war against Nkinakhizi simply dissolved. Navande II lived until 2146, and Nachügü until 2118.

It would be easy to call Napüningi the cause of the collapse, and the record does count three of his own defeats and his peace at Dadifi among its causes. Yet he lost no territory in fifty-one years and won most of his battles. What he did was wage war. He fought five wars, three of them holy wars, in a realm that barely grew, and paid for them in soldiers' lives at a rate his enemies could see. Queen Nakathash, who died seven years before him, judged him by the widows. The record gives us no reason to judge him more kindly.
