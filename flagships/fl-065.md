To the steward of my house in [[set:74|Tlayiyan]], and to the priests of the house of [[dei:0|Atlia]], say: thus says [[per:15759|Yi To'a]], king of [[pol:555|Tlayiyan]], whom the gods lifted up and set upon the throne, whom [[dei:5|Niezhashta]] goes before in battle.

With me it is well. With the army it is well. The [[evt:15865|war in Nis]] is ended. Hear how it ended, and then hear what you shall do.

In the year that the gods gave me the throne, I went out against [[pol:575|Niz]], for the men of Niz had shut the river road to the boats of Tlayiyan and taken a toll that was not theirs to take. At [[evt:15881|Litlitli]], in the next summer, the gods did not yet give me the city. The men of Niz would not come out. They threw fire from the walls. They threw stones. They poured boiling water on my men as women pour it on ants. And [[dei:3|Niele]] rose in the night, in his anger, not in his season, and took my camp. This was the work of the river and of cowards, not of soldiers. Let no one say otherwise in my house.

The summer after, I came again to [[evt:15891|Litlitli]], and [[per:15552|Iwa Imam]], king of Niz, stood in the gate himself. The omens were good: the liver was whole, the birds flew to the right hand. I broke the gate. His men fell like reeds before the scythe; I could not count them. At [[evt:15898|Mivimäkhi]] my general broke them again. I sat down before Litlitli and [[evt:15899|besieged it]], and they ate their asses and their dogs.

In this fifth year of my reign I came the last time to [[evt:15907|Litlitli]]. Again Iwa Imam stood in the gate, and again he fell back. In [[evt:15908|the siege]] that followed, the elders came out to me on their knees, with dust on their heads. They said: "Let our lord the king of Tlayiyan be father to Niz."

So I made [[evt:15909|the treaty]] with Iwa Imam at [[set:40|Litlatlti]], before Atlia who sees all, before [[dei:1|Uelti]] who weighs words. These are its words: the river road shall be open to my boats, and no toll shall be taken. Ten years there shall be no war between us.

And to keep the words, Iwa Imam has given me his son, the small one, the only son of his house, to dwell in Tlayiyan.

Now hear what you shall do. The boy comes to you with this tablet, with his nurse and two servants of his father. Give him the house by the temple of Atlia, the one with the fig tree. Give him bread, milk and honey; he is a small child and does not yet eat meat. Let the nurse sleep at his door. Let no one strike him. Let no one speak the name of his father with contempt in his hearing. Let the priests teach him the songs of [[mov:0|La'im]] and the names of the gods of Tlayiyan, that he may grow up knowing who his masters are, and love them.

Each new moon send me word: he eats, he sleeps, he is well. If he is sick, call the physician of my own house and pay him in [[cur:315|pi'a]] of good weight from my storehouse, not from the temple's.

For hear this also. A hostage who dies is a war that begins again. If the boy dies, Iwa Imam will say that I killed him, and the gods will hear it. But if the boy lives, and grows, and loves Tlayiyan, then when he sits on his father's throne, Niz will be mine without a spear.

And if Iwa Imam breaks the words of the tablet, the boy will still be fed. But he will not go home.

Written in the 5th year of Yi To'a, in the camp before Litlitli.
