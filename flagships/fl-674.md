For AY 4258 the record sets down two events at one spot. First the kingdom of Unushti founded a town there called [[set:310|Toimim]]. Then the district rose against Unushti, led by [[per:40256|Shimayuive]], who was twenty-six. He won its freedom in nine years and ruled it for thirty-eight, and the town outlived his kingdom. When Nambili dug at Toimim, the modern Dayimim, in 4887, he wrote: "No destruction layer was found: the city seems never to have been sacked" (PAL·EX·00302). What kind of man builds a state on ground his masters laid out that same year?

## A kingdom coming apart

Shimayuive was born in 4232, a Lizo, and his name means *eagle fire*. The record names no father or mother and says nothing of his life before 4258. It does give a wife, Oi Toe, whose name means *Eight Rain*, and two children born in 4257, a son, Ipil Ivie, and a daughter, Yai Foyatl. He went into rebellion as the father of infants.

[[pol:1700|Unushti]] had been beaten again and again by the Binge Empire, and the record names three of those blows among the causes of his revolt. At Lishimti in 4221 Binge brought 92,337 men against 2,901. In 4238, when he was six, Binge sacked the capital and burned its archives, and in 4239 it won again before the city. In 4250 a boy of fourteen, Wimafoyatl, came to the throne. At Chezhietle in 4256 he lost 179 men to Nemeth's 26. The court historian Litlame wrote that "because the omens had been neglected, the host fell back to the walls," and that "62 of ours were lost, but 65 of the enemy." The peace that gave up Mane and five other districts became a kindness: "out of mercy for the common folk, the king granted peace to Nimid" (PAL·CH·07176).

The surveys show what the chronicle hides. At the end of the 4240s Unushti held 22 districts and 498,328 people. By the end of the 4250s it held 10 districts and 231,474 people, with 2,553 men under arms. Six districts had gone to Nemeth in 4257. Six more went with the [[evt:40674|Revolt of Nis]] the next year and became the kingdom of [[pol:1725|Nis]]. They were a mixed country: the record finds five peoples there, the Lizo in only two districts. Four of the six already followed the [[mov:72|Ishiu]] faith, while Unushti's court kept the I'ishiu. No manifesto survives, but the line between the faiths is the clearest one the record draws.

## Four fields and one defeat

In the [[evt:40675|Nis War of Independence]] (4258–4267) Nis attacked in every battle and had more men in every one. At the [[evt:40727|Battle of Iwbetli (AY 4260)]] Shimayuive brought 1,433 men against 1,198 under [[per:40321|Yinuluisha]], the slimmest odds he ever had, and lost 55 to Unushti's 82. In 4261 his daughter died at four, of natural causes; the record says no more.

In 4262, without him, his general Yai Luisha attacked at Iwbetli with 1,817 men against Ie Muie's 728 and was beaten and killed. Nis lost 319 men to Unushti's 126, more than it lost in all four of Shimayuive's own battles together. Wimafoyatl's stone says "He killed 1,600 soldiers" (PAL·IN·03066). That is five times the record's figure, and the record names Ie Muie, not the king, as commander.

At the [[evt:40747|Battle of Iwbetli (AY 4263)]] he beat Yinuluisha again, 1,132 men against 701, losing 45 to 64. At the [[evt:40754|Battle of Iwbetli (AY 4264)]] he met [[per:40188|Wimafoyatl]] in person, a king of thirty-two against one of twenty-eight, 1,625 men against 873, and lost 67 to 120. At the [[evt:40762|Battle of Lishimti (AY 4265)]] he beat [[per:40368|Isyi Mof]], 1,138 against 830, for 31 dead to 149. In his four battles Nis lost 198 men and Unushti 415.

Was he a great captain? He never fought outnumbered, and his enemy was bleeding elsewhere. In 4265 Ölie, another Lizo kingdom, attacked Unushti too. In 4267 its king, Nistlushimin the Great, beat Wimafoyatl's 970 men near Lishimti with 41,865. That same year the Nis general Mishösfoyatl beat Yinuluisha's 398 with 1,703 at the First Battle of Lishimti, and at [[set:249|Lishimti]] the two kings signed the [[evt:40780|Treaty of Lishimti (AY 4267)]]. It granted independence and a truce of fifteen years. The truce outlived the other party: in 4269 Ölie annexed Unushti, and Wimafoyatl was executed at thirty-three. No text of the treaty survives. The record shows a rebel with a sense of timing and little taste for losing men.

## What the stones claim

His stele for Lishimti, raised in 4266, survives complete: "Shimayuive, king of Nis, defeated Unushti in Lishimti. He killed 400 soldiers. He erected this stele for Itlie, his lord. May Itlie destroy the name of the enemy" (PAL·IN·03068). The record gives 149 dead. A diorite stele of 4269 for the First Battle of Lishimti claims "He killed 300 soldiers" (PAL·IN·03069) against the record's 136, and gives the king a victory the record credits to Mishösfoyatl. It breaks off after "his lord. [...]". As a translator I find their sameness most telling. Wimafoyatl's stone and Shimayuive's call on the same sky-lord, Itlie, in the same words. Only the names and numbers change. The courts kept rival faiths, but in stone they boasted in one language.

In 4268, the first year of peace, Nis persecuted the I'ishiu at Toimim. The record does not say what was done or to how many. The Ishiu was a faith that sought converts. A rebel whose districts had broken from a court of the other faith turned, once safe, on those of his own subjects who still held it. A humane reading must set this beside the victories.

## Silver and grain

Nis fought no war from 4267 to 4316, so Shimayuive reigned twenty-nine years in peace. The army fell from 4,332 men at the end of the 4260s to 1,862 by the end of the 4270s. By the end of the 4280s, the last survey of his life, Nis had 267,315 people, up from 228,111, and its treasury had grown from 4,836 to 15,008 kilograms of silver. Grain at Toimim fell from about 7.4 grams of silver the measure in the 4260s to 6.5 in the 4270s. For the townspeople, peace meant cheaper bread.

In 4279, at forty-seven, he struck the [[evt:40809|First Coinage of Nis]], the [[cur:953|Isui]]. A corroded piece of the first issue, known from a hoard, shows his helmeted head and the legend *ŠIMAYWIVƏ PǪAƏ*, and on the back a bull walking and the mint mark of Toimim. It holds 5.8 grams of silver (PAL·NU·00406). The name was not new: Unushti had struck an Isui since 4226, with 4 grams. The record does not say why he kept the old name; the weight suggests why: the same money, honest and heavier by nearly half.

A drought, dated in the record from 4276 to 4282, covered Toimim and most of the kingdom. In 4290 he fixed a bronze plaque to the city's granary: "Shimayuive, king of Nis, restored this granary in Toimim. May Muivase bless the city" (PAL·IN·06378). Muivase was the earth-goddess. The record does not tie the plaque to the drought. Still, a king of fifty-eight mending a grain store is a different man from the king of the steles.

## The name of the enemy

Shimayuive died a natural death in 4296, aged sixty-four. The [[evt:41153|Accession of Ipil Ivie I]] brought his son [[per:40710|Ipil Ivie I]] to the throne at thirty-nine, and in 4297 Nis persecuted the I'ishiu again. Oi Toe outlived her husband by eleven years. Her son reigned seven years and her grandson, Ipil Ivie II the Last, fifteen. At Iwbetli in 4317 Ölie brought 88,521 men against 1,300, and King Baalmof of Ölie raised a stele that ends: "May Itlie destroy the name of the enemy" (PAL·IN·03112). Fifty-two years after Shimayuive cut those words against Unushti, they were cut against Nis. In 4318 Toimim was besieged and Nis annexed, sixty years after the rising.

The town remained. Toimim is the only settlement the record places inside Nis. By 4300 it held 55,323 people, a fifth of the kingdom, and the record counts 452,159 there in AY 4900. Shimayuive's stones lie, as the stones of his age did. The coin, the granary and the unburned ground of Toimim do not, and they are the better witnesses to a rebel who knew when to fight and then spent twenty-nine years keeping the peace.
