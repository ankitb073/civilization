title: Verses on the Arithmetic of King Wilau

*Verses passed from hand to hand in the taverns of Ölie. The author did not sign them, and was wise not to.*

Gods of [[pol:1497|Ölie]], each in your proper seat,  
the sky in its height and the earth beneath our feet,  
forgive a poor clerk who kneels to every one of you  
and cannot bring himself to kneel to the king as well.

His mother Nitllu Foyatl kept the realm with a careful hand.  
She counted every sheaf and every inch of land.  
Her son [[per:38166|Wilau]] has a hand that is careful too:  
he counts his victories, and lets the dead go uncounted.

*Hail Wilau, lord of the many and master of the few!*  
*Hail Wilau, who has never yet learned what a sum can do!*

At [[evt:38811|Nirun]] he marched with thirty men for one;  
the heralds cried "A victory!" when the day was done.  
But clerks who keep the tallies were careful to record  
that one of ours fell there for every two of theirs.

At [[evt:38820|Yimidiyi]] the next summer it went the same:  
the field was ours, and so were the widows and the shame.  
Then he signed the [[evt:38821|Treaty of Yimidiyi]] with a flourish of his quill  
and took five hundred talents, and the pyres are burning still.

*Hail Wilau, lord of the many and master of the few!*  
*Hail Wilau, who has never yet learned what a sum can do!*

Then he sent his drums off to Nemeth, though no one could tell why;  
the [[evt:38859|Ölie–Nemeth War]] marched out under a cloudless sky.  
It wandered over hill and fen, it wandered home again,  
and all it won for Ölie was a cartload of lame men.

Now the [[evt:38980|Ölie–Nishmam War]] has come and swallows the levy whole.  
At Myixim, four hundred men and more stood guard upon the wall.  
Against them came a host as many as grains of sand,  
and a captain who pleased the king was given the command.

Count it, you clerks, by the lamp, and count it again:  
seventy of the enemy died in the [[evt:38989|Battle of Myixim]],  
and for each of those he marched two thousand five hundred men.  
If the king calls that a triumph, then the plague is good for grain.

Then came the [[evt:38990|First Sack of Myixim]], and its books went up in fire;  
the scholars watched their archives burn upon a single pyre.  
Why should a lord who cannot count have any use for books?  
What he cannot read, he burns, and then he admires how it looks.

*Hail Wilau, lord of the many and master of the few!*  
*Hail Wilau, who has never yet learned what a sum can do!*

The priests of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] teach moderation in all things,  
justice in our dealings, and contempt for gold and rings.  
The wise draw back from appetite. The king has never heard;  
his appetite is land, and he will not believe a word.

Bread is cheap in Ölie, two-thirds of a [[cur:823|Myiu]] the measure,  
but a widow cannot eat the king's glory at her leisure.  
So I pray to every god, each in their place and due:  
keep [[set:264|Ölie]], and teach the king to count up to two.

And if the beadles find me, as beadles sometimes do,  
let them say I loved the realm. I did. I loved it more than [[per:38166|Wilau]].

*Written in the 23rd year of Wilau, by one who loves his country and fears the rope.*
