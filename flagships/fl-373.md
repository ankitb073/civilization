[[per:21550|Hihkehimin]], king of [[pol:945|Mane]], by ancient custom first among the servants of the old altars, to the priests who keep the houses of the gods in [[set:13|Tsivintuha]], greeting.

You will have heard it already in the market, for the market hears everything before the palace has finished saying it; but it is fitting that you should hear it also from me, and in writing, so that no man may say afterwards that the king spoke to you in one voice and to the city in another. In this year, the year 398 of the [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], as the scribes of the new teachers now reckon and as I too must now reckon, the kingdom of Mane has taken the I'ishiu for its own. The rites of the [[mov:25|Miyithuyishiu]] will no longer be the rites of the state. The [[evt:21980|Conversion of Mane to I'ishiu]] is done, and I have set my seal to it.

I did not seek this. Let that be said first, and plainly, since you will say it of me in any case. In the [[evt:21739|Collapse of the Tivinnu State]], ten years ago, when its provinces went each their own way like oxen when the yoke-pin breaks, it was to your altars that I came before I came to the throne. You will remember the morning. You read the signs, and the signs were favourable, and the [[evt:21741|Rise of Mane]] was proclaimed from your steps. I have not forgotten whose steps they were.

But a king who forgets whose kingdom it is will not keep it long. In ten years the teachers of the I'ishiu have gone into every street of this city. They have sat with the sick when the physicians would not. They have fed widows from their own bowls. They say that all things turn upon one wheel, that one power moves it, and that the release of the suffering is the whole of the law; and the people, who have suffered much since Tivinnu fell, have listened. I counted the households, as a king must, and the greater part were theirs before ever they were mine to give. I have not changed the faith of Mane. I have only ceased to pretend that it had not changed.

Consider, too, what the teachers themselves concede. They do not say your gods are nothing. They say the old gods are servants of the one power, or its shadows cast upon the world. A servant is not an enemy; a shadow is not a lie. If this is a demotion, it is a gentle one, and the gods, who are older than either of us, may bear it better than you fear.

Now hear what I have ordered, for I would not have you learn it from a tax-gatherer.

First: no house of the old gods in Tsivintuha shall be closed by the king's hand. Those that the people cease to visit will close by themselves, and I cannot help that.

Second: every priest now serving shall receive from the treasury, for the length of his life, the allowance he received this year, in good [[cur:478|Midnatl]] and not in grain.

Third: the sacred lands shall be counted, and what is counted shall not be taken without the consent of the priest who holds it.

Fourth: my son [[per:21774|Hihkehimin]], who bears my name, will be taught by the I'ishiu teachers, as he must be. But I shall also send him to you once in each month, that he may learn the old hymns and know what his fathers knew. A prince should understand what his father has set aside, even if he never takes it up again.

I am told that some among you mean to call this a betrayal. I do not ask you to love the decision. I ask you only to remember that a king's duty must come before his preference, and that I am, as I was ten years ago on your steps, attempting to do my duty.

Pray for me in whatever house still stands. I shall not forbid it, and I shall not ask whether you have done it.

Written at Tsivintuha, in the year 398 of the I'ishiu, by the king's own hand.
