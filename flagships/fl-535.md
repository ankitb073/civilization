*The words spoken by [[per:33435|Kiepadheyath]], by the grace of the One Power queen of [[pol:1431|Diebeguvebenga]], in the 36th year of her reign, before the altar set up in the field of [[set:278|Muethka]], when the peace was sworn; taken down by her chaplain.*

In the name of the One Power, who was before all things and will judge all things at the last; before whom the old gods are but servants and shadows; to whom every crown is a borrowed crown and every throne a footstool:

My lords spiritual and temporal, my vassals and liegemen, and you, lords of [[pol:1407|Nieve]], who stand today on the other side of this altar:

It is written that the Power sets kings upon the heights and brings them down to the dust, and that no one shall say to the Power, "What are you doing?" I am an old woman, and I have worn this crown six-and-thirty years. I have seen that saying fulfilled more than once. Today I see it fulfilled again.

Let no one here pretend that this war was of our seeking. The [[evt:34159|14th Diebeguvebenga Trade War]] was forced upon us by the bad faith of Nieve, who broke their word to our merchants, who closed their markets against our goods, who took the customs of our caravans and gave back nothing but insults. For eight years we sought justice by every road, and for eight years Nieve answered with the sword.

I will not hide that the Power tried us. At the [[evt:34201|Battle of Biniynge]] our host was broken, and many of our best knights did not come home. I wept for them then, and I have not ceased to pray for them. But the Power chastens those it loves, as a father chastens a son, and does not cast them off.

For behold what followed. At the [[evt:34239|Battle of Muethka]] the enemy fled before our banners like chaff before the wind, and their dead lay heaped in the furrows. Then came the [[evt:34240|First Sack of Muethka]], and the pride of this city was humbled, and its records went up in smoke; let that fire be a lesson to every city that trusts in its walls more than in its oaths. And when [[per:33531|Pevekman]] met us this very year with all the strength that remained to him, at the [[evt:34249|Battle of Muethka]], the Power gave him into our hand, and his host was scattered, and he fled within these walls. In the [[evt:34250|Third Siege of Muethka]] we sat down before them, and the Power softened his heart.

Lords of Nieve, I do not rejoice over you. The faith that we profess teaches that the good life is the life of compassion and of release from suffering, and I would be a poor daughter of that faith if I gloated over the vanquished. Your king has come out of his gate to make peace. That is enough. I ask no more of him than justice demands.

Hear, then, the terms of the [[evt:34251|Treaty of Muethka]], which are sworn here before the altar:

That Nieve yield to the crown of Diebeguvebenga the lands named in the instrument, to be held of us forever.

That Nieve pay to our treasury an indemnity in silver, reckoned in good [[cur:728|Nieleth]], for the losses of our merchants and the cost of this war.

That our merchants go freely in all the markets of Nieve, and trade there without hindrance.

That there be peace between the two crowns for fifteen years, and that whoever breaks it answer for it before the Power at the judgement.

My vassals: you followed me when I summoned you, as your oaths required, and you did not count the cost. I will not forget it. Every lord who brought his men to Muethka shall have his reward from my hand when we return to [[set:62|Viemunga]].

And you, [[per:33531|Pevekman]], king of Nieve: you have fought bravely, though in an unjust cause. Lay your hand upon the altar with mine. Swear, as I swear, before the One Power and the priests of [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]], who keep its truth in order from age to age.

Let the peace be sworn. Let the swords be sheathed. Let the dead be buried with honour, theirs as well as ours. And let the Power, which sees all oaths, remember this one.
