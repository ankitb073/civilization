*[Obverse. A lamentation tablet from Tagath; the refrain is marked in the margin with a single wedge and printed here in italics.]*

Woe for the great jar! Woe for the jar of [[set:9|Tagath]]!  
The jar that held the oil of many lands, it lies on the floor.  
It lies on the floor in six sherds. Who will mend it?  
*Woe for the great jar.*

[[dei:73|Tithu]] of the storm, did you strike it?  
[[dei:76|Tsilmu]] of wisdom, did you not see it falling?  
Pugazhu of the sun, you saw it from above. Why did you not cry out?  
They did not cry out. The jar fell. *Woe for the great jar.*

Once the house of [[pol:237|Tagath]] was the head of many heads.  
The river-lands brought it barley. The hill-lands brought it goats.  
The far lands brought it copper and salt and fine cloth.  
The silver came up the roads to Tagath, the [[cur:128|Tsimbe]] came heavy into the storehouse.  
Near four hundred years the jar stood full. Near four hundred years the oil did not spill.  
*Woe for the great jar.*

Then came the crack. Hear how it came.

The spears went out against the strangers, to Khambive, in the days of our fathers.  
That was the [[evt:9206|Battle of Khambive]]. Many went out; many did not come home.  
The strangers were few, but the field was theirs. That was the first crack.

Then Kambizhin, a city of our own, turned its face from us.  
It took its own lord. It shut its gate to the officers of Tagath.  
That was the [[evt:9286|Secession of Kambizhin]]. That was the second crack.

Then Kambizhin came out against the great host of Tagath,  
a few spears against many, six of ours to every one of theirs.  
That was the [[evt:9336|Battle of Dadavli]]. Hear it and weep:  
the few broke the many, and the many were spilled on the ground like water.  
That was the third crack, the deep one.

And in the next year but one, the jar fell.  
It did not fall in a battle. It did not fall in a fire.  
It fell as a jar falls when the cracks meet: all at once, in the night, with a small sound.  
That was the [[evt:9363|Collapse of the Tagath State]]. Woe for the great jar.  
*Woe for the great jar.*

*[Reverse.]*

Count the sherds, singer. Count them, and weep for each.

One sherd is [[pol:414|Tagath]]: the city keeps its own name; it is small now.  
One sherd is [[pol:415|Duntsinile]]; it has set its own lord on its own seat.  
One sherd is [[pol:416|Luthe]]; it does not send its barley to us.  
One sherd is [[pol:417|Puvu]]; it does not send its goats to us.  
One sherd is [[pol:418|Kakuge]]; its officers wear a strange cloth.  
One sherd is [[pol:419|Tyimbi]]; it weighs with its own scales.  
Six sherds. Six lords. Six storehouses. One floor.  
*Woe for the great jar.*

The scribes of the new lords write: the [[evt:9364|Rise of Tagath]], the [[evt:9366|Rise of Luthe]], the rising of this one and the rising of that one.  
They write rising. We write falling. Who writes truly? The teachers of [[mov:11|Kyif]] say: no one owns the truth.  
Then let us own our weeping, at least.

Fourteen years the sherds have lain on the floor.  
In the street of the potters the children ask: was it big, the jar?  
Big, we say. It held the oil of many lands.

[[dei:74|Binge]] of the grain, still feed the city that is left.  
[[dei:75|Bumbi]] of the sea, still bring the boats to whoever will trade with us.  
Tithu, the storm is over. Leave us the sherds.  
The wheel turns. What ends will begin again, say the teachers.  
Let it begin again, then. Let the potter come.  
*Woe for the great jar. Let the potter come.*

*[Lower edge.]*

> For the singing at the day of mourning in Tagath, in the 410th year of Tagath.
