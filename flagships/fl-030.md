*Here begins the lament for those who fell in the holy war, to be read in the churches on the day of the dead.*

Weep, O [[set:18|Lienunushal]], mother of cities, city of the founding;  
put ashes on thy towers and sackcloth on thy gates;  
for thy sons went out in the morning of the faith,  
and in the evening they did not return.

Fifteen years they bore the sign of the One into [[reg:7|Tuennga]];  
fifteen years they kept the vigil and the march,  
lord and vassal, knight and bowman, each to the service that he owed;  
for it is written: the servant who keeps his oath is not forgotten at the Judgement.

In the days of our fathers [[pol:1328|Pienpedhzhe]] came against us,  
and in [[evt:32569|that war]] the Most High broke their pride upon our walls.  
Therefore our lord took up [[evt:33116|the holy war against Pienpedhzhe]],  
to carry the light of [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]] to those who sat in shadow.

Remember [[evt:33128|Zhinlampive]], and the banners in the morning wind.  
Remember [[evt:33141|Jerathnu]], where the heathen queen gave way.  
There [[per:32551|Zhieyutuevan]] carried the standard, and the enemy fled before him;  
and in the year of sorrow the old captain lay down in his bed and was gathered to his rest.

For in that year came [[evt:33148|Virnadhu]]. Four times our hosts came to Virnadhu,  
and thrice the field was taken from us, and once we took it back.  
There our lord the emperor [[per:32684|Matafvafen]] stood among his knights,  
and the river was choked, and the meadow was red as the vestments of the martyrs.  
In the next year [[per:32801|Vienatdan]] went down and [[evt:33154|avenged them at Virnadhu]];  
but who shall avenge the avenger? The field turned again, and again.

At Nunnga [[per:32807|Pavvukhupiemen]] was driven back with his vassals;  
at [[evt:33173|Diviminnge]] he drove the enemy before him like chaff before the wind.  
So it went, a year to them and a year to us,  
as the sea comes in upon the sand and goes out from it.

And in those days the king of Pienpedhzhe, [[per:32814|Natdapuefnen]],  
bowed his realm before the One, and his people took up the faith.  
Then said the simple: now shall the swords be put away.  
But they took the Name into their mouths and not into their hearts,  
and they would not give up the land, nor open their gates to the true teachers.

Thrice at [[evt:33208|Nuwkennga]] our hosts came, and Vienatdan won the second field;  
but the first and the third were bitter, and many good men lie there without the last rites.  
At last at [[evt:33284|Munu]] the host was broken, and the emperor made peace,  
and swore [[evt:33285|the peace of Munu]] with the king of Pienpedhzhe for a term of years.

We gave no city in that peace. We gave no field, no tower, no river.  
We gave only our sons.

Do not say the sword of the enemy slew them. It was our sins that slew them:  
the pride of the great, the greed of the stewards, the sloth of the priests;  
the Most High weighed us in the balance, and we were found wanting.  
He chastens the son He loves; we are chastened, and we are loved.

Yet weep not as those who have no hope.  
Time runs in a line from the Making to the Judgement, and it does not turn back;  
and at the end of the line the trumpet shall sound over Virnadhu,  
and the dead of Nuwkennga shall stand up in their armour,  
and the Most High shall release them from all suffering,  
and wipe the dust of Tuennga from their faces.

Keep, O Most High, our lord Matafvafen, who bore the burden of this war;  
keep the widows, keep the orphans, keep the lands of the fallen for their heirs;  
and keep the faith of [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]] until the Judgement.

*Here ends the lament. Written in the year 4424 from the founding of Lienunushal.*
