To the house of the queen in [[set:0|Tallin]], to the keepers of the storehouse and the keepers of the gate, say:  
Thus says your servant, the scribe of the queen's tent before Pumi.

May [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the bronze arm keep you. May Dumamnul of the high sky keep you. May the barley in your storehouse be heaped up like a hill.

With the queen, my lady, it is well. With her body it is well. With her spears it is well. With her horses and her oxen it is well. Rejoice, my lords, and let the drum be beaten in the gate.

Hear the word from the beginning, as the queen commands it to be written.

In the thirty-sixth year of [[per:957|Shenshetkuth]] the queen sent her spears down into the land of [[reg:16|Yida]] by the hills, to take it from the hand of [[pol:26|Duvekopuvinga]]. The men of Duvekopuvinga had been broken once already, at the [[evt:816|Battle of Bimnge]], when they went out against another people and came home few. Their hand was weak. The queen said: "Take the land." And the spears went down.

In the thirty-ninth year, at [[set:135|Virathkanu]], [[per:1057|Puftovan]] the old general, the grey spear of Yida, stood against [[per:1082|Kulvalomin]], the general of Duvekopuvinga. Puftovan broke him. Two thousand three hundred and thirty-three of Duvekopuvinga lay down on that field. Of ours, four hundred and forty-one. This was the [[evt:911|Battle of Virathkhanu]]. In that same year our spears came before the walls of [[set:17|Pumi]] and ringed them; this was the [[evt:912|Siege of Himi]].

Now hear the word of this year, the forty-first year of the queen.

[[per:1097|Panshelon]], the king of Duvekopuvinga, came out himself with all the spears that were left to him. By Rela he came out. [[per:1113|Magunnufmalin]], the general of the queen, stood against him. The morning was cold. The ground was hard. Magunnufmalin went into them, and they ran. Eight hundred and eleven of Duvekopuvinga lay down. Six hundred and eighty-four of ours lay down, and we have burned them with honour, and their names are on a tablet for their mothers. This was the [[evt:918|Battle of Rile]].

Then the gate of Pumi was opened. Our men went in. They brought out its bronze. They brought out its oil jars and its wine jars and its oxen. They brought out its fine cloth and its sheep. This was the [[evt:919|Sack of Himi]]. The spoil is loaded on carts. The carts will come to Tallin by the river road. Let the keepers of the storehouse make room. Let them weigh the silver in Tunrun and set the count before the queen when she comes home.

Then Panshelon came before the queen in her tent at Pumi. He came with his hands open. He bowed his neck. He put his hand to the clay, and the queen put her hand to the clay. This is the [[evt:920|Treaty of Himi]]. The land by the hills is the queen's land. The villages there will send barley to Tallin, and the herds there will send their firstlings to Tallin.

Six summers the [[evt:884|First Yida–Duvekopuvinga War]] went on. It is ended. The field is Yida's. The field is Vulme's.

Let the priests of Jinru stand before the gods in order. Give a bull to Vulme for the queen's arm. Give a ram to Dumamnul for the clear sky. Give the first sheaf of the new land to [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] of the grain. Give oil to Eva of the hearth, that the fires of Tallin burn high. Let no god be forgotten, each in the god's own place.

The queen will set out for home when the moon is full. Let the road be swept. Let the gate be hung with green.

Written in the 41st year of Shenshetkuth, queen of [[pol:25|Yida]], at Pumi.
