To those who still keep the hearth of the [[mov:2|Zhenro]] in [[set:0|Tallen]] and in the villages, and who pour out at the turning of the year though the doors are shut: peace, and the favour of the gods of our fathers.

I write to you from the prison under the wall, by a lamp that a guard, who shall not be named here, has let me keep. I do not know how many more nights the lamp will burn. I have been told that I shall be brought before the judges of the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] when the season of their fast is ended, and asked once more to say that the gods of our fathers are shadows. I have answered them already, and I will answer them again, and so this letter is written in the plain expectation that it may be my last. Do not grieve at that. A wheel does not grieve that it turns.

You have heard what was proclaimed in the square. In the name of [[per:26771|Obelshewen]], our king, the household shrines are to be broken, the old priests are forbidden to teach, and whoever is found with a lamp lit before the ancestral images is to be given to the judges. This is the [[evt:27257|Persecution of the Jinru in Yeda]], as the clerks have written it on their rolls; and they have written it in the king's name. But the king is a boy of nine years. I do not believe he has ever seen a household shrine, nor wished one harm. It is not the boy who fears us. It is those who govern while he is a boy, and the teachers who govern them.

Consider, friends, what they say of us, and consider what has befallen. They teach that time flies like an arrow toward a single judgment, and that whoever does not stand on their side of the mark at the end is lost. We teach, as our fathers taught, that all things turn on the great wheel, and that what ends will begin again; that each god has a place and is owed a due; that a city which pays its dues is a city the gods prosper. For a generation now [[pol:1135|Yeda]] has been taught the arrow and has forgotten the wheel. And what has the arrow brought?

It brought the [[evt:27172|Battle of Baaliurinu]], where our army stood against the men of Lenunushal and gave way. It brought the [[evt:27186|Battle of Puthnimdu]] in the year following, and the [[evt:27198|Battle of Taulin]] in the year after that, under the very walls of this city, where our general fell. It brought the [[evt:27199|Second Sack of Taulin]], when strangers walked in our streets and carried out what they pleased. It brought the [[evt:27200|Treaty of Taulin]], by which towns that our grandfathers held were signed away for fifteen years of quiet. I do not say the old gods did this to punish us; I say only that the new teaching promised a protection it has not given. If prosperity is a sign of favour, as our fathers held, then the sign is easily read.

And now, having lost the field, the city looks for someone to blame, and finds us, who have no army and no seat in the council, and who have been easy to find because we never hid. Twenty-seven years ago, when I was young, they did the same thing. We endured it then; we endure it now. The wheel is patient.

To the brothers and sisters of the hearth I give this charge. Do not raise your hands against the judges; it is not our way to seek converts, and it is not our way to make martyrs of others. Pay what you owe; pay your dues to the gods and your taxes to the king, to the last [[cur:635|Yaf]], so that no one may say the people of the wheel cheat. Keep the hymns in your mouths if you cannot keep them on your shelves. Teach your children the names. What is broken will be made again.

As for me: I have been offered silver to recant, and the return of my house. I have declined. A house is a small thing. The wheel is great.

Written in the year 3841 from the founding of Tallen, in the prison under the wall.
