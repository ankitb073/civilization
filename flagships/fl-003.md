To the anointed priests of the house of Nothol in [[set:10|Navnga]], to the keepers of the order of [[mov:3|Ryethki]], and to the elders who sit in the gate, speak: thus says [[per:1313|Kukathren]], king of [[pol:8|Navnga]], the strong one, beloved of Nothol, the shepherd whom Novegu made wise.

With me it is well. With the army it is well. With the asses and the carts it is well. With the standards of the gods it is well. May [[dei:20|Nothol]] the fire keep you. May [[dei:21|Duminu]] the storm pass over your roofs. May [[dei:22|Vinba]] the earth fill your granaries. May [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] the river rise in its season. May Shineyka the sea lie calm before you. May Novegu the wise open your ears to my words.

Hear what I have done. In the 15th year of Kukathren, in the month of threshing, I went down into the land of [[reg:16|Yida]]. Three years now the army of Navnga has walked in that land, in [[evt:1045|this war of Navnga against Yida]]. Three years the men of [[pol:25|Yida]] have shut their gates. They have sent no gift. They have bowed to no god of ours. You know how in the days of our fathers, in [[evt:811|the holy war of Navnga against Yida]], the men of Yida mocked the gods, and the gods were patient. You know how at [[evt:1029|Zhidhzhinge]] the army of Yida was thrown down by the hand of strangers, and its strength was broken. The gods showed me this. The gods said: now. So I went down.

Before [[set:147|Binga]] the Yida came out against me. They were many. They were more than we. Seventeen thousand men they brought, spearmen and bowmen and men with slings. Their queen [[per:1292|Ayiviyath]] came out before them, she who sat on the seat of Yida, she whom they called the brave. She said: the men of Navnga are few. I said: the gods are not few.

At dawn I poured water to Navbuthen. At dawn I burned fat to Nothol. The omen was good. The liver was clean. I set the men of the city on the right, I set the men of the villages on the left, and I myself went in the middle with the standard of Nothol.

The Yida broke like a pot. They broke like dry reeds in the storm of Duminu. I struck them from the morning until the shadows were long. I pursued them to the water. I counted the hands of their slain: six thousand and five hundred I counted, and more beside, whom the river took, whom the dogs took, whom no one counted. Ayiviyath their queen fell among her spearmen. Ayiviyath their queen lies in the dust of her own field. Her standard I have taken. I have set it in my tent before the gods of Navnga. Of my own men, few fell. Their names are with the scribes. Their fathers will not be ashamed.

Thus [[evt:1052|the battle of Binga]] was mine. Let it be written on the tablet. Let it be sung in the gate.

Now hear my words. Let a bull be burned to Nothol. Let a ram be burned to Duminu. Let a jar of oil be poured into the river for Navbuthen. Let the priests weigh out of the treasury ten [[cur:2|muushi]] of silver for every house whose son fell at Binga, and barley beside, as is right. Let no one in Navnga say that the king forgets his dead.

Yet the war is not finished. The towns of Yida still shut their gates. Their elders still sit on their walls and look down at me. I will not turn back. As I sealed the tablet at [[evt:1018|Dainagafu]], and the land bowed, and the silver came, so Yida will bow. As the army of Navnga stood at [[evt:1002|Nimid]] in the days of my mother the queen, so it stands now.

Send me barley. Send me arrows and bronze for the spearheads. Send me the young men of the levy before the rains come. Send me word of the river and of the barley. Do not let the messenger sleep on the road.

Thus says Kukathren, king of Navnga, in the camp before Binga, in the land of Yida. May the gods of Navnga keep you. May the great wheel turn for us.
