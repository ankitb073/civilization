*[Obverse]*

To my cousin, son of my father's brother, say: thus says your cousin, the weaver, who was of [[set:26|Va'mi]].  
May [[dei:6|Nili]], the river, keep you. May [[dei:7|Yüb]], lady of the hearth-fire, keep your fire.  
May your house be well. May your wife be well. May your sons be well.

I am alive. My wife is alive. Two of my children are alive.  
The house in Va'mi is not alive. The street of the weavers is not alive.

Hear, cousin, how it was.  
In the 32nd year of [[per:7957|Shish'o Mab]], the king, the men of [[pol:292|I'ti]] came again.  
Two years ago they beat our king at Veümin. This year they beat him at Veümin again.  
That was the [[evt:7877|Battle of Veümin]]. Our men were more than their men.  
Our men were more, and our men fell. The men who came home said: they fell like barley.  
Of every ten who fell, nine were ours. That is what the men who came home said.  
Then the men of I'ti came on to Va'mi. The gate did not hold.

They came into the city at evening. They came with fire.  
We heard the shouting from the lower town. My wife took the small ones.  
I took the loom-beam on my back, the good beam, the beam of my father.  
At the end of the street I put down the beam. It was too heavy. I left it.  
A man of I'ti hit me on the head with a spear-shaft. He took my cloak.  
He did not kill me. [[dei:11|Nizhashta]], lady of war, turned his spear. I do not know why.

My eldest son was at the wall with the levy. He did not come to the river gate.  
We waited at the river gate until the moon went down. He did not come.  
Cousin, if you hear of a weaver's son of Va'mi, tall, with a burn on his left hand, send to me.

*[Reverse]*

That was the [[evt:7878|First Sack of Bami]]. So the scribes call it. "First," they write, as if there will be a second. May Nili not allow it.

Now there is peace. The king has made the [[evt:7879|Treaty of Bami]].  
The criers say: the river lands go to I'ti. The criers say: Va'mi gives tribute to I'ti.  
Tribute for thirty-nine years, they say. Thirty-nine years! My grandson will pay it.  
The king lives. May the king live. But the king gave the river lands.

Cousin, I am in the village of the potters, two days from the city, in the house of a stranger.  
He gives us the threshing floor to sleep on. He gives us one meal.  
A measure of barley is nearly one [[cur:171|Sazhon]], and I have no Sazhon.  
I had four Sazhon in a pot under the floor. The pot is under the ashes now.

Cousin, your father and my father were sons of one mother.  
They sat at one hearth. They gave to [[dei:7|Yüb]] at one hearth.  
I ask you by that hearth: take us in until the barley harvest.  
I can weave. My wife can spin. My daughter can carry water. The small one eats little.  
I will make you a cloak, a good cloak, for nothing. I will make cloaks for your sons.

Send word by the salt-carrier who brings this tablet. Give him bread. He is a good man.  
If you say no, I will not be angry. Everyone is poor this year.  
If you say yes, we will come at the new moon.  
Thus says your cousin. May Nili keep you. The tablet is finished.
