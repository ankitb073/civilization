In the name of the One Power, which set time running from the making of the world toward the judgement, and before which every crown will be laid down.

I, [[per:31732|Nakange]], by the favour of the One Queen of [[pol:1279|Dagafu]], daughter of King [[per:31330|Nayuvizhi]] and of Queen Nage, of the house of Nakathash, being old and weak in body but whole in mind, make this my testament. I make it in the sixty-third year of my reign, so that those who come after me may know what I received, what I did with it, and what I leave.

My father named me for the night, because I was born in the dark hours. He died when I was twelve years old, and I came to the throne as a child. My mother Nage held the realm for me until I was of age, and held it faithfully, and gave it into my hands undiminished. Let her be remembered in the prayers of every house of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] in this kingdom.

When I came of age I rode with the host myself. I do not write this out of pride, for pride is a vice the One despises. I write it as the truth: on every field where I rode in person, the One gave me the victory. Twice in my youth and twice in my middle years I broke the enemies of Dagafu, and both wars ended with the enemy suing for peace, giving up towns and paying silver into my treasury. The realm my father left me was smaller by a great deal than the realm I leave my son.

When I grew too old for the saddle, my captains rode in my name. They won twice at Lamligul, in the [[evt:32602|Battle of Lamligul]] and again the year after. They laid the [[evt:32608|Siege of Ajü]], and at Ajü I set my seal to the [[evt:32609|Conquest of Pièvi]], so that Pièvi came under the crown of Dagafu.

In these last years, when the people of Bige closed their markets to us, I did not suffer it. The [[evt:32643|19th Pyivi Trade War]] was fought to open the roads of trade, not out of greed. Twice my captains beat them before Bige, in the [[evt:32651|Battle of Bige]] and again this year, and twice they shut the town up in siege. This year I signed the [[evt:32664|Treaty of Bige]]: they pay us an indemnity, they open their markets to our traders, and they have sworn a truce of fifteen years. I tell my son that this peace was bought dearly and he should keep it. I say no more about its price.

Now hear my bequests.

To my son [[per:32192|Nazhige]], whom the law of seniority calls to the throne after me, I leave the crown of Dagafu, the seal, the sword of my father and my counsel, which is this. Do justice in your dealings, with the great and the small alike. Be moderate in all things, at table, in anger and in war. The One gives each person an inner light and reason, so listen to your councillors, but in the end judge by that light. Prosperity is a sign of the One's favour, so do not waste it on vanities, and do not think it can never be taken back.

To the houses of prayer in [[set:9|Dagafu]] I leave one thousand [[cur:713|Shiègi]], for lamps and for the poor, to be paid within the year of my death.

To the widows of soldiers who fell in my wars I leave the rents of my own fields for ten years, to be divided fairly.

All debts owed to my private purse by the poor of the city I forgive. All prisoners held for debt alone in the royal prisons are to be set free on the day of my burial.

Let me be buried beside my father, without gold or show, in a plain robe. The One weighs deeds, not ornaments.

I ask the One Power to forgive me whatever I have done amiss. Some of it I know, and some of it only the One knows. When the end of time comes and every ruler stands to be judged, may I be found to have given more than I took.

Sealed with my seal in Dagafu, in the year 3421 of the Uthungala.

**NAKANGE, Queen**
