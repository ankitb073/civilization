A translator meets [[per:26592|Ma'she Tlie]] under two names. On his own stones he is Ma'she Tlie, "great king, king of Ane", and his name means "Ten House", a name of solid walls and many rooms. In the chronicles of the peoples he conquered the scribes spell him Makhishe Tlie, and the epithets attached to him are not flattering. The palace scribe of Toshashfin says he set his hand against them "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore". A lost Tamesitsiyi history, quoted centuries later, calls him "swollen with pride". Later ages called him the Great. This chapter asks what the record allows each of these voices.

## Three kings in three years

He was born in AY 2957, a Lizo, in the royal house of [[pol:922|Ana]], the empire that had risen from the ruins of an older Ana four centuries before. A brother born the same year, perhaps a twin, died at four. His mother died when he was fourteen, and a younger brother did not live past twelve. One sister survived him by eighteen years. It was not an unusual childhood for its age, even in a palace.

The house itself was young. His great-grandfather had seized the throne in 2928 and held it for fifty-two years, dying in 2980 at ninety-one. Then the crown moved with alarming speed. The founder's son reigned two years and died in 2982. His son, [[per:26581|A'initlue'e]], whom posterity remembers as "the Brief", reigned barely one. When he died in 2983, at forty-nine, the [[evt:26950|Accession of Ma'she Tlie]] made a man of twenty-six the third new king in three years. He had married a woman three years younger, and their first son, born in 2981, would die in 2985. The heir, [[per:26935|Ba'aliuye'an]], was born the year after his father's accession.

## The war he inherited

He inherited a war as well as a crown. The [[evt:26936|Fourth Ana–Namosash War]] had begun in 2981 against [[pol:1157|Namosash]], a city-state. In 2982 his father had led an army of more than a hundred thousand against a defending force of 322, lost 53 men, and sacked the city, burning its archives. The new king finished the work. At the [[evt:26955|Battle of Namosash (AY 2983)]] he led 82,299 men against 201 under [[per:26596|Sotle]], and lost 11 of them; the defenders lost 70. The city was sacked again. The [[evt:26957|Conquest of Namosash]], sealed with its queen, [[per:26419|Piesimen]], who had reigned since 2970, annexed one district. Over the whole war Ana lost 64 men to take Namosash; the city lost 131. His stele by the city claims 400 enemy dead.

I translate such figures without comment in the apparatus, but here they demand one. The record does not explain why a host of that size was marched against so few, some four hundred Ana soldiers for every defender. Display, not need, is the likeliest cause. The stele, of course, says nothing of Ana's dead, and still multiplies the enemy's nearly six times.

## Onithie: the king and the queen

His second war was more nearly a war between equals. The [[evt:27011|Ana–Mieshlethie War]] (2989–2993) was fought against [[pol:1117|Mieshlethie]], a kingdom able to field seventeen to twenty-nine thousand men. In its first battle, in 2990, Mieshlethie's queen fell at the head of her army, and her daughter [[per:26682|Miemamovi I]] succeeded at twenty-two. A stele for the next year's victory at Fiukh credits Ma'she Tlie with "15,000 soldiers" killed. The record gives 2,157, and another man commanded. The king did take the field at the [[evt:27062|Battle of Onithie (AY 2992)]], against the young queen herself: 75,563 men against 19,725, with 438 Ana dead and 2,500 of hers.

The [[evt:27071|Treaty of Onithie (AY 2993)]] survives on a bronze tablet sworn at [[set:110|Onithie]]. "Peace is granted by Ma'she Tlie, emperor of Ane, to Miemamovi," it reads after the opening invocation. Mieshlethie gave up twenty districts, acknowledged Ana's overlordship, and promised to "send its levies when summoned". A daughter whose mother had died fighting Ana now owed Ana soldiers. She reigned another twenty-seven years. The war cost Ana 5,288 dead and Mieshlethie 13,851.

## Toshashfin and Likha

The [[evt:27162|Second Ana–Toshashfin War]] (3001–3004) returned to the old arithmetic. [[pol:1167|Toshashfin]] was a city-state. At the [[evt:27174|Battle of Likha (AY 3002)]] the king led 99,373 men against 995 under [[per:26784|Mashe Toa]], lost 45 and killed 229; the city was sacked and its archives burned. At the [[evt:27201|Battle of Thushashfi (AY 3004)]] he brought 82,475 against 680 and lost 88, one for every four defenders who fell. The [[evt:27203|Conquest of Toshashfin]] annexed eight districts from [[per:26715|Tlamfen Simen]], king since 2996, at Thushashfi itself. The Toshashfin chronicle, water-stained and broken, records no battles at all. It records only that the city was "[...] taken", and asks, "Who will now remember the kings of old?"

His last war he did not lead in person. The [[evt:27288|Third Ana–Tamesitsiyi War]] (3011–3018) against [[pol:1166|Tamesitsiyi]] was fought by his generals in five battles and cost Ana 1,734 men against 4,111. The Tamesitsiyi fragment remembers a defeat at Likha in 3013 as "treachery". In the middle of it, in 3013, the king restored the wall of his capital; the foundation tablet asks the moon god to "bless the city". The [[evt:27370|Treaty of Likha (AY 3018)]], sworn with Queen [[per:26869|Baetluee]] at [[set:273|Likha]], ceded twelve districts and the town itself. A boundary stone set up in the new lands bears his name; its last lines are worn away.

## The measure of greatness

He died in 3021, at sixty-four, of natural causes, and the [[evt:27412|Accession of Ba'aliuye'an]] passed the throne quietly to a son of thirty-seven. Baetluee died the same year. The son reigned twenty-three years, and his epitaph in the royal tombs gives him no title of his own beyond king. It says only that he was "son of Ma'she Tlie".

The case for the epithet is real. In thirty-eight years Ana grew from about 328 districts to 368, its people from some eighteen million to over twenty-one million, and every war of his reign ended in Ana's favour. The case against is the arithmetic of force. The four wars of his reign cost Ana about 7,200 dead against some 18,700 among its enemies, but twice he sent eighty to a hundred thousand men against small cities of a few hundred to a thousand defenders that could not have stood against him in any case. The empire's internal order was no stronger at his death than at his accession. His last war fed another war against Tamesitsiyi in 3040, and the empire he enlarged broke into six successor states in 3086, sixty-five years after his death.

The defeated scribes were partisan, but they were not simply wrong. "Swollen with pride" is unfair as a judgement of the man. As a reading of his campaigns against small cities, nothing in the record refutes it.
