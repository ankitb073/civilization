*[Obverse]*

Hear me, [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear, who goes before the host.  
Hear me, Dumamnul of the high sky, who sees the field from above.  
Hear me, Vinba, mother of the furrow, who drinks what the spear spills.

In the 24th year of Mirmakhkulin, king of [[pol:1|Tallin]], beloved of the gods, I who carried the spear set down the years of the spear. My hands are old. My knees are old. The words are not old. Let the sons of my sons hear them.

In the first year the king took the seat in [[set:0|Tallin]]. The men of the old house call it the [[evt:441|Usurpation of Mirmakhkulin]]. I do not call it so. The gods gave the seat; the gods do not give in vain. And the king said: "The land of [[reg:9|Tupujinga]] is good land. Its barley stands to the knee. Its ewes drop twins. Why does Jinontonu eat it? Let Tallin eat it." So the war went out.

*[Column II]*

In the second year we went down to Puthnimdu, by the walls of [[set:90|Putnimdo]]. That was the [[evt:447|Battle of Puthnimdu]]. We were seven thousand and four hundred and seventy-five. The men of [[pol:12|Jinontonu]] were few. [[per:571|Natululrinin]] led them, he of the ox-hide shield. But Vulme slept that day. The mud took our feet. The arrows came out of the reeds. Many of ours lay down by Putnimdo and did not rise. I do not count them here. Let the earth count them. Vinba has them.

In the third year the king said: "Again." We went down to Puthnimdu a second time. That was the [[evt:451|Battle of Puthnimdu]] again. Now we were thirteen thousand and four hundred and sixty-seven; the dust of us covered the sun. [[per:504|Dolulrinin]] stood against us with two thousand. The king himself, [[per:564|Mirmakhkulin]], went first, his helm of bronze shining. The men of Jinontonu broke. Six hundred and four of them lay down. The field was Tallin's. That night we burned fat on the fire to Eva, and the smoke went up straight, and the priests said: "It is well received."

In the fourth year we held the fords. We ate the barley of Tupujinga. That year I took a wound in the thigh. It closed.

*[Reverse]*

In the fifth year the general [[per:582|Tafbafununin]] led us to Zhinontunu, to the walls of [[set:6|Jinontonu]] the city. Natululrinin came out a second time with one thousand and one hundred and forty-seven. We were seven thousand and seven hundred and forty-three. That was the [[evt:457|Battle of Zhinontunu]]. He went back behind his walls. Then we sat down around the city. That was the [[evt:458|Siege of Zhinontunu]]. We dug. We waited. The moon of Yenan filled and emptied and filled.

In the sixth year the king came. We were twelve thousand and thirty-nine. Dolulrinin came out with nine hundred and fourteen, no more; the rest were hungry on the wall. In the [[evt:462|Battle of Zhinontunu]], the second at that place, the king broke him. Then the gate fell. Then we went in. That was the [[evt:463|Sack of Zhinontunu]]. I took a bronze bowl and a woollen cloak. The silver went to the king's house, weighed in the niga, niga upon niga, in sacks.

Then Dolulrinin came out on his knees. He put his seal beside the seal of the king. That was the [[evt:464|Treaty of Zhinontunu]]. Jinontonu gave land of Tupujinga to Tallin. Jinontonu bowed down to be a servant of Tallin. Ten years of peace were sworn by the gods of both.

Thus ended the [[evt:444|First Tallin–Chinontonu War]].

*[Lower edge]*

To Nidhikhti I gave a measure of barley for every year of the spear. To Vulme I gave the bronze bowl. Many went out from Tallin. Fewer came back. I came back. The gods turn the great wheel; what ends will begin again. May the king live. May Tallin stand.
