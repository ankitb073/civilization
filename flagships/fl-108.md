In the name of the one power, before whom the old gods stand as servants, and under the peace of our lord [[per:35365|Bishifin]], emperor of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], who broke the enemy at [[evt:35857|Nivirathyirshi]] and at [[evt:35862|Yirantudhu]], and by the [[evt:35863|Conquest of Biererenu]] enlarged the inheritance of his fathers: here is set down what was said at [[set:147|Binge]] in the year 4620 from the founding of [[set:0|Tallin]], between the Elders of the [[mov:69|Kiyiviki]] and me, [[per:35408|Loraamadhin]], the least of their brethren as I then was. I write it in the year 4622, that the account of my adversaries may not stand alone.

The disputation was held in the hall of the congregation, with the leave of the lord's reeve, who sat at the door to keep the peace. For though the altars of the realm are those of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]], our lord suffers his subjects of other faiths to dispute among themselves, provided they pay their dues and keep his roads.

**The first question: whether the shape of time is to be taught as a thing certain.**

The Elder said: "It is the teaching of our fathers that all things turn on a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. You have said in the market that you do not know this. How can a brother not know what his fathers knew?"

I answered: "Lord Elder, I do not know it, and I say so again. Of the world's beginning and its end, what has the seeker seen? I have seen the wheel of a cart, and I have seen the road; which of them is the world, it is not given to me to say, nor, I think, to you. When the house is burning, the wise man does not dispute whether the fire began in the kitchen or the stable. He carries out the children."

One Elder called this the speech of an unbeliever. I said it was the speech of a man who would rather be silent than false.

**The second question: by what authority I teach.**

The Elder said: "Who has given you leave to teach what the Elders have not taught?"

I answered: "You have. For it is written in the Sayings, which you read to us every seventh day, that each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason. If this is true, then I have found what I have found by the light you told me was in me. If it is false, then strike it from the Sayings, and I will be silent. But you cannot teach me that the light is mine, and then forbid me to see by it."

**The third question: what is the good life.**

The Elder said: "The good life is justice in dealings and moderation in all things. The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite."

I answered: "But justice weighs, and compassion gives. Withdrawal from wealth is a cure for the rich man's soul; what is it to the poor man's belly? In [[pol:1405|Yidhe]] grain is cheap, less than a [[cur:778|zhiflun]] the measure, and still there are widows in Binge who do not eat. Is that a want of grain, or a want of mercy? I say the good life is compassion, and the release of others and of ourselves from suffering. In this I add to the Sayings nothing but the hands."

**The judgment.**

Then the Elders went apart, and when they returned the Elder said that I had set my own light above the light of the congregation, and that I must recant before the next seventh day or go out. I said I would go out, and asked only that they pray for me as I would pray for them. Some thirty of the brethren went out with me, with their households. Of that going out others have made the [[evt:35887|Tuthtu Schism]], as though we had broken something. We broke nothing. We carried out the children.

We are called the [[mov:135|Tuthtu]] by those who mock us, and we have taken the name, as a man takes up the stone that was thrown at him and builds with it. Our message, like our fathers', is for all peoples; we hold, as they hold, that there is one divine power. We differ only in what we will not pretend to know, and in what we will not neglect to do.

Thus far the record. Pray for the emperor, for the Elders of Binge, and for me.
