*[Obverse]*

To the queen, the lady of [[set:130|Fiwi]], wife of [[per:17553|Boziniliean]] king of [[pol:690|Yiotl]], my mother by marriage, say: thus says [[per:17847|Esiema]], son of the king of [[pol:751|Pibetie]], your son by marriage; and thus says [[per:17844|Nobeni]] your daughter, by my hand.

May [[dei:0|Etlie]] of the sky keep you. May [[dei:2|Muevase]] of the earth fill your storehouses. May [[dei:3|Nielei]] of the river carry this tablet to you swiftly, and carry your answer back more swiftly.

First hear my own words, and they are few, for the greater part of this tablet belongs to your daughter.

Your daughter has come safely into my father's house. On the road the river was high, but the boat did not overturn. On the night we came to the gate a star fell from the east to the west, and the priests of [[mov:0|Leim]] read it: a light leaving one house and entering another, not extinguished. So they said, and so I believe. On the wedding day the liver of the ram was whole and without spot. The bride-price of silver, in good [[cur:375|Liwi]] of full weight, was counted out before the elders of both houses, and no one disputed the scales. Thus was the [[evt:17929|Marriage of Nobeni and Esiema]] made, and the gods witnessed it.

I will not hide from you that it is a hard year in Pibetie. The [[evt:17856|Second Famni–Pibetie War]] has entered its sixth year. This summer the men of Famni beat my father's host in the field, and my father came home with his arm bound. Bread is dear everywhere; even in Fiwi, I hear, a measure of grain costs above four Liwi. But the walls of my father's city are whole, and your daughter sleeps behind them in safety. I have sworn it to [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of the spear, and I swear it to you.

*[Reverse]*

Now hear the words of your daughter. She spoke them and I wrote them, and I did not change one word, though some of them are about me.

> Mother, I am well. Do not weep at the window. The house is cold in the mornings, but they have given me a brazier and two women of my own, and one of them knows the songs of Fiwi.
>
> My husband is young, but he is not foolish. He does not drink in the evening. He reads the tablets himself, and does not need the scribe, which is why he is writing this and not the scribe. He laughed when I said this. Tell my father that he laughed.
>
> I have set up the image of [[dei:4|Lashlae]] of the moon by my bed, as you told me. Every full moon I pour milk for him. Every new moon I pour water for [[dei:1|Wewtsi]], that he will give me wisdom in a strange house.
>
> Tell my father that the people here remember the [[evt:17714|Treaty of Fiukh]], and how he held his city against the men of Famni before you bore me, at the [[evt:17704|Battle of Fiukh]]. They say his name in the market. They look at me and say: "That is the daughter of the man of Fiukh." It is good to be the daughter of that man.
>
> Mother, send me the blue cloth from the chest by the door, and the comb of bone with the bird on it. Send the recipe for the honey bread. Here they put salt in everything.
>
> I kiss your hands. I kiss your feet. I will come to Fiwi when the war is ended, or you will come to me.

These are your daughter's words. I add only this: her father's name is honoured in my father's house, and her mother's name will be honoured in mine.

*[Left edge]*

Written in Pibetie, in the 33rd year of Boziniliean, by the hand of Esiema.
