title: The Brother Sword: Bapavvukhen and the Twenty Years of Diebeguvebenga

The kingdom of [[pol:1326|Diebeguvebenga]] lasted twenty years, from AY 3320 to AY 3340. The record keeps thirty-three battles that it fought, and it won ten of them. Eight of those ten were won by one man, [[per:30360|Bapavvukhen]], whose name means *brother sword*. He commanded in fourteen battles in all, and no commander of any state in the battles of the 3300s led more. Yet in what survives of the writing of his own age I have not found his name once. The stones credit kings, the chronicle credits the gods, and the general is left to the battle tables.

The record knows little of the man. He was born in AY 3300, of the Jinru people, and died of natural causes in AY 3357, aged fifty-seven. It names no father, mother, wife or child. It says nothing of his face, his gods or what he did before 3327. What it keeps are fields, figures and dates, and they are enough to show a life.

## A kingdom born at war

He was twenty when the Lienonoshal Empire broke into six successor states in AY 3320. Diebeguvebenga, with its capital at [[set:62|Bimnge]], was one of them, and its state faith was the [[mov:2|Jinru]] pantheon. The empire had been at civil war from 3312 to 3317. A merchant's day-book kept at Diviminnge in 3313 (PAL·LE·01645) notes in its margin: "Caravans are held back on account of the Second Lienonoshal Civil War." Bapavvukhen was thirteen.

The new kingdom held 158 districts at its birth and 164 by 3326. In 3325 six wars came against it at once, and in 3326 Iethithi's [[evt:30678|16th Baan Trade War]] made seven. Diebeguvebenga was the defender in every one. King Bapuefnen was killed at Rurin in 3326. The general Erun lost all ten battles he commanded and was killed at Budh in 3328. In 3327 the throne passed to the king's daughter [[per:30351|Takhmagunath]], aged ten, with her mother Magunmakhsheth as regent. In the same year the record names Bapavvukhen a general of the kingdom. He was twenty-seven. It does not say who chose him, or why.

## The years of losing

His first battle went well. At the [[evt:30714|Battle of Nidhinge (AY 3327)]], [[per:30344|Maxi Adha]] of [[pol:1322|Mono]] attacked his 13,846 men with 169 and lost 37 to Bapavvukhen's six. At the [[evt:30726|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 3328)]] he led 20,637 men against [[per:30222|Dyeyshabyevnen]] of [[pol:1308|Birnathu]], who had 1,482. Bapavvukhen lost 4,228 men and Birnathu lost 371. That year a peace was sworn at Nuwkennga itself. Diebeguvebenga gave up 27 districts with the towns of Nunnga, Nuwkennga and Anavun, paid an indemnity and accepted a fifteen-year truce.

Nuen, historian of Diebeguvebenga, wrote the kingdom's account of the [[evt:30671|Birnathu–Diebeguvebenga War]] at Bimnge in 3340. It survives as a paper codex with several leaves missing (PAL·CH·05536). Of Nuwkennga he writes: "because the omens had been neglected, our army gave ground. We lost 1,898; the enemy, 801." The record more than doubles his count of the kingdom's losses and puts Birnathu's at less than half of his. The treaty becomes an act of grace: "at the urging of the priests, the queen accepted the pleas of Viernadhu". The queen was eleven. Of the lost land Nuen writes: "Nothing of worth was lost." He blames the omens, not the general, whom the surviving leaves never name. In a faith of duty and right sacrifice, neglected rites were the natural culprit; his other entries blame omens and a rising river alike.

In 3329 everything gave way. At the [[evt:30743|Battle of Viririnu (AY 3329)]], [[per:30398|Luenen]] of [[pol:1305|Kungaruishrimu]] beat his 19,164 men with 4,570. Diebeguvebenga lost 3,798 and Kungaruishrimu 1,143. King Kyethun's damaged rock relief at Viririnu (PAL·IN·02306) takes the victory for himself, as kings' stones do, and claims: "He killed 14,000 soldiers." That is nearly four times the record's figure. At the [[evt:30745|Battle of Munu (AY 3329)]], [[per:30385|Vaalax]] of Mono, twenty-two and his city's king from that year, came against 8,750 men with 147 and won. Mono lost 37 men, Diebeguvebenga 3,182. Nothing in the record explains these figures. Then at the [[evt:30746|Battle of Yirantudhu (AY 3329)]] the emperor [[per:30364|I'shil Ash I]] of [[pol:1177|Iethithi]] brought 70,758 men, more than five times Bapavvukhen's 13,335. Diebeguvebenga lost 3,687 and Iethithi 1,209. The bronze tablet of the peace, excavated at Yirantudhu only last year, in AY 4954, by Nambili (PAL·TR·01990), gives Iethithi the land of Baan and two towns, "12 districts in all", and lets Iethithi's merchants trade "free of all tolls".

In about two years those four defeats cost Bapavvukhen 14,895 men. The 164 districts of 3326 had fallen to 36. Of the five battles the record lists among the causes of the kingdom's fall, two, Munu and Yirantudhu, were his. But fairness needs the rest of the account. The treaties of Budh and Himi, which took 72 districts between them, followed battles he never fought, and Erun had lost ten of ten before him.

## Holding what was left

What he did next is why he deserves a life. From 3330 to 3337 he fought nine battles and won seven. At the [[evt:30786|Battle of Turamtu (AY 3330)]] he met [[per:30353|Tienumvun]] of [[pol:1257|Vieneynga]] with 7,981 men against 8,115. Vieneynga lost 3,499 and he lost 1,101, the heaviest loss he ever inflicted. Tienumvun is the nearest thing his life has to a mirror. He was Jinru too, born in 3299, and made general of Vieneynga in 3327, the same year as Bapavvukhen. Vieneynga was itself a Jinru kingdom, so the kingdom's longest war, and the one in which the *brother sword* fought most often, was fought against his own people. They met twice more. In 3331 Bapavvukhen lost at Turamtu, 5,297 men against 7,630, with 677 lost to Vieneynga's 555. At Diviminnge in 3333 he won with 8,480 men against 5,571, losing 153 to Vieneynga's 295. Tienumvun died in 3355, two years before him.

Vieneynga sent other men. At the [[evt:30813|Battle of Diviminnge (AY 3334)]] he met [[per:30460|Tafmanen]] with 7,243 men against 6,988 and cost Vieneynga 1,749 for 311 of his own. At the [[evt:30834|Battle of Diviminnge (AY 3336)]], [[per:30482|Ulupiemten]] brought 11,083 against his 7,782. He held, losing 575 to Vieneynga's 906. Against Mono he did not lose again: Maxi Adha failed at Nidhinge in 3330 and Diviminnge in 3331, and at the [[evt:30800|Battle of Munu (AY 3332)]] Bapavvukhen beat Vaalax, 5,284 men against 219. The [[evt:30675|Mono–Diebeguvebenga War]] was the only one of the kingdom's seven wars that it won. Yet the Treaty of Munu gave it nothing but a fifteen-year truce, and over the whole war Diebeguvebenga lost 6,419 men to Mono's 301.

The decade surveys show what this bought. By the end of the 3330s the kingdom had 37 districts, one more than ten years before. Its towns held 214,576 people against 135,874, and a treasury 9,456 in deficit stood 7,151 in credit. The army had shrunk from 24,440 to 17,079. His last recorded battle was a defeat. At the [[evt:30854|Battle of Turamtu (AY 3337)]] he had 6,913 men against Tafmanen's 6,367 and lost 1,923 to Vieneynga's 819. He was thirty-seven.

## After the battles

The next year the queen, now twenty-one, took the field herself. She won at Turamtu in 3338 and raised a rock relief (PAL·IN·02311): "She killed 5,800 soldiers." The record gives Vieneynga's loss as 1,033. In 3340 the kingdom collapsed. The Kingdom of Kunnga rose from it, with its capital at Nidhinge, the town Bapavvukhen had twice defended. When Kunnga mustered for the First Battle of Nidhinge in 3347, its clay tablet (PAL·MU·00675) listed 4,221 men from Diviminnge and Nidhinge under one Biruykun. Bapavvukhen, then forty-seven, does not appear. The record does not say whether he served Kunnga, or where he spent the seventeen years between the fall of his kingdom and his death. Takhmagunath outlived him by a year.

I am an archaeologist, so I end with the ground. In AY 4905 Nambili cut trenches along the old wall at Turamtu (PAL·EX·00007). He found burnt layers dated to AY 3148 and AY 3613, and nothing between them. At Diviminnge, dug in AY 4901 (PAL·EX·00024), the burnt layers jump from AY 2876 to AY 3414. Seven of the kingdom's battles were fought near Turamtu and five near Diviminnge, and neither town shows a burnt layer from his lifetime. The record does not let us call that his work alone. But it is the monument good generalship leaves: not a stele, but a gap in the ash.
