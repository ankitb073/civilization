title: Two Branches, Not Five: Rereading the Shengnan Cognate Table

Few tables in comparative philology are reprinted as often as the five-word set for the [[lan:4|Shengnan]] family. It is usually offered as an illustration; I think it is evidence. Read against the dated sound laws, it shows that the family divides into two primary branches, one headed by [[lan:10|Korin]] and one by [[lan:11|Tavil]], and that its two most striking resemblances are false friends.

## The evidence

| | water | king | god | mother | silver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proto-Shengnan | *kaŋŋuy | *luknā | *nidāka | *moḷ | *nibba |
| Korin | kanguy | lukhne | nithge | mul | nivbe |
| Tavil | kangoy | lukhna | omna | muel | unuübi |
| Bibbu | khanguy | luekhne | niedhga | muel | nieva |
| Nebir | khenguy | lukhne | nithoge | muil | nivve |
| Temu | kangoy | lokne | omne | muel | neba |

(Forms are cited in each language's conventional spelling; Korin th and Bibbu dh evidently write the same spirant.)

The record dates eight laws in the Korin line. Five precede Tavil's separation, which the documentary consensus places c. AY -2137, and are common Shengnan: loss of final *k and *m after a vowel (c. -2991), apocope of final *e after VC (c. -2956), raising of *aː to ɔ (c. -2594), palatalization of *k before front vowels (c. -2547) and intervocalic voicing of *p *t *ʈ *k (c. -2277). Three follow Korin's emergence c. AY -2050: spirantization of intervocalic *b *d *g (c. -2030), merger of o with u (c. -1758) and syncope of e and ɔ between consonants (c. -1738). Three of the common laws leave no trace here (no proto-form ends in *k, *m or *e, and none has *k before a front vowel); they rest on the wider lexicon, not on this table.

## The word for god

The decisive set is *nidāka. Raising gives *nidɔka and voicing *nidɔga, both shared inheritance. In the Korin line spirantization yields *niðɔɣa and syncope *niðɣa: hence Korin nithge and Bibbu niedhga, with nothing between spirant and velar. Bibbu shows the full Korin sequence and belongs to that branch. [[lan:15|Nebir]] nithoge does not. Its o is most naturally the raised *ā, preserved, and a language that kept that vowel cannot have passed through the syncope of c. AY -1738; its spirant must be a parallel development. Tavil omna and [[lan:17|Temu]] omne have replaced the word outright, an innovation the two share, and the same pair agree on kangoy against kanguy elsewhere. Since Nebir's history begins c. AY -1401 and Temu's c. AY -1145, the simplest account is that Tavil made both changes between those dates.

## Two false friends

The first is initial kh-. [[lan:14|Bibbu]] khanguy and Nebir khenguy invite a grouping, but the god set forbids it: Bibbu has the Korin syncope and Nebir has not. Aspiration of an initial stop is among the cheapest of changes, and it has happened twice.

The second is the agreement of three languages on muel for *moḷ. Bibbu regularly writes ue where Korin has u (except before y) and ie where Korin has i: luekhne beside lukhne, niedhga beside nithge, nieva beside nivbe. Bibbu muel is therefore Korin mul, itself the product of the o–u merger, with Bibbu's breaking laid on top. Tavil and Temu muel reach the same shape from *o by another road. Identical forms, different histories: this is why we count shared changes, not shared spellings.

Two residues remain. Korin final -e against Bibbu -a (nithge, niedhga; nivbe, nieva) is evidently a Korin change later than Bibbu's departure c. AY -953, but Tavil lukhna, alone with -a for *ā, I cannot explain from five words. Nor does any law I know derive Tavil unuübi from *nibba; I set it aside as reshaped or borrowed rather than force it.

## The tree

The table places Bibbu securely with Korin, and Nebir and Temu with Tavil. For the rest we depend on the record's dates. On that evidence the Korin branch also holds [[lan:20|Balman]] and its daughter [[lan:37|Veruldu]] (AY 499–3399). The Tavil branch holds [[lan:21|Meta]], [[lan:31|Radape]] and [[lan:33|Nunmula]], and through the last two [[lan:39|Pamudu]] and [[lan:40|Shatto]], both attested from AY 599. Temu has had no speakers since c. AY 4199, Balman none since c. AY 4799. That five words can carry this much is a credit to the regularity of sound change; that they were long read as a fan of five equal daughters is a warning against printing tables without their dates.
