To the great king who has given me bread, to my brother, say:

Thus says [[per:7031|Ish'o Lu]], king of [[pol:155|I'ith]], your brother. With you may it be well. With your house, with your sons, with your fields, with your herds may it be well. With me it is not well.

My brother, hear my words and do not turn your face from me.

The gods gave me the throne of I'ith. In my first year I sat on it. [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river gave water. [[dei:10|Masto]] of the sun gave light. My granaries were full. The [[cur:105|Ikhal]] came into my treasury. The cities of I'ith brought their gifts to [[set:152|Tantethi]], the great city, the city of my fathers.

A land withheld its tribute from me. I went out against it in my second year. I went before my army with the spear of [[dei:11|Nizhashta]] in my hand. I struck that land. I won. In my third year I went out against it again. Again I struck. Again I won. Ask anyone who was there: Ish'o Lu did not turn his back. My army was not broken. My spear was not broken.

But while I was in the field, my servants turned against me. The men I had made great in my cities ate my bread and plotted behind my back. They said to one another: "The king is far away. Let each of us take his own city." They shut their gates. They did not send me grain. They did not send me men. They put their hands on the throne and each made himself a king.

Hear their names, my brother, so that you do not trust them. In Tantethi, my own city, a throne was set up: that is [[pol:291|Tantethi]]. In another city a throne was set up, and it took the name of I'ith, as if it were I'ith: that is [[pol:292|I'ith]]. A throne was set up in [[pol:293|Wal'a]]. A throne was set up in [[pol:294|Wa'mi]]. A throne was set up in [[pol:295|Tueminna]]. A throne was set up in [[pol:296|I'emim]]. One kingdom has become six thrones, and none of them is mine.

Why have the gods done this? I have asked the priests of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]]. They read the liver. They read the oil on the water. They say: "The wheel turns. What ends will begin again." I say: why did it turn on me? Did I not give [[dei:7|Yuf]] of the fire her due? Did I not pour for [[dei:8|Nish'il]] of the sky? Did I not bring a bull to [[dei:9|Shiminto]] of the storm? I gave. Every god had its place and its due. Nothing that I owed was held back. The fault is not mine. The fault belongs to the oath-breakers. May Nizhashta break their spears. May Shiminto break their roofs. May Nili turn his water from their fields.

Now I have come to you with a few men. We have no grain. We have no silver. My household weeps. My servants sleep on the ground. My brother, you are a great king. Your fathers and my fathers sent gifts to each other. Give me a house. Give me bread for my men. Give me fodder for my donkeys. And if the gods turn the wheel again, and I sit again on the throne of I'ith, I will repay you. I will send you silver. I will send you oxen. I will send you fine cloth. I will not forget.

Do not let my messenger wait. Send him back to me quickly with your word. Your silence is heavy to me.

Written in the 4th year of Ish'o Lu, king of I'ith.
