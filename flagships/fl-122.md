title: Account of the Veiled Sun, by a Priest of Domamnol at Tallen

I, a priest of [[dei:16|Domamnol]] in the high house at [[set:0|Tallen]], servant of the sky, write this in the 34th year of [[per:17082|Ololomen]], king of kings in [[pol:664|Yeda]], so that the priests who come after me may know what the gods did, and what was done for the gods.

**The burning of the hill.** In the 18th year of Ololomen, in the month of the barley harvest, a sound came from the hills like the bellowing of a bull as large as the world. Three times it came. The doors of the temple shook in their sockets. Then the sky in the hill country grew black, and the blackness came toward Tallen as a flood comes down a valley. That was [[evt:17383|the burning of the Golden Hill]], which the hill-folk call Shenlamuokhner.

By evening ash fell on Tallen like grey snow. It lay on the roofs a finger deep. It lay on the barley. The oxen would not eat. The priests of Eva said: the fire of the earth has come up to meet the fire of the sky. We did not understand it.

**The veiling of the sun.** In the morning the sun rose red, like a coal taken from the hearth. At noon it was the colour of copper. For many days it was so. At night [[dei:19|Yenan]] rose the colour of blood, and the old women hid their faces. The stars were few.

That year the barley did not ripen. Frost came in the month when the figs should be gathered. In the next year it came again, and in the next. The farmers said: [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] has turned away; she does not hear us. They brought her the best of what little they had, and she did not hear.

**The cold years.** Hear what the priests remember of [[cli:104|the long winter]]. The rivers froze where they had never frozen. The vines died. A measure of barley was sold in the market for near two [[cur:364|Kerel]], and the poor sold their daughters for bread. The king opened the storehouses of the temple, as is right in hunger, and he gave bulls to Domamnol with his own hand at the new moon and the full moon, year upon year. Yet the sun stayed veiled.

The priests read the livers. The livers were pale. The priests read the birds. The birds flew low. We said: the gods are angry, for prosperity is the sign of their favour, and there is no prosperity. We said: let every man do his duty and make the right sacrifice, and the wheel will turn.

**The enemy in the cold.** In those years the enemy thought Yeda was weak with hunger. They came against us at Baaliurinu. But [[dei:14|Volme]] did not forget Yeda. At [[evt:17510|the first battle of Baaliurinu]] our men, hungry as they were, broke the enemy. In the next year they came again, and at [[evt:17519|the second battle]] they were broken again, and went home with their shields on their backs. Then the king made [[evt:17520|the peace at Baaliurinu]], and they swore not to come again for ten years. The priests said: see, Volme fights for Yeda even when Nedhekhte is silent.

**The turning of the wheel.** In the last year the king sent the army out against Linnada, but the gods did not give it a victory; [[evt:17575|that war]] came to nothing, and the army came home. The priests said: it is not the time for spears; it is the time for barley.

And so it was. This year, the 34th year of Ololomen, the sun has risen gold. The sky is clean as washed linen. The barley stands high in the fields below Tallen, and the frost has kept away. Grain is still dear, but it is less dear than it was. The figs are ripe.

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The cold has ended. Let the warm years begin. [[dei:15|Venba]], take back the ash into your body. Domamnol, keep your face unveiled. Let the king Ololomen see many harvests.

Here ends the account of the veiled sun, written by a priest of [[mov:2|Jenro]] at Tallen.
