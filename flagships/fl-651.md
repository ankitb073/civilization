title: The Stones Call Him King: Najiègimbu of Dunjènile

Among the ninety-two inscribed finds from Nanjil's excavation at Kavu in AY 4920 is a limestone stele, weathered but legible, which Nabuli translated:

> Najiègimbu, king of Dunjènile, defeated Gane in Kavu. He killed 500 soldiers. He erected this stele for Khuthaze, his lord. May Khuthaze destroy the name of the enemy.

Nearly every clause needs a gloss. [[per:16801|Najiègimbu]] (AY 2047–2118) was not a king. He was the elected head of the Merchant Republic of [[pol:720|Dunjènile]], chosen in 2103 for a term of twelve years and replaced when it ran out. The record counts 233 dead of Gane at Kavu, not 500. And the name his god was asked to destroy outlived the prayer.

Of the man himself the record keeps little. He was a Khufiènji, born in 2047. It names no parent, wife or child, and no office before his election at fifty-six. A merchant republic invites us to make him a merchant, but the record lists no merchant house at Dunjinil in his lifetime and says nothing of his trade. What remains are two inscriptions, two battles, a treaty, a coin and the surveys of his state.

## A kingdom in two pieces

In 2085, when he was thirty-eight, the old Kingdom of Dunjènile collapsed. Its last king, Najige the Last, had come to the throne at seven; he was ten when it fell, and lived on without office until 2120. The port of [[set:109|Dunjinil]] kept sixteen districts. The City-State of [[pol:721|Gane]], ruled from Gyi, took ten. Dunjinil was governed first by [[per:16666|Nakhiènde]], until in 2091 [[per:16712|Nandune]] deposed him and made the city a merchant republic.

The republic kept the kingdom's faith and its money. [[mov:24|Uthungala]] taught one divine power, with the old gods as its servants or shadows, and held prosperity to be a sign of divine favour; merchants could live with that. For twenty-seven years the republic went on reckoning in the [[cur:361|Ngathe]], the kingdom's standard of 10.7 grams of silver, set in 1958.

## The war he inherited

From 2093 the Kingdom of Nilu made war on Gane and won every battle the record lists. In 2100 the Treaty of Gyi cost Gane four districts, a town and an indemnity, and made it Nilu's vassal. Its ruler, [[per:16711|Nakathambe]], had inherited at sixteen. Gane's palace scribe later blamed storms, neglected omens and absent allies, and halved his own side's dead: for the battle at Gyi in 2100 he wrote "It cost us 59 men, and them 288", where the record has 118 of Gane's and 114 of Nilu's.

The republic struck at this beaten neighbour. The record traces the [[evt:16817|Dunjènile–Gane War]] (2099–2104) to Nilu's victory at Gyi in 2098. In 2101, near Kavu, Nandune beat Nakathambe, losing 31 men to Gane's 208. In 2102 he marched on Gyi with 1,735 men against 550 and was routed, losing 539, nearly a third of his army, to Gane's 103. That year Gane issued a silver standard of its own, the Githe, at 8.3 grams.

Nandune's term ended the next year, and the republic chose Najiègimbu.

## Two battles and a stone

He took the field himself. In the [[evt:16860|Battle of Kavu (AY 2103)]], near a town the republic had held since 2085, he led 3,218 men against Nakathambe's 654, nearly five to one. He lost 36; Gane lost 233. In 2104 he went to Gyi with 3,342 men, almost twice the force Nandune had taken there, against 818. At the [[evt:16868|Battle of Gyi (AY 2104)]] he lost 76 and Gane 200. The city was besieged, and the [[evt:16870|Conquest of Gane]] was signed at [[set:144|Gyi]] by Najiègimbu and Nakathambe that year. The republic grew from sixteen districts to twenty-two, and by the end of the 2100s counted 733,280 people, against 600,148 ten years before.

The sums are kind to him. His two battles cost the republic 112 men; Nandune's had cost 570. Over the whole war the dead were nearly even, 682 for the republic and 744 for Gane, and that is Nandune's day at Gyi. Nothing in the record makes Najiègimbu brilliant. He was careful, and came with enough men.

His stele is careful too. It doubles Gane's dead, but of the more than 2,700 victory steles in the archive fewer than one in thirty exaggerates so little. Its neighbour at Kavu, set up by Nakükh of Luthe in 2053, claims 4,800 dead of Dunjènile where the record has 713.

Then there is the word king. The record does not say whether he chose it or his stone-cutters did. The same formula stands on steles of kings of Luthe and Nilu, and the epitaph of Navavun, who held the same elected office from 2120 to 2130, also calls him "king of Dunjènile". But in 2104 the old kingdom's last king was alive, aged twenty-nine, and for the first time since 2085 one government ruled at both Dunjinil and Gyi. Najiègimbu had put twenty-two of the old kingdom's twenty-six districts back together.

The most recent destruction layer Nanjil found at Gyi dates from 1864; the city was besieged in 2104 but not burnt. There he found a clay copy of a chronicle by Nambugthu, who signs himself "scribe of the palace of Gane" and dates it "in the 33rd year of Gane", that is, 2117. Thirteen years after the conquest, in a city the republic ruled, a scribe still counted Gane's years. His account is of Nilu's war and stops in 2100; several leaves are missing, and if he wrote of Najiègimbu, nothing of it survives. The archive holds no chronicle of this war from either side.

## Palace and coin

In 2112, the tenth year of his term, Najiègimbu built a palace at Dunjinil. Its foundation tablet, excavated by Nanjil in 4915, reads: "Najiègimbu, king of Dunjènile, built this palace in Dunjènile. May Nijinande bless the city." Nijinande is a goddess of war; the war had been over for eight years. That year the republic replaced the Ngathe with the [[cur:378|Shigin]], at 8.1 grams of silver, about a quarter less, and close to conquered Gane's Githe. The record does not say why, or who gained by it.

The surveys show a richer state, not a steadier one. The treasury held 25,587 kilograms of silver at the end of the 2100s and 42,639 at the end of the 2110s, a decade whose first five years were his. The army grew from 5,943 men to 7,301. But the towns shrank from 77,519 people to 72,955, and the record's measure of stability fell below its level at the end of the 2090s.

For ordinary people these were years of dear bread. At Dunjinil a measure of grain cost about 20 grams of silver in the 2080s, 39 in the 2090s and 42 in the 2100s, more than twice the average of the world's markets, before easing to 31 in the 2110s and 21 in the 2120s. War on the border explains some of it; the record also logs drought in four of the republic's districts from the 2090s.

## After the term

His term ended in 2115, when he was sixty-eight. The republic elected [[per:16910|Navande]], who died within the year. Najiègimbu died in 2118, aged seventy-one, of natural causes. No epitaph of his is known. Nakathambe, the young man he had beaten twice, lived until 2139.

Thirteen years after his death the republic was gone. The record traces Nilu's war of 2129 to the usurpation of Nadazum in Nilu in 2116, not to anything Najiègimbu did. In 2130 Nilu's 17,339 men crushed the republic's 4,869 at Kavu, and Dunjinil was sacked; Nanjil recorded that sack as "a thick layer of ash over collapsed walls". In 2131 all twenty-two districts passed to Nilu. Nadazum's stele for that year's battle at Dunjinil claims 3,200 dead, five times the record's 641.

I was born three years after my own country took most of these districts from the Kingdom of Luthe, by a treaty signed at Kavu in 4926. Our chronicle of that war, printed in 4927, says: "Lüth is no more, and its people serve Khishkhih." Najiègimbu's stele asked for the same thing more briefly. Of the victory steles raised for battles at Kavu, his boasts least; beside his own republic, the word king is the falsest thing on it. The surer witness to his years is a scribe at Gyi, still counting the years of a state that no longer existed.
