To the elders of [[set:0|Tallen]], to the priests of the house of [[dei:14|Wolme]], and to the scribes who keep the tablets of the kingdom: thus says [[per:15318|Ballokhnon]], king of [[pol:664|Yeda]], beloved of Wolme and of [[dei:15|Wenba]].

Now I set down the words that passed between my envoys and the envoys of [[pol:666|Navnga]], and the words that passed between me and their king, so that what was sworn is not forgotten and no one in later days may say: "The king of Yeda took more than was given to him."

Hear how it began. Navnga had withheld its gifts for many years. I sent to [[per:15260|Nathuanaven]], king of Navnga, and said: "Send the gifts your fathers sent to my fathers." He did not send them. So began the [[evt:15633|Fourth Yeda–Navnga War]]. The gods judged between us. At the [[evt:15680|Battle of Bakhriytil]] my captain broke their army. At the [[evt:15689|Battle of Himi]] I went out myself, and Wolme went before me. Their general fell there, and four thousand of his men fell with him. Then I came to the walls of [[set:10|Navnga]] and sat down before them. That was the [[evt:15690|Siege of Navnge]].

On the third day of the siege the priests read a ram for me, and the liver was whole and bright. On the seventh day a hawk came down on the city wall and took a dove. I said: "The gods have spoken."

On the ninth day the first envoys came out of the gate. They brought small gifts: cloth, oil, two horses. They said: "Our king asks the king of Yeda to go home, and he will send gifts as before." I said: "Before, he sent nothing. Go back and say to your king: the gifts of before are not enough." I sent them back with their horses.

On the twelfth day the second envoys came out. They were older men, with dust on their heads. They said: "Our king will send silver every year, but let the king of Yeda leave the land of our fathers to us." My envoy answered them: "The land your fathers held on the river was taken from the fathers of Yeda. Is a thief's son not a thief? The king of Yeda will have the silver and the land."

For three days they went in and out. They weighed their words like merchants weighing grain. Grain is dear, and at Tallen a measure costs more than two [[cur:241|Noype]] and a half, so I did not let them waste my time.

On the sixteenth day Nathuanaven sent to say: "Let the king of Yeda come inside the walls, and we will swear in the house of the gods." I went in with my guard. He met me at the gate. He did not bow to the ground, but he bowed. I did not mock him. A king who has lost a war should still be spoken to as a king.

These are the words we set on the tablet of the [[evt:15691|Treaty of Navnge]], and both of us set our seals on it:

> The merchants of Yeda shall come and go in Navnga, and no one shall shut the road before them.  
> For ten years no spear shall be lifted between Yeda and Navnga.

We swore by Wolme, and by Wenba, and by [[dei:16|Domamnol]] who sees from the sky, and by [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] who fills the granary, and by [[dei:18|Ewa]] of the hearth, and by [[dei:19|Yenan]] who counts the months. Whoever breaks the oath, may the gods of both lands strike him.

Elders of Tallen, I have done what the gods of [[mov:2|Jenro]] require: right sacrifice, right order, and the due of Yeda restored. Keep this tablet with the tablet of the treaty. Read both each year, so that the people know what was sworn.

Written in the 28th year of Ballokhnon.
