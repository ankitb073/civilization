In AY 3836 a man of Ane named Bozinshima came before a royal judge and asked for his father's fields back: 185 measures of sown land, taken when the district passed to Ölie by treaty. The steward of the royal lands replied that the fields "were found empty and were given to settlers". The judgment, preserved on parchment with its three witnesses, is one line long: "The claim is dismissed. Let him seek his fields in the land of his former lord." The king in whose thirty-second year that sentence was written was [[per:35110|Isyi Toe]] of [[pol:1497|Ölie]]. This chapter is about him, but I begin with Bozinshima. More men like him felt the reign than ever saw the king.

## The son of the Victorious

He was born in 3781, a Lizo; his name means "One Rain". His father, [[per:35062|Fyiyan II]], called the Victorious, had been king since the age of two and spent his son's youth at war, conquering one neighbour in the 3780s and humbling another in the 3790s. In 3799 the prince's sister, then fourteen, was married into the royal house of [[pol:1499|Yiotl]]. Seven years later her brother would be at war with that house. A much younger sister died at thirteen.

He married a woman five years his junior. Their first son was born and died in 3805, the year of the [[evt:36057|Accession of Isyi Toe]]. The heir, [[per:35675|Oi Toe]], followed in 3806, and three more children lived to adulthood. He came to the throne at twenty-four, inheriting an empire of about sixteen million people whose internal order was as firm as it had been in living memory. It is worth keeping that starting point in mind.

## Nine years at Toiyoe

The [[evt:36068|First Ölie–Yiotl War]] (3806–3815) began from a battle between Yiotl and Tlue two years earlier, and almost all of it was fought near one town, [[set:111|Toiyoe]]. The first battle, in 3807, was lost by a general: Ölie lost 37,477 men, Yiotl 1,758. The king then took command himself. At the [[evt:36084|Battle of Toiyoe (AY 3808)]] he led 74,651 men against 33,116 under Yiotl's queen, [[per:34898|Isyi Yuive II]]. He held the field, but each side lost about eleven thousand men. In 3809 the same general who had lost in 3807 lost nearly 34,000.

Then he found his footing. At the [[evt:36115|Second Battle of Toiyoe (AY 3810)]] he beat [[per:35619|Nyina]], losing 3,463 men to Yiotl's 8,194. It was the best day of his career. At the [[evt:36125|Second Battle of Toiyoe (AY 3811)]], against [[per:35587|Yo Foyatl]], the exchange was nearly even again. A translator must note that the later compilers who named these battles called two of them "the second", a sign of how the fighting around one town blurred together in memory. The queen died in 3812, and her daughter, [[per:35635|Isyi Yuive III]], succeeded at twenty-one. At the [[evt:36157|Battle of Toiyoe (AY 3815)]] the king brought 141,149 men against 11,327 under [[per:35672|Wimayuive]], lost 15,388 and killed 4,971. His diorite stele by the town claims "36,900 soldiers", seven times the truth.

The [[evt:36158|Treaty of Toiyoe (AY 3815)]] survives complete on bronze. Yiotl ceded eleven districts with Toiyoe and a second town, acknowledged Ölie's overlordship, and promised to "send its levies when summoned". A boundary stone set up in the new lands asks the god to "curse the enemy". The war had cost Ölie 114,521 men and Yiotl 42,443.

## Plague, and the field at Fyinan

In 3821 plague came. Over seven years it killed about one person in six across a wide region, Ölie among them. The record ties the next war to the empire's own growth, not to the sickness, and in 3822 the king began the [[evt:36226|Ölie–Ane War]] against [[pol:1498|Ane]], the kingdom that held the ancient city of [[set:7|Ane]]. At the [[evt:36236|Battle of Fyinan (AY 3824)]] he led 170,869 men against 43,832 under [[per:35771|Isyi Ivi]] and was destroyed. Ölie lost 70,446 men, four in ten of the army, against 4,408 of the enemy. The record does not say how. An army raised in plague years may have been half-beaten before it marched, but that is my inference, not the record's. No stele marks the field. His mother died the same year.

After Fyinan he never commanded again. Every later battle of the war was fought by his generals. They won in 3825, lost once more in 3827 and then won steadily, and twice, in 3831 and 3832, they laid siege to Ane itself. The lost Ölie chronicle of the war, known from one quoted line, gives the credit where the record does: to the general who "drew them into the hills". The king's stelae tell another story. One claims "47,100 soldiers" for a victory at Tlomesh in 3830, where the record counts 12,431 enemy dead and names a different commander. His wife died in 3831.

The [[evt:36313|Treaty of Ane (AY 3832)]] was sworn at Ane with its king, [[per:35536|Yi Foyatl]], and its bronze is water-stained. Ane ceded thirteen districts, paid an indemnity and became Ölie's vassal. Two years later Ölie annexed it outright, and Ane, a city founded in AY −298, more than four thousand years before, passed into the empire. The war had cost Ölie 155,563 men. Bozinshima's fields lay in the ceded land.

## A quiet end

Isyi Toe died in 3843, aged sixty-two, of natural causes. His epitaph in the royal tombs calls him only king of Ölie and "son of Fyiyan". The [[evt:36437|Accession of Oi Toe]] passed the crown to a son of thirty-seven, who died after four years; the throne then passed to a boy of fourteen under a regent. Ölie itself endured until 4114. Yiotl, bled at Toiyoe, collapsed in 3861, and the record names his victories there among the causes.

On paper the reign was a success. The empire grew from about 314 districts to 370, and from sixteen million people to nearly twenty-three million, most of them new subjects. Weighed differently, it was the reign of a man who commanded five battles, won four, and lost the fifth so badly that he never led an army again. That he stopped may be the most sensible decision the record shows him making. Two wars cost his people some 270,000 soldiers. They also cost men like Bozinshima the land of their fathers, under a judgment that told them to look for it somewhere else.
