To [[per:15505|Tathlemen]], king of [[pol:660|Nedhenga]], my son, say: thus says [[per:15062|Shethvenenath]], the great queen, queen of [[pol:580|Yennga]], whom [[dei:14|Volme]] of the battle goes before.

With me all is well. With my house, my horses, my granaries and my city of [[set:155|Yennga]] all is well. May it be well with you also, now that you have learned to listen.

Hear concerning the youth of your house, whom you gave into my hand at the gate of [[set:237|Nedhenga]] on the day the oaths were sworn. He is well. He eats at my table, at the lower end, as is fitting. He has a bed of wool and a servant to wash his feet. He has learned to pour to [[dei:16|Domamnol]] of the sky as the priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] pour, and my priests say his hand does not shake. No man in Yennga has struck him. So it will be for as long as you keep the oaths. You know what it will be if you do not.

Remember how this came to pass, for the young forget quickly. Your house withheld the gifts that were owed to mine, and your counsellors laughed at my messengers. Therefore I went out. Four summers I went out against you in the [[evt:15579|Yennga–Nidinga War]], and four times the gods gave me the field. Not once did your people stand where I had come.

Before the battle at [[evt:15610|Yirantudhu]] the priests opened the sheep, and the liver was whole and bright; and in the night [[dei:19|Yenan]] the moon came up red over your camp. I said: "The god has shown me their blood." In the morning I went down myself in my chariot, and your men fell before me like barley before the sickle, by the hundred, by the hundred. Their shields lay in the furrows; the farmers of that country will be ploughing them up for a generation.

In this year I came to Nidhinge, to the very gate of your city, and there [[evt:15619|at Nidhinge]] your last host was broken. My soldiers went in over the wall; the [[evt:15620|sack of Nidhinge]] filled my carts with your silver, your bronze and your cloth. I did not burn the house of your gods. I am a queen who keeps the gods' due, even the gods of others.

Then your elders came out with dust on their heads, and you with them, a boy of seventeen summers, and we made the [[evt:15621|Treaty of Nidhinge]]. Hear its words again, so that you do not say afterwards that you did not hear them. The fields by Yirantudhu, and the town beside them, are mine. For fourteen years you will send me tribute of silver, weighed in [[cur:303|dothtol]] by my scribes and not by yours. You are my son and my servant; your enemies are my enemies. Your merchants will open their roads to mine. For ten years no spear will be lifted between Yennga and Nedhenga. All this you swore before the gods, and the youth of your house is the seal upon it.

I send you with this tablet a measure of grain from my own fields, blessed by [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]], so that you may know the queen of Yennga is generous to those who obey.

Below, my scribe has written the words of the youth, as he spoke them, and nothing added.

> To the king my lord, thus says your servant: I am well. I eat bread. The great queen is just. Keep the oaths, my lord, and send the silver when it is due. May the gods keep you. Remember me.

Written in the 17th year of Shethvenenath, at Yennga.
