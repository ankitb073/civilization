[[per:36818|Yai Nyime]], whose name means "Four Flower", ruled [[pol:1497|Ölie]] for thirty-seven years, from AY 3918 to 3955, and is remembered as "the Conqueror". She earned the title in the plainest sense. Her reign saw three states annexed, and she led her armies in person in three of its battles. But the record also lets us do a sum that her court never did. Her three conquests added fifteen districts to an empire that already had more than four hundred. They cost Ölie about 226,000 soldiers dead, against a little over two thousand of the enemy. This chapter tries to understand a ruler, and an age, for whom that sum was acceptable.

## A daughter under a law of sons

She was born in AY 3886, a Lizo of the house founded by Fyiyan I "the Silent", which had held Ölie for a century and a half. Her grandfather was remembered as Sitlyitlathe "the Great". Her father, [[per:36355|Yithifoyatl]], took the throne in 3895 and spent much of his reign at war. His armies won him lands from three neighbours, and he set up a victory stele after the last of those wars in 3917, the year before he died.

Ölie's law of succession favoured the eldest son. The record does not tell us why a daughter inherited, and we should not invent a reason. It tells us only that at the [[evt:37350|Accession of Yai Nyime]] in 3918 she was thirty-two, married, and the mother of a son of twelve and a daughter of seven. The empire she received had some 446 districts and thirty-two and a half million people. Its army was more than four hundred and sixty thousand strong.

## Onitie: the city of two years

Her first conquest was of a state barely older than the war itself. The city-state of [[pol:1589|Onitie]] was founded in 3930 around the old town of [[set:110|Onithie]]. Ölie attacked it in the same year, and the [[evt:37534|Ölie–Onitie War]] lasted until 3932. In 3931 one of her generals defeated a defending force of 709 men with an army of 192,593 and lost 25,978 of them. The next year the queen took command herself. At the [[evt:37562|Battle of Onithie (AY 3932)]] she led 210,992 soldiers against 581 under the city's ruler, [[per:36926|Oi Ivi]]. She won, and the record gives her losses as 33,094. The defenders lost 105.

The [[evt:37564|Conquest of Onitie]] annexed two districts. Oi Ivi signed it, and the record states that he was executed in the same year. It does not say by whose order, and the court sources are silent. The Onitie account of the war (PAL·CH·06682), written by a palace scribe four years later, begins: "Yai Nyime of Ölie broke the peace and sent raiders across the border of Onitie." It claims that Ölie lost 97,677 men in the first battle and 124,433 in the second. Its narrative ends: "The royal city was taken. The gods had turned their faces from us." The scribe nearly quadrupled the enemy's dead, as defeated chroniclers do. The true figure for the war, 59,072, needed no inflating.

## Kivona and Ishuiithie

The [[evt:37589|Ölie–Kivona War]] (3938–3943) was larger and slower. [[pol:1586|Kivona]] was a city-state of nearly twenty years' standing, a fragment of an older kingdom that had adopted a foreign faith. The queen, now in her fifties, commanded at the [[evt:37644|Battle of Kivunge (AY 3941)]] with 378,922 men against 1,887 under [[per:37084|Mihihilianuo]]. She lost 42,050 and the enemy 277. At the [[evt:37653|Battle of Kivunge (AY 3942)]] she beat the same general again, more cheaply. He died in a third battle the following year, and after a siege of [[set:226|Kivunge]] the city's ruler, [[per:36871|I'ath]] "the Convert", sealed the [[evt:37667|Conquest of Kivona]]. It added twelve districts.

Only one line of the Kivona account survives, quoted by a much later writer (PAL·CH·06692). It is a formula: "Yai Nyime of Ili'a, forgetting the oaths her fathers swore, set her hand against Kivona." The scribe changed only the pronouns. The enemy's language had no other form of words for a conquering queen than the one it used for kings.

The last war of the reign was the most disproportionate. [[pol:1590|Ishuiithie]] was founded in 3940 in the ancient city of [[set:3|Ishwyithie]]. It struck its own silver coin in 3948, and in 3950 a young ruler, [[per:37190|Maniza II]], inherited it. The [[evt:37717|Ölie–Ishuiithie War]] (3949–3952) was fought by the queen's general. In 3950 he brought 354,683 men against a garrison of 310 and lost 61,930 of them. The city was sacked twice. A soldier of Ölie scratched a line on a gate column there in 3952 (PAL·GR·00213): "Shiminfoyatl took the wine of this house. It was good." The [[evt:37790|Conquest of Ishuiithie]] added one district. For it Ölie paid 82,313 dead, and Ishuiithie 161.

## How such numbers happen

Why should armies of hundreds of thousands lose tens of thousands of men to garrisons of a few hundred? The record gives the numbers, not the reasons. Old walled cities, siege camps in summer, the fevers that travel with great hosts and the hunger of long supply lines would all play a part. The figures suggest that the enemy's weapons killed few of these men. Disease, exhaustion and the walls killed most of them. A conqueror who brings the whole weight of the empire against a city of a few hundred defenders still pays heavily, and most of the price is paid by her own army.

The empire's own measures show the strain. Its population grew by about a million and a half between 3920 and 3950. The treasury fell by a tenth between 3930 and 3950, and the record's measure of order in the realm slipped below where it had stood at her accession and stayed there, low and unimproved.

## The queen's end

Her son died in 3951, aged forty-five, four years before his mother. Yai Nyime died of natural causes in AY 3955, at sixty-nine. The [[evt:37807|Accession of Lyimashme]] passed the throne to her daughter [[per:37263|Lyimashme]], who was forty-four. A second woman thus succeeded under a law of sons, which suggests the queen had made her daughter's position secure. That may have been her most lasting work. Lyimashme reigned fourteen years. The house of Fyiyan I held the throne until a usurper seized it in 3993, and the empire of [[pol:1497|Ölie]] itself survived until 4114.

"The Conqueror" is a true title. [[per:36818|Yai Nyime]] took three states, all of them younger than her own reign. She commanded in person, and she never lost. The modern reader may still think the more remarkable facts of her life are the ones the title leaves out. She held a throne her law had not meant for her and handed it on to a daughter. And she accepted, over three wars, a price of a hundred of her own soldiers for every enemy dead.
