title: Within Living Memory: The Historical Ihishe and the Rulers of Tivintukhe
---
Of all the founders of his age, [[per:17440|Ihishe]] is the best served by his sources, and his case shows how little even good sources can do. The Sayings of Ihishe were set down within living memory, by a man who could have heard him. Yet they give us the formulae of a tradition, not the voice of a teacher. The faith he founded, the [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], which now counts some 110 million adherents, owes its shape less to him than to the rulers who later adopted it in his city.

## The man

The record is brief. Ihishe was born in AY 2125, a man of the Unsatlmum people and a subject of the Kingdom of Mankhe, whose capital was at Yuftuy and whose state cult was then the Retti religion. The [[evt:17491|Founding of the I'ishiu Faith]] took place at Tivintukhe, then a provincial town, in 2166, when he was about forty-one. He died of natural causes in 2187, at sixty-two, after some twenty-one years of teaching. No office, family or quarrel with authority is recorded. The silence tells us that the court did not think him worth noticing.

## The tablet

The Sayings of Ihishe (PAL·SC·00029) is a clay tablet dated AY 2197, ten years after his death. Its compiler, the priest Kidilath, was born in 2167, the year after the founding, and was twenty when Ihishe died. Its heading claims no more than that the words were "set down after his death", a modesty that is itself a mark of good faith. Few founders' collections have a stronger claim to rest on a living witness.

And yet every one of the six lines on the tablet (PAL·SC·00029) is older than Ihishe. The turning wheel, the one power "above all the gods", the command to "obey those who hold the teaching", the charity to the hungry stranger, the message "for every nation" and the warning that "anger is a fire that burns the one who carries it" all occur in earlier sayings collections. The oldest of them, the Sayings of Nakavzi (PAL·SC·00001), goes back to AY 106. Even a compiler who had heard the teacher wrote in the inherited language of his genre. Nearness to the source does not guarantee originality. What it preserves reliably is the shape of the community's teaching: the wheel, a priesthood in ordered succession, compassion, a message for all peoples. That shape matches the faith's later doctrine exactly. It does not reach behind it to the man.

## The rulers of Tivintukhe

The archaeological record of Tivintukhe tells a clearer story than the tablet. By 2370 the town had become the capital of the Kingdom of Mane. In AY 2399 Queen Shail the Pious set up the Palace inscription of Shail at Tivintukhe (PAL·IN·04979). In the same year she issued the Edict establishing the I'ishiu (PAL·DE·00183): "Its priests shall be fed from the royal granaries", while the older rites "shall no longer be paid for by the treasury". The founder had been dead for two hundred and twelve years.

The effect of that patronage can be measured. At the founding the faith held about four districts and two centuries later about eight. In the century after Shail's edict it more than doubled, and it kept growing. A later Kingdom of Mane adopted it afresh in 2563, and the gate, granary and temple inscriptions set up at Tivintukhe over the next two centuries record a royal city. The legend of a founder whose preaching carried his faith across the world has the chronology backwards. The preaching produced a local community; the crown produced a religion.

The later history confirms how far the faith travelled from its founder's people. Both of its offshoots, the [[mov:144|Nyinsiwiwtsi]] of AY 4198 and the reforming [[mov:161|Ishyidig]] of 4648, arose among the La'im, not among the Unsatlmum. Each, presumably, appealed to the Sayings. Neither could have found in them anything that was certainly Ihishe's.

The historical [[per:17440|Ihishe]] is therefore an Unsatlmum teacher of Tivintukhe who taught for twenty-one years and was remembered, within a decade of his death, in words his tradition already owned. The [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] as a religion of nations begins not with him but with Shail's edict.
