*Register of those laid in the western burying-ground of [[set:243|Daadigi]], outside the Potters' Gate, in the year 3121 from the founding of [[set:84|Kaazhe]], in the reign of [[per:28221|Nadaazum]]. Kept by the digger of the ground, as the law of the city requires, so that no citizen goes into the earth uncounted.*

**In the month of the threshing.**

Laid: an old porter of the grain-wharf, by age.  
Laid: an infant of the tanners' street, by a flux.  
Laid: a woman of the upper town, by childbed; her child with her.  

**In the month of the first rains.**

Laid: a weaver, aged about thirty, by a cough with a red sputum. The physician of the quarter said it was a winter cough come early.  
Laid: the weaver's mother, by the same.  
Laid: two children of the weaver's neighbour, by the same.  
Laid: a fuller; a fuller's apprentice; a boy who carried water to the fullers; by the same.  

*Remark.* The physicians have now given the thing a name, and call it the [[evt:29148|Scarlet Cough]], for the colour of what the sick bring up. A name is a small comfort, but it is a comfort; a man would rather be killed by something that has a name than by nothing at all.

**In the month of the cold rains.**

Laid in the long trench by the wall, by the Scarlet Cough: fourteen of the tanners' street; nine of the potters' street; six of the street of the ropewalk; three whose houses no one could tell me. The names are in the second leaf.

*Remark.* I have dug this ground twenty years. In a common year it takes the old at the turn of winter and the infants at the turn of summer, and it is moderate in its appetite, as the priests of [[mov:38|Zhibinjizu]] bid all things be. This year it has no moderation. It takes the young and strong first, the men who stand at the vats and wharves and breathe one another's breath.

*Remark.* The magistrates have closed the wine-shops, which is wise, and the baths, which the people hate. Grain stands at two [[cur:641|Mbigin]] and a little more the measure, which is no great sum; but a man who cannot rise from his bed cannot carry two Mbigin to the market, and his children go without. I have seen the neighbours of the tanners' street leave bread on the sills of the sick, and knock, and go away quickly. That too is a kind of civic virtue, though the orators do not speak of it.

*Remark.* It is said in the market that the cough has gone along the road to [[set:231|Laave]] and the other towns, and that the people there say it came out of Daadigi. They are right. I do not know whose fault it is, and the physicians do not know, and I notice that the physicians die of it as well as the porters.

**In the month of the cold rains, later.**

Laid: the physician of the quarter, by the Scarlet Cough.  
Laid: my second assistant, a freedman, who served under the colours at the [[evt:29064|Conquest of Banditadu]] and told me often that no war had frightened him. By the same.  

*Remark.* The orators in the great square say that a city is measured by how it treats its dead. If that is so, let them come and measure us: I have no lime, I have no wood for coffins, and my first assistant has run away to his mother's farm. I do not blame him. But I remain, for someone must write the names, and if no one writes them it will be as if these people had never been citizens at all.

I set down this leaf by lamplight. The trench is open for tomorrow. I do not know how long it must be.
