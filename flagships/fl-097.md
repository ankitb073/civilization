title: The Confession of I'a Lo

I, [[per:19924|I'a Lo]], a man of [[pol:740|Pivish]], who was rich and am poor, who was blind and now see a little, set down these words in the 23rd year of Ba'aliof the king, that those who come after me may know how the light came to me, and may not think that I was born with it.

I confess: I was a servant of the many gods. I fed them all. I fed [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river at the flood, I fed [[dei:7|Yof]] of the fire at the turning of the year, I fed [[dei:10|Masto]] of the sun, I fed [[dei:11|Nizhashta]] of war before every battle. I gave as the priests of [[mov:1|Ya'kam]] told me, and when I gave, I counted what I gave, and when the gods gave back I counted that also. I believed that my storehouse was the measure of my goodness. When my grain was high, I said: the gods love me. When my neighbour's roof fell in, I said: the gods have judged him. This I confess.

I confess: I went out in the [[evt:19721|war against Iti]] with a full purse and a new spear. At [[evt:19740|Tanshedi]] I saw the gods fail. We were few and they were many, as the stars are many, and Nizhashta whom I had fed did not come. I lay in the reeds among the dead until night. A man of my own street lay beside me, and he had fed the gods more than I. He did not get up.

I confess: I did not learn. I went home and fed the gods more. When [[per:19558|Ba'aliof]] our king went down to [[evt:19746|Iemi]] I gave a bull for his victory, and the king went down. When our army went down to [[evt:19757|Iemi]] again I gave two bulls, and the army went down again. Then came [[evt:19758|the treaty]], and the king's men went from house to house for the silver to pay the men of Iti, and they took my [[cur:395|puma]], and they took my storehouse, and they took my fields. Then I was poor. Then, by the reckoning of the priests, the gods had judged me.

I confess: for many years I was angry. I cursed the gods of the river and the sky. I cursed [[dei:8|Nish'il]] who sees, because he saw and did nothing.

And then I sat down by the river in the dry season, with nothing in my hands, and I did not pray, because I had nothing left to pray for. And I began to reason, as a man reckons a debt. I said: if the gods are paid, and do not deliver, then they are not lords. They are servants, and poor servants. Then whose servants are they? And in the stillness the answer came to me, not from a priest, not from the liver of a sheep, but from within, as a lamp is lit in a dark room. There is One above them. The many gods are His servants and His shadows. He is not bought. He does not count bulls.

And I saw the wheel, as our fathers taught, turning; but I saw that the wheel does not turn for gold. The rich man goes down and the poor man goes down, and all come round again. What the wheel asks is not wealth but justice, not feasting but measure. The man who has little has less to lose when the wheel turns. The wise put down their wealth before it is taken from them.

This is what I have taught since the last year, at [[set:205|Pivish]] by the river, to any who will sit with me. I do not say: believe me. I say: sit, and be still, and reason, and see whether the lamp is lit in you also. This is the way of [[mov:69|Kievi]], which [[evt:20057|began among us]] in poverty. It is not for Pivish alone. It is for the men of Iti also, who took my fields. They too have a lamp.

I confess these things. I hide nothing. Let the priests of the many gods say what they will.
