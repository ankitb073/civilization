To the king [[per:41483|Bubiruyin]], my lord, my sun, lord of [[pol:1765|Biniynge]], say: thus says your servant, the dust under your feet.

At the feet of the king my lord seven times and seven times I fall, on the belly and on the back.

May the Light of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] keep the king my lord. May it keep his house, his sons, his horses and his city. May the old gods, the servants of the Light, stand at his door.

Let the king my lord know: since the day the king my lord stood up in Biniynge and cast off the yoke of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]], on the day of the [[evt:41869|Secession of Biniynge]], your servant has guarded the road of the king. I have not slept in my house. I have not eaten at my table. I have guarded the road of the king.

Let the king my lord know: I went down with [[per:41502|Lomidun]], your general, to [[set:121|Riniy]] in the land of [[reg:8|Diviguvivinge]]. We were thirty and four. Thirty and four we were, the king's men, no more.

The men of Yidhe came out against us. They were as the sand of the river. They were as the locusts in the barley. They covered the hills, they covered the fields, they covered the road. [[per:41496|Tuvbilkhun]] led them, the same who stood against us at Linidhagshal.

We struck them. From the morning to the evening we struck them. Their dead lay like reeds cut for the roof. Their dead were more than a hundred thousand; the vultures could not eat them all. Of the servants of the king my lord, six fell. Six fell, and the rest stand.

But the field is not ours. The gods gave the field to Yidhe at the [[evt:41903|battle of Riniy]]. Their living walk upon their dead, and the road to Riniy is in their hand.

So it was at [[evt:41888|Linidhagshal]] in the 2nd year of the king my lord, when [[per:41495|Khuthnathdan]] went out with twenty and eight men by [[set:161|Linidhagushal]], and four fell, and the dead of Yidhe were heaped like grain on the threshing floor, and still the field was given to them. Our men are lions; but lions are eaten by the jackals when the jackals are many.

Let the king my lord hear the words of his servant. Why does the king my lord not send men? Send men. Send archers. Send bread. Send oil. If the king my lord does not send men in this year, the land of Diviguvivinge will not be the land of the king. Who will guard the road of the king? Your servant will guard it alone, with the few men who are left to him.

I have heard, and my heart is sick in my belly, that the enemy has come into the city of the king my lord, the [[evt:41908|sack of Biniynge]]; that the house of tablets was burned; that the tablets of the fathers are ash. I have heard that the king my lord has set his seal to a peace in Biniynge, the [[evt:41909|Treaty of Biniynge]], and given towns and fields to the enemy. Your servant does not question the king. The king is the sun; the sun knows where it walks. But let the king my lord remember: the men of Yidhe have made no peace with us. This [[evt:41870|third war of Yidhe]] is not ended. The sword of Yidhe is still in the hand of Tuvbilkhun.

Let the king my lord be strong. Let the king my lord be like the storm. Let the king my lord remember his servant who guards the road.

May the Light keep the king my lord. May it keep [[set:11|Biniynge]], the city of the king, though its walls are broken.

Written in the 4th year of Bubiruyin.
