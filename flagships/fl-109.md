Here begins the record of the disputation held in the chapter-house at [[set:131|Shiüshi]], in the 16th year of our lord [[per:36620|Shiilu]], king of [[pol:1398|Imshi]], between the elders of the old obedience of [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] on the one part, and [[per:36833|Iminimin]], priest, who writes this, on the other; set down faithfully, so far as a sinner may, that those who were not present may judge.

**Of the occasion.** In the year before, the brethren of Shiüshi who hold with me had separated themselves from the old obedience, and the elders called it the [[evt:37371|Shamu schism]], and wrote against us to the court. The king's steward, being of the faith of [[mov:69|Kievi]] and no party to the quarrel, commanded that the matter be disputed openly before him, lest the peace of the realm be troubled in a year of war. To this both parts consented, and the steward sat in the high chair, and the elders sat on his right hand, and I sat on his left.

**The first question.** The eldest of the elders asked: "Do you, Iminimin, hold that time runs in a line from the Creation to the Judgement, and that there is one divine power, and that the truth is kept by an ordained priesthood, handed down in order?" I answered: "All this I hold, as my fathers held it, and as you hold it. In none of these things have I departed by a hair." The steward wrote it down, and the elders could not deny it.

**The second question.** The elder asked: "Why then do you say that the riches of this world are a sign of the favour of the One? For the Teaching says that the merciful shall be released from suffering, and nowhere that the rich are beloved." I answered: "The Teaching says also that the One rewards the faithful in this world as a token of the next. Look about you, fathers. In this year and the last our lord the king has gone out against his enemies. At [[evt:37333|Ninshinuesnun]] his captains were victorious; at [[evt:37340|Nuvun]] the king himself rode in the van and was victorious; his host sat down before Xath at the [[evt:37341|fourth siege]], and the [[evt:37342|conquest of Shathi]] followed, and all that land is now joined to the crown. The granaries of Imshi are full; the [[cur:766|Nadush]] is heavy and good. Shall we say that the One does not see this? Or that He sees it and means nothing by it?" At this the steward smiled, and the elders were silent a long while.

**The third question.** Then the elder said, in some heat: "But you have also taught that the faith belongs to one people, and that we must not go out to make converts among the nations; whereas the Teaching commands that the word be carried to all peoples." I answered: "Fathers, you have carried it to all peoples these many generations, and what have the peoples done with it? They have cut it to their own measure and made sects of it, until every market has its preacher and every preacher his own gospel. I say that a vineyard must be walled before it is enlarged. Let the faithful of Imshi be faithful first; let the priesthood keep the truth in order among its own; let the One, who gave this people victory, decide whether others shall be added to it." Here many in the hall murmured assent, and some of the younger clergy of the old obedience came over to our side of the chamber.

**The fourth question.** The elder asked: "Do you then forsake compassion?" I answered: "God forbid. The poor shall be fed at our doors as at yours, and the dying comforted. We differ not in mercy but in the reading of the signs."

**Of the end.** The steward rose and declared that he found no treason in either part, and that the king's peace extended to both; and he commanded that neither should drive the other from the churches by force. The elders departed displeased. We departed giving thanks.

So ends the record. Let the reader judge whether the brethren of [[mov:137|Shamu]] have departed from the faith, or returned to it. Written at Shiüshi, in the 16th year of Shiilu, by the hand of Iminimin, priest.
