Five inscriptions survive in the name of [[per:39742|Tozhush]], and I have stood in the trench beside only one of them. In AY 4945, along the old city wall of Tliyiya, we lifted a diorite stele that only minor chips have marred. The other four, with the epitaphs of the boy he replaced and the son who followed him, came out of the ground between 4902 and 4922 in the excavations of my older namesake, the elder Nambili. These stones, one hostile chronicle and a son's testament are nearly all that speaks for a man who took the throne of [[pol:1655|Ölie]] at twenty-nine and held it for forty-one years. Read against the record, they say less than they claim and more than they mean to.

## A crown in a crisis year

Tozhush was born in AY 4179, a man of the Lizo people. The record names no father and no mother; his house begins with him. His boyhood ran through the long Ölie–Ishwyithie War (4183–4199), in which the kingdom's armies fought four times at Tliyiya alone; whether he marched in any of it, nothing says. He married a woman born a year after him; two sons came in 4200 and 4202, and both were dead by 4204.

The throne was in weak hands. Shimilyian III died in 4201 and left a five-year-old, [[per:39647|Nuishme]], under the regency of his mother, Nitllu Foyatl. The kingdom's generals won the defensive [[evt:40178|Myizinitie–Ölie War]], but it was the boy, at eleven, whose name went on the Treaty of Onithie in 4207.

In 4208 Nuishme died, aged twelve. The record calls his death natural; in the same year it enters the [[evt:40216|Usurpation of Tozhush]] and marks it a crisis. It does not say how the one followed the other, and I will not supply a dagger the evidence withholds. What it does preserve is the boy's sarcophagus lid from the royal tombs at [[set:264|Ölye]], cut that year (PAL·IN·06263):

> Nuishme, king of Ölie, son of Shimilyian, dwells in this tomb.

In the first year of the new reign, someone still paid a mason to call the dead child king. The survey at the end of the decade found the realm's stability lower than the kingdom had yet recorded.

## Victories that needed winning

The record ties two of his wars directly to his seizure of power: both the [[evt:40285|Second Ölie–Izimue War]] (4215–4221) and the [[evt:40417|Ölie–Yiutl War]] (4227–4231) list the usurpation among their causes. A king without a pedigree wants victories, and Tozhush fetched them in person. At the [[evt:40299|Battle of Ashmath (AY 4217)]] he brought 37,198 men against the 7,505 of [[pol:1701|Izimue]] under its king, [[per:39802|Shiminilyian]], and lost 92 to their 607. Twice at Iazhash he beat the general [[per:39827|Tyie]], with 42,112 men in the [[evt:40312|Battle of Iazhash (AY 4219)]] and 51,145 in the [[evt:40364|Battle of Iazhash (AY 4221)]], against armies never larger than 7,625. In all five battles he is recorded as commanding, he outnumbered the enemy at least four to one, and won.

The [[evt:40365|Treaty of Iazhash (AY 4221)]], sworn with Shiminilyian, took sixteen districts of Nis and imposed a fifteen-year truce. Tozhush marked the new border with a stone, found at Ashmath in 4902, whose final lines are lost (PAL·IN·06959): "Tozhush, king of Ölie, erected this boundary stone in Nis. [...]"

Against [[pol:1705|Yiutl]], Myizinitie's successor, we hear the other side. Its historian Iinmamshi (PAL·CH·07140) says Tozhush "broke the peace and sent raiders across the border of Yiutl" in 4227; the fifteen-year truce sworn at Onithie had in fact run out in 4222. Of the defeat at Nyinsitlasa in 4229 he writes: "We lost 905; the enemy, 1,163." The record gives Yiutl 2,014 losses and Ölie 503, under Ölie's general Luishaluisha. Of the [[evt:40470|Battle of Pivithie (AY 4231)]], where Tozhush himself led 56,665 men against the 4,255 of [[per:40006|Nitllu Nyime]], the chronicle says nothing. It ends in mercy: "out of mercy for the common folk, the king accepted the pleas of Ölie". That king, [[per:39889|Wimamamshi II]], was seventeen, and the [[evt:40471|Treaty of Pivithie (AY 4231)]] he swore ceded five districts, made Yiutl a vassal, granted Ölie trade, and paid an indemnity of 19,174 kilograms of silver, some 1.3 million [[cur:917|nyinan]]. Victors do not plead for such terms.

## Stones for Itlie

The victory steles that survive begin only after Pivithie, when Tozhush was fifty-four, and all four repeat one formula. The first, at [[set:91|Pivithie]] (PAL·IN·03044), reads:

> Tozhush, king of Ölie, defeated Yiutl in Pivitie. He killed 4,700 soldiers. He erected this stele for Itlie, his lord. May Itlie destroy the name of the enemy.

Yiutl lost 727 men there, of 4,255. Every one of the four steles claims more dead than the enemy put in the field: 14,900 at Tliyiya against an army of 9,584 (PAL·IN·03046); 13,000 at Kheye against 9,750 (PAL·IN·03049); 12,900 at Mivimäkhi against 12,486 (PAL·IN·03052). The record's losses are 4,051, 2,394 and 2,179. Two of those battles were not his: the record gives the command at Kheye in 4240 and at Mivimäkhi in 4243 to Luishaluisha. The king's name went on the stone; the general's did not.

The [[evt:40493|Holy War of Ölie against Khyie]] (4237–4240) has a bleak symmetry. [[pol:1702|Khyie]], a breakaway born in 4217 of a revolt against Ishwyithie, had been seized in 4230 by another usurper, [[per:39999|Fispeshimin]], born a year after Tozhush; the record names that usurpation as the war's cause. In 4236 Fispeshimin laid a foundation tablet at [[set:83|Kheye]] (PAL·IN·06346): he "built this temple for Nyizhashte, his lady". An Ölie king, Mashe Ivie, had used the very same words for the same war goddess at Ölye in 4164 (PAL·IN·06260). The faiths differed, Setlietlpovi in Kheye and the [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] cult in Ölye; the goddess did not. Within a year Ölie marched on Khyie in the name of religion. At the [[evt:40501|Battle of Tliyiya (AY 4238)]] Tozhush, now fifty-nine, broke a Khyie army whose general, [[per:40047|Nitllu Ivie]], died on the field at thirty: born, that is, in the year of the usurpation. My stele was raised for that day. The elder Nambili had found Fispeshimin's tablet and Tozhush's Kheye stele in the same season of 4917. The [[evt:40537|Conquest of Khyie]], sealed by both kings at Kheye in 4240, absorbed sixteen districts. Fispeshimin outlived his conqueror by eight years.

## A field after a flood

[[pol:1446|Mii]] tested the old king in 4242, marching on Nis, the province he had won. Ölie's generals beat its armies twice at [[set:192|Mivimäkhi]], and in 4245 Tozhush, at sixty-six, sealed the [[evt:40569|Treaty of Mivimäkhi (AY 4245)]] with Queen [[per:39922|Luinisnesh]]: a fifteen-year truce and nothing more.

His private life thins into dates. His wife died in 4235; of six children, three died young. The son born in 4209, the year after the usurpation, heir under Ölie's male primogeniture, died in 4247 at thirty-eight. Tozhush died two years later, in 4249, a natural death at seventy, and the [[evt:40589|Accession of Nistlushimin]] gave the crown to his youngest son, [[per:40013|Nistlushimin]], aged twenty-seven.

No epitaph of Tozhush has been found. In the royal tombs his name survives only on his son's lintel (PAL·IN·06265): "Nistlushimin, king of Ölie, son of Tozhush, dwells in this tomb." In the son's testament of 4312 (PAL·DE·00928) he is "King Tozhush of blessed memory", whose realm the son received "as a field after a flood" and left "as a field at harvest". The surveys are fairer. Between the end of the 4200s and the end of the 4240s Ölie grew from 184 districts to 223 and from about 11.1 to 12.7 million people, and its stability rose at every survey from that low ebb. The treasury did fall, by about a fifth, to 611,527 kilograms of silver. If there was a flood, it ran through the treasury, not the fields; it is the habit of sons to inherit floods.

The curse on his steles asked Itlie to destroy the name of the enemy. It failed: Shiminilyian, Tyie, Nitllu Ivie and Fispeshimin all stand in the record beside him. What the stones destroyed was the count. Their inflated thousands bury the real dead on both sides; Ölie's own losses in his four wars come to about 8,500 men, and no stele names one of them. The house he founded held Ölie for 172 years. Its founder is best seen as I saw him at Tliyiya: through a stone that lies about the dead and tells the truth about the man who paid for it, that he needed to be believed.
