**A TRUE RELATION of the new art of making writing-leaves out of rags and old cordage, lately found out in the city of [[set:200|Shityi]]; set down by the finder of it, the least of the servants of the king, to be read in the chapter-houses and at the doors of the markets.**

In the name of the gods who share the world, each in his place and each in her due; and with leave of the ordained priests of [[mov:25|Myitsiyishio]], who keep the truth in order; and under the favour of our lord [[per:28165|Sitlyitliyuive]], king of [[pol:1227|Shityi]], whose servant I am.

I, [[per:28391|Ishuisimin]], a maker of cords by trade, make known to all who read or hear this relation the following things, that no one may say afterwards that the art was hidden or that it came by sorcery.

**I. How the art was found.** It has long been reported by travellers that in certain lands beyond our borders men write upon leaves that are neither skin nor bark. I do not claim to be the first under heaven to make such leaves. I claim only that no one in Shityi knew the way of it, and that I sought it, as the priests teach us to seek, with patience and moderation, for many years. I beat flax and old rope in water until they were as soft as porridge. I spread the porridge upon a sieve of fine cords, and pressed it, and dried it in the sun. Many times it tore. Many times the rats ate it. In the twenty-sixth year of our lord's reign, which is two years past, a leaf held together under the pen and took the ink without running. I fell upon my face and gave thanks.

**II. What the leaves are good for.** A calf gives one skin, and the skin must be scraped and stretched and whitened for many days, and a scribe will pay many [[cur:663|Fashin]] for a good one. But a vat of rags, which the poor throw into the street, will give a whole quire of my leaves in a morning. Therefore the word of the king may go out to every village and not only to the great towns. Therefore the accounts of the granaries may be kept in full and not cut short to spare the skins. Therefore the novices of the chapter-houses may copy the sayings of the priests for themselves, and learn them better for having copied them.

**III. What they are not good for.** I will not boast, for the wise withdraw from appetite, and boasting is appetite of the tongue. My leaves are weaker than skin. They do not love the damp. A charter of the king, which must last for ever, I would still write on skin; and indeed this relation, which my lord's secretaries wished to keep in the treasury, is written on skin, for they do not yet trust my leaves with a thing that must last. Let them try my leaves for ten years, and then judge.

**IV. Of the times in which this was done.** Let no one say that the art was found in idle years. In the years that I laboured at my vats, the spears of our enemies came against the kingdom. At [[evt:28857|Thushashfi]] the gods turned their faces from us. At [[evt:28860|Eol]] they turned them back. Last year at [[evt:28874|Ivu]] we were driven from the field; this year, at [[evt:28882|Eol]] again, our people stood and held. I have seen my neighbours go out to those fields and some not return. It may be asked whether a man should make leaves while his neighbours make war. I answer: the captains also need leaves, to write their musters and their orders and the names of the fallen, and I have given them leaves, and asked no price.

**V. A petition.** I ask the priests to bless the vats as they bless the plough. I ask the lords to send me their worn linen rather than burn it. I ask our lord the king, of his justice, that the art be taught to any honest man who will learn it, and not shut up in one house; for an art that is shut up dies with its keeper, and I am no longer young.

Given in the year 2632 of the Myitsiyishio. May the gods keep the king, and keep the kingdom, and keep the rags of the poor from the fire.
