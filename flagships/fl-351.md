*[Obverse.]*

O [[dei:0|Atlie]] of the high sky, you saw it. O [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon, you saw it by night. O [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river, you carried the ash away. I also saw it. I set it down in clay, so that the gods may know that their servants saw, and so that the children of [[set:2|Mieshlothie]] may know.

In the seventh year of [[per:1050|Esiesh Foyatl]], our lady, the men of [[pol:6|Ana]] came against the city again.

Seven years ago the city lifted its head. In the [[evt:752|Rise of Mieshlothie]] the lady took the throne, and the city stood by itself, and the priests said: the gods are with Mieshlothie. Then came the [[evt:769|Ana–Mieshlothie War]]. Then the men of Ana came down into the land of Yiutl, many as the reeds, many as the locusts.

Last year they came. Last year was the [[evt:778|Third Sack of Myishlithie]]. I saw it. My father's house was opened, my father's jars were broken. We had not yet set up the doors again. We had not yet plastered the walls again. We had not yet sown the field that they burned.

This year they came again.

Our guard was small. A few tens of men stood at the gate, no more. Who could stand against such a host? Thousands upon thousands they came, five thousands and more. Yet our men went out. At the [[evt:785|Battle of Myishlithie]] they stood in the gate of the river. Many of the men of Ana fell before them, many hundreds fell before them. I saw them carried back. I saw the men of Ana dragging their dead. But our men also fell. Twelve of ours fell there, at the gate. Twelve. I knew their faces.

Then came the [[evt:786|Fourth Sack of Myishlithie]]. This is what I saw.

I saw the gate broken with axes.  
I saw the men of Ana in the street of the potters.  
I saw them carry out the oil, the wool, the barley.  
I saw them lead away the oxen and the goats.  
I saw them take the bronze bowls from the house of [[dei:1|Uelti]].  
I saw an old woman sit in the road and pour dust on her head.  
I saw a child looking for its mother, and no one answered it.  
I saw the smoke over the market, and the smoke over the granary.

*[Reverse.]*

I hid in the reeds by the river with my sister and her children. Niele'i hid us. Niele'i covered us with the reeds. Three days we were in the reeds. We drank the river water. We ate nothing. At night we heard them singing in the city, singing to their own gods.

On the fourth day the men of Ana went out of the city to their camp, and we came back. The house of my father had no roof. The jar of seed was gone. But the house of the gods still stood. The priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] were in the court, pouring water on the stones, washing the blood away.

Then our lady Esiesh Foyatl went down to the gate to meet the men of Ana. She went with the elders. She went on foot. There in the city, in the court of the gods, the [[evt:787|Treaty of Myishlithie]] was sworn before the gods. The land of Yiutl that was ours is theirs. Our lady bows her head to Ana now. Every year the city will send silver and grain to Ana, every year for many years. This was the price of the city's life.

Some say the lady should not have sworn. I say: I saw the gate. I saw the street of the potters. I saw the child in the road. Let those who say she should not have sworn go and stand in the gate.

O [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of the war, why did you turn your face from us? O [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth, why did you let them walk on you? We have given you right sacrifice. We will give you right sacrifice again. The wheel turns. What has been brought low will rise again.

Let this tablet be placed in the house of the gods. Let whoever reads it pour water for the twelve who fell at the gate.
