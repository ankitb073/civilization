*[Obverse, column i.]*

O [[set:6|Chinontonu]], Zhinontunu of the old songs, mother of cities,  
O city of the river-gate and the barley-gate, city of the bright bronze,  
four times the gate has been broken; four times the fire has gone in.  
I sing the fourth. Who will sing a fifth? Let there be no fifth.  

[[dei:55|Dumamnul]] of the high sky, you saw it.  
[[dei:56|Nabuthin]] of the river, you carried the ash.  
[[dei:58|Chithal]] of the spear, where was your spear?  

*Weep, Chinontonu, weep; the wheel has turned against you.*  

First the men of Yida bowed us down. At Puthnimdu the old lord set his hand to the oath;  
this was the [[evt:765|Treaty of Puthnimdu]]: the silver went out to Yida, the land went out to Yida.  
The yoke was set on our neck, and our neck was bowed.  
The men of [[pol:8|Navnga]] saw the bowed neck. They said: "Now."  
This was the [[evt:760|Navnga–Chinontonu War]]. They came down like the flood of spring.  

At Poyonga they came, they were as many as the stalks in a field.  
Our men were a few sheaves standing. Our general stood; our general lay down.  
This was the [[evt:768|Battle of Poynge]]. The field was not ours.  
At Puthnimdu they came again; a handful of ours stood against the sea of theirs.  
A handful stood; the sea went over them.  
This was the [[evt:770|Battle of Puthnimdu]]. The road to our gate was open.  

*Weep, Chinontonu, weep; the wheel has turned against you.*  

Then they came to the gate. This was the [[evt:771|Sack of Zhinontunu]].  
They came in at the river-gate; they came in at the barley-gate.  
They took the bronze of the house of the lord. They took the cloth of the women.  
The jars of oil they broke; the oil ran in the street with the blood.  
The weights of the market they carried away; the [[cur:8|Yong]] of the merchants they carried away.  
The singer's lyre they broke on the threshold.  
The grandmother who had seen the third fire sat down in the ash and did not rise.  
The child who saw the fourth fire did not weep; the child had no more weeping.  

[[dei:57|Bubin]] of the night, you saw it with your silver eye.  
[[dei:59|Winba]] of the earth, you opened your mouth; you were not full.  

*[Column ii.]*

Then the elders went out with dust on their heads.  
Then the old lord set his hand to a second oath, at our own gate.  
This was the [[evt:772|Treaty of Zhinontunu]]. Our fields were given to Navnga.  
Two oaths in three years; two yokes upon one neck.  

*Weep, Chinontonu, weep; the wheel has turned against you.*  

Then the people said: the hand of the old lord is weak.  
Then the gods said: let a new hand take the seat.  
So the new hand took the seat, in the fourth year after the fire.  
Now [[per:1075|Inuvunin]] sits on the seat. Now his hand is on the gate.  
The gate is mended. The street is swept. The river-gate is hung again.  
May his hand be strong. May his hand not be weak.  

All things turn on the great wheel, say the priests of [[mov:8|Ligay]];  
what ends will begin again. The burned field gives the green shoot.  
Chinontonu has fallen four times; four times Chinontonu has stood up.  
Shineyta of the sea, send the boats back to our quay.  
Chithal of the spear, take up your spear for [[pol:12|Chinontonu]].  
Let there be no fifth fire. Let there be no fifth song.  

*Weep, Chinontonu, weep; and when you have wept, rise.*  

> Sung in the 6th year of Inuvunin, lord of Chinontonu, at the mended gate.
