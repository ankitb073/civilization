Of [[per:46336|Boziniluisha]] the record keeps a birth, a death and ten battles. He was born in AY 4861, a Lizo, in the year the kingdom of [[pol:1970|Muvush]] proclaimed itself an empire, and he was killed in battle in 4922, aged sixty-one, two years before I was born. His name means *victory stone*. The record names no parent, wife or child, and is silent on how he rose. No general of Muvush has yet led its armies into more battles.

What lifts him out of the tables is that he speaks. Six of his telegrams to the Ministry of War survive, complete, in the state archive of Muvush. As a translator I know no form of writing that flattens a voice more: every general on the wire reports "our losses about" and "enemy losses estimated". Read against the record, the six forms still say a good deal about the man.

The empire grew around him: its survey of the 4860s counted 22.4 million people in 185 districts, that of the 4920s 55.2 million in 279. Bread rose too; Muvush newspapers quote grain at 1.05 [[cur:1119|Losh]] the measure in 4886 and 2.5 in 4921.

## Tliyiya, the first time

He first appears at twenty-seven, in the [[evt:47312|Holy War of Muvush against Tomzel]], opened in 4886 under [[per:46307|Pitheliluisha II]]. At the [[evt:47333|Battle of Tliyiya (AY 4888)]] he led 214,923 men against 111,495 under [[per:46337|Sasshe]] of [[pol:1967|Tomzel]], and won: Tomzel lost 37,724, Muvush 18,724. His wire (PAL·TG·00173) is a young man's: "ENEMY BROKEN NEAR TLIYIAN STOP PURSUIT CONTINUING STOP OUR LOSSES ABOUT 16,800 STOP ENEMY LOSSES ESTIMATED 62,100 STOP REQUEST REINFORCEMENTS TO EXPLOIT". He trimmed a tenth from his own dead and added nearly two-thirds to the enemy's. Sasshe's wire (PAL·TG·00174) gave about two-thirds of his own and asked for ammunition "URGENTLY".

The Muvush account of 4895, now fragmentary (PAL·CH·08263), alone of the war's chronicles names him: he "brought up the guns at the decisive moment". It gives Sasshe 254,438 men, more than twice his real strength, and counts 135,564 Tomzel dead, more than his whole army. The Tomzel account of 4931 (PAL·CH·08264) has its host "outnumbered three to one" (it was under two to one) and turns the dead around: "It cost us 20,292 men, and them 64,747." The court annals of Muvush (PAL·AN·00874) record another general's victory at Foyitlithyi a year earlier and omit Tliyiya.

The peace signed at Ishwyithie in 4888 gave Muvush 28 districts and three towns, Lyismiyi among them. Its fourth article (PAL·TR·02900) was the war's religious prize: "Throughout the land of Tomzel the worship of Myithiyishiu shall be permitted and its shrines protected". The prize did not keep. In 4893 Tomzel returned to its old faith, the I'ishiu; in 4894 Pitheliluisha's edict (PAL·DE·00862) made the [[mov:83|I'ashakh]] the faith of Muvush and ruled that "The rites of the Myithiyishiu shall be kept only in the old temples." Six years after Tliyiya, neither state professed the cult in whose name it was fought.

## Silent years, and a war that was hardly one

Then the record loses him for eighteen years. He returns at forty-five, in the last battle of the [[evt:47498|Second Muvush–Ölye War]]. At the [[evt:47541|Sixth Battle of Edeye'a]] in 4906 he led 207,290 men against 189,892 under [[per:46479|Poavye]] of [[pol:1968|Ölye]]. Ölye lost 42,777, Muvush 16,857. We can watch his dead shrink as the news travelled: the Muvush Observer (PAL·NP·00783) printed 11,800, and Isyi Mof's chronicle of 4916 (PAL·CH·08287) 3,646, adding that "Boziniluisha held the heights against them, and providence favoured our arms." Ölye's chronicler (PAL·CH·08288) blamed "a night of rain that bogged the guns", which the record cannot test. That same year Muvush sacked Ölye; in 4911 the excavator Nambili found its "burnt destruction layer, ash and fallen brick" (PAL·EX·00219). The record names no commander at the sack, and I will not lay it at his door.

The [[evt:47620|Muvush–Omatse War]] of 4914–4918, under the Empress Yi Tlyi, was hardly a war. [[pol:1993|Omatse]], a city-state founded in 4900, met him at the [[evt:47626|Battle of Omatse (AY 4915)]] with 7,101 men against 533,375. He commanded all three of its battles: Muvush lost 576 men, Omatse 2,993, and each of his wires roughly doubled the enemy's dead. After Iazhash, Omatse's paper (PAL·NP·00811) said only that its army "has fallen back to prepared positions. The censor has withheld further details." Its chronicler (PAL·CH·08300) kept only the last fight, the [[evt:47645|Battle of Dainagafu (AY 4918)]], which he calls Taenagath and Boziniluisha's wire Tyinakhath: "171 of ours were lost, but 208 of the enemy." The record has 344 and 81. When the city fell its ruler since its founding, Etlietlmoga, was executed; the record does not say on whose order.

In 4917 the record puts him at Iazhash with 506,619 men and, in another province, at Tliyiya with 510,105: together more than the 841,647 under arms in the empire's survey of the 4910s. Whether one army marched between fronts or was counted twice, the record does not say.

## Holding with difficulty

The real war began in 4916, when [[pol:1922|Yiutl]], which had swallowed Tomzel in 4906, declared war. The [[evt:47631|Yiutl–Muvush War]] was fought over the frontier of his youth: near Tliyiya, now Yiutl's, and at Lyismiyi, won for the empire by his first victory. He commanded five of its six battles, and in every one Muvush had the larger army. At the [[evt:47638|Battle of Tliyiya (AY 4917)]], 510,105 against 439,197 under [[per:46471|Oi Lyizi]], he was badly beaten: Muvush lost 118,877, Yiutl 64,194. No wire of his from that day survives. At the [[evt:47648|Battle of Lyismiyi (AY 4918)]] [[per:46589|Tsyitlmu]] beat him narrowly, 55,234 dead to 52,505, and this time his wire (PAL·TG·00258) survives:

> LINE GIVING WAY NEAR LYISMIYI STOP HOLDING WITH DIFFICULTY STOP OUR LOSSES ABOUT 42,300 STOP REQUEST RELIEF STOP

It is the most honest thing he left: it understates his dead by nearly a quarter, but offers no guess at the enemy's.

In 4919, on the same ground, he had his best day. At the [[evt:47654|Battle of Lyismiyi (AY 4919)]] Oi Lyizi attacked again and lost 115,367 men to Muvush's 30,533, and his own life. His wire from that field (PAL·TG·00261) uses Boziniluisha's words of the year before: "HOLDING WITH DIFFICULTY STOP OUR LOSSES ABOUT 99,300 STOP REQUEST RELIEF". The telegraph made every beaten general sound alike. At the [[evt:47684|Battle of Lyismiyi (AY 4921)]] Tsyitlmu too was killed, but the fight was nearly even: Yiutl lost 22,983, Muvush 21,219. Boziniluisha's wire, sent at 0445 (PAL·TG·00264), reported "ENEMY BROKEN", "MORALE EXCELLENT" and 47,800 enemy dead. The Muvush Clarion (PAL·NP·00818) printed his own as 11,492, just over half the truth. "The church bells rang at noon."

## The last field

In 4922 he met [[per:46633|Nuitluel]], twenty-eight years his junior, near Tliyiya, where his career had begun. At the [[evt:47692|Battle of Tliyiya (AY 4922)]] he had 382,894 men to 299,467 and lost again, 82,542 dead to 35,492, and he was killed. No wire or Muvush account of the day survives. The victors' paper, the Thimisitsiy Messenger (PAL·NP·00826), announced "VICTORY AT TLIYIYA" and a thanksgiving service without naming him; further down the page it reported that my own country, Khishkhis, had opened hostilities against Lude. Only Tlamfin Mof's Yiutl chronicle of 4934 (PAL·CH·08301) records his death: "Boziniluisha himself was killed." It gives him 558,089 men and 303,257 dead, and ends the war with Muvush, "having gained nothing", returning "home in shame".

The phrase fits Yiutl as well. In 4926 the treaty of Ishwyithie (PAL·TR·02921) agreed that "The frontiers shall remain as they were before the outbreak of hostilities." The war cost Yiutl 341,201 dead and Muvush 335,480; 308,405 of the Muvush dead fell in his five battles.

He won seven of ten battles, never outnumbered. Three wins were over a city-state with a few thousand men; against real armies he won four and lost three, all to Yiutl, all with the larger force. Across the ten, Muvush lost 344,562 men and its enemies 374,035, a near-even exchange for a general who always had more. His wires shaded the truth as every commander's did; the papers and chroniclers of Muvush did far worse in his name. The war he died in moved no frontier. What remains is a man of sixty-one at the head of nearly four hundred thousand, on a field he had first crossed at twenty-seven, and one evening in 4918 when, alone among his surviving wires, he told the ministry that the line was giving way.
