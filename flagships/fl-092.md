*[[per:18752|Nanüdi]] to his companions, greeting. You asked me to set down what was said between me and the priest of [[dei:48|Kudze]] at the temple gate of [[set:86|Ndulamlule]], on the evening that many now call the [[evt:18859|founding of the Nidunde faith]]. I set it down as nearly as I remember it, in the year 2211 from the founding of the city. Judge it by reason, as I asked him to.*

PRIEST. You are the man who tells the porters that the gods are servants. Sit, then, and tell a priest.

NANÜDI. Gladly, if you will answer as well as ask. Tell me first: two years ago the old state broke apart. Did you see it?

PRIEST. Who did not? The [[evt:18738|Collapse of the Ndulamlule State]] was seen by every child in the market. And in the same year the kingdom rose again, under [[per:18635|Nabule]]. This is the wheel, Nanüdi. What ends begins again. The [[evt:18739|Rise of Ndulamlule]] is the proof of what [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]] has always taught.

NANÜDI. Then the men who died in the breaking, have they begun again?

PRIEST. Their sons stand in their places.

NANÜDI. Their sons are not they. The kingdom that rose has a new king and new borders and the same name. Is a man who takes his dead brother's name his brother? I say time does not turn; it runs, as a river runs, from its spring to its end, and at its end there is a reckoning. The wheel is a comfort for those who wish to be excused from the end.

PRIEST. A clever saying. But the gods disagree with you, and they are older than you.

NANÜDI. Let us count them. Kudze holds the sky. [[dei:49|Tuvi]] holds the underworld. [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] holds war, [[dei:51|Talu]] the earth, [[dei:52|Bumbi]] the sea, and [[dei:53|Bine]] the fire. Each has a place and a due. Is that your teaching?

PRIEST. It is.

NANÜDI. And who gave each a place? When the steward gives each servant his task, there is a master behind the steward. When the gods have each a place, is there not one who placed them?

PRIEST. The places are as they have always been.

NANÜDI. That is not an answer but a custom. I say there is one divine power, and the gods you name are its servants, or its shadows on the wall. I do not insult them. I only put them in their order.

PRIEST. And who ordained you to say this? Truth is kept by the priesthood and passed down hand to hand. Whose hand passed it to you?

NANÜDI. No one's. I found it by the light that is in me, and by reasoning, as I am reasoning with you now. The same light is in you, and in the porter, and in the woman who sells barley at the gate. If the truth can be found only by those who are ordained, then the gods have made most men blind for the convenience of a few.

PRIEST. You will empty the temples.

NANÜDI. I will fill them with people who know why they came. Tell me: what is the good life?

PRIEST. Duty, order and right sacrifice.

NANÜDI. And when grain costs near three [[cur:334|guvu]] the measure, as it does this year, and the widow cannot buy both bread and a dove for the altar, which shall she buy?

PRIEST. The dove, and trust the gods for bread.

NANÜDI. There we differ most. I say the good life is compassion, and release from suffering, and that the one power is better pleased by the bread you give the widow than by the dove you take from her. In one thing only we agree: that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. Then let us make the city prosperous by mercy, and see whom the favour follows.

PRIEST. And the strangers? Our gods belong to our people.

NANÜDI. Then the man of another city has no god, or a smaller one? I say this teaching is for all peoples, and I will tell it to anyone who will sit with me, as you have.

*Here the priest rose and went in to the evening sacrifice, and did not answer. But the porters who had listened stayed with me until the lamps were lit, and asked to hear it again. These were the first of the [[mov:62|Nidunde]], in the kingdom of [[pol:838|Ndulamlule]].*
