**ON THE METHOD OF TRIAL: A REPLY TO CERTAIN LEARNED CRITICS**

*By [[per:44027|Ae Niem]], of the city of [[set:76|Nemeth]]. Communicated to the editor on the 7th of Yuel, in the year 2450 of the E'eshio.*

Sir,

It is now two years since I first laid before the public, in the pages of your paper and in a small pamphlet sold by the booksellers of the city, the account of what I then called, for want of a better name, *the method of trial*; and since your correspondents have done me the honour of abusing it in nine letters, I beg leave to answer them together, and in order.

**I. What the method is.** It is not a doctrine. It is a manner of asking. When a question arises concerning the workings of the world (why a thing falls, how fast it cools, what makes a seed swell), one does not first consult what has been said of it; one first contrives a trial, so arranged that nature must answer yes or no; one measures the answer with instruments that another may borrow; one repeats the trial until the answer ceases to change; and one publishes the whole of it, failures included, so that any person in [[pol:1755|Tsiey]] or beyond may repeat it and find me out if I have erred. That is all.

**II. An example, since examples are wanted.** I hung a lead weight from a silk cord in the stairwell of my father's house and counted its swings against the water-clock of the temple. I then hung a weight of iron, three times as heavy, from a cord of the same length. The swings were the same in number to within one in a hundred. I shortened the cord to a quarter, and the swings came twice as fast. I did this on forty evenings, and every count lies open to inspection at my lodging. Now, the learned held, and still hold, that the heavier weight should swing the faster. I do not ask anyone to believe me against them. I ask them to borrow a cord.

**III. Of the objection from religion.** It is said that I set up the trial against the priesthood, since the teachers of the [[mov:57|E'eshio]] hold that truth is kept by an ordained priesthood and passed down in order. I answer that I have never put a cord to the One Power, nor weighed the wheel on which all things turn. The priesthood keeps the truths of the soul and of release from suffering; I have meddled with neither. The old gods, the teachers say, are servants or shadows of the One Power. I have only asked the servants how they go about their work. If that is impiety, the servants have not complained.

**IV. Of the objection from my sex.** I will not answer it. The cord does not know who holds it.

**V. Of utility.** It is asked what profit there is in a swinging weight. I reply that a swing of known length keeps time better than the water-clock, and that a city which keeps better time keeps better accounts. Since the [[evt:44594|Treaty of Lishimti]] opened new trade to our merchants, the counting-houses of Nemeth have weighed more silver in a month than they once weighed in a year; any merchant of the city would pay a [[cur:1032|Ta]] for a clock that kept true. And now that the kingdom is again at war (the [[evt:44661|Tsey–Ool War]] being the talk of every tavern) your correspondents may ask the gunners whether they would rather know or guess how a heavy body moves.

**VI. Conclusion.** I am told that the learned of other nations have begun to speak of an [[evt:44623|Invention of experimental science in Tsey]], and to attach my name to it. I accept the credit only on this condition: that it be understood that what was invented is not a body of knowledge but a habit of mind, which anyone may acquire for the price of a cord and a little patience. I commend it to my critics, and to the long reign of His Majesty [[per:43961|Mezhash]], under whom, by the favour of the One Power, this kingdom has had leisure enough to swing weights in its stairwells.

I am, Sir, your obedient servant,

**AE NIEM**
