*Verses found nailed to the door of the house of the priests in Ölie, in the year 3188 of the Myitiyishiu. Copied before they were torn down.*

**I.**

Hear, good people of [[pol:1371|Tomzel]], a song without a harp,  
for the harpers are all at court, and sing what they are paid.  
I sing of our lord [[per:34201|Sitlyitlilyian]], whose name is "Bright Smoke";  
and well was he named, for smoke is what he made.

**II.**

When good [[per:34018|Une]] his father went the way of all the kings,  
the boy was set upon the throne, and fourteen years he had.  
The priests anointed him with oil and bade him rule with measure;  
the oil ran down his forehead, and the measure ran down with it.

**III.**

He went to [[evt:34812|Bakh]] with banners, and he took the town by storm,  
and set the house of writings there ablaze from roof to floor.  
A thousand years of wisdom went up in the smoke at Bakh —  
and Bright Smoke stood and warmed his hands, and called the fire glory.  
He brought home land by [[evt:34813|treaty]], and the land was good, we grant it;  
but who will give us back the books? Not he, nor all his heralds.

**IV.**

Then he was hungry once again, and looked toward the land of Tsey,  
and the [[evt:34869|war of Tsey]] was seven years, and seven years of levies.  
At [[evt:34894|Likhtli]] went our sons, more than three to every Tsey man,  
and twenty thousand did not come home. Where was Bright Smoke? At home.

**V.**

At [[evt:34913|Iazhash]] he rode out at last, when the enemy was weary,  
and three to one he had them there, and so he won the day.  
Now he has it painted on the wall of the great hall at [[set:264|Ölie]]:  
the king upon a white horse. The painter left out the general.

**VI.**

For it was the general, not the king, at [[evt:34928|Ashmath]] in the mud,  
at Ashmath twice, at Ashmath thrice, who bled and won and bled.  
The king came down to [[evt:34954|Nimid]] when the walls were nearly broken,  
and wrote his name upon [[evt:34955|the peace]], in letters large as a door.

**VII.**

The holy priests of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] teach that the wise grow lean,  
that justice is the measure kept, and all things in their place.  
The king has kept no measure but the measure of his table;  
the only thing he has kept lean is the purse of the ploughman.

**VIII.**

The steward comes at harvest with his scales and with his ledger,  
and takes the tenth for the war, and the tenth for the king's new hall,  
and a tenth for the victory feast, and a tenth for the painter,  
and leaves the ploughman his [[cur:749|sozhne]] — one sozhne, to buy a candle.

**IX.**

Yet hear me, I am no rebel; I hold my field of my lord,  
and I will pay him what is owed, as my fathers paid his fathers.  
I pray the gods to keep him, every god in his due place.  
I only pray the gods to teach him that smoke is not a fire.

**X.**

And now there is a little prince, [[per:34689|Minasilyian]],  
three summers old, who plays with wooden soldiers in the garden.  
The gods grant the boy learn from his tutors, and not from his father;  
the gods grant he be a little flame, and not a little smoke.

*Envoi.*

Tear this down, good steward; I have written it again.  
The priests have parchment, and the people have long memories.
