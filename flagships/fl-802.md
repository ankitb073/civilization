To my father, from your son in the fort at [[set:6|Jenontono]], in the twenty-second year of the queen [[per:20661|Rathtothsheth]]. May [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] of the grain fill your barn. May the lady of war look the other way from our house.

I am well. They have not sent us to the great war. The host went with the queen against the Nebe, and we were left behind to keep the old frontier in [[reg:9|Tobejenga]], twenty of us old men and boys on a hill, with one mule and a cracked cistern.

News came up the road ten days ago. The queen has won at Binge, at the [[evt:21014|Battle of Binge]], and the enemy's captain is dead. The priests sacrificed a ram for it and we were given beer. But the men who brought the news said the Nebe dead lay in the barley for a mile, and our own came home in a few wagons under cloths. A victory, they say. The ravens agree.

Father, you never told me how much of this country is grandfather's war. Now I know, because the old men of the fort tell it every night, whether we ask or not. Forty years ago and more, in the [[evt:20604|Tallen–Rerol War]], our army came here and fought the men of [[pol:805|Rerol]] under this very hill. They say the slope was so thick with the fallen that the grass did not grow for three years. Then the next summer at [[set:160|Nonolnga]], where grandfather fell. Then the last battle, and the [[evt:20667|Treaty of Pinpidhzhi]], and this hill and this fort and the fields below became ours. The old men say it as if it happened to them. Perhaps it did.

And then you went yourself, when you were young, in the [[evt:20765|Fifth Tuvizhinge Trade War]], and came home with the scar on your hand that you said was from a sickle. The sergeant laughed when I told him about the sickle.

The people of the villages here still talk of Rerol among themselves, and stop when we come into the yard. They sell us eggs and onions and they are polite. They are very polite. I do not think they love us.

There are omens. Last week the augur watched the crows over the pass and said they flew to the left, which is bad, and then he watched them again and said they had flown to the right after all, which is good. We have a careful augur.

Grain is one [[cur:417|Te]] and a third the measure in the market below the fort, which the cook says is robbery, and the cook knows robbery. My pay is enough. I send you two te with the carter, and a good knife I bought from a man of Nonolnga. Do not give it to my brother.

Tell my mother I eat, and I sleep, and I keep my feet dry. Tell her I have not seen a battle and do not expect to see one on this hill. Tell her the grass grows well here now.

Your son, on the old frontier.
