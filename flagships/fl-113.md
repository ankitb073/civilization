To the overseer of the merchants in [[set:3|Eshueethie]], and to the heads of the trading houses who sit with him at the weighing-place, say: thus says [[per:17680|Mashe Ada]], queen of [[pol:755|Tomözel]], who holds the scales of the land.

Is it well with you? With me it is not well. With the land it is not well. I do not write to you of profit this year. I write to you of the dead.

Hear how the year went. In the spring the gods gave us victory. At [[evt:18012|Ivu]], in the year before, my general broke the men of Pibetie. At [[evt:18020|Nyinsitlasa]] I myself went out, and the gods put the enemy in my hand, and at [[set:123|Nyinsitlasa]] the lords of Pibetie set their seals to [[evt:18021|the treaty]] and gave me the land I asked for. The [[evt:17972|war with Pibetie]] was ended. I said: now the roads are open. Now the caravans will go out. Now the silver will come in.

The gods gave with the right hand. With the left hand they took.

In the month of the barley harvest [[evt:17909|the white sickness]] came out of [[set:15|Miezenitie]]. It came first upon the men of the river boats. They grew hot; then cold; then the white sores came on their skin, and on the third day or the fourth they died. Who brought it? The traders of Miezenitie, who hid it, and sold their goods in the market while their brothers lay dying in the back room. That is my judgement on them, and let the gods judge them also.

From Miezenitie it went down the river roads, as goods go. It went to [[set:2|Mieshlethie]]. It went to [[set:110|Onetie]] and to [[set:130|Fiwi]]. It went to [[set:174|Faamni]] and to [[set:7|Ana]]. It went to Nyinsitlasa, where my army had camped, and my soldiers who had come through the battle without a wound died in their tents. It went even to Pibetie, to [[set:91|Pibetie]] of my enemies; let no one say the gods spared them to punish me. And now it is here, in Eshueethie, in the street of the potters, and in the street of the dyers, and the drums of the dead are beaten every night.

Now hear what you shall do.

Close the river gate to boats from the north. Let no bale come in that has not stood seven days on the far bank in the sun. Let no merchant of Eshueethie go out to the cities that are struck. Let the caravan that is ready for the south wait. I know what it costs you. I know that the cloth rots in the storehouse and the debts fall due. Write down every debt that falls due this year, and I will hold it over until the sickness passes; no man shall be sold for a debt in the year of the white sickness. This I swear by the gods of [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]].

Do not raise the price of grain to the poor. The priests teach that the wise withdraw from appetite, and I tell you: a merchant who grows fat in the year of the white sickness, the gods will remember him, and so will I. Sell at the old weight. If you lose, I will make it good from the house of the queen, in [[cur:403|loda]] of full weight, when the roads open.

And send me word, each new moon, of the houses. Which house has lost its head. Which house has no one left to keep its book. Their goods shall not be stolen. Their goods shall be sealed with my seal and kept for whoever lives.

I have made offerings at every altar. I have poured out oil and wine and given the best of the spoil of Pibetie to the priests. The gods do not answer. Perhaps they are angry that I went to war. Perhaps they are angry with Miezenitie and strike us for being its neighbour. I do not know. I am the queen, and I do not know.

Keep the scales honest. Keep the gates shut. Keep the lamps burning in the house of the gods.

Written in the 23rd year of Mashe Ada, in the palace, with the doors closed.
