*Here are written the words that the lord king [[per:29364|Lafmodhalen]] spoke before the altar at [[set:265|Mono]], when the king of Mono and his lords knelt to swear the peace, as they were taken down by the clerks of the chapel royal and fair-copied by the king's command, in the year 4124 from the founding of Lienonoshal.*

In the name of [[dei:16|Duemamnol]], lord of the high sky, who sets kings upon their seats and casts them down; in the name of [[dei:14|Buevme]], lady of the sword, who goes before the host of the righteous; and in the name of all the gods of [[mov:2|Zhienro]], each in their place and each with their due: hear me.

Lords of [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]], my vassals and companions; lords of [[pol:1232|Mono]], who were our enemies until this hour; priests, captains, and people: give ear.

It is written in the old songs that the wheel turns, and that what ends will begin again. Five years ago the men of Mono shut the roads of Tunnge against our merchants, and laid tolls upon our wool and our wine, as if the roads of the earth were theirs and not the gods'. That was the beginning of the [[evt:29866|19th Tunnge Trade War]]. Today, by the will of heaven, it ends.

Remember how it went. At Virnadhu I myself led the host, and the field was ours, and the gods showed plainly whose cause they favoured. At the [[evt:29883|Battle of Nidhinge]] my captains met the host of Mono, and the men of Mono fell before them as the barley falls before the sickle. At the [[evt:29895|Battle of Jerathnu]] their king himself, [[per:29276|Batathren]], who kneels before me now, came out against us, and he turned his back, and fled, and his men lay so thick upon the field that the crows could not eat them all. And at Nidhinge again, the [[evt:29906|Battle of Nidhinge]] of this year, they were broken a second time upon the same ground, as if Buevme would teach them the lesson twice. Then we sat down about their city, and they learned hunger as they had taught it to our merchants.

I do not boast. The victory is not mine but the gods'. Prosperity is a sign of divine favour, as the priests teach; and who can look upon this day and say the gods have not shown their favour?

Hear now the peace, and let each man remember what he swears.

The king of Mono yields to the crown of Lienonoshal seventy-one districts, with their towns and fields and the oaths of those who dwell in them; and Nidhinge, where his men fell twice, is among them, and Anavun also.

He pays into our treasury silver to the weight of some eight million [[cur:625|mava]], to make good the losses of our merchants and the blood of our soldiers.

He opens his markets to the merchants of Lienonoshal, and lays upon them no toll that he does not lay upon his own.

And there shall be peace between our houses for fifteen years, sworn upon the altar of the gods.

Batathren, you have sworn. I accept your oath, as a lord accepts the homage of one who has erred and repented. Keep it, and you will find me a just neighbour. Break it, and you will find me what you found me at Jerathnu.

And you, my lords: remember that peace, too, is a field to be held. Each of you holds his lands of me as I hold the crown of the gods. Let each give his due in his place, and render his service when it is asked, and the realm will stand as the hills stand.

To my son [[per:29468|Tuemagunen]], who stands at my right hand, I say before you all: mark this day. What the father wins by the sword, the son must keep by justice. A conquest that is not ruled well is only a sack that lasts longer.

Now let the priests of [[dei:18|Eba]] light the fire upon the altar, and let [[dei:17|Niedhekhte]] bless the fields that change hands this day, and let [[dei:15|Bienva]] receive the dead of both peoples into one earth. And let the bells be rung in [[set:18|Lienonoshal]], that the city may know the war is ended and the king has kept his word to it.

*Here end the words of the king at Mono.*
