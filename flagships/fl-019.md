**I.**

Weep, [[dei:4|Lashlae]], lamp of the night, weep over the fields of [[reg:5|Yiotl]].  
Weep, [[dei:3|Nielei]], river of the plain, carry the weeping down to the sea.  
The sons of [[pol:612|Pibetie]] went out, and the sons of Pibetie did not come back.  
The mothers of Pibetie stand at the gate, and the road before the gate is empty.  

**II.**

In the days of the fathers we went against [[pol:649|Eshueethie]].  
In [[evt:16169|the first war]] the god of war hid his face, and the fathers came home few.  
The old men remembered it. The old men said: do not go.  
But when [[evt:16849|Toneziwikhizimi broke away]] from the house of Eshueethie,  
the priests opened the ram and read the liver, and the liver said: the jar is cracked.  
Then [[per:16816|Iwshashmovi]], shepherd of Pibetie, took up the spear of [[dei:5|Nieshashta]].  
So began [[evt:16920|the second war against Eshueethie]], and its end was weeping.  

**III.**

At [[set:126|Sitlyitlinuise]] the king went out with twenty-three thousand.  
Eshueethie came out with thirty-three thousand, like locusts on the green barley.  
The sun stood still to see it. The dust rose and hid the face of Etlie.  
Nine thousand of ours went down; who can count them? I cannot count them.  
Weep for the spearmen. Weep for the bowmen. Weep for the boys who carried water.  
Their captain [[per:16673|Tlamfen Ibe]] fell there also, by the spears of Pibetie.  
Let Eshueethie weep for him. We have no tears to lend.  
That was [[evt:16956|the day of Sitlyitlinuise]]: the field was theirs, the grief was ours.  

**IV.**

At [[evt:16960|Faamni]] [[per:16886|Nitllo Muea]] held the line from dawn until dusk.  
Twice as many of ours as of theirs lay down in the furrows of Faamni.  
And the field was theirs, and we went back across the river.  
O Faamni, where the dead of two peoples sleep side by side,  
let Muevase the earth be gentle with them; let her not ask whose sons they were.  

**V.**

At [[evt:16965|Tamesitsiyi]] [[per:16889|Setlietliluesha]] led out the last of the young men.  
They were fewer than the enemy. They knew it, and they went.  
Six thousand went down at Tamesitsiyi. The reeds were red to the water's edge.  
Setlietliluesha too has gone down since into the earth, full of years and full of sorrow.  
Then the king sat at Tamesitsiyi and set his seal on [[evt:16966|the tablet of Tamesitsiyi]].  
The silver of Pibetie went across the river as tribute.  
For ten years the spears were bound with cord, and the granaries were opened to strangers.  
Yiotl we did not take. Yiotl we never took.  

**VI.**

Now hear the last part of the lament, for the lament is not finished.  
In the three hundred and thirty-fifth year of Pibetie I sing it, and the house is broken.  
[[evt:17152|The house of Pibetie is broken]] in four, like a loaf at a funeral meal.  
Tamesitsiyi, where the king bowed, sits now under its own lord.  
Mieshlethie has its own lord. I'a'ie'i has its own lord.  
And [[set:91|Pibetie]] the city is Pibetie alone, a mother with no children at her knee.  
Was it for this the sons went down at Sitlyitlinuise?  
Was it for this the reeds were red at Tamesitsiyi?  
The great wheel turns. What ends will begin again. So the priests say. So the priests have always said.  

**VII.**

Pour out the water, pour out the oil, pour out the barley-beer at the threshold.  
Call their names into the dark, though the dark does not answer.  
Lashlae, moon of the night, you saw them fall; remember them.  
Wewtsi, keeper of wisdom, you knew the end; why did you not speak?  
Nielei, river of the plain, carry the weeping down to the sea.  
The sons of Pibetie went out, and the sons of Pibetie did not come back.
