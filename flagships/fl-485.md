*[Obverse. Words of the king, spoken in the court of the house of the gods before the elders of the clans, set in the clay at his command.]*

Thus says [[per:180|Cui'dilli'an]], king in [[set:13|Tivintusa]], beloved of [[dei:83|Sada]] of the high sky, beloved of [[dei:85|Nil'i]] of the river, shepherd of the people, keeper of the weights.

Elders of the clans, hear me. Men of the river-quarter, men of the hill-quarter, men of the reed-quarter, hear me. You have spoken against the new order at the well and at the threshing floor. Now I speak. Hear me and then speak again, if you still wish to.

In the days of our fathers, how was it? Each clan had its chief. Each chief had his own gate. Each gate had its own weights. The barley of the river-quarter was weighed with one stone, the barley of the hill-quarter with another stone. The man of the reed-quarter came to the hill-quarter to sell his fish, and he was cheated, and he went home and took a spear. The chiefs sat in council, and the council quarrelled, and the quarrel went on from the new moon to the new moon. Was it so?

*The elders answer: It was so.*

Two years ago, in the 8th year of Cui'dilli'an, I went up to the house of the gods. I gave the bull to Sada. I gave the barley to [[dei:86|Shu'i]] of the grain. I gave the white kid to [[dei:84|Lashli'i]] of the night. I asked: "Shall Tivintusa be many, or shall Tivintusa be one?" The priests of [[mov:13|Unsatlmum]] opened the liver. The priests said: "One."

So it was done. This was the [[evt:213|City state Reform in Tivintusa]]. Hear what it is.

One wall, around all the quarters. Not a wall for each clan; one wall.  
One gate of judgment, where the king's judges sit. Not a gate for each chief; one gate.  
One stone for the weighing of barley; one stone for the weighing of silver.  
The [[cur:3|Awa]] is weighed in the market of the river-quarter as it is weighed in the market of the hill-quarter.  
One house of the gods, where the priests of all the clans pour together.  
One tally of the tax, kept by the scribes of the king.  

And the chiefs? The chiefs are not cast down. The chiefs sit at the gate of judgment as elders. The chief who judged his clan now judges the city. Is that less? It is more.

You say: "The old way was the way of our fathers." I say: our fathers did their duty in their day; I do my duty in mine. The gods each have their place and their due. Sada has the sky; Nil'i has the river; [[dei:87|Sutlpi]] has the place below. They do not quarrel over the boundary-stones. Shall the clans of [[pol:13|Tivintusa]] quarrel where the gods do not?

You say: "Show us that the gods are pleased." Look, then.

Two harvests have come in since the one wall was raised. Two harvests full. The granary of the city is full. The river did not rise over its banks; Nil'i was gentle. The fishers of the reed-quarter sell in the hill-quarter, and no one is cheated, for the stone is one stone. No spear has been taken up between the quarters in two years. Is this not prosperity? And prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour.

*The elders answer: It is the sign.*

Therefore let the one wall stand. Let the one gate judge. Let the one stone weigh. Let no man say "my clan" at the gate of judgment; let him say "my city." Whoever takes up a spear against the one city, let [[dei:88|Mastu]] of the sun withdraw her light from his field.

> Spoken in the 10th year of Cui'dilli'an, king in Tivintusa, in the court of the house of the gods.
