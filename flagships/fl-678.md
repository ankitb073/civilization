A translator first meets [[per:43460|Baelmaf]] as a question of spelling. In Nahuvdhi's versions of the chronicles of [[pol:1755|Tsey]] he is Baelmaf, a name meaning *wise snake*. In Navaavun's version of the account kept by his enemies in Manki he is Bayilumaf. The doubling is a fair place to start, because the two peoples who wrote about him used him for opposite purposes.

The record of his life is spare. He was born in AY 4521, a man of the Unsatlmum people, and died a natural death in 4578, aged 57. It names no parents, wife or children, and is silent on his birthplace, his rise and his death. What it keeps in full are thirteen battles, with every army's size, the dead on both sides and the victor. Of the more than thirteen thousand commanders the record names, only fourteen led armies in thirteen battles or more. His king, [[per:43372|Esh Mua]], born a year after him, had reigned at [[set:76|Nimid]] since 4538, when he was sixteen; he ruled for sixty-one years and is remembered as the Victorious. Of the nineteen battles Tsey fought in that reign, Baelmaf commanded thirteen.

## Three summers against Khyie

He first appears as a general in 4547, aged 26, in the [[evt:43927|First Tsey–Khyie War]], which Tsey waged to take land from [[pol:1856|Khyie]], a kingdom then five years old. At the [[evt:43956|Battle of Khash (AY 4547)]] he faced Khyie's king, [[per:43423|Lyie]], with 28,894 men against 22,870, and won: 1,848 of his men died, 3,866 of theirs. At the [[evt:43967|Battle of Khash (AY 4548)]] he beat [[per:43470|Tozhu]] by a thin margin, 901 dead to 1,205. Then, at the [[evt:43974|Battle of Ashash (AY 4549)]], he had the smaller army for the only time in his career, 21,543 against 22,811, and won the most lopsided victory of the war: 1,881 dead to 7,487.

Its two historians wrote mirror images. The Tsey historian A, writing at Nimid in 4554, has Lyie "swollen with pride" coveting Tsey's lands; Fispemamshi of Khyie, writing at Kheye in 4551, gives the same words to Esh Mua. A names Baelmaf in all three battles: at Khash he "brought up the guns at the decisive moment, and heaven was with us"; at Ashash he "turned their flank at noon". But every enemy host in A is almost exactly three times the record's figure ("Liee had gathered 68,362 men there", against 22,870), and Tsey's own dead are cut. Fispemamshi never names Baelmaf. His Khyie always withdraws for some reason other than defeat, "when the allies failed to come" or "through the failure of the supply trains". At Khash in 4548 he has Khyie "outnumbered three to one"; the odds were about three to two.

The last battle, at Khash in 4550, was won by another general, Yethmoga; the record does not say why. By the Treaty of Khash, Khyie gave up thirteen districts, among them the town of Tivnu, paid an indemnity and swore a twenty-year truce. A made it "21 districts in all".

## Khash, and the price of trade

In the [[evt:44048|27th Mikhukhizhi Trade War]] Tsey turned on the [[pol:1865|Hinidhiyidhdiy]] Empire, one of the states left when the Binge Empire broke apart in 4553. In 4558, near Khash again, Baelmaf met [[per:43574|Bilidhin]], 36,567 men against 35,085: the closest to an even fight he ever had, and the bloodiest of his victories. 6,351 of his men died, and 12,286 of the enemy. The two sides' historians agree on almost nothing but a number. Kaesh of Tsey wrote: "Of the enemy 22,349 were slain; of ours, 3,604." Diviyin of Hinidhiyidhdiy, whose chronicle survives only in fragments, wrote: "6,485 of ours were lost, but 21,636 of the enemy." Each gave his side about twenty-two thousand kills. Bilidhin died the next year at Nivanga, fighting another enemy, Bene. By the Treaty of Ontiedzey in 4559 the empire ceded eleven districts, Khash and Lunge among them, with an indemnity and trading rights. Diviyin called them "villages of no account".

At Ometh the merchant Netllo Ash wrote in her house's ledger for 4559: "The roads are unsafe because of the 27th Mikhukhizhi Trade War."

## The holy war

The war that made his name, and nearly unmade it, was the [[evt:44159|Holy War of Tsey against Manki]] (4565–4576), fought for Tsey's state faith, [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], whose message, it teaches, is for all peoples. [[pol:1864|Manki]], another heir of Binge, was twelve years old and kept the Veshethmekh faith. Baelmaf commanded nine of the war's ten battles; Esh Mua fought the tenth himself, at Akhtse in 4574.

It began badly. At the [[evt:44176|Battle of Ene (AY 4566)]] he brought 50,778 men against 27,610 and won, yet lost more men than the side he beat, 2,084 to 1,724. Then he met [[per:43554|Vaamaagunin]] in earnest. Near Ometh in 4568 he lost 3,343 men to 859. At the [[evt:44189|Battle of Ometh (AY 4569)]], with 42,752 men against 26,622, he lost 19,311 to 5,189. At the [[evt:44231|Battle of Ene (AY 4571)]], with 52,883, the largest army he ever led, against 21,517, he lost 18,332. In the 47 battles the record gives Tsey before 4569, its worst day had cost 7,336 dead. Each of these two cost more than twice that.

Shozheniem's Tsey account, printed at Nimid in 4621 by a man born six years after Baelmaf died, names him in each of his six victories in the war and in none of his three defeats. Of Ometh in 4569 it says the host, "outnumbered three to one", fell back to the walls: "It cost us 9,697 men, and them 11,760." In fact Tsey had the larger army, by some sixteen thousand. Ene in 4571 is put down to "the blunders of the general staff". Ngivuthden of Manki, writing at Tivintukhe in 4577, does the reverse. He names Bayilumaf only in the three battles Manki won, each time with a host about three times its true size: "Bayilumaf came against us at Kukhmith with 127,962 men." Manki's defeats have no Tsey general in them. The court annals of Tsey, kept by Emanme, never name him at all: "The king took the field against Mane."

The record places the battles of Dainagafu in 4570 and of Ometh in 4572 on land Tsey had held since its founding in 4331; Shozheniem still has Baelmaf in 4572 cross "into the enemy's country". Yet he recovered. He beat [[per:43678|Tubumen]] at Dainagafu and Vaamaagunin near Ometh. At the [[evt:44240|Battle of Lunge (AY 4573)]] he beat Vaamaagunin again, and the old general, 63, died on the field. They had met six times, and each had won three. Ngivuthden wrote: "There fell Vaamaagünin, whose name shall be remembered." In 4575 Baelmaf beat [[per:43694|Tutafbafin]] at Tivintukhe, and in 4576 Tubumen again at Akhtse. That year the royal city fell. Ngivuthden ended: "Let this record keep what the sword has taken."

## The reckoning

Add up the record and the general looks like neither chronicle's. He won ten battles and lost three. Under his command 59,352 men of Tsey died, and 55,731 of their enemies: he lost more men than he killed. His ten victories cost 18,366 lives, his three defeats 40,986. Of Tsey's 48,852 dead in the holy war, more than three in four fell at Ometh in 4569 and Ene in 4571.

Yet Tsey won all three wars. Their treaties moved fifty-nine districts: thirteen from Khyie, eleven from Hinidhiyidhdiy and all thirty-five of Manki, which Shozheniem made "68 districts". Tsey counted 71 districts and 5.2 million people at the end of the 4540s, and 142 districts and 11.5 million at the end of the 4570s. The faith spread more slowly: of Manki's thirty-five districts, twenty-nine still kept Veshethmekh when Baelmaf died, and twenty-four in 4650. Money moved faster. Tivnu, taken in his first war, was by 4564 the seat of the Nyimiu Bank, whose keeper Baalash noted in 4568: "Insurance on goods doubled for the Holy War of Tsey against Manki."

Baelmaf died in 4578, two years after Manki fell. The epithet went to the king. When Esh Mua swallowed Khyie in 4587, Lyie, the first king Baelmaf had beaten, was executed. The record keeps nothing of Baelmaf's thoughts, only the thirteen days on which he mattered to the state. Read against the chronicles, they show a commander who could win outnumbered, who twice led great armies to slaughter and was allowed to go on, and a country whose historians wrote his name only on the days he won.
