Everything the record says of [[per:16769|Onatdan]] would fit on a single tablet. He was born in AY 2067, a Jego man of the [[pol:664|Yeda]] Empire. In AY 2100 he founded a merchant house at Puthnimdu that dealt in timber, bronze, olive oil and spices. He had one recorded son. He died a natural death in 2121, aged fifty-four. No letter, seal or account of his survives. What earns Onatdan a chapter is what outlived him. The [[hou:382|House of Shemnal]] lasted 245 years. Of the 2,505 houses, banks and companies the record knows, only four lasted longer, and all four were founded more than a thousand years after his.

## A child of a beaten kingdom

He was born into a defeat. In 2067 the army of Yeda lost the Battle of Linidhagshal to Yennga, 7,630 of its men falling against 1,926 of the enemy's. In 2068 it lost again at Nilu and sued for peace. The Treaty of Nilu, in the Yennga scribe Valshethdolen's copy (PAL·TR·01132), states its first term bluntly: "Yeda shall pay 2,207 talents of silver by the Kerel standard to Yennga, before the next new year." The [[cur:364|Kerel]], 8.5 grams of silver, was the money of his whole life; its weight held from 1993 to the fall of Yeda.

Of his parents, his childhood and his training the record is silent. His name means *brave just*; whether he was either, it cannot say. His son [[per:16992|Vanofmalen]] was born in 2088, when Onatdan was twenty-one, to a mother the record does not name. By then [[per:16651|Ololomen V]] was king, and would fight three wars in a reign of thirty-one years. The state faith, [[mov:2|Jinru]], taught that prosperity was a sign of divine favour, a doctrine a merchant could live with.

## The year of the house

The [[evt:16822|Founding of the House of Shemnal]] came in 2100, when Onatdan was thirty-three. It was a year of victory. The [[evt:16791|Yeda–Nüvi War]] ended with the Treaty of Zhiyiyzhikhti, by which Nüvi ceded fourteen districts and paid an indemnity. The court made the most of it. Ololomen's victory stele claims 1,200 enemy dead in the last battle (PAL·IN·01220). The palace chronicle, written forty-one years later, counts 617 and swells the cession to "30 districts in all" (PAL·CH·03111). The record gives 295 dead. Whether Onatdan's venture answered the peace or only shared its year, nothing says.

[[set:90|Puthnimdu]] was an inland town of some 11,600 people. Its excavator found no destruction layer: it seems never to have been sacked. Its roads ran to [[set:0|Taulin]], the capital; to the port of Nin; to Baaliurinu; and over the border to Zhinontunu in the Kingdom of Jenontono, with which Yeda fought no war in Onatdan's lifetime. These are the partners the record gives his house; the roads to Taulin and Zhinontunu were the busiest.

The town's price lists show his trade, if not his books. In the 2090s a beam of timber cost about 0.43 Kerel there, a mina of bronze 1.70, a jar of oil 1.07 and a mina of spices 7.4, when a measure of grain cost 1.25. In the decade that opened in 2100 every one of his goods stood dearer, spices at nearly 10 Kerel. In the decade from 2110 they fell back; in the 2120s they rose again. Both dear decades opened in years of war. The lists do not say why.

For the inside of such a business we must borrow a neighbour's books. The House of Evey had been founded at Puthnimdu nine years before Shemnal, and it too dealt in timber and bronze. Its day-book of AY 2113 (PAL·LE·00523), damaged by fire at the edges, was dug up in 4922 and translated by Nami. It begins: "Accounts of the house of Evey, kept at Potnemdo by Belononen, in the 31st year of Ololomen [AY 2113]. Sums in Kerel. Opening balance 4,790." If Onatdan kept such a book, it has not been found.

## The last years

The [[evt:16890|Yeda–Jego War]] of 2106–2109, against a short-lived rebel kingdom bearing the name of Onatdan's own people, ended without a settlement. Ololomen died in 2114. His son Rondhan, aged twenty, opened the [[evt:16990|Second Yeda–Tiy War]] two years later. At Zhiyiyzhikhti in 2117 Rondhan won but lost more men than the enemy, 1,304 to 1,007. At Khungumnu in 2120 the Tiy lost 6,522 to Yeda's 1,632, and by the Treaty of Nimid they gave up twenty-three districts and promised tribute for twenty-seven years.

Onatdan died in 2121, the year after the peace. Vanofmalen took the house at thirty-three, the age at which his father had founded it.

## Seven generations

The rest is the house's life, but it is Onatdan's only monument. Vanofmalen led it for forty-seven years, until his death at eighty in 2168. Then came Belramadhen, the grandson born ten years after Onatdan died. After him came Lodemenen, Balsheykolvan, Kolnathben, Kosheshoyath, a woman, and her daughter Lotakhshalath, each the child of the head before. Yeda collapsed in 2199. Puthnimdu passed to the Tallen Empire, and the house went on. None of the sixteen other houses founded anywhere between 2090 and 2109, Evey among them, lasted past 2223.

The one Shemnal tablet we have is a day-book of AY 2300 (PAL·LE·00531), complete but for minor losses. Lotakhshalath, Onatdan's descendant in the seventh generation, kept it: "Accounts of the house of Shemnal, kept at Potnemdo by Lotakhshalath, in the 58th year of Balsheyramadhen [AY 2300]. Sums in Te. Opening balance 4,440." The Kerel was gone; the formula was not. Lotakhshalath died in 2345, and the house was dissolved the same year. The record names no child of hers.

So we know almost nothing of the man and a good deal about what he made. The kings of Yeda left steles that inflate the dead. Onatdan left a name at the head of a list, and a house whose heads were still writing its name at the top of a page nearly two centuries after his death.
