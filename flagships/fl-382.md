*Spoken by [[per:25774|Maeshi Mish]] in the hall of audience at [[set:52|Ihaemiw]], before the council, the household and the priests, on the day the court received the faith; set down by the scribes of the hall.*

Lords of the council, priests of the old altars and priests of the new, and you of my household who stand at the back and think the king cannot see you: hear me, for I speak today not as one who gives judgement but as one who has been judged.

Our fathers taught us that the gods are many, that each has a place and a due, and that a full granary is the sign of their favour. It was a comfortable teaching. When the harvest was good, we said, "The gods love us." When the caravans came in heavy, we said, "The gods love us." When the [[cur:599|Mum]] rang true and the grain was cheap in the market, we said, "See how the gods love Ihaemiw."

I ask you now to apply that teaching honestly to the years we have lived.

In the sixth year of my reign our army went out at [[evt:26147|Iazhash]] and was broken. In that same year the enemy came through our gates; it was [[evt:26148|the fifth sack]] this city has suffered since the memory of the scribes began. Two years later I myself led the citizens out at [[evt:26171|Iazhash]], and I was beaten, and I came home to find [[evt:26172|the sixth siege]] closing about these walls. And then I sat at a table and set my name to [[evt:26173|the treaty]], and gave away fields that our grandfathers had ploughed, and paid out silver for ten years to a foreign power, and bound Ihaemiw to its will.

If a full granary is the sign of the gods' love, then by our fathers' own measure the gods hated us. I do not believe it. I do not believe that the gods we served so carefully were cruel. I believe, rather, that we were measuring with the wrong rod.

That is why I have listened to the teachers of [[mov:77|Ashahatl]]. They do not tell us that the old gods were nothing. They tell us that there is one divine power over all things, and that the old gods are its servants, or the shadows it casts upon the world, as a man's shadow falls in many shapes as the sun moves. Honour given to the master does not dishonour the servants.

They tell us also that time is not a field that lies forever in the same place, but a road. It runs from the making of the world to a final judgement, and every one of us walks it once. At the end of it no one will ask how full our granaries were. They will ask whether we kept our duty, whether we kept order in our houses, whether we offered rightly, and whether we held our wealth loosely or let it hold us.

And here, lords, I must say something you will not like. The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite; so the teachers say, and so I have come to believe. I do not ask you to go and live in caves. I ask you to remember that the silver we paid out after the treaty did not make us less than we were. It only made us poorer. A city may be poor and righteous. A city may be rich and damned.

The faith we take today is not a faith for one people only. Its message is for all peoples. Then let it be for the enemy too, who sacked us. Let it be for the villages beyond the walls. Let the priests ordained in it go out, not with swords, but with the teaching.

The greater part of the city has gone before us into this faith; the court comes last, as courts often do. Let no one say that the king led the people. The people led the king. I am content to be led into the truth.

Priests of the old altars, you are not cast out. [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]] taught our fathers duty and order and right sacrifice, and those we keep. Only the measure is changed.

Now let the new priests come forward, and let the court kneel, and I will kneel first.

*Given in the year 332 of the Ashahatl, the twenty-third year of the reign.*
