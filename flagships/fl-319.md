*[Obverse.]*

To [[dei:21|Duminu]] of the storm, to [[dei:20|Nothol]] of the fire, to all the gods of [[pol:8|Navnga]], who sit each in their place: hear me.

I am a servant of Navnga. I carried the spear in the land of [[reg:19|Mankha]]. Now my hair is white and my knees are weak. In the 22nd year of [[per:981|Kupimkun]], king of Navnga, I set these words in clay, so that my sons and the sons of my sons may know what their fathers did. Thus I speak.

Before my time the men of [[pol:13|Kivinkuthakhu]] came against Navnga and were beaten, and they swore the [[evt:640|Treaty of Tivintukhe]], and they paid silver. But the land of Mankha they kept. The fields of Mankha are black and wet, the barley there stands to a man's chest. Navnga looked at the fields of Mankha. Navnga said: "That land shall be ours."

In the days of [[per:898|Rinugoren]], who took the throne of Navnga with his own hand, the spears went out. That was the beginning of the [[evt:672|First Navnga–Tivintusa War]]. I was young. I went out.

**The first battle.** We came to Tivintukhe in the second year. Our general was [[per:917|Dyeykathren]], a hard man, a man of few words. Against us stood [[per:819|Nitsulu Kash]], king of Kivinkuthakhu, with his own guard. Nearly seven thousand were we; five thousand were they. At the [[evt:677|Battle of Tivintukhe]] we pushed and they pushed, from morning until the sun was red. Four hundred of ours lay down. Eight hundred of theirs lay down, and more. Then they gave ground. Duminu gave us the field. I took a cut on the arm. I did not feel it until night.

**The second battle.** At the [[evt:688|Battle of Ene]] the king himself led us, Rinugoren, on the right hand. Their general broke like a reed. Thirteen hundred of the men of Kivinkuthakhu lay in the barley. Of ours, fewer than two hundred. I saw the crows come down. I saw the women of Ene come out to look for their men. I did not look at them long.

Then there were years of watching. We sat in the forts. We ate millet. We counted the moons. My mother died, and I was not there to pour for her.

**The third battle.** At the [[evt:700|Battle of Tivintukhe]] the gods turned their faces from us. Why did they turn? Perhaps an offering was poorly made. Perhaps the omens were read badly. Their general held the high ground, and we went up against it, and we came down. Seven hundred of Navnga died there. My brother's son died there. I carried him back on my shield. Nothol, take him. Duminu, take him. He was a good boy.

**The fourth battle.** The next year Rinugoren went out again with a great host, nine thousand. At the [[evt:702|Battle of Ene]] we paid them back. Six hundred of theirs and more fell. The river was red to the ford.

*[Reverse.]*

**The fifth battle.** In the last year Dyeykathren led us again, and again Nitsulu Kash stood against him. At the [[evt:704|Battle of Ene]] they broke at the first push. A thousand of theirs fell; two hundred of ours. After that day there was no more host in Kivinkuthakhu.

Then we came to the walls. In the [[evt:705|Siege of Tivintukhe]] we sat down before the gate. We did not have to climb. The kings met. Rinugoren and Nitsulu Kash swore the [[evt:706|Treaty of Tivintukhe]] before the gods. [[set:64|Inikhi]] was given to Navnga, and the land of Mankha with it, and silver was promised year by year, and the spears were set down for ten years.

Thus the war ended. Thus Mankha became ours. I went home. I took a wife. I planted barley in the black earth of Mankha, the earth that I bought with my blood.

Rinugoren has gone to the gods. Dyeykathren has gone to the gods; only last year he went, an old man, the same man who went out again at the [[evt:768|Battle of Poynge]]. Many of my companions have gone. I remain. I pour water for them at every new moon.

Hear, sons of my sons: the land does not come to the lazy. It comes to those who stand in the line when the line is hard. Honour the gods, each in their place. Honour the king. Do not run.

*[Colophon.]* Written in the 22nd year of Kupimkun. Whoever breaks this tablet, may the storm break his roof.
