*To the Most Serene [[per:42203|Shithkhukhuth]], Empress of [[pol:1785|Tallin]], in whose young reign the old victories are renewed, this canto is humbly offered. Printed at [[set:0|Tallin]], 27 Ningikh 2179 of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]].*

**The Argument**

The poet, having sung in the former cantos how [[per:41815|Tupufnen]] four times led the armies of Tallin against [[set:90|Puthnimdu]], and there thrice prevailed and once was thrown back, now brings him in the last year of [[evt:42347|the war]] before the walls of [[set:6|Zhinontunu]] itself; relates the fall of [[per:41972|Bivshen]], [[evt:42404|the siege of the city]], and [[evt:42405|the peace]] by which the province of [[reg:9|Tuvizhinge]] was joined to the Empire; and concludes with a meditation upon the price of victory.

*A word to the reader.* Certain chroniclers, counting heads like tax-farmers, have written that Tallin bought Tuvizhinge too dearly, and that the dead of our side outnumbered the dead of [[pol:1786|Zhinontunu]]. I do not dispute their arithmetic; I dispute their scales. They weigh men; I weigh what men purchased. Let the reader judge, when he has read the whole, whether a province that has fed the Empire these eight and twenty years was dear.

**Canto V**

*[The first stanzas of the canto are wanting in this copy.]*

…so at the fourth return to Puthnimdu,  
where twice the eagles stooped and once fell back,  
came [[per:41967|Maabaaluthin]], in his master's stead,  
for [[per:41961|Ubaaluthin]], thrice met, came not again.  
There man for man the two hosts paid their due,  
as if one Power held the beam and would not let it lean;  
yet when the dust lay down, the field was ours.

Then said Tupufnen to the captains: "Not here  
the war is ended, but beneath their towers.  
A province is not taken in a field;  
it is taken at the gate where it is fed."  
And in the spring he struck his tents and went.

Before Zhinontunu, upon the seaward plain,  
young Bivshen waited, newly given the command,  
too brave to wait behind his walls, too young  
to know that bravery is not the same as war.  
He came out at the dawn with all his banners;  
by noon his banners lay along the ground;  
by evening he was carried in, and did not wake.

Here let the Muse of Tallin lay her trumpet down.  
The teachers of the Vishithmikh have said  
that all who suffer, suffer as one flesh,  
and that the victor's tears are not a weakness  
but the first fruits of the victory.  
Tupufnen wept for Bivshen. Let it be written.

Then round the city ran the lines of earth,  
and no man went out of Zhinontunu, and none in;  
yet he forbade the burning, and the books  
of that old city sleep unburned today.  
Within a little season came the heralds,  
and on the town's own threshold peace was sworn:  
Tuvizhinge to Tallin, gold for the widows,  
and twenty years of quiet on the border.

*[A lacuna of uncertain length.]*

…and now, O Empress, in your morning years,  
the sons of those who fought at Puthnimdu  
have stood at [[evt:42669|Tiyinal]] and [[evt:42679|Dininge]] and borne the standard on;  
and this same year, [[evt:42704|the peace of Pinpidhzhi]]  
has set your seal where his was set before.  
So does the Power that draws the line of time  
from the first morning to the judgement day  
write, in each age, the same word: *Tallin*.

*[The rest of the canto is lost.]*
