title: Paired Ruin: War and Collapse in the Age of Iron
---
The Age of Iron (AY 1866–2439) opened with a catastrophe, and is usually remembered for it. Within nineteen years four great states broke apart: the [[evt:14851|Collapse of the Tomözel State]] in AY 1892, the [[evt:14987|Collapse of the Va'mi State]] in 1906, the [[evt:15030|Collapse of the Yeda State]] in 1910 and the [[evt:15052|Collapse of the Turamtu State]] in 1911. My argument is that this opening cluster was not an accident of timing but the characteristic form of collapse in the age. Iron-age states seldom fell alone. They fell in pairs and sets, bound together by the wars they fought with one another, and the victor's advantage was often short.

## The opening cluster

Begin with the pair the record links most closely. The kingdom of [[pol:466|Turamtu]] made war on the [[pol:529|Va'mi]] empire between AY 1886 and 1894, won it, and took land by treaty. Battles of that war stand among the recorded causes of both collapses. Va'mi broke in 1906; Turamtu, the victor, followed five years later, weakened further by a pestilence of 1853. The kingdom of [[pol:492|Tomözel]] had likewise spent itself in two wars, against Va'mi and against Tlayiyan, before its fall in 1892, and the [[pol:439|Yeda]] empire faced two civil wars and a war with Yennga in the years around 1890.

The successors show how much survived. Turamtu's collapse left a [[pol:670|Turamtu]] empire that lasted into the 2190s; Yeda's left a new [[pol:664|Yeda]] beside [[pol:665|Jenontono]]; Tomözel's produced the [[pol:649|Eshueethie]] empire and the city-state of [[pol:650|Fienanna]]. The map was redrawn, but ruling houses, and very often names, went on.

## Winners who lasted, and winners who did not

The next century supplies a test. Eshueethie, itself an heir of Tomözel, defeated the [[pol:555|Tlayiyan]] empire in a trade war that ended in AY 1978, and the [[evt:15974|Collapse of the Tlayiyan State]] followed in 1995, with the kingdom of [[pol:693|Ana]] and the [[pol:694|Mii]] empire among its heirs. Here, I must concede, the victor did well: Eshueethie outlived its rival by more than a century. What I am proposing is a tendency, not a law.

The second great cluster, between AY 2101 and 2137, saw the collapses of Puvu, Los, Tiy, Pibetie and Eshueethie itself within thirty-six years. The recorded causes are again overwhelmingly military, with civil war close behind. The [[evt:17060|Collapse of the Tiy State]] in 2124 followed a war with the second Yeda, in which a Tiy city was sacked in AY 2120 and its archives burned. That detail should give every historian of the age pause: some of the silences in our sources were made deliberately, by fire. The [[evt:17180|Collapse of the Eshueethie State]] in 2137 gathered almost every cause the age knew: a civil war in 2102, wars against its neighbours, a disputed succession in 2119 and a pestilence in 2121. From its ruins came, among others, a new [[pol:755|Tomözel]], so that a name extinguished in 1892 returned in 2137.

## Weighing the causes

By my count of the record, nearly every collapse of the age follows on recent war, and roughly a quarter on civil war; plague figures in about one in nine, disputed succession in very few. Neither drought nor famine appears among the recorded causes at all. That silence is worth stressing, because it is tempting to blame an age of such repeated ruin on hard seasons. The record does not support it. Iron-age states were not starved into collapse; they fought one another into it.

What defined the Age of Iron, then, was a system of rival states large enough to ruin one another and too evenly matched for any to inherit the whole. The later collapses of [[pol:637|Kazhe]] in 2216 and of [[pol:580|Yennga]] in 2221 fit the type; the first left behind, among other heirs, the kingdom of [[pol:792|Khishkhizi]], a name readers of this journal will recognise. Whether such a system could have produced a lasting peace is a question the record cannot answer, because it never did. The age's last listed collapses, of Yide in 2370, Iti in 2382 and the second Tomözel in 2412, all struck states descended from earlier collapses on this list: heirs, again, dying as heirs.
