> I write to you from the prison under the wall, by a lamp that a guard, who shall not be named here, has let me keep. I do not know how many more nights the lamp will burn.

The writer kept the old Jinru rites, which he calls by their period name, Zhenro. He was held at Taulin in AY 3008 and expected to be brought before the judges of the Veshethmekh faith when their fast was over (PAL·LT·01748, *A Letter from the Prison Under the Wall*). His letter is the right place to begin this chapter, because it gathers nearly all its themes on one leaf. He blames the edict against his faith not on the king, "a boy of nine years", but on "those who govern while he is a boy, and the teachers who govern them". He lists what the new faith had brought the city: defeat at Baaliurinu, defeat at Puthnimdu, a battle under the walls of Taulin in which "our general fell", a sack, and a treaty that signed towns away. And he notes that the same thing had been done to his people "twenty-seven years ago".

Every one of those claims can be checked, and every one holds. The kingdom of Yeda, the remnant that kept Taulin after the fall described in the last chapter, had persecuted the Jinru in 2981 and formally adopted Veshethmekh in 2984. Unusually, the record lists the conversion among the causes of the war that [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]], under its queen [[per:26185|Shiennatdath]], called the Great, began against it in 3000. Lienonoshal won every battle. At Taulin in 3004 its 41,924 men met 13,820, and the Yeda commander Baramadhen was killed. The treaty took eleven districts and two towns, and the king who sealed it, Obelshewen, was then five years old. Taulin had held about 210,000 people in 3000; a century later it held some 74,000.

This chapter is the story of how the faiths that had won courts by persuasion in the previous chapter became established churches with judges, fines and cellars. It is also the story of a world in which grain, weather and the lists of the dead still decided more than any creed.

## The fall of the house of Ane

At the start of the century [[pol:922|Ana]] was the largest state in the world, and it went on growing. Under [[per:26592|Ma'she Tlie]], called the Great, it took Toshashfin and fought a third war against Tamesitsiyi from 3011 to 3018. That war cost Ana 1,734 dead and Tamesitsiyi 4,111. *The Song of Yi'otl* (PAL·PO·00009), composed at [[set:7|Ane]] in 3009, catches the mood of the court: the long wars had bought "the furrow and the law," and the fallen had not died "for gain." Under [[per:26935|Ba'aliuye'an]] and then [[per:27078|Eshueshfoyatl]], who reigned from 3044 to 3086 and is remembered as the Last, Ana reached 25.7 million people and 416 districts in 3080, and its treasury still held nearly 1.8 million kilograms of silver.

Yet the record's measure of internal order in Ana had been falling for decades, and by the 3080s it stood at almost nothing. In 3083 Ana went to war with [[pol:1122|Eshueethie]]. At the First Battle of Edeye'a in 3084, 161,152 men of Ana met 23,647. Ana lost 39,925 and Eshueethie 5,487. It is one of the most lopsided defeats of a great army in the record to that date, and the record gives it as the sole cause of the [[evt:27950|Collapse of the Ana State]] two years later. Ana broke into six. One heir was the kingdom of [[pol:1211|Mii]]. Another was a new state seated at [[set:264|Ölye]] that called itself [[pol:1212|Tomzel]], and it would dominate the next chapter. A third, [[pol:1216|Shetie]], broke up in turn in 3103. Eshueshfoyatl outlived his state by six years.

The Yiotl inheritance that the last chapter left in dispute had been settled long before, though by a draw. The War of the Iethithi Succession ended in 3005 with a return to the old borders, a truce and a marriage. Two sources on it survive, and translating them side by side is instructive. Queen I'shil Ash of Imshi, writing from her camp in 3003 (PAL·LT·01665), says her army of "about twenty-six thousand" beat nearly twenty-seven thousand at Tanshedi, burying "one thousand nine hundred of our own" and killing "more than three thousand". The record gives 26,054 against 26,826, with Imshi losing 1,887 and Iethithi 3,017. Her numbers are the record's, rounded, her own dead included. The Iethithi account (PAL·CH·04948), written in 3061, leaves out six of the war's eleven battles. For the one at Tanshedi in 3004 it claims that the enemy brought 58,835 men and lost 11,660. The record gives 24,653 and 6,144. The historian more than doubled the army. But a queen writing to her own council, who would have to answer for the war, counted honestly, her own dead with the rest. A court historian writing fifty-six years later inflated the enemy and left out more than half the war. Nine years later, in 3014, the kingdom was refounded as the [[pol:1177|Iethithi]] Empire. Its capital was [[set:104|Yiethithi]], and it would last until 3517.

## Bread, drought and the cluster of the 3070s

The world of this chapter was slightly cool, 0.13 °C below the long-run mean on average, with sharp dips after the eruptions of Mount No'o in 3018, Mount Tam'a in 3124 and Mount Nuezho in 3211. The 3120s were nearly half a degree colder than normal. More people than ever came to live under states: 143.1 million in 3000, 159.2 million in 3120, 170.1 million in 3240. More people meant more mouths to feed when the rain failed.

It failed worst in the 3060s and 3070s. A drought settled on Daadüdhih in 3065, and in 3073 a far larger one began, the [[evt:27776|Onset of the Great Drought in Daadüdhih (AY 3073)]], covering 452 districts. It lasted until 3117. The famine of 3073, which the record ties to the earlier drought, killed 680,427 people, about one in eight of those it struck. The markets show it. At [[set:18|Linnshal]], the Lienonoshal capital, a measure of grain cost 6.6 grams of silver in the 3000s, 20.9 in the 3060s and 31.6 in the 3070s. That was nearly five times as much in a single lifetime. At [[set:92|Dambakhu]] it reached 23.3 grams in the 3060s, and at Taulin 19.6 in the 3070s.

Within those same twenty years seven states broke apart, among them these four: the [[evt:27758|Collapse of the Diebegovebenga State]] in 3067, the [[evt:27816|Collapse of the Tsiy State]] in 3073, Tambakh in 3076 and Ana in 3086. The record is careful here, and so should we be. It lists wars, treaties and secessions as the causes of these collapses, not the weather. Diebegovebenga had just lost the [[evt:27654|Yarel–Diebegovebenga War]] and been forced to pay tribute for twenty-three years. [[pol:1110|Tsiy]] had suffered the [[evt:27725|Secession of Miwimai]] in 3062. [[pol:1132|Tambakh]], once the empire of [[per:26677|Natatha]] the Convert, had suffered two risings at Fili, in 3053 and 3067. The second was led by a younger Natatha, a woman, not the emperor, and the two are easily confused. Tambakh's last ruler, [[per:27240|Nayangithi]], had come to the throne as a ward. His empire gave way in 3076 to [[pol:1206|Kambambül]], which made [[set:246|Kamvamvil]] its capital. Still, all four fell in the decades when bread at Linnshal and Dambakhu cost three to five times what it had. A historian of ordinary people cannot leave that out.

Later disasters followed the same pattern. The [[evt:28604|Onset of the Great Drought in Dyimbi (AY 3139)]] covered 545 districts, the widest of the period, though it lasted only two years. A famine in Mana in 3176 killed 446,093 people in the [[pol:1137|Yarel]] Empire, and the record names that famine among the causes of Yarel's fall in 3198, alongside its defeat by Lienonoshal in 3177–3179. In 3182 an epidemic the sources call the Scarlet Cough spread from Daadigi to eight more places and killed 608,735, three in ten of the people it reached.

## The established faiths

The new faiths of this chapter were born in hard places. [[mov:104|Vyiminambi]] began in 3001 when [[per:26840|Nadigi]] sat down to teach in the burnt market of Nzhinduh, two years after that city was sacked. *The Hymn of the Emptied Hands* (PAL·PO·00093) remembers him telling the merchants that "what the strangers carried away was never yours to keep." [[mov:107|Yeish]] was founded by [[per:27615|Khieshewiluesha]] at Nirun in 3089. Four more faiths and a schism followed by 3193, along with three schools of philosophy. The kingdom of Tunnga went over to [[mov:67|Dankhüdhil]] in 3051. The old cult of Mie'to'ieshio still held about a quarter of the land in 3120.

But this was also when the faiths that had won courts began to use them. The record lists five persecutions between 3008 and 3168, three by the Veshethmekh establishment and two by the I'ishiu. The surviving voices are those of the people persecuted, and they survived because they were hidden. *A Prayer for the Hidden Altar* (PAL·SC·00140) was found sewn into the lining of a garment. It was written at Tamesitsiyi in 3079, two years after the house of the gods was shut there, and its rubric reads: "If the lamp must be put out, say it without the lamp." *A Prayer for the Cellar, to Be Said without a Lamp* (PAL·SC·00141) comes from Peme in 3160, after a usurper's edict had the community's books "burned before the people". A second hand has added at the foot: "Copy this, and burn the copy you were given." The most moving of them is *On the Year of the Shuttered Shrines* (PAL·CH·08472), a weaver's account of the persecution at Owa in 3148. He describes the crown's officers weighing the shrine's bronze vessels in the street, an old priest who "lived three days after", and a fine of twenty Xixli that made poorer neighbours sell their looms. He refuses to curse the queen, and he offers a judgement that needs no translation: "A city is not made stronger by forcing men to lie about what they believe. It is made full of liars."

Faith could now be written into a treaty. In the [[evt:27422|Holy War of Turamtu against Eshueethie]] of 3022–3028 the kingdom of [[pol:1052|Turamtu]] fought for its gods and won. By the Treaty of Ishwyithie, Eshueethie gave up twenty-three districts in [[reg:4|Ayshi]] and Yimla and agreed to adopt the victor's faith. It was Turamtu's last triumph. At Jamladi in 3025 it had lost 10,684 men out of 50,789. The record lists that battle, together with the [[evt:27160|Secession of Nimshi]] in 3001 and the defeats at Nuidaduth described in the last chapter, among the causes of the [[evt:27520|Collapse of the Turamtu State]] in 3036, eight years after the treaty. King Nuethdan II's stele for the last battle, at Nuvun (PAL·IN·02030), claims 18,000 enemy dead. The record has 8,770, so the stele merely doubles the figure, which is modest by the standards of the age.

The established faiths also split from within. In 3114 Wemaliean of Ove excommunicated the overseers of his own Veshethmekh church because they taught that time ran like a road to a single judgement, not round like a wheel (PAL·LT·01697). His opponents named it [[evt:28458|The Totheyi Schism]]. What sets his letter apart is its last clause: whoever takes "so much as a single Khove" from a condemned overseer's house "is no member of ours." At least in his own pages, the [[mov:108|Totheyi]] asked nothing of the magistrates. Not every reformer of the age could say as much.

## Rag paper and a lighter coin

In 3154 a cord-maker in the kingdom of [[pol:1227|Shityi]] made the first paper the record knows of, although he admits that travellers had told of such leaves in other lands. He beat old flax and rope into pulp, spread it on a sieve and dried it in the sun. The record credits the invention to Eshuesimen. His own announcement, *A True Relation of the New Leaves Made from Rags* (PAL·NP·01081), spells him Ishuisimin in the Shityi of 3156, but they are one man. He may be the least boastful inventor in the archive. He writes that "a calf gives one skin," but "a vat of rags, which the poor throw into the street, will give a whole quire of my leaves in a morning," and he concedes that the leaves "do not love the damp." For a translator the finest detail is that this relation was itself written on skin, because the king's secretaries "do not yet trust my leaves with a thing that must last." Shityi had come out of the [[evt:28189|Collapse of the Shetie State]] in 3103 and fought the seventeen-year [[evt:28548|Shityi–Lishe War]] to a draw. By the 3150s it was at war again, and its paper was made, as its maker says, because "the captains also need leaves." In 3182 the crossbow was invented a third time, independently, in Kambambül.

Speech changed as steadily as writing materials. The record dates ninety-nine sound changes to the period, among them vowel raising in Haalood around 3031, voicing between vowels in the same language around 3109, and the loss of double consonants in Ngano around 3214. It also records three new languages: Üdiam and Menento, which had split from Mioim and Nekhko by 3099, and Himitlnim, which had split from Ithim by 3199. A translator learns to read such changes in the sources' spellings. Tanshedi is Tanshethi in the queen's letter, Ölye is Ölee in the Tomzel hymn, and Miuhitl is Miezhitl. None of these is a scribal error. Each records how the name was actually spoken.

Trade grew alongside. Merchants founded 199 houses in the period, and rulers introduced 46 coinages and 17 weight standards. Only one coin was debased. In 3231 the kingdom of Tadudüvi cut its silver Mbiv from 7.3 grams to 5.698, a loss of more than a fifth. The record names the causes plainly: the battle of Jyijinjivji in 3210 and the Third Sack of Khankhush in 3211, both suffered at the hands of [[pol:1078|Luthe]]. Here, at least, debasement plainly followed war.

Luthe was the giant of these years. From [[set:25|Kavu]], [[per:26504|Nakambège II]] beat off Tambakh in the [[evt:27289|Tambakh–Luthe War]] of 3011–3019 and took an indemnity. Under [[per:27700|Najithu]] the Great it reached 24.5 million people by 3120. It held 12 per cent of the land that year, more than any other state, and it peaked at 27.9 million people and 388 districts in 3210. Its armies were enormous and its enemies often tiny. At Dyizhih in 3146 it sent 144,326 men against 2,258 and lost 69 of them to the defenders' 187. In 3169 the rebels of Kabave took more than two-fifths of its districts for two years. The secession was crushed, but the record names it as the cause of Luthe's breakup in 3219. That breakup produced the [[pol:1279|Dagafu]] and [[pol:1277|Puvu]] empires, which shape the next two chapters.

## Listlitl

Of Ana's heirs, Tomzel rose fastest. Under [[per:27983|Mashe Muee II]] and then the queen [[per:28280|Lashlaliean]] it grew, and she fought Mieshlethie to a standstill in the [[evt:28819|Tomzel–Mieshlethie War]] of 3149–3163. Both queens raised victory stelae all the same. Lashlaliean's at Sase (PAL·IN·02155) claims 25,200 enemy dead where the record has 4,516. Her rival Ishuitlimi's at Myishlithie (PAL·IN·02170) claims 33,200 where the record has 6,654. Then [[pol:1172|Miuhitl]] attacked. Miuhitl had risen from the Kaddanul collapse of 3005 at [[set:235|Kadhdanul]], a collapse the record traces partly to the Long Sickness. The [[evt:29308|Miuhitl–Tomzel War]] of 3195–3214 cost the attacker 49,066 dead against 43,770. Under [[per:28871|Weme]] Tomzel won the war but gained only a truce.

His successor, the queen remembered as I the Fair, finished the work. In the [[evt:29768|First Tomzel–Miuhitl War]] of 3234–3249 her armies fought seven times before Listlitl. The *Hymn for the Victory at Listlitl* (PAL·PO·00014) sings of Miuhitl under its Tomzel name, Miezhitl, and claims that Tomzel won eight times in all and lost three. The record agrees exactly. It inflates, though, the enemy who fell before Pitelmamshe at Listlitl in 3240 to twenty thousand; the true figure is 16,311. The hymn leaves out the next year, when Pitelmamshe lost 19,089 men on the same ground to a defence that lost 5,044. In 3249 [[per:29291|I Tli]], the last queen of Miuhitl, signed the Treaty of Listlitl and gave up twenty-nine districts. Her state had no measure of internal order left.

Of the other great houses, [[pol:1137|Yarel]] had its best years under [[per:27386|Yegan I]], called the Great, and the queen [[per:28078|Komagonath]], called the Conqueror, and reached 17.2 million people in 3160 before the famine and Lienonoshal's armies broke it. Iethithi grew from under 4 million people in 3020 to 14.7 million in 3240. The queen [[per:27786|Milamaf]] held it steady for almost forty years, from 3101 to 3140, and a later queen, [[per:28814|Nilo Mish]], called the Victorious, nearly doubled its districts after 3189. Kambambül, under [[per:27486|Nalathundu I]], his namesake [[per:28426|Nalathundu II]] and [[per:29004|Nambuvethi]], called the Great, grew to 12.5 million people and 325 districts by 3240. Under [[per:28212|Vievmagonen]] and his successors Lienonoshal kept winning wars.

In the same years Lienonoshal, under the usurper Lafmodhalen, won the [[evt:29866|19th Tunnge Trade War]] against [[pol:1232|Munu]] and took seventy-one districts. Lafmodhalen's rock relief at Nidhinge claims 76,500 enemy dead; the record has 10,439. In 3240 Lienonoshal had 32.7 million people, more than any state before it. Grain at Linnshal was back to about 7 grams. Of the 750 routine documents that survive from this chapter, 418 are inscriptions, and the archive is still mostly the voice of kings. Its best pages, though, were written by cord-makers, weavers and people praying in cellars. The next chapter opens in 3250 with Lienonoshal near its peak and Miuhitl close to collapse.
