To my brother the merchant, who keeps our house in [[set:17|Pumi]], say:  
Thus says your brother, who keeps our house in [[set:0|Tallin]].

May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the high sky keep you. May [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the earth keep you. May the red sickness pass over your door as the hawk passes over the stone.

With me it is well. With my body it is well. With the house it is not well. With the city it is not well. Hear, my brother, and do as I say quickly.

Do not send the wool. Do not send the oil. Do not send the donkeys on the river road. Keep them in Pumi. Keep them in the stall. Hear me, and do not be greedy.

The sickness is in Tallin. It began here, in our own city, in the lower town by the river, after the barley harvest. First the fever. Then the red marks on the arms and the neck. Then the bloody flux. On the third day a man cannot stand. On the fifth day he goes down to the wheel. The men in the market call it the [[evt:1784|Scarlet Flux]]. The priests do not call it anything. They pour.

Hear what I have seen. The street of the potters is shut. The street of the weavers is half shut. The boat-men will not take a load. The dead are carried out at night, so the living do not count them. In our own house the old porter is dead, and the girl who ground the barley is dead, and her mother sits by the door and does not speak.

The queen, [[per:2097|Unath]], has spoken in the gate. She has said: "Let no man carry goods out of the lower town. Let the dead be burned outside the wall." The queen is in the high house. The fires of [[dei:18|Eva]] burn on the high house all night. The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] pour in order, each god in the god's own place, and they pour more than in the years of plenty. They say the gods are angry. They do not say which god.

Hear now the news of the roads. I have it from the donkey-men, who still come and go, though they should not.

The sickness has gone down the river road to [[set:1|Noni]].  
It is in [[set:56|Vuvo]]. It is in [[set:10|Navnga]].  
They say it is in [[set:18|Lenunushal]] and in [[set:6|Jinontonu]].  
They say it is in Pumi already, my brother. If it is, then may the gods forgive me for writing so late.

Hear also this. Nunulnga has stood up against Vuvo. This is the [[evt:1796|Revolt of Nunulnga]]. The men of Vuvo who beat our fathers at Taulin, who made Tallin bow its neck at the [[evt:1628|Treaty of Nin]], who have looked down on us since that day, now they have the sickness in their streets and spears in their own villages. I do not rejoice in another man's sickness. But I do not weep for Vuvo either.

Hear now what to do.

Sell what wool you have in Pumi, even at a low price. The barley is cheap now; a measure goes for less than a [[cur:19|Tunrun]] in our market, for no one buys and no one sells. When the sickness has gone, the barley will be dear, and silver in the hand will be better than wool in the store. Keep the silver under the floor. Tell no one.

Keep your wife and your sons inside. Burn cedar in the house. Pour to [[dei:19|Yenan]] the moon at each new moon. Do not go to the market at noon, when it is full.

I do not know when it will end. The priests do not know. The wheel turns; what ends will begin again. I pray only that it takes no more from this house than it has taken.

If you do not hear from me by the next new moon, the house in Tallin is yours. The tablets of the debts are in the red jar. Collect them gently.

Written in the 4th year of Unath, queen of Tallin, with a cloth over my mouth.
