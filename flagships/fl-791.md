*[Obverse.]*

Say to my wife, at the house by the fig tree, in the village of the potters: thus says your husband, of the levy of the river villages, in the camp of the queen.

May [[dei:33|Nabudzin]] of the river keep the water in our channel. May [[dei:36|Nidziktti]] of the grain fill the bin. May Tsevni of the moon light your door. I am alive. My hands are whole. My feet are whole. My eyes are whole. I say it first.

This is the 21st year of our lady [[per:1950|Ttamatsettkut]], queen of [[pol:59|Yinnga]]. This is the third summer of the [[evt:1769|Holy War of Yinnga against Tupchinga]]. You know why we went. The priests said it in the square: the men of [[pol:64|Tuptsinga]] do not give the gods their due; they dishonour the river; the land of Tuvizhinge must be cleansed. I do not know these things. I know the priest said it, and the levy-master came with the tally-stick, and I went.

Last summer we fought before [[set:155|Yinnga]], in the first [[evt:1776|Battle of Yinnge]]. Dumnutuban the general led us. The men of Tuptsinga stood on the hill and we went up the hill. Many of ours lay down on the hill. The son of the miller lay down. Our neighbour's two sons lay down, both. But we took the hill, and the men of Tuptsinga lay down twice as many, and the general said it was a victory. I did not write to you then. I could not make my hand do it.

*[Reverse.]*

This summer we fought again, in the same place, in the second [[evt:1790|Battle of Yinnge]]. Wife, hear this: the queen herself came. Ttamatsettkut came in her chariot, with the bronze on the horses and the standard of Dumnu over her. She stood up in the chariot before the whole host and said: the gods are with us; who is with the gods? And we shouted until the hills shouted back.

The men of Tuptsinga were many, more than we were, two of them for each of us. Their general Tuvanan came against us at morning. But the queen sent the chariots around by the dry stream-bed, and they came on Tuptsinga from the side, and the men of Tuptsinga broke like a dropped jar. We followed them to the river. We did not stop. Seventy-eight of ours lay down, the scribes say. Of theirs, more than a thousand. I saw the river. I will not tell you about the river.

I took nothing from the field but a bronze pin, a good one, with a bird on the head. I will bring it for you. Do not ask me whose it was.

Here barley is cheap, less than one [[cur:36|Niba]] the measure, for the quartermasters took the barns of Tuptsinga. But our pay has not come since the spring. The levy-master says it will come after the next battle. He says that after every battle.

The war is not finished. The priests say the land is not yet clean. They say Shedartsi below the earth is hungry, and we must feed her. I think she has eaten well these two summers.

Is the barley in? Did the black goat kid? Does our son still ask for me? Tell him his father carries the spear well and is coming home. Tell him I said that. Tell him I promised.

Kiss the fig tree for me. You will laugh. Do it anyway.
