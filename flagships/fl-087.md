In the 14th year of [[per:15880|Mapimtümen]], king of [[pol:661|Veni]], an omen was seen at [[set:207|Nilthabammeth]]. At midday the fire in the house of [[dei:7|Yov]] went out of itself, though no wind blew. The priests said: "It is an evil sign. The goddess is angry." On that day a priest of [[mov:1|Yakhkamu]] came out to the gate where I sat teaching, and spoke with me before the people. I, [[per:16030|Vethpemteth]], have set down our words as they were spoken, so that those who come after may judge between us.

**The priest said:** Woman, why do you sit at the gate and tell the people that the gods are shadows? The fathers taught that many gods share the world, each with a place and a due. [[dei:6|Nilkhu]] has the river, Yov has the fire, [[dei:8|Nizhügel]] has the sky. Will you take their portions from them?

**Vethpemteth said:** I take nothing from them. When the king sends his steward to gather the tribute, do you bow to the steward, or to the king who sent him? The river and the fire and the sky, and Nilkhu and Yov and Nizhügel with them, are servants of the One. I bow to the One.

**The priest said:** The fathers taught also that all things turn upon the wheel. The barley is cut and the barley springs up. What ends will begin again.

**Vethpemteth said:** The barley springs up, yes. But tell me, priest: when you buried your father, did he spring up? The year turns, but a man walks one road. And at the end of the road there is a judge, and the judge is not bought.

**The priest said:** Who taught you this? Were you ordained? Did you receive the teaching in order, from hand to hand, as we received it?

**Vethpemteth said:** No hand gave it to me. A lamp was lit in me, and I looked by its light, and I reasoned. Is the light in your own chest less holy than the lamp in your temple?

**The priest said:** Then hear me, since you love reasoning. When the offerings were full, [[dei:11|Nizhazhuda]] gave the king the field at [[evt:16018|Nivanga]]. When the offerings were short, we were broken at [[evt:16011|Khuele]] and again at [[evt:16029|Nirun]]; the enemy sat down before the royal city in [[evt:16030|the siege]], and the king set his seal to the [[evt:16031|Treaty of Hiviyu]] and gave away the fields of our fathers. This is the wheel. This is the due of the gods.

**Vethpemteth said:** Were the offerings short at Khuele? I was a young woman then, and I saw the oxen led in, fat and garlanded. They were not short. Yet the army was broken. Did the enemy, then, buy the goddess of war with fatter oxen? If a god can be bought, priest, that god is a merchant, and I will not kneel to a merchant. The One is not bought. The One asks justice in dealings and moderation in all things: honest weights and honest words.

**The priest said:** And yet you yourself say that the rich are favoured.

**Vethpemteth said:** I say that prosperity is a sign of favour, and in this we agree. But see whose house prospers. The merchant who weighs the [[cur:341|ngyiru]] honestly in the market prospers, and his sons after him. The cheat prospers for a season, and then the judge remembers him.

**The priest said:** So you would set up a new altar for Veni.

**Vethpemteth said:** Not for Veni only. The One did not make Veni alone. The men who sat before the royal city walk the same road to the same judge. I would teach them also.

**The priest said:** You would teach the enemy?

**Vethpemteth said:** I would teach the enemy, so that he is no longer an enemy. What tribute is better than that?

Then the priest rent his garment and went away to [[set:105|Pviye]] to tell the king's officers. But many of the people stayed at the gate and asked me for the teaching, and I gave it. They were the first to be called by the name of [[mov:51|Shenlamtanko]].

Written by the hand of Vethpemteth at Nilthabammeth, in the 14th year of Mapimtümen, in the year of the [[evt:16123|founding of the Shenlamtanko faith]].
