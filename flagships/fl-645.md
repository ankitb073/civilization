In AY 4910 I excavated the citadel mound of [[set:172|Tungaruikhrimu]], twenty-four destruction layers deep. Two of its inscribed finds frame this chapter. One is the sarcophagus lid of King Panufmalin, broken away below its first line: "Panufmalin, king of Tungarvikhrim, dwells in this tomb. [...]" The other is the palace chronicle of the war with Egu. Neither names the woman who governed that kingdom for eleven years between them, and no surviving document does. Of [[per:3318|Tinlamadath]] the record keeps dates, two titles, a husband and three children. This chapter asks what those bare entries can bear.

## A widow at nineteen

She was born in AY 647, a woman of the Mienibe. Her name means *golden light*. The record names neither her parents nor her birthplace. She was five when the Talenonoy Empire broke apart in 652 and the Kingdom of [[pol:121|Tungarvikhrim]] rose from it with thirty-six districts and its capital at Tungaruikhrimu.

She married [[per:3277|Dantanen I]], four years her elder, who succeeded his father Panufmalin in 661 at eighteen. In 664, at seventeen, she bore two sons, both named Dantanen: twins, it seems, though the record does not say so. A daughter, Lotyinmomath (*daughter dawn*), followed in 665.

The marriage sat inside a war. The [[evt:2937|First Tonnga–Tungarvikhrim War]] began in 663. Tonnga, the successor state that kept the old imperial capital, won in 664 and 665. In 666 Dantanen I led 821 men against 2,790 under Edonbalütin outside his own capital. He lost 209 men to the enemy's 38 and died on the field, aged twenty-three. The peace took eleven of the kingdom's thirty-six districts, bound it to pay Tonnga 140 kilograms of silver a year for eleven years, and imposed a ten-year truce. The record lists one signatory, Memat the Generous, who had become queen of Tonnga that year. No hand from Tungarvikhrim appears; perhaps there was no king to give one until 667.

## Regent for two boys

At the [[evt:2964|Accession of Dantanen II]] in 667 her elder son was three, she was twenty, and the record names her regent. That same year an unnamed poet in the old capital, Taalinnuy, now Tonnga's, wrote the Lament for Talenonoy, Mother of Lands (PAL·PO·00024). It counts the empire's breaking: "The one land became three lands. The one seat became three seats." It grants that "Tungaruishrimu sits on its own seat." Its occupant was three, and his mother held it for him.

In 673 Dantanen II died of natural causes, aged nine. His brother succeeded at the [[evt:3005|Accession of Dantanen III]], and Tinlamadath, now twenty-six, was named regent a second time. The record also gives each boy a wife, born in 669 and 666: at those ages, betrothals at most.

On her government the record is silent: no decree, letter or seal survives in her name. It keeps only the surveys. The kingdom held twenty-five districts when she became regent, and no district changed hands again until 682. It fought no battle from 667 to 678. By the end of the 660s it counted 164,992 people; by the end of the 670s, 166,902. The treasury, owing Tonnga its silver, stood at 5,362 kilograms at the end of the 660s and 5,394 a decade later; whether the tribute was paid in full the record does not say. Of these figures only the army shrank, from 1,258 men to 943, though the record's measure of internal order fell from 0.36 to 0.08 in a decade that closed with a usurpation and a defeat. The later figures postdate her regency but show what she handed on.

My colleague Nashilith, writing of the empire these kingdoms broke from, warns: "A crown held by regents for long stretches is a standing invitation to provincial notables" (PAL·SP·00053). In Tungarvikhrim that invitation stood open for eleven years, and, so far as the record shows, nobody accepted it.

## The kingdom after her

The truce ran out in 676. The palace chronicle I took from the mound (PAL·CH·00452) was written in 689 by the scribe Tathrilulrinin, and it opens there: "In the 4th year of Dantanen [AY 676], there came word that Natubimanin of Igu had gathered an army against Tungarvikhrim." It dates the news by the boy, who was twelve, not by the regent who received it. The [[evt:3031|Egu–Tungarvikhrim War]] brought no battle while he lived. In 678 Dantanen III died of natural causes at fourteen, and [[per:3414|Lallindinin]], a man of fifty from another house, seized the throne. Under the kingdom's law of male succession her daughter, thirteen that year, had no claim.

Here the record contradicts itself. It keeps her title of regent until 680, two years after her last son died and the usurper took power. Whether Lallindinin kept her on for continuity, or her office was simply never closed, it does not say.

Ruin followed. In 679 Egu beat the kingdom near its capital, and Tonnga, its old tribute run out, came back for more in the [[evt:3044|Second Tonnga–Tungarvikhrim War]]. In 680 Lallindinin met Tonnga near Tungaruikhrimu with 229 men against 6,898 and died there. Tonnga's chronicle of 703 gives him 499 men and 233 dead, and adds that "his head was brought to the queen". The record counts 60 of his men killed. By the end of the 680s the kingdom held thirteen districts and 77,871 people. In 697 the [[evt:3157|First Bäni–Tungarvikhrim War]] ended it: Bäni annexed the last nine districts and their 29,220 people.

## Golden light

Tinlamadath lived through all of it. She died of natural causes in 712, aged sixty-five, fifteen years after her kingdom ceased to exist; where she spent those years the record does not say. Her daughter outlived her by four years. No epitaph of hers has come out of the ground at Tungaruikhrimu, and I no longer expect one.

The surveys serve as one. Widowed at nineteen, she held a broken kingdom at its new size for eleven years, kept its frontier quiet, and handed on the twenty-five districts she had received. After her the kingdom lasted nineteen years and lost everything. The chronicle remembers her sons by their regnal years. The record, read patiently, shows what she did with those years.
