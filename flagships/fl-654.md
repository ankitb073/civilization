Two clay tablets dug up at [[set:151|Nudhuruigirimu]] in AY 4950 open with the same courtesy: "To Ekel Shemen, king of Keketh, my brother, say: thus says Talenornenath, queen of Nodoruegeremo, your sister." The kinship is diplomatic form. But [[per:17515|Talenornenath]] knew better than most what it cost to be a sister. One of her sisters rebelled before her and was executed. The brother who crushed that rising was the ruler she rose against, and in the end he inherited her crown.

## The second rebellion

She was born in AY 2175, the youngest of four children of [[per:17366|Shefnofmalen II]], ruler of the [[pol:580|Yennga]] Empire. Her name means 'bright rain'. Her mother, Vokhketh, died in 2177, when she was two. Of her childhood the record says nothing. Her father's empire was old and vast: a state since 1721, with 309 districts and more than nine million people by the end of the 2180s.

In 2196 her father died and her brother [[per:17512|Vellokhnen]], six years older, took the throne. A water-stained muster roll from his first year, excavated only last year (PAL·MU·00241), lists the levy he led to Yirantudhu: 39,874 men from eight towns, among them 3,102 archers from Nodoruegeremo. It ends: "The horses to be counted again at the river."

In 2205 her eldest sister, Sheshoypemteth ('mercy eagle'), proclaimed a state of 112 districts with its capital at Nudhuruigirimu. In 2211 Vellokhnen met her near Linidhagshal with 33,766 men against her 11,138 and beat her; the two signed the peace there, and she was executed that year, at thirty-nine. The Yennga palace chronicle of 2247 (PAL·CH·03310) gives 10,502 rebel dead and boasts that "197 districts were joined to the realm"; the record has 3,215 dead and 112 districts. Where Talenornenath stood in her sister's war, it does not say.

She married Tarjelen, six years her senior; the record gives no date. Their son, Ngodhan, was born in 2213 and died in 2219, aged six. In 2220 she claimed the throne of Yennga. The record does not connect the two; it tells us only that she rebelled, at forty-five, a year after her only child died.

## Bright rain on a burning city

She took her sister's capital and exactly her 112 districts. The [[evt:18052|Seventh Yennga Civil War]] had no recorded battle and ended without a settlement, because in 2221 the old Yennga collapsed. The five-hundred-year empire broke into five states, one still called Yennga, and the record names among the causes her [[evt:18051|Secession of Nodoruegeremo]], her sister's of 2205, and the battle of 2159 in which their grandfather fell. Her brother's reign ended with it. Where he spent the next eleven years, the record does not say.

After four years of silence, in 2225 she built a temple to Eba, Eva the fire god of the [[mov:2|Jinru]] pantheon, and its bronze plaque survives (PAL·IN·04754): "built this temple for Eba, her lord. May Eba protect the queen." That year [[pol:772|Turamtu]] and [[pol:773|Tunnga]], two of the kingdoms carved in 2192 from the old Turamtu Empire that had killed her grandfather, attacked, the first for tribute, the second for land. In 2226 her general Shemenen lost at Nidhinge and was killed at Diviminnge, and Tunnga sacked her capital. The record traces the next two wars, with [[pol:717|Iti]] and [[pol:758|Tungarvikhrem]], to that sack. By 2227 she was at war with four states at once.

Her letters of that year (PAL·LT·01154, PAL·LT·01155) are short and hard. To Icuil Ximin of Iti, spelled Ekel Shemen by her scribe: "Why have your soldiers crossed into my land? Withdraw them, or the gods will judge between us." To [[per:17664|Narniyen III]], whose herdsmen, she complained, grazed past the boundary stones: "Keep them on your side, or I will drive them off with spears." No reply survives.

## A general who could not win

From 2227 she took the field herself, and fared worse than her words. At the [[evt:18121|Battle of Nidhinge (AY 2227)]] she led 15,460 men against [[per:18021|Tatheluwrinin]]'s 3,835 and lost 1,799 to his 506. At the [[evt:18122|Battle of Viririnu (AY 2227)]] [[per:17678|Riwban]] of Tunnga beat her 11,417 with 5,075. Tunnga sacked her capital again, and the peace sworn there (PAL·TR·01220) took 22 districts, the town of Nidhinge and 72 talents of silver a year for twelve years, declaring: "The war which Talenurnenash stirred up is ended." The record says Tunnga began it.

In 2228 Iti's general [[per:17994|Maxi Taa]] broke her general Malon outside the capital; 4,989 of her men fell, and 1,820 of his. Iti sacked the city, the third sack in three years, and one of its soldiers scratched into a temple's plaster: "Gods of Nwidhedhiwicuidhim, forgive Minaximin" (PAL·GR·00099). Icuil Ximin's stele (PAL·IN·01318) claims 15,500 dead. The chronicler Valothlokhnen (PAL·CH·03358, AY 2242) says "our army withdrew in good order. 2,103 of ours were lost, but 6,074 of the enemy." The stele trebled her losses; the chronicle reversed who lost more.

At the [[evt:18130|Battle of Nidhinge (AY 2228)]] she had 19,261 men against [[per:18010|Tethikhdurin]]'s 3,384, nearly six to one, and still lost, 1,509 to 846. In 2229 at Yirantudhu, where her father's army had won in 2183 and her brother in 2196, she lost 1,993 men to Tethikhdurin's 946, and signed the [[evt:18144|Treaty of Yirantudhu (AY 2229)]] with [[per:18028|Telethnan I]]: seven districts and a second tribute of the same weight, for eleven years. Her husband died that year. In 2230, at the [[evt:18174|Battle of Ivun (AY 2230)]], Maxi Taa's 13,703 men overran her 5,963, killing 1,371 for 189. The capital was sacked a fourth time, and Iti took nine districts with Ivun in a treaty that ended "The war which Talenodnenafi brought upon her own land" (PAL·TR·01223). Valothlokhnen's verdict: "Nothing of worth was lost."

## One victory, four tellings

That same year, at the [[evt:18177|Battle of Khuele (AY 2230)]], Narniyen III came in person with 5,070 men against her 5,263 and was beaten, losing 993 to her 138. It was her only victory, and the only one of thirteen battles her state won in its twenty-nine years.

Three sources tell it, besides the record. Her rock relief (PAL·IN·01320) says "She killed 3,900 soldiers." Navvenon, her state's chronicler, wrote in 2247 (PAL·CH·03360) that Narneyen "came against us at Kebele with 10,773 men," that "2,715 of them lay dead on the field, and of ours only 76," and that "Talenornenath held the ford against them, and Nedhekhte fought before our ranks." He gave the glory to the goddess of grain; she had thanked the god of fire. Shomeren, writing for Tungarvikhrem in 2271 (PAL·CH·03359), admits a retreat "in a great storm" and gives "We lost 515; the enemy, 388." Her stone quadrupled the enemy's dead. Navvenon doubled the enemy's army and halved her losses. The loser's chronicler halved his own dead and nearly tripled hers. Each source leans toward the side that made it.

## The brother's inheritance

She died a natural death in 2232, at fifty-seven, after twelve years' reign. With no child living, the [[evt:18193|Accession of Vellokhnen]] gave her crown to the brother whose empire she had broken and who had defeated their sister, now sixty-three. His son Elelen signed away 32 more districts in 2235; his grandson, Shefnofmalen the Last, who bore her father's name, reigned until the state collapsed in 2249.

Her state held 112 districts at its founding and 74 at her death. Ten battles between 2226 and 2230 cost it 20,648 soldiers. By the end of the 2220s its treasury held 3,595 kilograms of silver, under a hundredth of Yennga's at its fall, against two yearly tributes. The record knows fifteen sacks of Nudhuruigirimu across its history; four fell in her reign. Nambili counted fifteen destruction layers there but described only the top four (PAL·EX·00187); hers lie among "11 earlier destruction horizons, not yet fully excavated."

The record calls her 'the Ill-Starred'; no document of her own age does. As a commander she earns it: she lost five of her six battles, three of them with more than twice the enemy's numbers. But she was the defender in every one, each of her four later wars began with a neighbour's attack, and the treaties that blame her were written by the men who took her land. She chose rebellion knowing what it had cost her sister. She did not choose four enemies at once. What the archers of 2196, the penitent Minaximin and the people of her shrinking districts made of their bright rain, the clay does not say.
