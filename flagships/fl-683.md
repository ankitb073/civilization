Almost everything the record keeps of [[per:45136|Shomiethe]] falls in a single year. He was born in AY 4710 and died, of natural causes, in 4754, at forty-four. In 4748 he showed an engine, took out a patent and wrote one letter to the press. The record names no father, mother, wife or child, no birthplace and no grave; it gives only his people, the Lizo, and his name, which means *bull deer*. I am an archaeologist of Khishkhis, which his country fought in his last years, and I distrust a life with so few layers; but his are unusually honest.

## A federation at war

He grew up in [[pol:1656|Ane]], a kingdom in name and a federation in fact, with presidents elected for five-year terms. When he was twenty-one, President [[per:45064|Ipil Tlyi]] put to the Assembly a motion for the [[evt:45756|Ane–Onitie War]]; the official report (PAL·PD·00014) records it lost, 128 to 186, and the war went ahead. Ane fought six wars in his lifetime, was at war in more than half the years he lived, and won every one that ended before his death, the [[evt:45793|Tsey–Ane War]] among them.

The surveys show a large state that had stopped growing: about 34.5 million people at the end of the 4710s, about 34.6 million at the end of the 4740s. What grew was the price of grain. A measure at Ane cost about 12.7 grams of silver in the 4710s and 20.6 grams in the 4740s, while the [[cur:1038|Withe]] slid from about 4.18 grams of silver to 3.71. That arithmetic is the arithmetic of his engine.

## The engine at the east gate

In 4748 newspapers in three states reported his demonstration of a steam engine. The Ane Intelligencer (PAL·NP·00320) puts it "before a crowd of investors. Sceptics were silenced." The Thimisitsiy Chronicle of Yiutl (PAL·NP·00342), calling him Shomiede, has it "before the academy. Shares in rival firms fell." The Yethithi Herald of Wemu (PAL·NP·00341), Ane's enemy, printed it too, as Xamiede, beneath its report of the battle at Namosash: "our army has fallen back to prepared positions. The censor has withheld further details."

His patent (PAL·PT·00004) runs to two numbered sentences. It claims "an engine in which steam, condensed in a separate vessel, drives a piston to and fro", and that "By condensing the steam apart from the cylinder, the loss of heat is reduced by one half."

Only in The Engine at the Foundry Gate (PAL·NP·01086) do we hear him. The engine stood at a foundry by the east gate of [[set:7|Ane]], pumping water and working bellows, "doing the labour of twelve horses without eating their oats." Grain, he notes, "stands at five and a half Withe the measure", as the record's prices confirm, and "Coal does not eat, and does not lie down." The work "cost me eleven years and, I am sorry to say, the eyebrows of two apprentices"; if so, he began at about twenty-seven.

Letter and patent do not describe the same machine. In the letter the steam is "cooled by a jet of cold water" and "the air outside presses the piston home again"; no separate vessel appears, and "Every part of it was known to the smiths of Ane before I was born." The patent's one claim to novelty is that vessel. The record does not say why. Perhaps the foundry engine was the older pattern; perhaps a man whose application, he complains, "lies on a desk at the President's office" kept his secret out of the newspaper. The editors also cut "two sentences concerning a rival". Someone else was close.

## An honest witness

The letter's worth is its candour about the war. "Our soldiers were beaten at Itlishithyi and at Faamni," he writes, and the record agrees. At Itlishithyi in 4746 Ane fielded more than twice Wemu's numbers and lost 115,670 men to Wemu's 42,519; at Faamni in 4747 it lost 32,049 to 9,094. Oi Lyizi's Ane chronicle of the [[evt:45860|First Wemu–Ane War]] (PAL·CH·08042, AY 4756) turns both defeats inside out: at Itlishithyi "our army gave ground", with "40,488 of ours were lost, but 150,193 of the enemy". Shomiethe also names Ane's victories at Nzhivi in 4747, a defeat for my own country in the [[evt:45867|Ane Colonial War in Daadüdhih]], and at Namosash, then refuses the easy boast: "I do not say my engine won those battles."

With the priests he was as careful. The state cult of [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] taught that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, and some priests called the engine "a thing of appetite". He answered: "the engine has no appetite; it only has a firebox. The appetite, if there is any, is in the men who own it."

## What the engine outlived

He died in 4754, a year after the war with Wemu ended in Ane's favour; the war with my country ran on to stalemate in 4758. He had warned of "engines of Ane's invention sold back to Ane at a profit"; the record has the engine in Wemu by 4756 and in Khishkhis by 4757, though not how it travelled. Ane's town population, about 3.7 million at the end of the 4740s, was 9.2 million ten years later; Wemu and Khishkhis made a like leap only in the following decade. The record does not say the engine caused it, but the order is suggestive.

Nangash observed in 4950 (PAL·SP·00044) that the states credited with steam and with railways both collapsed: "Invention did not protect its inventors." Ane fell in 4834. The elder Nambili's trenches at Ane in 4920 (PAL·EX·00049) crossed the palace quarter and the lower town and found, beneath the modern town, the burnt debris of the sack of 4791; his preliminary report names no foundry. The engine's stratum lies in ice cores and tree rings, where Nami read it in 4917 (PAL·SP·00004): "Since the spread of the steam engine the record shows a warming without precedent in the archive." A man who apologised in print for two apprentices' eyebrows left the widest layer of all.
