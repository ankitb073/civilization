To His Majesty [[per:43821|Ishuishima]], by the favour of the gods King of [[pol:1656|Ane]], Lord of the Provinces Old and New: the humble letter of [[per:43826|Ipil Nyime]], citizen of [[set:7|Ane]], founder in metals and lately a maker of books.

Majesty,

I write to lay before you an art which I have brought to working order in my house by the Tanners' Gate, and to ask, with the respect owed by a subject to his sovereign, that it may be protected and employed in Your Majesty's service. I shall state first what the art is, then what it may do, and lastly what I ask; and I shall try to be brief, though a man in love with his invention is seldom brief.

**What the art is.** Hitherto every book in this kingdom has been copied by hand, at great cost and with many errors; and the few who have tried to cut a page whole upon a wooden block have found that a block once cut serves one page and no other. I have instead cast each letter separately, in metal, in a mould of my own design, so that every letter of a kind is the same as every other. These letters are set in rows within a frame to compose a page; the page is inked, pressed, and printed; and when enough copies are taken, the letters are taken apart and set again for the next page. Thus the same hundred pieces of metal may print a thousand different pages. I call it the art of movable letters.

I do not claim to have drawn it out of nothing. Merchants have brought into our ports, now and again, sheets printed in far countries by some like method; I bought such sheets, studied them under the glass, and judged by the evenness of their letters that they were not cut from blocks. But I have not seen any man in Ane practise it before me, and the moulds, the metal and the ink in my workshop are my own contriving, tested through three winters and many failures.

**What it may do.** Your Majesty will recall the late war: the victory at the [[evt:44335|Battle of Zhaalakhazhash]], the [[evt:44347|Battle of Kheye]], the [[evt:44348|Tenth Siege of Kheye]], and the [[evt:44349|Treaty of Kheye]] by which new provinces came under the crown of Ane. Those provinces must now be governed: their tax rolls registered, their judges instructed, Your Majesty's edicts proclaimed in every market town. With pens this is the work of a hundred clerks for a year. With movable letters, two journeymen and an apprentice may print an edict in a morning, in as many copies as there are towns, and every copy the same to the last stroke, so that no clerk may alter a word by malice or by sleep.

I am aware that the reverend priests of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]], who keep the truth in its proper order, may fear an art that multiplies words so easily. I answer that the press multiplies whatever is set before it, and nothing else. Let the priests license what is printed, and the press will be their servant as faithfully as it is Your Majesty's. Their own rites, now copied by tired hands with many errors, might be printed once correctly and distributed to every altar in the kingdom.

**What I ask.** First, that for twenty years no other person in Ane may cast letters for printing without my licence, so that the art may not be spoiled by careless imitators before it is perfected. Second, a sum of four hundred [[cur:944|Myiu]] toward a second press and a supply of metal. The teachers say that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, and I do not ask for wealth; I ask for lead, tin, and time.

I enclose with this letter a sheet printed in my workshop, being the first page of the rites of the new moon, that Your Majesty may judge with your own eyes whether I have spoken truly. If the [[evt:44391|Invention of movable-type printing in Ane]] pleases you, it is yours; if it does not, I shall return to casting bells.

Your Majesty's most obedient subject,

Ipil Nyime

*Written at Ane, 25 Muie 4069 of the Myitiyishiu, in the fifth year of Your Majesty's reign.*
