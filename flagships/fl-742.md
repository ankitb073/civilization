*In the sixth year of Fi'oliüp.*

Hear, people of [[set:240|Tü'e'i'osh]], hear and remember,  
hear what was done in the sixth year of the queen.

There were signs before, and we did not read them.  
The river [[dei:6|Nili]] ran low in the month of high water.  
A dog howled on the roof of the house of [[dei:7|Yüp]] three nights together.  
The priest looked into the liver of the ram and covered his face.  
Who listens to a dog? Who listens to a liver?

In the year before, the men of [[pol:526|Lüs]] came and sat down before our walls.  
All summer they sat, in the [[evt:16893|Fourth Siege of Tiaiosh]].  
They ate our orchards. They cut our vines for their fires.  
Our few went out against them by the river and came back fewer.  
Then they went away, and we said, It is over.

In the spring our queen [[per:16797|Fi'oliüp]], the wise one, went out.  
She went out to Nisshudi with a handful of spears,  
not a thousand against a host like the sand of the river.  
At the [[evt:16898|Battle of Nisshudi]] the host of Lüs was cut and cut again,  
and still it was a host, and the handful was fewer.

Then they came to the gate. They came to the gate.  
This is the [[evt:16899|Second Sack of Tiaiosh]]; this is what we sing.  
They came over the wall by the potters' quarter.  
They took the bronze from the altar of Yüp.  
They took the oil out of the jars and the jars out of the houses.  
They took my mother's loom-weights. What did they want with loom-weights?  
They set fire to the roofs, and the roofs of Tü'e'i'osh burned  
like a field of stubble in the month of burning stubble.  
My sister and I lay in the cistern with the water to our chins,  
and we heard the city burn above us, and we did not weep, for fear of being heard.

When we came up, the queen had made the peace.  
The [[evt:16900|Treaty of Tiaiosh]] was sworn among the ashes.  
Nisshudi and its fields are given to Lüs.  
Silver goes down to Lüs each year, ten years of silver,  
[[cur:376|Pimtüm]] counted out on the table, pimtüm in sacks on donkeys,  
and the donkeys go down the road and do not come back.

Hear us, [[dei:8|Nish'il]], sky over the city: was it for our sin?  
Was it the dog on the roof? Was it the river running low?  
The priests say the gods were angry.  
The potter says the men of Lüs were many.  
I do not know which is true. I think both are true.

Now we are building. We carry the stones. We have burned the dead.  
We sing this so that the children will know  
why the wall has black stones in it,  
why there is no orchard below the gate,  
why the donkeys go down the road in spring.  
Hear, people of Tü'e'i'osh. Hear and remember.
