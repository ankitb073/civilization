Say to my lord [[per:13098|Nanjenvi]], king of [[pol:578|Khanji]], king of [[set:49|Adhgumgi]], beloved of [[dei:28|Khudhaze]] of the sky: thus says [[per:13118|Nangashu]], your servant, the scribe of the storehouse, the dust under your feet.

At the feet of my lord I fall seven times and seven times. May it be well with my lord. May it be well with his house, his son, his horses, his chariots and his land.

My lord, hear the words of your servant. I write them to you in the old signs, the signs of my fathers, the many signs, so that the scribes of the palace may read them to you. Hear them to the end.

My lord knows the old signs. There is a sign for the ox, a sign for the barley, a sign for the king, a sign for the god. There is a sign for each word. A boy goes into the house of tablets at seven years. He comes out at seventeen years, and still he does not know all the signs. Only the priests and the scribes can read.

My lord, I have made new signs.

Hear how it was. In the years after [[evt:13082|the old kingdom was broken]], when [[evt:13083|my lord's kingdom rose]] in its place, I was sent down to the harbour to count the jars from the ships. The sailors of the far coast came in their ships. On their jars they scratched marks. The marks were few. I asked them: what is this mark? They said: it is the sound at the beginning of "ox". I asked: and this? They said: it is the sound at the beginning of "house". They did not write words. They wrote the sounds of words, one sign for one sound.

I did not sleep that night. I sat up with a lamp and a tablet. I said: if a sign is a sound, then the words of Khanji also are sounds, and a man needs only as many signs as there are sounds in the mouth. I counted the sounds of our speech. They are not many; fewer than the fingers of three men. I made a sign for each. Some I took from the marks of the sailors. Some I took from our old signs, cut short. Some I made new.

Then I wrote my own name with them. Then I wrote the name of my lord. Then I wrote "barley", and "silver", and "the king has given". I gave the tablet to a porter's boy of the storehouse, who knows few of the old signs. In the morning he read it to me. In the morning, my lord, a boy read it to me.

This is the thing I have made. I lay it at my lord's feet.

Think, my lord, what it is. The steward in the far town can write to my lord in his own hand. The merchant can write the weight of his [[cur:290|kavu]] and his barley, and no scribe can cheat him. The king's word can go out to every village, and the headman can read it himself.

My lord, I know there will be speech against me. The priests of [[mov:4|Khupande]] will say: the signs are holy, and were given to us in order, and are kept by us in order, and this man has made them common. Let my lord hear them, for they are the servants of the gods. But let my lord also hear me. I have not touched the old signs. Let the priests keep them for the gods. Let the temple keep the old signs for [[dei:30|Pugazhu]] and [[dei:31|Khumbe]], for the hymns and the omens. Let the new signs be for the storehouse and the market and the king's messengers. The gods lose nothing.

I ask my lord for this: that I may teach the new signs to ten boys of the palace, and that my lord look on what they have learned at the new moon after next. If they do not read, let my lord break my tablets and send me back to the jars. If they read, let my lord remember his servant.

I have sent with this tablet a second tablet, written in the new signs. It is small. It says: *Nanjenvi is king. May [[dei:29|Bumbe]] of the sea bring him ships.*

Written in the 9th year of Nanjenvi, in the storehouse.
