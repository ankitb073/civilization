To the most serene lady [[per:37476|Yiviynifkuth]], sovereign of [[pol:1565|Binge]], whom the One has set over us; to the reverend bishops of the church of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]]; and to the elders of the brethren of [[mov:69|Kiyiviki]] in the city of [[set:147|Binge]]: [[per:37587|Nikhzhin]], the least of the seekers, sends greeting and asks a hearing.

It has been said against me in the hall of the elders, and carried thence to the court, that I have rent the seamless garment of Kiyiviki, that I have drawn away the brethren after me, and that I have set up a new teaching under a new name, which the common people already call [[mov:138|Tutpothtu]]. It is said that what I have done is [[evt:38133|a schism]], and that a schism is the beginning of rebellion. To these charges I make my defence, as a liegeman answers before his sovereign.

First, as to my obedience. I am the sworn man of my lady Yiviynifkuth. I have paid the hearth-tax in good [[cur:882|shitmu]] every year of her reign, and those who hear me pay it likewise. When the muster was called, the young men who hear me went out with their spears. I do not preach against the sovereign's peace, nor against the bishops of Vishithmikh, whose churches I pass with my head bowed. The One whom they serve is the One whom I seek. I ask no lands, no tithes, no bench in any council. Let it be written that Nikhzhin owes his sovereign all that a vassal owes, and withholds none of it.

Second, as to the charge that I have left Kiyiviki. I have not left it. It has left itself. Consider what the fathers of our fellowship taught: that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. Now consider what has happened since [[evt:38006|the sack of Nudhuruigirimu]], and since [[evt:38017|the victory before Nudhuruigirimu]] and [[evt:38019|the peace that followed]], when the silver of that city flowed into ours. I do not grudge the realm its victory; it was won in a just quarrel. But the silver flowed also into the hall of the elders. They have built a new house with a stone floor. They take gifts at the door. They have learned to say that a full purse is no hindrance to a pure heart. Is it written so? It is written: the hand that grasps the coin cannot hold the lamp.

Third, as to authority. The fathers taught that every seeker may find the truth by the inner light and by reason, and that no man owns it. Now the elders have made themselves owners. They say: believe this because we have said it. They forbid the brethren to read and dispute. I have only done what the fathers commanded: I have reasoned, and I have let others reason with me. If that is schism, then the fathers were schismatics before me.

Fourth, as to the wheel. We hold, with the fathers, that all things turn, and that what ends will begin again. But the elders have grown bold to say they know the measure of every turning, and they sell foretellings of good years and bad. I say the wheel turns, but its measure is hidden in the One; and he who claims to read it is a merchant of the future, not a seeker of the truth.

Fifth, as to what I ask of the brethren. I ask justice in dealings and moderation in all things. I ask that the steward give true weight and the elder give true counsel. I ask that we keep our doors open to every people, as the fathers kept them open, for the light is not the property of Binge alone. This is not a new teaching. It is the old teaching washed.

Therefore I beseech my sovereign lady not to treat as rebels those whose only offence is to have taken the fathers at their word. Let the bishops examine me. Let the elders dispute with me openly before witnesses, and let reason decide. If I am found in error, I will be silent. If I am not, let me and those who walk with me keep the name of Tutpothtu in peace, under her protection, as loyal subjects.

Given at Binge, in the 9th year of Yiviynifkuth, by the hand of Nikhzhin, seeker.
