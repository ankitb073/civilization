title: The Fourth Clause: Ineshimen and the Peaces of Ana

In AY 2219, at Biyitl, the terms of a peace between [[pol:756|Ana]] and Mii were cut into a tablet of silver. The fourth clause read: "To bind the two houses, Mi'ishim'a, child of Nitllo Ive shall be given in marriage to Sho'a Yueva, child of Nitllo Shim'a." The last line reads: "Written by Ineshimen, the scribe". That year the couple's son, Yi To'a, was born. [[per:48084|Ineshimen]] would go on to write the peace that ended the war in which the boy's mother was killed, and then the peace at which the boy, grown, swore for both sides at once.

## A hand without a voice

The record of his life is thin. He was born in 2183, a man of the Lizo, and his name means *oak storm*. The record names no parents, wife or child, and says nothing of his training. He became scribe of Ana in 2203, at twenty, the year after the death of Sho'a Lieze, who had held the office since 2169. He kept it until he died in 2259, and until 2258 the record names no other scribe of Ana.

In 2202 Ana had adopted the Esiezhetl alphabet. He took office the next year, and all six documents that survive under his name are written in it. The record does not say where he learned his letters.

The six are four treaties and two lawsuits. The treaties speak in the king's voice, and Ineshimen appears only in the closing line, which gives the king's year and the place. That line is all of him we hear.

## Biyitl, in the plague years

The survey of the 2180s found Ana a kingdom of 46 districts and about 2.07 million people, ruled from [[set:7|Ane]]. Under [[per:17674|Nitllo Ive]] the [[evt:17801|First Ana–Pibetie War]] (2200–2208) added land, but no text of its peace survives. The [[evt:17973|First Ana–Mii War]] (2215–2219) ended at Biyitl. Mii gave up fourteen districts and owed fifty talents of silver a year for thirty-seven years, "by the Fue'la standard", the old weight of Mii.

The tablet was written in the second year of the White Death. By 2226 the sickness had killed 1,955,314 people across the lands it reached, Ane, Biyitl and Muvush among them. The Mii chronicler O'e Mov wrote of the peace in 2228: "Nothing of worth was lost."

Ineshimen recorded what was lost. In 2227 Ie'a, son of Ue, a man of Mii, asked the court at the capital for his father's 270 measures of sown land. The tablet is water-stained, and a gap falls in the middle of his complaint. The steward answered: "The fields were found empty and were given to settlers." The judgment was: "Let him seek his fields in the land of his former lord." The record does not say why the fields were empty. Plague and war had both passed over them.

## The boy who signed for Mii

The marriage did not keep the peace. Sho'a Yueva became queen of Mii in 2223, and her husband became king of Ana in 2226. The [[evt:18169|Second Ana–Mii War]] began in 2230. At Muvush in 2231 Mi'ishim'a led 7,728 men against her 1,034. Ana lost 55 men and Mii lost 200, and Sho'a Yueva was killed. In 2237 the Ana chronicler Luesh'a'ob nearly doubled her army, to 1,951, and cut Ana's dead to 21. He adds that "her head was brought to the king", without saying the king was her husband.

Their son became king of Mii in 2232, at thirteen, with a regent. In 2233 Ineshimen wrote the peace of Muvush, which father and son signed for opposite sides. Its preamble says: "The war which Yi To'a brought upon his own land is ended." The record names Ana as the attacker in that war, and in every war Ineshimen's surviving treaties ended, yet the formula always blamed the other ruler. In 2233 the other ruler was a boy of fourteen whose mother had just died fighting his father. Luesh'a'ob claimed "27 districts in all" for Ana; the record gives eleven.

In 2240 Ineshimen recorded a second suit at Biyitl, by then an Ana town. Pi'telfoyatl, son of I'pil Ash, asked for 265 measures. Except for the treaty's name, the steward's answer and the judgment repeat the words of 2227 exactly.

## Two seals

Mi'ishim'a died in 2243, and Yi To'a, at twenty-four, became king of Ana while keeping the crown of Mii. In 2245 Ineshimen wrote the peace of Kivunge, which ended the [[evt:18300|Ana–Khivona War]]. Then Yi To'a's two kingdoms went to war with each other. In the [[evt:18425|Third Ana–Mii War]] the record has him commanding both armies at Likhtli in 2256. There 12,889 men of Ana met 311 of Mii, and Mii lost 132 men to Ana's 12. At Tiyinmiw in 2257 he stood with Mii and lost to his own general.

The peace of 2257 is the last of Ineshimen's works to survive; he was seventy-four. It made Mii "a servant of Ana" and set its tribute at 9.2 talents a year for twenty-two years. The treaty speaks in Yi To'a's own voice, yet it keeps the old formula: "The war which Yi To'a stirred up is ended." The record cannot tell us whether the scribe smiled.

Yi To'a's speech at the swearing (PAL·PM·00058) survives in a medieval copy: "In my right hand is the seal of Ana. In my left hand is the seal of Mii." He ordered the scribes of both houses to write, and "let neither add a word the other has not heard". Yet the speech weighs the tribute "in Omo", where Ineshimen's tablet says "by the Titha standard", the weight of Mii. The record does not say which is right.

Ineshimen died in 2259, at seventy-six, of natural causes. By the end of the 2250s Ana held 118 districts, and his four surviving peaces alone had ceded forty-five to it. His memorial is the fourth clause of 2219, which bound two houses into one, and the later treaties he wrote record what became of that marriage.
