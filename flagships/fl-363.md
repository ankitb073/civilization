**OFFICE OF THE MASTER OF THE MINT, [[set:85|Nambi]]**

**Memorandum for the President of the Republic and the Committee of the Treasury. Not for publication.**

Nambi, 25 Nikh 2321 of the Vimikhulu

### 1. The ordinance and its execution

In accordance with the ordinance of the President, Citizen [[per:45462|Nadhani]], the standard of the [[cur:901|Shikh]] has been reduced from 7.600 grammes of fine silver to 5.467 grammes. The reduction is 2.133 grammes per piece, or 28.1 per cent. The new piece carries 71.9 per cent of the silver of the old.

The change was made at the presses on the date fixed, without interruption to striking. The steam presses of the lower hall were re-dressed with the new dies over a single night; the hand presses of the old building have been retired to blank-cutting. From one kilogramme of fine silver the Mint formerly struck 131.6 pieces; it now strikes 182.9. The Treasury will be aware that this is an increase of some 39 per cent in the number of shikhs obtainable from the same metal.

### 2. The causes

The Committee does not need to be reminded of the causes, but the Mint owes the record a plain statement of them.

The Republic has carried, since 2294, the cost of the first [[evt:45934|Battle of Nzhivi]], in which 381,569 of our soldiers were lost, and since 2299 the cost of the second [[evt:45980|Battle of Nzhivi]], in which a further 146,245 were lost. Pensions to the households of the fallen, and the rebuilding of the armies, have been paid for a quarter of a century out of a coinage that did not expand.

To these old charges were added the campaigns of the last two years. At the [[evt:46171|Battle of Kamvamvil]] the Republic took the field with more than a million men; at the [[evt:46179|Battle of Mbilu]] with above one million one hundred thousand. The victory was complete, and the [[evt:46181|Conquest of Mbilu]] has added its districts to [[pol:1638|Khishkhih]]. But victory is paid in coin. The losses at Mbilu alone, 195,869 men, will fall on the pension rolls for forty years.

The spoil of the [[evt:46180|Fifth Sack of Mbilu]] came into the Treasury chiefly as goods, not bullion, and does not relieve the Mint.

### 3. Effects to be expected

Grain stands at present at about 1.57 per measure in the markets of the capital. The Mint expects it to rise, and the Committee should expect it also. Merchants and bankers weigh coin; they are not deceived by a stamp. The Mint's own estimate is that prices will move toward the silver, not toward the face, within one or two harvests, and faster in the ports than inland.

The Mint therefore recommends:

- that the old pieces be called in at par through the provincial treasuries, and not left to the money-changers, who will melt them;
- that the new standard be announced by semaphore and express courier to every prefecture on the same day, so that no district learns it from rumour;
- that soldiers' pay in the Mbilu garrisons be raised in proportion, before the men discover the arithmetic for themselves;
- that the penalties for clipping and sweating be enforced without exception for the next two years.

### 4. A note for the record

The teachers of [[mov:70|Vimikhulu]] say that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, and some of them have already preached that a lighter coin is a just rebuke to a greedy age. The Mint does not argue theology. It observes only that a household which withdraws from appetite still buys bread, and that it will now buy it with more pieces.

The Mint has done what it was ordered to do. It has done it accurately. It records here that the [[evt:46106|Debasement of the Shikh]] is a measure of necessity, and that a second such measure would not be.

*By order of the Master of the Mint.*
