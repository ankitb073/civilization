title: Money, Steam and Heat: What Broke States in the Age of Engines
---
The Age of Engines (AY 4741–4947) takes its name from three inventions: the steam engine in Ane in AY 4748, the combustion engine in Yiutl in 4770, and railways in Fyinan in 4793. My argument tonight is that the engines changed not only what states could do but what could break them. War remained the great destroyer. But the record of this age adds two causes of ruin that earlier ages scarcely knew, money and heat, and it is those additions that define it.

## War in millions

Begin with scale. In the long duel between [[pol:1769|Wemu]] and [[pol:1656|Ane]], single battles in the 4790s set armies of half a million against one another, and that war cost the two states more than a million dead; in 4829–4832 Ane attacked [[pol:1942|Fyinan]] with close to nine hundred thousand men. The [[evt:46425|Collapse of the Wemu State]] (4806), the [[evt:46675|Collapse of the Ane State]] (4834) and the [[evt:46959|Collapse of the Fyinan State]] (4855) all follow on these wars. The irony is worth stating plainly: the two states the record credits with steam and with railways both collapsed within the age. Invention did not protect its inventors. Fyinan, indeed, was itself an heir of the [[evt:46015|Collapse of the Tsey State]] in 4761, which followed a lost war against Ane and two revolts.

## Money

The first new cause is money. For the first time, the record lists the failure of a currency among the causes of collapse. Wemu's coin lost more than ninety-nine per cent of its value in a hyperinflation of 4787; Ane's suffered the same fate in 4800. [[pol:1785|Tallin]] debased its coin by about a tenth in 4800, a year after a usurpation, and broke in the [[evt:46656|Collapse of the Tallin State]] of 4831, leaving the [[pol:1959|Yidhe]] empire and the kingdom of [[pol:1960|Navnge]] among its heirs. A state could now be undone in its treasury as well as on the field.

Beyond single treasuries lay the whole economy of a continent. [[evt:45919|The Great Contraction of AY 4753]] saw trade on one landmass fall by more than half over thirty years. The record ties it to an earlier drought and to a run of conquests, and it touched [[pol:1674|Mbilu]], [[pol:1893|Unzhinlathu]] and their neighbours. The [[evt:45893|Onset of the Great Drought (AY 4756)]] came while it was still under way.

## Heat

The second new cause is the climate itself. [[cli:251|The Industrial Warming]], under way since AY 4818, is tied by the record to a run of famines. The deadliest in proportion were the [[evt:47454|Famine of AY 4902 in Nuekhmokh]], which killed about two in five of the stricken population of [[reg:0|Nuekhmokh]], and the [[evt:47756|Famine of AY 4937 in Mbambanlule]], which killed nearly half in [[reg:29|Mbambanlule]]. These are not collapses of states, and I want to be exact: the record names neither drought nor warming among the causes of any collapse in this age. Heat killed people; it had not yet, on the record's evidence, killed a state. Whether that will remain true is not a question for a historian.

## Our own part

A Khishkhis scholar should add, without pleasure, that our own state's victories stand among the recorded causes of three collapses of the age: the [[evt:45963|Collapse of the Lamlivje State]] (4756), after the trade war we won in 4739; the [[evt:47399|Collapse of the Fuvu State]] (4893), after our conquests of 4857–4859; and the [[evt:47745|Collapse of the Yiethithi State]] (4929), after the holy war we won in 4896. Our schoolbooks celebrate those victories. The record shows what they set in motion for the defeated. Other collapses had other roots: the [[evt:47041|Collapse of the Bene State]] (4860) followed costly wars and two persecutions of a dissenting faith.

Weighing the causes, then: war is still behind almost every collapse of the age, twenty of the twenty-one the record counts. Money appears in three. Faith appears in one. Climate appears in none, though it appears in the deaths of millions. What defined the Age of Engines was not a change in the first cause of ruin but an enlargement of the list. A state of this age could be beaten, bankrupted or starved, and the engines that made it strong made each of those dangers larger.
