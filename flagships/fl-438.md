To my lord and father, [[per:23680|Nadigi]], king of [[pol:973|Kabav]], from his daughter [[per:23771|Navavun]], who was sent out from his house as an envoy is sent, and who now, as an envoy must, gives her report.

You asked me, when I left [[set:12|Kabav]], to write for the archive an account of the marriage, in the manner your envoys use: what was seen, what was said, what was promised, and what was meant. I did not write it at once. An envoy who reports on the first day reports only the banquet. Two years have now passed, and I can report what it has meant.

**Of the ceremony.** The marriage was made in the eighth year of your reign, in the autumn, when the roads are dry. I was received at the boundary by the elders of [[pol:977|Tadudüvi]], who came out on foot, which they told me was an honour they do not pay to merchants. They led me through a city hung with green boughs. The houses of the two peoples walked behind me in their order, ours first, because I was the guest.

**Of the bridegroom.** [[per:23778|Nakagum]] was fifteen years of age on the day we were joined, and I was thirty. I set this down not as a complaint but as a fact, for an envoy who conceals the age of a bridegroom misleads her king. He stood very straight. His voice broke once in the vows, and he did not let it break a second time. I thought then, and I think now, that a boy who masters his voice before strangers will master greater things.

**Of the gifts.** Our house sent the silver you appointed, counted in [[cur:522|Tügin]] before the stewards of both houses, and the bolts of dyed cloth, and the two horses. Their house sent oil, timber and a cup of old workmanship which they said had belonged to their grandmothers. I have it on my table as I write. It is heavier than it looks, as is much in this place.

**Of what was said.** Their eldest steward spoke of the two houses as two hands that wash each other. I answered, as you instructed me, that two hands may also join in prayer, and that the One whom we in Kabav serve does not ask us to give up our neighbours but to deal justly with them. They received this well. I do not know whether they believe as we believe; they did not say, and I did not ask. The teachers of [[mov:24|Udunkala]] hold that each seeker must find the truth by his own inner light, and it would ill become me to hold a lamp to another's face at his own wedding.

**Of what was meant.** Here an envoy must speak plainly. The houses of Kabav and Tadudüvi have been joined, not merely a woman and a boy. The marriage was a promise that the roads between us will stay open, that the tolls will be moderate, that neither house will lend its spears against the other. In two years I have seen nothing to make me doubt that the promise is being kept. The merchants of Kabav come here and are not cheated. I have been treated with courtesy, and lately with something warmer.

**Of my own condition.** You will want to know this, though it is not an envoy's business. I am well. The house is orderly. My husband now asks my counsel before he answers his elders, and he has grown taller than I am. If prosperity is a sign of divine favour, as our teachers say, then this marriage has been favoured, for the granaries of both houses were full last harvest.

I commend myself to you, my father, and to the One who judges at the end of all things, before whom envoys and kings alike will give their reports.

Written in the tenth year of Nadigi.
