[[per:22613|Balsheyononen]], king of [[pol:777|Tallen]], to the council and the people of Tallen, greeting.

If you are well, it is well; I and the army are well, and I write to you from the camp in [[reg:8|Devegobevenga]], in the 2nd year of my reign, so that you may hear the truth from me before you hear lies from the market.

You will have heard of the day at [[evt:23010|Riniy]]. I will not hide it from you, for a king who hides his reverses from his people teaches them to doubt his victories. Our army went up before the walls of [[set:121|Reney]] under [[per:22826|Ongen]], a general of tried courage, with a great host, well armed and well fed, eager for the work. It was not courage that failed us that day, nor numbers. It was the manner of the enemy.

Consider how the men of [[pol:939|Reney]] make war. They do not come down into the open plain, as free men do who trust their arms and their cause. They keep to their walls, their ditches and their marshes; they choose the narrow ground where a great army cannot spread its wings; they strike at the flank and fall back, and strike again. Their general [[per:22827|Valothekewewen]] is praised among them as a fox is praised by chickens. Against such an enemy a great host is like a great ship in a narrow harbour: its very size is its danger. Our men pressed forward, the ranks behind pushed upon the ranks in front, the ground gave way beneath them, and many who had never seen the face of the enemy were lost in the press. Fortune, which loves to humble the proud, did her work that day.

But do not believe the criers who go about the city counting our dead by the hundred thousand. Grief is a poor arithmetician, and fear a worse one. The army stands; its standards are not lost; Ongen lives, and with him the larger part of the host. And the enemy did not buy his field cheaply. The walls of Reney are full of mourning. Their best men lie under the earth that they say is theirs, and the earth knows better.

I remind you of what our fathers did in the [[evt:22164|first war against Reney]], when they too were told at the beginning that the thing could not be done, and did it. I remind you of the fields of [[evt:22807|Togannge]], not twenty years ago, when the rebels of the [[evt:22815|Zhienro revolt]] thought that the empire was a rotten wall that would fall at a push, and learned otherwise. Tallen is not judged by a single day. A city's virtue is shown not in the morning of good fortune but in the evening of bad.

Therefore I have come myself to the camp. I will not sit in [[set:0|Tallen]] and read of my soldiers' deaths on parchment while they read of my comfort in rumours. I have sacrificed to [[dei:14|Volme]], who gives victory to the patient and not to the hasty; I have offered to [[dei:16|Domamnol]], who sees the whole field from above; and the priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] tell me that the signs are good. What turns, turns again. The wheel that went down at Riniy will come up.

I ask of you three things. First, that the pay of the soldiers be sent to me in good [[cur:482|medha]], full weight, and without the delay of clerks; a soldier who is unpaid has two enemies. Second, that the levy of the new year be gathered without murmuring, for I mean to fill the ranks and to fill them with men who have something to avenge. Third, that you silence those who speak in the assemblies as if the war were already lost. A city that despairs at the first wound does not deserve to heal.

Let the temples of [[dei:18|Eva]] keep their fires burning until I write again. I will write again from nearer the walls of Reney. Farewell.
