## Three words at the foot

Of [[per:49068|Muflin]] the record keeps almost nothing he did not write himself: his years, AY 4500 to 4568; his people, the Jego; one office, envoy of [[pol:1785|Tallin]] from 4520, when he was twenty, until his natural death at sixty-eight. It names no parents, wife, child or birthplace. What survives are four sealed parchments in the state archive of Tallin, treaties of 4530, 4552, 4553 and 4566, each closing with the same words: "Drafted by Muflin." His life must be read out of them, and out of what others said of his peace.

He served three kings, Pavbukhshifin I the Great, [[per:42941|Nivlalin I]] and Buyuen the Conqueror. In the survey of the 4500s, the decade of his birth, Tallin counted some 39.4 million people in 295 districts; by the end of the 4560s, the decade of his death, 48.0 million in 387, most of the new districts taken in war. After his colleague Puftuven, who drafted the treaty closing the [[evt:43707|23rd Diebeguvebenga Trade War]], died in 4529, Muflin was for twenty-seven years the only envoy of Tallin the record names.

## Yinnge, AY 4530

His earliest treaty is the most damaged (PAL·TR·02720: water-stained, partly illegible). It closed the [[evt:43725|Pienpedhzhe–Tallin War]], which Pienpedhzhe began in 4526, the year Luekhulun I took its throne. At Yinnge in 4530 Tallin's defenders numbered 286,890 against 17,053; the attackers lost 3,522 men, Tallin 614. Tallin's Shifin swelled the enemy to 30,233 men and their dead to 8,420 (PAL·CH·07706). Pienpedhzhe's Puefnenathven gave Tallin the heavier losses and ended: "at the urging of the priests, the king granted peace to Kavle... The honour of the realm was preserved" (PAL·CH·07705).

Muflin's parchment says otherwise. Its second article makes the vanquished pay "by [...] indemnity the sum of 3,208,800 Nuyivin". The water took the formula's middle words and spared the figure: by the coin's weight some twenty-four tonnes of silver, about a tenth of Pienpedhzhe's treasury at the end of the 4520s. The signatories were a king of seventy-eight, two years from his death, and one of twenty-eight; the envoy was thirty. Pienpedhzhe's annalist gave it one line: "Envoys came and went; the Treaty of Yinnge was sealed" (PAL·AN·00815). Pienpedhzhe, which had fought Tallin three times since 4425, never did again.

## A wedding for a holy war

Then twenty-two years without a surviving line; even the annexation of Tunnge in the [[evt:43833|Third Tallin–Tunnge War]] left no treaty text. He returns in 4552 at Nidhinge, closing the [[evt:43942|Holy War of Tallin against Kawnunuy]], fought in the name of religion against a state of another faith, the Dankhüdhil. His treaty (PAL·TR·02731) says nothing of faith, except that it is dated by the era of Tallin's own [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]]. It takes the land of Tunnge "with the towns of Nidhinge (7 districts)", an indemnity and a twenty-year truce, and adds an article found in none of his other texts: "To bind the two houses, Buyuen, child of Nivlalin shall be given in marriage to Khidhikhuwukhuth, child of Numalbiruyin." The groom was seventeen, the bride nineteen.

The cession reads three ways. The parchment says seven districts. Tallin's Yaanin, thirty-three years later, made it "12 districts in all" (PAL·CH·07745); Kawnunuy's Bankirath wrote that "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor" (PAL·CH·07746). The surveys side with the envoy: Kawnunuy fell from 22 districts and about 2.68 million people at the end of the 4540s to 15 and 1.95 million by the end of the 4550s, a drop its 9,759 men lost in the war cannot explain. The villages were not empty. The marriage held; its son, born in 4554, would reign as Pavbukhshifin II.

A year later Muflin ended the [[evt:43970|Zhinontunu–Tallin War]] (PAL·TR·02732), which Zhinontunu began and lost in three battles against armies five to nine times its size. His second article is pure tact: "Zhinontunu places its foreign relations under the protection of Tallin." Tallin's chronicler had Zhinontunu sending "envoys with gifts and pleas" (PAL·CH·07748); Zhinontunu's own names no battle and has its king grant the peace (PAL·CH·07747). Muflin's sentence let both stories stand. The two states never fought again.

## Signing is the battle

His last treaty, done at Piyikhzhidhe in 4566 (PAL·TR·02742), closed the [[evt:44088|First Tallin–Binge War]] and was signed for Tallin by Buyuen, the groom of the wedding clause, now a king of thirty-one. Generals had won its battles; verses by an unknown hand, sold in the capital in 4582, jeered that for Buyuen "signing is the battle he will face" (PAL·PO·00042). Muflin wrote the terms of that battle: the land of Yidhe "with the towns of Virathkhanu, Khungumnu and Rurin (42 districts)", and 6,425,714 Tuthshi, some forty tonnes of silver, when Binge's whole treasury at the end of the 4560s held about twenty-five. Yaanin later counted "73 districts in all" (PAL·CH·07777); Binge's Hithden said only "The border was settled." (PAL·CH·07778).

What a cession meant on the ground survives in a lawsuit heard at Khungumnu in 4570 (PAL·LS·00736). Yurdunun, son of Shithshin, formerly a subject of Binge, asked for his father's 250 acres back. Counsel for the Crown answered: "The plaintiff did not swear allegiance within the year." The claim was dismissed, with costs. Nothing in Muflin's articles speaks of an oath or a year; where the rule came from, the record does not say. In 4919, after a later Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe, another son heard the same answer, though he was let stay as a tenant (PAL·LS·00784).

Muflin died in 4568, the year the water-stained Yinnge parchment was catalogued in the archive of Tallin. Each of his four peaces was done where its war's last battle had been fought; the record counts some 78,000 men lost on both sides in those wars. Every text promises "a firm, sincere and perpetual peace" and then a truce of twenty years. The formula was not his; Puftuven had used it before him. What was his, so far as we can tell, were the figures, and they stayed closer to what happened than the chronicles written in [[set:0|Taulin]].
