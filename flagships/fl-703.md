title: The Silo on the Harbour Road: A Hunger Song from Fova
---
*Printed for the Fova poor-kitchen. Sing it to the old net-mending tune. Take a copy, pay what you can, and if you can pay nothing, take two.*

**1.**  
The silo on the harbour road stands higher than the harbour light,  
They raised it when my father was a barefoot boy on the quay;  
It held the wheat of [[reg:0|Nuikhumukh]] from one sowing to the next,  
And every child in Fova had its dust in their hair.  

*Knock on the granary, knock on the door,*  
*It rings like a bell, like a bell, and no more.*  

**2.**  
The first year it was only rain that would not keep its season,  
The crier called out "shortfall" and the crier called "reserves";  
They opened up the hatches and the reserves came down like water,  
And we said thank you to the wardens, and we went on.  

**3.**  
The second year the carts came half, and then the carts came seldom,  
The bread-ticket went yellow and the yellow went to grey;  
My mother cut the loaf in five and then she cut it in seven,  
And put her own piece back in the crock and said she'd had her share.  

*Knock on the granary, knock on the door,*  
*It rings like a bell, like a bell, and no more.*  

**4.**  
The third year men in good coats came to measure the silo's echo,  
They wrote it in a ledger and they sailed back up the coast;  
The broadsheet called the [[evt:44776|Famine in Nuekhmokh]] "a passing dearth of the season",  
"A question of carriage." We said it was a question of bread.  

**5.**  
Who took it? Ask the bakers, and the bakers blame the millers,  
The millers blame the shippers and the shippers blame the sky;  
Some said it was sold abroad and some said it was hoarded,  
And the old man at the ferry said the rats had signed a contract.  

**6.**  
We boiled the nettles by the dock, we boiled the bark of lindens,  
We stood at the granary window from the dark to the dark;  
The almoners weighed the little ones each Thursday on a steelyard  
And wrote the numbers in a book, and the numbers went down.  

*Knock on the granary, knock on the door,*  
*It rings like a bell, like a bell, and no more.*  

**7.**  
One in six of us is gone from Fova and the farms around it,  
Some to the burying hill and some on the night boat north;  
The school has three benches filled where it used to fill five,  
And the fourth bed in our house is made up, and nobody sleeps in it.  

**8.**  
And now they say it's over, the [[evt:44776|Famine in Nuekhmokh]],  
The sheets print the price of bread, and it falls the way it should;  
The carts are running Tuesdays and the granary keeps its hours,  
And I am glad, I am glad, and I do not trust it yet.  

**9.**  
So when you pass the silo on the harbour road at evening,  
Put your hand flat on the stonework, it is cold the way graves are;  
And say the names you have to say, the short ones and the long ones,  
For all of [[reg:0|Nuikhumukh]] is listening, and it has not much else to do.  

*Knock on the granary, knock on the door,*  
*It rings a little duller now. Grain, or the dead. Knock once more.*
