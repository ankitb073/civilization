*[Tablet, obverse]*

To my lord [[per:981|Kupimkun]], king of [[pol:8|Navnga]], great king, whose hand is heavy upon the lands, say: thus says your servant, the scribe who walks with the host of my lord in the land of [[reg:9|Duputsinga]].

May [[dei:20|Nothol]] of the fire keep the hearth of my lord. May [[dei:21|Duminu]] of the storm thunder for my lord and not against him. May [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] of the river carry the boats of my lord. May [[dei:25|Novegu]] of wisdom sit at the right hand of my lord. With the host of my lord it is well. Very well.

Let my lord hear. In the fifteenth year of my lord, in the month of the threshing, the host of my lord came to [[set:68|Poyonga]]. The great general [[per:917|Dyeykathren]], the old lion of my lord, led them. Thirteen thousand and four hundred and fifty-eight were the men of my lord. They covered the fields as locusts cover the fields. They drank the wells as the dry ground drinks the rain.

The men of [[pol:12|Tsinondonu]] came out against them. They were eight hundred and thirty-eight. Eight hundred and thirty-eight against thirteen thousand! The scouts laughed. I wrote it twice and counted it twice; it is so. Since the [[evt:748|Battle of Zhinontunu]] the men of Tsinondonu are few, and their houses are empty, and their young men lie in the ground. My lord knows this. Therefore my lord sent the host. My lord saw well.

The general of Tsinondonu was [[per:1059|Palulrinin]]. He was a man of courage; I do not take that from him. He stood at the front of his men with a bronze axe. He did not turn.

*[Tablet, reverse]*

At sunrise the spears met. The men of Tsinondonu held one hour. Then they broke. They ran to the river; they ran to the hills. Palulrinin did not run. He fell on the field. The spearmen of my lord brought his axe to the general, and the general has set it aside for my lord.

That is the [[evt:768|Battle of Poynge]]. The field is the field of Navnga.

Of the men of Tsinondonu, two hundred and eighty-seven lay dead. Of the men of my lord, seven hundred and forty-eight lay dead. Let my lord not be grieved: many were killed by the arrows at the ford before the lines met; the rest fell in the press. The priests gave them to [[dei:22|Vinba]], mother of the earth, with oil and with barley. The fire of Nothol burned all night upon the hill.

Now let my lord hear the word of the general Dyeykathren. Thus says the general: "The road to Tsinondonu is open. There is no host between my spears and its walls. Let my lord say 'Go', and I will go. Let my lord say 'Sit', and I will sit. But the barley of Duputsinga is ripe now, and it will not wait for the word."

The host has bread for twenty days. Let my lord send grain upon the river. Let my lord send silver, one weight of [[cur:2|Mulshi]] for each spear-leader, so that their hearts stay warm. The men of the Poyonga country bring sheep and bow down. I have written their names on another tablet.

*[Left edge]*

The [[evt:760|Navnga–Chinontonu War]] goes well. The gods of Navnga turn the wheel for Navnga. Let my lord send his word quickly. Sealed in the camp by Poyonga, with the seal of the general.
