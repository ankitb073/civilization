title: Prayer of a Farmer of Vemafshi for the Return of the Sun

To the gods of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]], each in their place and each to their due; from a farmer of the barley-lands below Vemafshi, who owns eleven terraces and a yoke of oxen and a debt; in the twentieth year of [[pol:1232|Munu]], in the reign of [[per:27965|Dyeybuthnilin]]; at the household altar, with salt and a little oil, since there is no more than a little oil.

**I. The complaint.**

Among our gods there is none for the sun. The old men say the sun is a lamp the gods set out, and that it was never ours to command. I accept the saying. But a man may pray to the servants when the master is not at home; and so I pray to you, who are at home in the sky and the field, to ask what has become of the lamp.

Early in this year the earth shook, and men said that a mountain had broken, the [[evt:28459|Eruption of Mount Tam'a]], far off, beyond places I have heard of only in the songs of carters. I did not believe a mountain so far could touch my terraces. I was wrong. First there came a red sunset that lasted from evening to night. Then a grey dust on the leaves that tasted of iron. Then the sun itself grew small and pale, like a coin seen at the bottom of a well, and has not grown again.

**II. What the cold has done.**

The barley headed late and filled thin. In the month that should have been the hottest of the year, my wife found frost on the water-trough. The beans are black. The oxen stand with their backs to a wind that should not blow in summer. My neighbour has killed his pig early because he cannot feed it, and we ate pork in a month when no one eats pork, and it was not a feast.

I say this not to accuse you, for a man who accuses the gods has already lost the argument; but so that it is written somewhere, and the writing reaches you, if writing reaches you.

**III. The petition.**

O [[dei:136|Dueminu]], lord of storms, who sweeps the sky: sweep it now. Take your great broom of wind and clear away this grey veil. You have made the heavens dirty before and made them clean; do it again.

O [[dei:139|Nithikhti]], lady of the grain: the barley is your daughter, and she is cold. Stand by her in the field. Breathe on her. Hold her up until the harvest, if there is a harvest.

O [[dei:138|Tuel]], lady of the moon: you have been red every night since then. Some here say it is an omen of blood, because of the war with Vemgenga and the soldiers who went from our village to the [[evt:28531|Battle of Viririnu]]. They came back, most of them, and boasted of a victory. I say a victory is a poor thing to eat. Turn your face white again, and quiet the talkers.

O Vinba of the earth, hold the warmth in the soil for the roots. O Navthin of the river, do not freeze.

**IV. The vow.**

If the sun returns to its proper size before the autumn sowing, I will give a lamb at the shrine of Nithikhti, though I have only two; and I will give the first measure of next year's barley to the widows of the village, without counting it.

I do not ask to understand. A farmer understands only what grows. The philosophers in the city may dispute whether the gods send such winters or only permit them; let them dispute it by a good fire. I ask only for the lamp to be set out again, at its full height, over Vemafshi.
