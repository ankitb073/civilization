*[Inner face of the sherd]*

To [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]], lady of the grain, who sits at the threshing floor and weighs the year: hear a woman of the villages of [[reg:19|Manke]], who has no altar but this broken jar, and no offering but the truth.

I do not come to you as the rich come, with a bull and a flute-player. The philosophers in the city say that prosperity is a sign of the gods' favour; if that is so, then you have turned your face from the whole of our valley, for there is no house in it that prospers. I do not accuse you. I ask only how long. The wheel turns, the priests say, and what ends will begin again. Then let this end, lady, so that the other thing may begin.

This was a jar of barley. I broke it myself when it was empty, because I could not bear to look into it. Now I write on the piece of it.

Hear what the year has been. In spring [[dei:16|Domamnol]] of the sky shut the clouds like a miser shutting his box. In summer the collectors came for the army that marches against Peme, and took the tenth, and then took the seed, saying the soldiers of the empress must eat before the farmers of the empress. Is that just? I have heard the orators say that a city is a body, and that the hand does not complain when it feeds the mouth. But the hand that is not fed does not lift the plough.

In the market of the town a measure of grain is two-thirds of a [[cur:628|Pemte]], and the sellers say it as if it were nothing. Two-thirds of a Pemte! Who has two-thirds of a Pemte, when the field gave nothing and the jar is broken? I sold my wedding bracelet for less than a Pemte and a half, and it fed us eleven days.

*[Outer face of the sherd]*

They tell us that our people won at Binge, and won at Binge again, that the [[evt:29109|Battle of Binge]] this year was a great victory, and that the [[evt:29086|Second Yarel–Peme War]] will bring lands and silver. My eldest son is with the army. He is nineteen. If he has eaten, I am glad of the victory. If he has not eaten, what is the victory to me?

[[dei:15|Venba]] of the earth, mother under my feet, I do not forget you. [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, who watches the women at night grinding acorns instead of wheat, I do not forget you. But it is to Nedhekhte that I scratch this, because it is the grain I need.

My husband's father says that sixteen years ago, when the great host passed through and fought at Ene, the fields of the valley were trampled flat and then came back green within three years. He says the earth forgets. I do not know if the earth forgets. I know the children do not.

Let [[per:28253|Yegan]], our empress, open her granaries in [[set:245|Yarel]] to the villages of the valley, as a good ruler should. I pray for her long life. I pray also that she hears of us.

Lady, if you send rain before the autumn sowing, I will bring you the first sheaf with my own hands, and the second sheaf, and a cake of the first flour baked on my own hearth. If you do not, I will bring you this sherd, and set it at your door, and you will know that I asked.

So I ask. So I have written it. Let the gods who keep accounts keep this one.
