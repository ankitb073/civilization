title: Winning Wars, Losing Provinces: The Breakup of Egu in AY 840
---
The kingdom of [[pol:58|Egu]] that broke apart in AY 840 had won every war the documentary consensus connects with its fall. That paradox is the subject of this article. The record lists five causes for the [[evt:4213|Collapse of the Egu State]]: four battles, all of them defeats, and one usurpation. I shall argue that the defeats matter as costs, not as verdicts, that the usurpation decided the moment, and that the shape of the collapse was fixed long before, by the way Egu had grown.

## Two victorious wars

The first war was one of conquest. In 767 Egu attacked the young Waga Empire, founded only in 760, for the region of Yizamashti, and by the Treaty of Shidyun in 777 it had won. But both battles at Yinishme went against it. At the [[evt:3765|Battle of Yinishme (AY 771)]] Queen Pukuipanset, commanding in person, brought 10,657 men against 5,151 and lost 3,374 against 285. At the [[evt:3780|Battle of Yinishme (AY 775)]] Egu lost 1,516 men against 406. Over the war Egu lost 8,276 men to Waga's 3,582. It took the province but paid more than twice the price.

The second war was defensive. In 820 Tonnga attacked, and Egu won eight of the ten pitched battles and the Treaty of Jerathnu in 830. The two exceptions were both at Nuwkennga, and both were heavy. At the [[evt:4068|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 823)]] Egu fielded 8,034 men against 4,028 and lost 3,296 against 279; in the same year Igu itself was besieged. At the [[evt:4095|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 826)]] Egu lost 4,248 men from an army of 11,314 against an enemy loss of 215. Over the war Egu lost 13,894 men to Tonnga's 6,107. In 826, the year of the second disaster, Queen Kayibiyat died and the [[evt:4092|Usurpation of Bepimttumin]] followed.

The court's view of these years survives in the Annals of Egu, AY 800–839, and it is instructive for what it omits. Tonnga's invasion appears as "Tunnga raided the border", the usurpation in a single plain line, "Bepimttumin made himself king", and the peace of 830 without a word of Nuwkennga. On the annalist's own selection, three of the fifteen battles Egu fought in those years are mentioned. A court that had just changed hands by force had every reason to present the war as a border incident handled and closed.

## Where the kingdom broke

The successor states show where the strain lay. The core kept Igu and the name of [[pol:184|Egu]] until 1229, and the Kingdom of [[pol:187|Runiy]], at Riniy, had been Egu's since 480. The other three formed in conquered land. The City-State of [[pol:188|Yisamashti]] arose at Namosash, the prize of the Treaty of Shidyun in 777. The City-State of [[pol:185|Tomözel]] arose at Ishwyithie, conquered in 785 and once held by an older Kingdom of Tomözel, whose name it revived. The Kingdom of [[pol:186|Birnadu]] (840–880) arose at Virnadhu, taken in 585. The kingdom that broke in 840 was, in large part, the kingdom Egu had assembled by war since the sixth century.

## Weighing the causes

The economy was not the problem. On the record's estimates Egu's population rose from about 3.2 million in AY 720 to about 4.7 million in 830, and the treasury stood roughly level through the Tonnga war. Climate offers little: the 770s were cold, and the Marsh Pestilence touched Egu between 765 and 771, but the record does not count either among the causes, and the kingdom grew through both.

What the record's measure of internal order does show is a collapse in 820, to almost nothing, followed by only a partial recovery. The defeats at Nuwkennga, the siege of the capital and the death of the queen came together within four years, and the throne passed to a usurper in the middle of the war. Bepimttumin won that war and another against Noltennga in 836–838, but he could not make a seized crown command the loyalty of provinces taken by the sword. When Egu broke in 840, it broke into the units from which it had been built. Its victories had added provinces. Its defeats and the usurpation had taught those provinces how little held them.
