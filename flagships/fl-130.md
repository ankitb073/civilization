*Written in my retirement, and finished on the 12th of Mue, 2456 of the [[mov:57|E'eshio]].*

**To the reader.** The printers who have agreed to set this book will no doubt call it the apology of a deposed king. I have chosen another title, and I ask the reader to suspend his laughter until he has heard my reasons. I was, I maintain, the first revolutionary in [[pol:1755|Tsiey]]. [[per:44072|Netleniem]] made the revolution with his speeches; I made it with my mistakes; and of the two contributions, mine was the larger.

**I. The debt of the old wars.** I was born into a kingdom that had not finished paying for its victories. Every child of my generation knew the names of [[evt:44189|Ometh]] and [[evt:44231|Ene]], where our armies were thrown back with a slaughter that the old soldiers still spoke of in lowered voices. That the war was afterwards won does not alter the arithmetic. Tens of thousands of families had given a son, and the crown had promised them, in return, a share in the greatness of the kingdom. The greatness came. The share did not. I inherited the promise and the unpaid account together.

**II. Of my own error.** I will not hide behind my forebears. In the fifteenth year of my reign I made war on Ool, and in the following year I sent against it the greatest host I could raise, under Mevash, a man still young, of excellent courage and insufficient patience. I have read the reports a hundred times. They say that the commissaries failed, that the rains came early, that the enemy knew our roads better than we did. All of this is true, and none of it is the reason. The reason is that I wished for a victory to set beside the old defeats, and I wished for it quickly. At [[evt:44667|Tunavükhini]] Mevash fell, and with him more than a hundred and forty thousand of the subjects of [[per:43961|Mezhash]]. I put my own name to that sentence deliberately.

**III. The days in Nemeth.** I shall be brief, because the scenes are well known and I do not come out of them well. Two years after that disaster the crowds filled the squares of [[set:76|Nemeth]]. They did not, as some pamphlets claim, break the palace gates; the guards opened them, which is worse. Netleniem came to me with a paper. He was civil. I signed. So began [[evt:44674|the revolution]], which the printers now date with such confidence, as though a kingdom ends on a particular morning. It had been ending for fifty years. I merely held the pen.

**IV. What the Republic has done.** Justice compels me to record that the Republic has done in three years what I failed to do in three. Its generals beat the army of Ool at [[evt:44699|Yinishme]], and again in the following summer; they sat down before Eol [[evt:44700|in the one year]] and [[evt:44730|in the next]], and took it; and by [[evt:44731|the conquest]] which President [[per:44114|Tlamen Ash]] signed last year, Ool is ours. I note, without bitterness, that the regiments which marched on Eol were raised and armed in my reign, and paid in the [[cur:1032|Ta]] notes that I first caused to be printed. A republic, like a son, inherits more than it acknowledges.

**V. On Netleniem.** He died last year, in his bed, at forty-four. I did not rejoice, and I was not asked to the funeral. He was a better speaker than he was a governor, and a better man than either. Had he been born in my place, and I in his, I believe we should each have done exactly as the other did.

**VI. Conclusion.** The teachers of the E'eshio say that the good life is compassion and release from suffering, and that all things turn on a great wheel. I have been on the top of the wheel and I am now beneath it, and I find that the view is clearer from here. If my countrymen ask whether they were right to put me aside, I answer: they were right to put aside a system in which so much depended on so fallible a man. They will discover, in time, whether a republic can be fallible too.

*Mezhash, formerly the second of that name.*
