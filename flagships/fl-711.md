**Early spring.** Third week without rain. The moisture reading on the cooperative's board says "deficit, severe" in red letters, which is the first time I have seen red on that board since I married into this farm. He says it will turn. His father said the same, he says, and it turned. I wrote it down so I can hold him to it.

**Spring, later.** Sowed the south field anyway. The seed loan from the co-op is due at harvest whether there is a harvest or not; that is in the contract, small print, page two. The girl helped me cut potatoes for planting and asked why we were burying food. I said so that it comes back more. She thought about it and said, "If it comes back."

**Start of summer.** Pump on the borehole has dropped its draw twice. The engineer from A'ja came out on his motorbike, put his gauge down the pipe and said the water table has gone down past the pump. A new pump goes deeper; a deeper pump needs more power; more power needs money. He was kind about it. He had three more farms to tell the same thing before dark.

**Summer.** Radio from the coast this morning: a man from some agency, with a soft voice, speaking about [[cli:251|Industrial Warming]] as if it were a guest who had stayed too long. Structural, he said. Our neighbour says it is the factories across the water, our neighbour's wife says it is the satellites. I only know the maize stopped at my hip and tasselled early and the cobs are the length of my thumb.

**Summer, late.** Sold the second cow. The buyer offered half what we paid for her, and he was right to; the auction yard is full of cows and nobody has hay. The cow knew. She would not go up the ramp until I went first.

**Harvest month.** Harvest was a quarter of a bad year. The co-op extended the loan by one season, with interest. I cried in the car where the children could not see.

**Autumn.** The news has started calling it the [[evt:48087|Famine in Mbambanlule]]. Strange to hear a name put on your own kitchen. There was a map on the screen at the post office with [[reg:29|Mbambanluli]] coloured dark orange, and a woman beside me pointed at it and said, "That's us," as if we had been picked for something.

**Autumn.** Relief lorry at the school on Thursday: flour, oil, dried beans, one tin of milk powder per child under five. My boy is six. I told them he is five. Let whoever keeps the accounts up there forgive me; the clerk already did. She looked at him and wrote five.

**Autumn, later.** The family at the end of the valley road has gone to A'ja to look for work at the port. The house is shut with a wire through the latch. Their dog comes to us for scraps and I give it the bones after we have boiled them twice.

**Early winter.** Bread is dear in town and dearer every week, and they say prices will rise again before spring. At the co-op meeting one man stood up and read out a long paper about [[evt:46452|the Industrial Warming]] and emissions and what the rich countries owe, and everyone agreed with him, and then we went home, and the field was still dry.

**Winter.** Rain, finally, three nights. Too late for anything but the weeds. I stood out in it in my nightdress like a fool. He came out and stood beside me. Neither of us said anything about his father.

The girl asked tonight if we will plant again in spring. I said yes. I do not know what with.
