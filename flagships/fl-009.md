*[Tablet broken at the top; the first lines are lost.]*

[…] the barley stood high […]  
[…] and the herdsmen of [[reg:24|Pyivi]] drove their cattle down.

Hear me, [[dei:73|Tithu]], lord of the storm, whose voice breaks the cedar.  
Hear me, [[dei:77|Pugazhu]], lord of the sun, who sees the field from end to end.  
Hear me, [[dei:74|Binge]], mother of the grain, who feeds the spearman and his ox.  
I will sing the [[evt:10052|war of Tagath and Kabave]],  
I will sing the twelve years and the thirteenth year,  
I will sing the men of [[pol:414|Tagath]] who went down into Pyivi.

Pyivi is a good land. Its fields are black, its water is sweet.  
The men of [[pol:377|Kabave]] said, "Pyivi is ours."  
The men of Tagath said, "Pyivi is ours."  
Therefore the spears were lifted. Therefore the oxen were yoked to the carts.

At Dadifi, by the walls of Tadüth, the first year was bitter.  
Tithu was angry; the rain came sideways; the carts sank in the mud.  
[[per:10296|Nangatsu]] the long-armed led the spears, and the spears came back few.  
Let the singer not count them. Let the singer go on.

At [[evt:10094|Dadifi]] a third time the spears went down,  
Nangatsu went down against [[per:9868|Nagungi]], king of Kabave, the old one.  
Nangatsu had many spears, Nagungi had few spears.  
Nagungi turned his back. The field was Tagath's.

*[Some lines lost.]*

[…] Then the king rose up, [[per:10255|Nakthi]], king of Tagath,  
Nakthi the young bull, Nakthi of the bronze collar.  
He poured beer to Tithu, he poured milk to Binge, he burned fat to Pugazhu.  
He said: "I will go down. I myself will go down."

At [[evt:10129|Dadifi]] Nakthi stood against Nagungi.  
From morning to evening they stood. The dust rose like smoke from a kiln.  
At evening Nagungi went back. The field was Nakthi's.

At [[evt:10132|Khavazhu]], by the walls of [[set:167|Kavazhu]],  
Nakthi stood against [[per:10304|Naguvu]], the general of Kabave.  
Naguvu's men fell like barley under the sickle,  
three thousand fell, and more than three thousand.  
Of Nakthi's men, a few. Of Naguvu's men, a harvest.

Then the spears of Tagath went up to [[set:12|Kabave]],  
Kabave of the high walls, Kabave the proud city.  
They went in at the gate. They went in at the breach.  
They brought out the bronze, they brought out the cloth, they brought out the oxen.  
This was [[evt:10133|the sacking]] of the city. Remember it.

At [[evt:10146|Dadifi]] a last time Nakthi stood against Nagungi.  
Nagungi had more spears, Nakthi had fewer spears.  
Five hundred of ours lay down; three thousand of theirs lay down.  
Pugazhu stood still in the sky to see it.

*[A gap of uncertain length. The lines that follow are damaged.]*

[…] at Khavazhu, by the river […] Nanüthi […]  
[…] the spears were weary, the oxen were weary […]

Then the elders of Tagath and the elders of Kabave sat down at Kavazhu.  
They poured the water out on the ground. They swore by the gods.  
"What is yours is yours; what is ours is ours," they said.  
Ten years the spear sleeps. Ten years the ox goes to the plough.  
What ends will begin again. The wheel turns.

*[Reverse.]*

Now [[per:10428|Netithmi]] is king in [[set:9|Tagath]],  
in the fourth year of Netithmi I set down this song.  
Netithmi has gone out against the men of Nginakhzi.  
Tithu, go before him. Pugazhu, light his road. Binge, fill his carts.  
As Nakthi stood, let Netithmi stand.
