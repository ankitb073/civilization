title: The Song of the Long Road, as the Umbumbi Sing It in Khishkhis

*Sung by the [[cul:7|Umbumbi]] at the evening fires, in the year 3004 of the [[mov:24|Udhungala]].*

Sing of the road, for the road was our only country  
seven years, from the dry hills to the green river;  
a country one cart wide and a thousand days long,  
whose towns were the fires we lit and left in the morning.  

In [[reg:21|Dadüdhif]] the rain forgot our names.  
The wells went down like old men into their graves,  
the barley stood up white and did not ripen,  
and the [[evt:27777|famine in Daadüdhih]] came in at every door.  

We buried the old first, as is fitting, and then the young,  
which is not fitting, and no god has explained it.  
Then the elders said: the earth will not have us living;  
let us go where the earth is kinder, and carry our dead in our songs.  

What is a homeland? the philosophers ask at their leisure.  
Is it the ground where the fathers lie, or the ground where the children eat?  
We answered with our feet, which is not philosophy,  
but a hungry man's logic is short, and seldom wrong.  

We came down into [[reg:20|Khishkhis]] with the dust on our shoulders,  
and the land was at war with the men of Kaazhe, over their gods;  
they had lost at the [[evt:27840|battle of Alunzhi]], and feared all strangers,  
and who could blame them, seeing so many of us on the road?  

Yet they did not shut the river against us.  
The emperor [[per:27402|Navavun]] made his peace at the [[evt:27866|Treaty of Luyululi]],  
and the men of Kaazhe paid for their war in silver;  
then the officers of [[pol:1125|Khishkhis]] came with ropes and measured us land.  

Land by the marsh, land that the Khishkhis did not want,  
land of frogs and reeds and midges; we drained it.  
The first year we ate the reeds. The second we ate barley.  
The third we sold barley in the market for good [[cur:630|Mbumbin]].  

A measure goes for three parts of a Mbumbin and a little more;  
our children laugh at us for counting every coin twice.  
They were born on the road, or after it. They do not remember  
the white barley, the wells, the graves we did not finish digging.  

The preachers of the Udhungala come to our fires and teach us:  
one power over all, and the old gods its servants;  
a straight road from the making to the judgement.  
We listen. We know something of straight roads.  

Some of our young men have gone up to [[set:85|Nambe]] to the great city,  
to carry loads and learn the speech of the market;  
they come back with new shoes and a new way of greeting.  
Let them. A people that cannot change is a people that walks again.  

Owe to the land that fed you, say the Khishkhis magistrates;  
pay your tax, keep the dikes, send your sons when the drum calls.  
This is fair. A guest who eats at the table should carry wood.  
We carry wood. We have carried heavier things.  

Sing of the road, children, though you never walked it;  
sing it at the fire, so the road will not have to be walked again.  
And when you are old, in this marsh made into fields,  
tell your children's children: we were [[cul:7|Umbumbi]], and we arrived.
