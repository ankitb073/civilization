The name [[per:28987|Natdatathrelen]] means "just strong", and his court called him "the Conqueror". The second word fits the record better than the first. In thirty-five years as emperor of [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]], from AY 3201 to 3236, he commanded in six battles and won all six. He conquered two states and made a third his vassal. Few rulers in this series were so consistently successful in the field. Yet he was also the last of a house that had ruled Lienonoshal since its founding three and a quarter centuries earlier, and he died leaving it to a usurper. This chapter asks what the justice and the strength amounted to, and why neither could keep the throne in his family.

## Heir to an old house

He was born in AY 3174, a Jinru of the house of Talediemenen I, remembered as "the Cruel", who had founded Lienonoshal in 2911 on the ruins of the old Yeda empire. By Natdatathrelen's birth the house had produced eleven rulers. His father, [[per:28212|Vievmagonen]], reigned for forty-two years and was himself a conqueror. In 3179 he took land from the Yarel empire, and in 3192 he annexed a city-state after a long war in which, by the record's count, Lienonoshal lost fewer than three hundred men. Vievmagonen raised steles for his victories. His son grew up knowing that a king of Lienonoshal was measured by them.

The [[evt:29378|Accession of Natdatathrelen]] in 3201, after his father's natural death, gave him the throne at twenty-seven. The empire was large and, by the record's measure, steady. It had some 280 districts and twenty-two million people, and its capital was Lenunushal, where twenty-three years later he restored the palace. The limestone slab recording that work survives (PAL·IN·05432): "May Yienan bless the city."

## The holy war on Tallen

He inherited a war. In 3200 his father had launched the [[evt:29372|Holy War of Lienonoshal against Tallen]] against a kingdom that had broken away from its neighbour Peme forty years before and had lately been seized by a usurper, [[per:28938|Vevbon]]. Its capital was the ancient city of the Yeda emperors. In his first campaign Natdatathrelen met Vevbon at the [[evt:29391|Battle of Puthnimdu (AY 3202)]], with 116,964 men against 11,727. Vevbon died on the field. His son [[per:28994|Nathbipavbukhin]], a boy of eleven, inherited the war. The emperor beat the boy-king's general, [[per:29039|Baltafbafen]], at the [[evt:29426|Battle of Baaliurinu (AY 3207)]] and again at the [[evt:29438|Battle of Shivange (AY 3209)]]. The [[evt:29439|Conquest of Tallen]], sealed at [[set:66|Shivange]] by the seventeen-year-old king, annexed thirteen districts. Nathbipavbukhin, called "the Last", lived another fifty-seven years.

The defeated side's account (PAL·CH·05334), written nine years later by a historian of the fallen city, claims that Lienonoshal lost 2,708 men at Baaliurinu and 1,821 at Shivange. The record gives 794 and 534. The account passes over two of the four battles, including Puthnimdu, where its own king fell. Its narrative ends with a formula that other defeated chroniclers also used: "The royal city was taken. Who will now remember the kings of old?" The war had cost Lienonoshal 2,409 men and Tallen 6,119.

## Yarel's last fragment

The [[evt:29533|Lienonoshal–Yida War]] (3215–3218) was aimed at the city-state of [[pol:1264|Yida]], which held the old city of Yarel itself. It was one of the fragments of the Yarel empire, which had broken apart in 3198. Natdatathrelen commanded both battles. At the [[evt:29569|Battle of Poynge (AY 3217)]] he beat [[per:29171|Tathtuvan]] with 91,810 men against 5,957, losing 1,072 of them, and his army went on to sack Yaaril. At the [[evt:29586|Battle of Yaaril (AY 3218)]] he beat Tathtuvan again. The [[evt:29588|Conquest of Yida]] was signed at [[set:245|Yaaril]] by [[per:29181|Zhiymagunin]], whose reign lasted only that year and earned him the name "the Brief". The Yida account survives only as two sentences quoted by a later writer (PAL·CH·05358). The second reads: "In the 1st year of Zhiymagunin [AY 3218], the last of the strongholds fell." Sixteen districts passed to Lienonoshal.

## The trade war and the stele

His last campaign was for markets, not land. The [[evt:29674|Eleventh Diebeguvebenga Trade War]] (3225–3227) was fought against the city-state of [[pol:1269|Zhidhzhinga]], founded only in 3200. At the [[evt:29696|Battle of Zhidhzhinge (AY 3226)]] the emperor, now fifty-two, led 115,152 men against 1,061 under [[per:29283|Kultafbafin]]. He won, losing 35 men to the enemy's 337, and the city was sacked and its archives burned. His diorite stele there claims "He killed 2,300 soldiers" (PAL·IN·02228), seven times the truth. The bronze text of the [[evt:29707|Treaty of Zhidhzhinge (AY 3227)]] (PAL·TR·01932), granted to [[per:28944|Pofdon]], the city's ruler since its founding, shows what the war was for. One district was ceded. Zhidhzhinga became a vassal, and "the merchants of Lienonoshal shall trade in the markets of Zhiedhzhenga free of all tolls". Like other treaties of the age, its oath closes: "Let it be read aloud in the markets of both lands." The war had cost 74 men of Lienonoshal and 390 of the defenders. The heavier price for toll-free markets was paid by the city.

One gain came without a war. In 3212 two neighbouring kingdoms had sworn a thirty-year alliance against Lienonoshal. By 3230 one of them, Oshodhonga, had been annexed outright, and the record shows no battle.

## The end of the house

Natdatathrelen's family is a short entry in the record. He had one daughter, born in 3194, and she died in 3228 at thirty-four. No son is recorded. His wife outlived him by eighteen years. He died of natural causes in AY 3236, aged sixty-two. In the same year the [[evt:29778|Usurpation of Lafmodhalen]], which the record marks as a crisis, gave the throne to [[per:29364|Lafmodhalen]], a man of no recorded lineage. The house of Talediemenen ended after three hundred and twenty-five years. A trade war already under way was concluded by the usurper. [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]] itself lasted until 3320.

The record allows a fair judgement. Under Natdatathrelen the empire grew to some 340 districts and more than twenty-six million people. Its treasury grew and its order held for most of the reign. Against that stand some 4,000 soldiers of Lienonoshal killed in his three wars, and some 11,000 of their enemies. Tallen and Yida were swallowed whole, and Zhidhzhinga's archives burned for the sake of its tolls. "The Conqueror" is exact. "Just" is harder to find in the record, which shows only his strength. That strength was enough to win every battle he fought, but not to pass the throne on to his own house.
