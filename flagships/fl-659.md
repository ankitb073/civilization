This series files [[per:24377|Nuefmaleduen]] among the founders, and the label needs defending. He founded no faith and left no teaching. What he founded was a constitution. In AY 2780 he led the reform that made the city-state of [[pol:1061|Ego]] a merchant republic. Seventeen states in the record made that change. Ego was the fifth, and the only one whose republic did not outlive the year it was proclaimed. In that same year Nuefmaleduen signed his state away in two pieces and was put to death. I have read every document the record keeps from his city's last decade, and his name is on none of them.

## A man without a household

The record is thin, and I will say where it is silent. He was a man of the Jinru people, born in 2737; his name means *lion son*. The record names no father, mother, wife or child, and gives him no office before the one he took. Whether he was himself a merchant, it does not say.

His world we can see. He was born a subject of Rieney, under Queen Shieyenen, who reigned 2718–2771. When he was eleven, the town of Igu sent 669 heavy infantry to Rieney's muster for the Battle of Yishdhagshal; the roll survives in a medieval copy at Riniy (PAL·MU·00391). Rieney lost that war and became a vassal in 2749. In the surveys of the 2740s and 2750s its stability stood at zero, the floor of the scale. In 2771, when he was thirty-four, the kingdom broke apart. [[set:63|Igu]], a port of some twenty thousand people whose scribes wrote its name "Ego", became the capital of a new city-state of eighteen districts.

## The queen's city

Ego's first ruler was [[per:24226|Luelath I]], later called the Brave, then twenty-six. Her bronze plaque from the city granary, dated 2773 and still legible, says she "restored this granary in Ego. May Niedhekhte bless the city" (PAL·IN·05311). It is the only surviving voice of Ego's own government. The state had a historian, Tafetuevan, appointed in 2778. Nothing he wrote survives.

In 2777 two kingdoms attacked together. One was [[pol:1009|Zhievampeva]], ruled since 2775 by a usurper's daughter. The other was [[pol:1013|Diebegovebenga]], whose army at the end of the 2770s numbered some 30,000 men. Ego fought the [[evt:24530|Zhievampeva–Ego War]] and the [[evt:24531|Diebegovebenga–Ego War]] at once and lost all six battles. Three were fought at Togannge in two years, against both enemies. Luelath I fell commanding there in 2778, aged thirty-three. Her daughter, [[per:24357|Luelath II]], came to the throne at twelve under a regent, Vieduen. The general Shiefmanen commanded the other five battles. At the second battle of Togannge in 2779 he led 3,256 men against 19,385.

The victors' stones tell it their own way. Takhadhath's stele for the first battle of 2779 says "She killed 3,500 soldiers" (PAL·IN·01809). The record counts 1,043 Ego dead, and her general Tamagonen, not the queen, held the command. Tuebanaven's stele for the second battle claims 4,800 dead (PAL·IN·01810); the record counts 801.

## The year 2780

The record dates all that follows to one year, without months; I follow the order of its entries. Nuefmaleduen, then forty-three, led the [[evt:24572|Merchant republic Reform in Ego]], and the thirteen-year-old queen was deposed. The record does not say why. The state faith, [[mov:2|Jinru]], held that prosperity is a sign of divine favour, and a city of traders could read two years of defeat as a judgement on its dynasty. The same faith prized duty, order and authority handed down in sequence. That was the child queen's argument. Which was spoken aloud, nothing records.

Then the army, though it outnumbered the enemy 5,800 to 4,232, was beaten near Ushdhnge, losing 2,142 men to Zhievampeva's 400. Nuefmaleduen made peace. The [[evt:24579|Treaty of Ushdhnge (AY 2780)]] was signed at [[set:94|Ushdhnge]], a port held by Rieney, the other successor of the old kingdom. By it he gave [[per:24291|Takhadhath]] five districts, among them Togannge. It lay at the far end of Igu's busiest trade road and was the seat of a merchant house. In return Ego received a fifteen-year truce. Takhadhath, who would live to be called the Old, was twenty-five.

The truce did not save the state. Diebegovebenga's general Duemagonen beat Shiefmanen at Igu, 6,539 men against 5,246, killing 2,299 for the loss of 144. At Igu, Nuefmaleduen put his name to the [[evt:24581|Conquest of Ego]] beside [[per:24024|Tuebanaven]], a king of sixty-four who had reigned for thirty years. The last thirteen districts passed to Diebegovebenga. The record lists his death as an execution tied to the conquest, without saying who gave the order.

## What the stones remember

The two wars cost Ego 7,635 men and its enemies 1,593. Others survived the fall. Luelath II, whom he had deposed, lived until 2821. Shiefmanen, who lost five battles, died a natural death in 2795. The record styles both the girl and the man who replaced her *the Last*.

Nambili's report on Igu passes straight from a destruction layer of 1981 to one of 3938 (PAL·EX·00089). Whatever the conquerors did in 2780, they did not burn the city. Perhaps the surrender spared it; perhaps a king who meant to keep a port had no reason to burn it. The record does not decide. Igu rose again as Ego in 2806 under a claimant, Ovieth, as a kingdom, not a republic; the revolt was crushed in 2810.

Two and a half centuries later, a diviner at Biniynge copied its omen for his empress: "the prince will set his throne in the palace of his enemy. It is the omen of Tuebanaven, who conquered Ego" (PAL·OM·00252). Three of that tablet's twenty-six omens concern the war. None names the man who signed the surrender. In his city's worst year he took a crown from a child to make a merchant republic, then signed twice for a city he could not defend. Reformer or merely the man left holding the seal, he paid with his life. His conquerors' scribes forgot him; only the record keeps his name.
