The Age of Sail and Print (AY 4593–4741) is named for its inventions, and the inventions deserve their fame. The record credits [[per:43826|Ipil Nyime]] with the [[evt:44391|Invention of Movable-type printing in Ane]] in AY 4593, three years before his death; the [[evt:44508|Invention of Joint-stock companies in Shivange]] followed in 4601, and the [[evt:44623|Invention of Experimental science in Tsey]] in 4613, credited to Ae Niem. (A single name attached to an invention is a convenience of the record, not a biography; I use the names as the record gives them.) My argument tonight is that these were not what the age's own people would have named it for. The event that shaped their experience was the meeting of two continents, and what the ships carried home.

## Contact and the cough

In AY 4623 came the [[evt:44706|First Contact at Igu]], linking the ports of [[set:41|Dyigini]] and [[set:63|Igu]]; our own [[pol:1638|Khishkhis]] was one of the two parties. Five years later [[evt:44707|The Marsh Cough]] broke out at Igu. By 4638 it had reached more than fifty towns, among them [[set:43|Luluunul]] and Togannge, falling on populations that had never met it. The record names the contact among its causes, puts its mortality at a little over two in five of those it reached, and its dead at nearly thirty million. A figure of that size deserves caution, and I give it with caution; but even halved, it would make the Marsh Cough the defining catastrophe of the age.

## Collapse, war and weather

Five great states fell between 4628 and 4664: the [[evt:44767|Collapse of the Batlshi State]] (4628), the [[evt:44813|Collapse of the Dagafu State]] (4634), the [[evt:44891|Collapse of the Fiw State]] (4640), the [[evt:44902|Collapse of the Binge State]] (4642) and the [[evt:45115|Collapse of the Tomzel State]] (4664). It is tempting to lay them all at the cough's door. The record does not allow it. Only the [[pol:1862|Binge]] empire's collapse is tied to the plague directly, and even Binge had just lost a great war against Tallin, ended in 4621. The rest follow war and civil strife: Batlshi had three civil wars in thirteen years; the [[pol:1670|Dagafu]] empire broke after campaigns against Tomzel in 4632–4633 in which it put armies approaching a million into the field; Fiw had failed in a war against [[pol:1656|Ane]]; and the [[pol:1795|Tomzel]] kingdom fell after losing a holy war to [[pol:1755|Tsey]] and enduring two civil wars of its own.

Then there is the weather. The [[evt:44705|Onset of the Great Drought (AY 4628)]] came in the very year of the cough, and that drought, lasting until 4704, was followed by others: [[cli:242|The Great Drought of AY 4669–4680]], [[cli:245|The Great Drought of AY 4691–4717]] and [[cli:247|The Great Drought of AY 4715–4768]]. They overlap so closely that from 4628 to the end of the age some region was always in drought. Yet the record names drought among the causes of none of the age's collapses. My verdict, weighing the three, is this. War remained the commonest killer of states. The cough was the greatest killer of people. Drought was the age's constant companion, and on the record's evidence it ruined harvests rather than thrones; if it weakened states, it did so in ways the record does not trace.

## What defined the age

Seen this way, the inventions and the catastrophe belong to one story. The ships that made contact possible made the kind of voyages joint-stock companies existed to finance; the same sea-lanes that carried trade carried the cough. Printing multiplied texts, and with them, I suspect, the record's own capacity to notice a plague on this scale. The age's name tells us what later centuries valued. Had the people of Igu and Luluunul named it, I think they would have named it for the cough.

The age opened, it should be remembered, with battles on a scale earlier ages never knew, such as the [[evt:44439|Battle of Navnge (AY 4593)]], fought in the very year of the first printed page. Connection did not replace war. It added a second way for the world to wound itself.
