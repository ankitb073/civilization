*[Obverse. A ruled tablet; each strophe is closed by a double line.]*

Weep, O [[dei:15|Vinba]], mother of the black earth, you who keep the bones,  
weep, O [[dei:19|Yenan]], lamp of the night, you who count the dead,  
weep, O [[dei:18|Eva]] of the hearth, for the hearths that are cold,  
weep for the sons of [[pol:31|Tathrelbu]], weep for the nineteen.

In the first year of Tathrelbu the city stood up.  
The city of [[set:96|Tathrelbu]] stood up from under the hand of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]].  
[[per:1089|Ukutnakhin]] stood up, Ukutnakhin of the bronze arm,  
he broke the yoke-pin, he cut the yoke-strap, he said: "We are our own."  
This was the [[evt:814|revolt of Tathrelbu]]; the gods saw it.  
The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] turned the wheel at the gate,  
and the men of Tathrelbu said: "What ends will begin again."

Weep, O Vinba. Weep for the nineteen.

In the second year of Tathrelbu they went out to Linnshal.  
Thirty-nine went out, thirty-nine captains of shields went out,  
and seven thousand spears went out behind them.  
Against them came the queen of Lenunushal,  
[[per:1072|Jilialmagunath]], the queen with hard spears,  
fewer than ours, but each spear sharp as the reed of the river.  
At the [[evt:821|battle of Linnshal]] the thirty-nine stood.  
They stood like the stone of the threshold; they did not run.  
Thirteen lay down at Linnshal; Vinba took them.  
And with them lay their men, two thousand and seven hundred,  
for each of theirs four of ours and more, in the barley of [[reg:8|Duvekopuvinga]].  
Twenty-six came home. Twenty-six came home to Tathrelbu.

Weep, O Yenan. Weep for the thirteen.

In the third year of Tathrelbu they went out to Nilu.  
Seventeen went out, seventeen captains went out, and two thousand and five hundred behind them,  
for the others were wounded, and the others were old.  
Against them came the queen a second time, with spears like the rain.  
At the [[evt:826|battle of Nilu]] the seventeen stood.  
Six lay down at Nilu, and five hundred of their men; Vinba took them.  
Eleven came home. Eleven came home to Tathrelbu.

Weep, O Eva. Weep for the six.

*[Some lines are lost. Traces: "[…] the gate […]", "[…] his hand under her hand […]".]*

[…] then Ukutnakhin went out from the gate of the city.  
He went out alone; he put his hand under the hand of the queen.  
This was the [[evt:827|suppression of the Tathrelbu revolt]]; the city bowed its head.  
They did not let him come home. They put him to death, Ukutnakhin,  
he who was the king of Tathrelbu, he who was ours.  
He went down under the earth; Vinba took him.  
Who will pour the beer for him? Who will set the bread for him?  
We pour it. We set it. We of Tathrelbu pour it.

Weep, O Vinba. Weep for the king.

*[Reverse.]*

Thirteen at Linnshal, six at Nilu, nineteen.  
Nineteen, and the king the twentieth.  
Let the singer say it twice: nineteen, and the king the twentieth.  
Three thousand and two hundred lay down with them. They are not forgotten.  
The great kings count their dead by the thousand;  
Tathrelbu counts her dead by name, at every new moon, at every harvest.  
Their names are not on this tablet, for the tablet may be broken.  
Their names are in the mouths of their mothers.

Hear, O [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear, who loves the brave:  
you saw the thirty-nine; you saw the seventeen.  
Hear, O [[dei:16|Dumamnul]], lord of the high sky, who sees the roads:  
you saw the road to Linnshal; you saw the road to Nilu.  
The wheel turns. What ends will begin again.  
Nidhikhti makes the barley stand up after the sickle.

Weep, O Yenan. Weep, and keep the count.

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Written in the 27th year of Tathrelbu, for the new moon of the dead.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Vinba refuse his bones.
