*Ode for the Fortieth Year of the Reign of His Imperial Majesty [[per:46334|Shifin]], Emperor of [[pol:1959|Yidhe]]. Printed by the Imperial Printing Office for distribution in all schools, barracks and railway stations, and to be read aloud at the jubilee assemblies of 5 Ningikh 2672 of the Vishithmikh.*

I.

Ring out, you bells of [[set:0|Taulin]], and let the wires carry the sound  
from the capital's high windows to the farthest frontier town;  
let the engines on the iron road whistle all along the line:  
for forty years Shifin has reigned, and forty years have been fine.

II.

He was a son of kings. His father, [[per:46138|Nathuunun]], wore a crown;  
yet the son was not content to wear the greatness handed down.  
At twenty he took the throne of Yidhe while the old men shook their heads.  
Forty years on, the old men sleep, and the Empire stands where he led.

III.

He did not wait for cities. He built them out of the plain:  
the [[evt:47562|Founding of Nuthudunath]] was his word made stone, lane on lane,  
where once the wind went over grass and nothing moved but the sheep,  
now the lamps burn electric all night, and the people are able to sleep.

IV.

At the [[evt:47594|Battle of Piyikhzhidhe]] five millions marched beneath the eagle's wing.  
The enemy broke like river ice when the floods come in the spring.  
And at the [[evt:47595|Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe]] he set down his pen  
and gave the land of the borderers twenty years of peace with his men.

V.

When the [[evt:47633|Yidhe–Divigvivinge War]] called him he did not shrink from the call.  
In the [[evt:47650|Tenth Battle of Luunge]] the guns were heard on the wall,  
and of all ten battles there, that is the one the grandchildren will recall:  
the field was ours by midsummer, and the gates of Himi gave way by the fall.

VI.

At the [[evt:47656|Battle of Budh]] nine million stood in their ranks, a host no eye could measure,  
and half a million faced them across the trenches, hoarding the ground like treasure.  
They held, and then they did not hold. The telegraph clattered all night,  
and in every town of Yidhe the windows were bright with light.

VII.

Then at Himi the gates were opened, and the [[evt:47658|Treaty of Himi]] signed,  
and the provinces came home to the Empire, and the old border was redrawn.  
Twenty years of truce were sworn there. Let anyone who doubts it look  
at the cornfields round Luunge and the ledger in the treasury book.

VIII.

Nor did he forget the sea, nor the shores beyond the brine:  
he made the [[evt:47700|Yidhe Claim against Miup]] and set it down in a firm line.  
He sent the ships and soldiers of the [[evt:47701|Yidhe Colonial War in Anel]]  
so that every coast of the world should know the colours of Taulin.

IX.

Ask the workman at the furnace and the farmer with his plough,  
ask the baker at his counter what a measure costs him now:  
less than a [[cur:1121|Tafbaf]] buys it, and no oven stands cold.  
That is the empire Shifin built, and it is worth more than gold.

X.

The [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] teaches mercy, and the ending of all pain,  
and that every road runs onward to a judgement, never to come back again.  
When the One Power weighs the rulers, let this reign be weighed as well:  
forty years of bread and order, more than any tongue can tell.

XI.

And may his son, [[per:46814|Nathuunun]], keep the lamp that his father lit;  
may he learn the craft of ruling at the foot of the throne, and sit  
in the council at his father's side until the day that the One allows.  
Long live the Emperor [[per:46334|Shifin]]! The Empire renews its vows.

*The One Power save the Emperor. Copies of this ode may be had at the Imperial Printing Office at one-tenth of a Tafbaf, the proceeds to the fund for veterans.*
