**OFFICIAL GAZETTE — SPECIAL SUPPLEMENT**  
**[[set:0|Tallin]], 7 NINGIKH 2590 OF THE VISHITHMIKH**

*His Majesty has commanded that the following letter, written in his own hand in answer to a letter from a private soldier of the Yiwutu Expeditionary Army, be printed in full and posted in every barracks, railway station and post office of the Empire.*

* * *

**[[per:45868|Alintin]], by the grace of the One, King of [[pol:1959|Yidhe]], to a soldier of the Expeditionary Army in [[reg:5|Yiwutu]], greeting.**

My lad,

Your letter reached me by the field post, stained with the mud of the river country, and I have read it three times. You wrote, among other things, these words, which I set down here so that the whole nation may read them with me:

> *"Sire, they told us at the depot that the natives would run at the first volley. They did not run. At [[set:15|Miyidhinithiyi]] we went forward in our hundreds of thousands, and our hundreds of thousands lie there still. I do not ask to come home. I ask only to know what we are doing here."*

You deserve an answer, and you shall have one, from one soldier to another; for a king who sends men to war is the first soldier of his nation, or he is nothing.

**First, the truth.** At the [[evt:46850|Battle of Myizinithie]] the field was not ours. I will not insult you, who were there, by pretending otherwise. General [[per:45988|Baaluthvaanavin]] led you forward with a courage that no one who saw it will question; and the enemy's commander, [[per:45984|Yuyazhu]], held his ground behind earthworks and wire that our intelligence officers had not reported. Our losses were grievous. Every widow's pension that leaves the Treasury this winter bears my signature, and I sign each one.

**Second, the other truth.** The enemy has not printed his own butcher's bill, and he will not, because he dare not. Those who have come over to our lines tell of whole districts emptied, of trenches filled from end to end, of a native army that won the field and could not afterwards stand upon it. Mark me: the men of [[pol:1966|Miyidhinithiyi]] paid for their day more dearly than their chiefs will ever admit to their own people.

**Third, what we are doing there.** Last year the Empire laid its lawful claim to Yiwutu, by the [[evt:46829|Yidhe Claim against Myizinithie]], and the chiefs of that country answered with rifles. The [[evt:46830|Yidhe Colonial War in Yiutl]] is the consequence of their refusal, not of our ambition. We go to Yiwutu to bring roads where there are footpaths, wire where there are drums, and the teaching of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]], which is addressed to every people under heaven, where there are only the rites of a small cult. If that is not worth a hard campaign, nothing is.

**Fourth, remember what this nation can do.** In this same year our armies broke the enemy at the [[evt:46844|Battle of Yinnge]], and I myself set my name to the [[evt:46846|Treaty of Riniy]] upon terms that our grandfathers would not have dared to ask. The Empire that did that has not forgotten how to win. It has merely been reminded that victory is not delivered by the parcel post.

**Lastly, what I ask of you.** Hold your line. Write to your mother. Do not believe the café strategists of Tallin who have never smelt cordite. Bread is dear at home this year, dearer than any of us would wish, at six [[cur:1092|Ir]] and a half the measure; but the nation is feeding you first, and will go on doing so.

I do not know how long this war will last. No honest man does. I know only that it will not be ended by our despair, and that the One, who sees the line of time from its beginning to its judgement, does not forget the men who stood their ground.

Your King, and your comrade,

**ALINTIN**

* * *

*Copies may be had gratis at any post office. Commanders will read this letter aloud at evening parade.*
