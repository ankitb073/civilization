title: Ola of Tomözel: the Victorious queen who outlived her empire

Four inscriptions survive in the name of [[per:21220|Ola]], queen of [[pol:890|Tomözel]] from AY 2512 to 2560. Three are victory monuments and one is a boundary stone. All are cut in the same stiff formula. The first reads: "Ola, queen of Tomözel, defeated Anavzo in Zobon. He killed 1,400 soldiers. He erected this stele for Etlie, his lord." The masculine pronoun is not a slip of the mason. The formula was made for kings, and the stonecutters kept it when a queen commissioned it. To an archaeologist the inscriptions are a reminder that a monument records its own conventions before it records anything else. Ola's reign needs reading with that in mind. Tradition calls her the Victorious, and she won most of her battles. She also lost more than fifty districts to a single treaty, saw her niece executed after a rebellion, and lived four years after the state she ruled had ceased to exist.

## The orphan queen

She was born in 2494, a La'im princess of the Setlietlsima house, which had ruled Tomözel since the empire's founding in 2412. Her father was [[per:20934|Shoe Toa]], called the Fair, who had reigned since 2486. In 2512 both her parents died, and the [[evt:21355|Accession of Ola]] made her queen at eighteen of some five and a half million people, ruled from Ishwyithie. She never married, and the record gives her no children. Her nearest kin was a younger sister, Nimesh, who shared their father's epithet, and later Nimesh's daughter.

## The years of conquest

Her first war was one of conquest. In the [[evt:21382|Tomözel–Anavuru War]] of 2517–2522 she led armies of thirty and fifty thousand against the old kingdom of Anavuru, which had stood for more than a century and was then ruled by [[per:21003|Nuflukhnen]], called the Pious. Its field armies numbered barely thirteen hundred men. Her rock reliefs claim 1,400 and 1,700 enemy dead. The record gives about three hundred in each battle, and nearly 17,600 dead on her own side over the war. The town of Anavun was besieged for the ninth time in its history, and the [[evt:21441|Conquest of Anavuru]] in 2522 added thirteen districts.

In the [[evt:21490|Second Tomözel–Yisamashti War]] she defeated another ruling queen, [[per:21254|Fitlma]] of Yisamashti, thirteen years her elder, at Yinishme in 2530. The [[evt:21537|Treaty of Bakh (AY 2530)]] gave Tomözel seventeen districts, three towns and an indemnity. Bakh itself had been sacked in two successive years. The boundary stone she set up in the new lands is among the best preserved of her inscriptions. Its chips do not touch the curse it calls down on her enemies. By 2530 her empire held nearly three hundred districts and more than six million people. It was the high point of the reign.

## Two invasions

In 2536 the [[pol:801|Yennga]] empire attacked, beginning the [[evt:21564|Yennga–Tomözel War]]. In 2538 [[pol:866|Yide]] attacked as well, in the [[evt:21581|Yide–Tomözel War]]. For three years Ola defended two frontiers, and at first she defended them well. She threw back a Yennga army at Nuvun in 2538. In 2539, at the [[evt:21599|Battle of Kheye (AY 2539)]], she met a Yide army larger than her own and destroyed it. Yide lost more than twenty-five thousand men and Tomözel under nine thousand. It is the one victory of her reign that needs no exaggeration. The [[evt:21639|Treaty of Kheye (AY 2541)]] made Yide pay an indemnity and open its trade. It was sworn with the Yide emperor [[per:21150|Shuyalun I]], who died in battle that same year.

The other war went the other way. At the [[evt:21637|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 2541)]] a Yennga army of eighty-four thousand broke hers, and in 2542 she lost again at Nuvun. Each defeat cost about thirteen thousand dead. In 2542 her capital, Ishwyithie, was sacked, and in 2543 it was besieged. The [[evt:21654|Treaty of Ishwyithie (AY 2543)]], sworn in her own ruined capital with the Yennga emperor [[per:21391|Omanen]], took fifty-four districts. Among the towns lost was Anavun, her first conquest. Tomözel was also bound to pay tribute for thirty-seven years. The empire that had grown under her by some thirty districts now lost more than fifty in one stroke. Her treasury never recovered. By her last decade it was less than a third of what she had inherited.

## The niece's rebellion

Then her own family divided. In 2544 the province of Shetsi seceded, and the [[evt:21662|First Tomözel Civil War]] began. Its general, [[per:21487|Esie Toa]], fought the queen twice at Tiaiosh with eighteen men and then with twenty-eight. Both times he lost, and both times the royal army lost thousands. In 2547 her niece [[per:21509|Setlietlobi]], twenty-two years old and the daughter of Ola's sister, became ruler of Shetsi. She signed the [[evt:21688|Suppression of the Shetsi Revolt]] at Tiaiosh that year, and that same year she was executed. Her mother, Nimesh, died the same year. The record does not say who ordered the execution, and it tells us nothing of the queen's feelings. It tells us only that by the end of 2547 Ola had no living kin whom the record names.

The last decade brought no relief. In the [[evt:21679|Fourth Tamthal Trade War]] Ana attacked, and at the [[evt:21696|Battle of Foyitlithyi (AY 2549)]] Ola suffered her third defeat. The [[evt:21734|Treaty of Fyinan (AY 2552)]] ended that war with trading rights from Ana, which was more than a defeated queen could have hoped for. In 2551 Shetsi rose again, opening the [[evt:21727|Second Tomözel Civil War]], under another ruler named [[per:21568|Esie Toa]], a woman remembered as the Brief. This time the queen was not on the field. A stele of 2554 claims a hundred enemy dead at Tunavükhini. When the revolt was put down in 2555, its leader was not executed. She lived for another thirty-three years.

## After the empire

In 2560 the Tomözel empire collapsed into six successor states, and with it ended the house that had held it for a century and a half. A new Tomözel empire rose among them, but the record does not make Ola its ruler. She lived on, without a throne, until 2564, and died a natural death at seventy. It is an unusual fate for a ruler.

The epithet is not wholly undeserved. She commanded in ten battles and won seven, one of them, Kheye, against a larger army. Yet the victories against small kingdoms cost thousands of her soldiers, and the defeats against Yennga cost her the conquests themselves. The inscriptions record none of this. They give her a man's pronoun, a god, and some numbers in round hundreds.
