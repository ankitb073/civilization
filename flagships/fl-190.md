*Lecture delivered by Chairman [[per:47103|Nazhigi]] before the National Academy of History, [[set:85|Nambi]], 2 Bini 2532 of the Vimikhulu. Official text, as authorised for publication by the Secretariat.*

Comrades, members of the Academy, citizens listening by wire in the provincial halls:

I have been asked why the Chairman should spend an evening on events forty centuries old, when there is a war to direct and a harvest to bring in. I answer that a people which does not know how states die will not know how to keep its own alive. The archive is not a museum. It is an arsenal.

Consider the figures, which the Academy's own archivists have compiled. In a span of some six and a half centuries, beginning about forty-five centuries before our day, no fewer than fourteen great states of the ancient world broke apart. [[evt:1589|Yida]] went first, into six pieces. Then [[evt:2822|Talenonoy]], into three. Then, within a single generation, [[evt:4038|Navnga]] into six, [[evt:4077|Tallin]] into five, Nelo into six, Egu into five. Later [[evt:5441|Mii]] fell into six, and Luthe into six, and Nelo a second time into four, and Yida a second time into two. [[evt:6358|Khie'a]] split in two; Bäni into four; [[evt:6753|I'ith]] into six; and at the last [[evt:6958|Tallin]] a second time, into six. Fourteen great states became sixty-seven lesser ones.

What brought them down? Not, in most cases, a foreign conqueror. The tablets of those years speak of usurpers, of pestilence, of governors who kept the tribute for themselves, of kings who could not command the loyalty of the next valley. They died of division; of a want of discipline.

And here the archive teaches a second lesson: the ancients lied. They lied about their wars as freely as they lied about their kings.

Take [[evt:548|the First Navnga–Vuvo War]]. One account preserved in the archive says Vuvo began it and lost fourteen districts. Another says no battle was fought at all, and that the war ended in an honourable peace with no land changing hands. The Academy, weighing every surviving source, reckons two battles, both won by Navnga, and eight districts gained. Three accounts, three wars.

Take [[evt:1110|the Talenonoy–Shue'ethie War]]. One chronicle gives the dead of Shue'ethie as eighteen thousand and more. Another gives the dead of Talenonoy as above nine thousand and calls the war a stalemate settled over a few villages. The more careful reckoning is six battles, three to each side, some five thousand dead for Talenonoy and some six thousand seven hundred for Shue'ethie, and six districts to Talenonoy.

Take [[evt:1416|the Second Virnado–Talenonoy War]]. One account claims forty-five districts won. Another, a few villages. The archive's best reckoning is nineteen.

Every scribe made his master the victor, and made the enemy the aggressor. A state that is divided within must lie to itself without, for it has no single truth to tell.

I say to you that [[pol:1638|Khishkhih]] is not such a state. Our figures are published. Our losses are entered in the registers. Our archive is open to the Academy. It is because we are united that we can afford to be honest, and it is because we are honest that we will stay united.

The teaching of [[mov:70|Vimikhulu]] reminds us that all things turn on a great wheel, and what ends will begin again. The ancients took this as an excuse; we take it as a warning. The wheel turns; but a disciplined people can choose where it stands when it turns. The wise withdraw from appetite. The good life is duty, order and right sacrifice. These are not phrases for the temple only. They are the programme of the State.

You know what that discipline has achieved in the years since [[evt:48363|the Treaty of Dadunde]]: [[evt:48386|the colony at Yindu]], and now [[evt:48410|the war with Yisamashtsi]], which our soldiers prosecute with a resolve the papers report to you each morning. Grain stands above twenty [[cur:1109|agni]] the measure. Every family is asked for sacrifice. But no family is asked for more than the Party asks of itself.

Comrades, the ancient states fell because no one in them believed the whole was worth more than the part. Let it never be said of us. Let the Academy keep the archive, and let the people keep the faith.

*The Chairman's remarks were received with prolonged applause.*
