*[Obverse. Inscribed in a deliberate, heavy hand; the tablet was pierced at the top to hang on a cord.]*

I am [[per:231|Nitllo Ta'a]]. I was queen in [[pol:4|Wa'mi]]. I am queen in Wa'mi still, in the eyes of the gods.  
Forty-five summers I sat on the seat of [[set:26|Wa'mi]].  
Forty-five harvests I gave the first sheaf to the gods.  
Forty-five times I poured the oil at the new year. I did not forget one god; I did not forget one year.

Now hear, O gods, what [[per:398|Yi Yub]] has done.

He came into my house with spears. He came in at night, like a thief, like a jackal in the fold.  
He did not ask the elders. He did not ask the priests. He did not ask the gods.  
He sat down on my seat while it was warm. He put my seal on his own wrist.  
He sent me to the women's rooms like a widow without sons.  
That is his kingship. Let it be written: a thief's kingship.

He calls himself by the name of [[dei:7|Yub]] of the fire. But Yub is not his mother.  
O Yub, lady of the hearth-fire, you know whose hearth this is.  
You know who fed your flame forty-five years. Do not warm him.  
Let his hearth be cold. Let his bread be raw. Let the smoke go into his eyes and not up to the sky.

O [[dei:8|Nish'il]], lord of the high sky, look down on him.  
Let your rain fall on the fields of others and not on his.  
Let your eye find him in his chamber and give him no sleep.

O [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river, turn your water from his gardens.  
Let his date-palms stand dry. Let his oxen go down to the bank and find mud.

O [[dei:9|Shimintlo]] of the storm, break his roof.  
O [[dei:10|Masto]] of the sun, burn his barley in the ear.  
O [[dei:11|Nizhashta]], lady of the spear, turn the spears he stole against his own throat.  
Let the men he made captains leave him in the day of battle.  
Let him look behind him and see no one.

As he took my seat, let his seat be taken.  
As he broke my seal, let his seal be broken.  
As he shut my door, let his door be shut against him.  
Let no son of his sit where he sits. Let his name be scraped from the wall.  
Let the scribes forget how to write it.

I am old. My breath is short. My hands shake on the stylus, and my women hold the clay.  
I do not ask the gods for my seat again. I ask them for justice.  
I have served them. Let them now serve me this once.

Hear also, you elders of Wa'mi, you who stood by and said nothing:  
the gods of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]] keep a good count. Each god has his place; each god has her due.  
The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. What he took will be taken.  
When it is taken, remember who cursed him, and pour one cup for Nitllo Ta'a.

Whoever reads this tablet and does not curse him, let the curse fall on him also.  
Whoever breaks this tablet, let him be as Yi Yub.  
Whoever hangs it at the gate of the house of Yub, let the gods be good to him.

*[Reverse.]*

> Written in the year that the thief calls the 1st year of Yi Yub. Nitllo Ta'a, queen, has spoken it; her women have written it.
