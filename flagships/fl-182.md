*In the name of the One, who was before the beginning and will judge at the end. Here is written the testimony of the sovereign, to be read aloud in the churches of the realm and kept in the treasury of the cathedral of [[set:0|Tallin]].*

I, [[per:35795|Yuthzhifpunin]], by the mercy of the Most High sovereign of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], of the house of Nuftafbafin and the people of Jego, son of [[per:35369|Tumaanin]] of blessed memory, have caused these words to be written in the year 4699 from the founding of Tallin, being now in the thirty-eighth year of my reign. I set them down not for my own glory, for the glory of kings is grass, but that my sons and their sons may know how the One has dealt with this house, and may fear Him and be grateful.

I was a young man when my father was gathered to his rest and the crown came to me. I was afraid, and I said so to my confessor, and he answered: "Fear is the beginning of wisdom in a king; it is the end of wisdom only in a coward." I have tried to remember it.

In the years of my youth and of my middle age the Most High enlarged the borders of Yidhe more than once, and I received the homage of provinces my fathers had not held. Of these I will not write at length, for the chroniclers have done so.

In the year 4694 the King of Viemunga, refusing the dues he owed, was met by my host at [[evt:36631|Biniynge]], and his army was scattered. In the year following his host was met again at [[evt:36640|Budh]], and scattered again. Then by [[evt:36641|the peace sworn at Budh]] he yielded to my crown the town of Budh and the lands about it, paid what he owed and more, and placed himself under my protection as a vassal under his lord. So does the Most High humble the proud.

But in that same year there befell the realm a grief worse than any foreign war. The province of Lininadhe, which had eaten of my bread, lifted its heel against me. [[evt:36633|The secession of Lininadhe]] was the work of a man who called himself sovereign there, and of those who listened to him for the sake of gain. So began [[evt:36634|the war of brothers]], which I did not seek and which I would have given much to avoid; for it is written that a house divided is a house open to the thief.

Three times the rebels came against us. At [[evt:36656|Nivirathyirshi]] they came, and were thrown back. At [[evt:36668|Yuftuy]] they came again, and their captain fell upon the field, and his followers fled. And in this present year at [[evt:36678|Tivintukhe]] they came a third time, and I went out myself, old as I am, at the head of my knights, that no man might say his sovereign stayed at home while others bled. The One gave us the field. The rebels' chief came out to me and bent the knee, and by [[evt:36679|the submission made at Tivintukhe]] Lininadhe was restored to the realm.

I did not hang him. Let it be written that I did not hang him. Mercy is the right hand of justice, and the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] teaches that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering. I would rather stand at the Judgement having spared one rebel too many than having hanged one too many.

I have built, in the years of my reign, more churches than I can count from memory; I have paid the clergy their tithes in full weight of [[cur:778|zhiflun]]; I have kept the roads and the markets in order. Where I have failed, I ask pardon of the One, who knows it better than any chronicler.

To my sons, [[per:36196|Torinin]] and [[per:36197|Tafbafzhiyshen]], I leave this charge: love one another, for you have seen what the hatred of brothers costs a realm; keep faith with your vassals, so that they keep faith with you; and never forget that the crown of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]] is lent, not given.

Amen. So be it. Written at Tallin by the hand of my chancellor, and sealed with my seal.
