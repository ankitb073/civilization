*[Obverse.]*

To [[per:13514|Nanüthnan]], great king, king of [[pol:414|Tagath]], king of the four roads, beloved of [[dei:73|Tithu]] of the storm, say:  
thus say your servants, the elders of the villages of [[reg:25|Nkinakhizi]], who bow down seven times and seven times at the feet of the great king.

May Tithu keep the great king. May [[dei:77|Pugazhu]] the sun shine on his house. May [[dei:74|Binge]] of the grain fill his storehouses. May the great king live.

Hear, O great king, the words of your servants. Hear them and do not turn away.

In the days of your fathers, the land of Nkinakhizi was the land of your servants. Our fathers ploughed it. Our fathers watered it. Our fathers poured to the gods in it, to Tithu, to Binge, to Pugazhu, each in their place and with their due. The fields were ours, the wells were ours, the pastures were ours, and we paid the tax of the great king in barley and in [[cur:228|Gathu]], and the great king was pleased with us.

Then the famine came upon the land of [[reg:26|Khantsi]]. In the [[evt:13314|Famine in Khanji]] their children died, their cattle died, their fields were dust. And the [[cul:50|Khukhuthul]] rose up out of Khantsi, and they came down into Nkinakhizi. They came with their wagons, they came with their goats, they came with their old men and their children.

At first we said: they are hungry, let them eat. We gave them bread. We gave them water. Is it not right sacrifice to feed the hungry stranger? We did it in the sight of the gods.

But they did not go. In the first year they came, and in the second year they came, and in the eleventh year they are still coming. They are more than the reeds of the river. They are more than the stars over the threshing floor. A hundred thousand of them, and more, the scribes say. This is the [[evt:13315|Migration of the Khukhudhul into Ng'jinaguh]], and it has no end.

*[Reverse.]*

Hear, O great king, what the Khukhuthul have done.  
They have put their goats on our pastures. Our pastures are bare.  
They have drawn from our wells. Our wells are low.  
They have cut the trees of the hill shrine. The shrine stands naked.  
They have ploughed the field of the boundary stone. The stone they threw down.  
They do not pour to Tithu. They do not pour to Binge. They pour to their own gods, at night, in the open field.  
They do not speak our speech. When we speak to them, they laugh.  
When we bring them before the judge, they say: "We do not know your judge."  
When the tax-man comes, they are gone into the hills.

Hear, O great king, what we fear.  
Now comes the [[evt:13524|Secession of Kambizhin]]. Kambizhin has turned its face from Tagath. The [[evt:13525|Sixth Tagath Civil War]] has begun in the land.  
Your servants have heard it said in the market: the Khukhuthul will go to Kambizhin. The Khukhuthul will carry the spear for Kambizhin.  
We do not know if it is true. We are afraid that it is true.

We have heard also that the great king's host went out against the men of Khantsi. At the [[evt:13530|Battle of Adhgumgi]] the great king's host prevailed, and the [[evt:13532|Treaty of Adhgumgi]] was sworn, and the land of Khantsi bows down to Tagath. Your servants rejoiced. Your servants poured beer in the fields. We said: the great king is strong. Let the great king be strong here also.

Therefore your servants ask the great king:  
Let the great king send his men into Nkinakhizi.  
Let the boundary stones be set up again.  
Let the wells be given back to the villages.  
Let the Khukhuthul be counted, and let them pay the tax as we pay it.  
Let those who will not pour to the gods of Tagath go back to Khantsi, now that Khantsi is the great king's.

O great king, we are your servants. Our fathers were your fathers' servants. Do not let the stranger eat the house of the servant.

May [[mov:11|Kyif]] and all its gods keep the great king. May [[per:13514|Nanüthnan]] live.

*[Colophon.]* In the 3rd year of Nanüthnan. Sealed with the seals of the elders.
