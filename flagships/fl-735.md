*[Obverse.]*

In the 8th year of [[per:6643|Yawni Ad]], queen in [[set:76|Nimit]], before the elders who sit at the gate of [[dei:85|Niw'i]] of the river, a servant of the house of the oil-seller in the street of the jars came and spoke. The elders asked. The servant answered. The scribe pressed it.

The elders asked: Where were you on the day of the [[evt:6447|Sack of Nimid]]?

The servant said: I was in the cellar. I was in the cellar under the house of my master, among the oil jars. I swear it by Niw'i. I swear it by [[dei:86|Shu]] of the grain, whose bread I eat.

The elders asked: Why were you in the cellar?

The servant said: My master's wife said: go down, girl, and take the little one, and stay. She gave me the little one, her son, two years old. She gave me a lamp with no oil in it, so the light would not be seen. I went down. She put the board over the hole. She put the mat over the board. She did not come down. I heard her feet go away.

The elders asked: What did you hear?

The servant said: First I heard the horns. The horns of the men of [[pol:216|Tiy]], far off, many horns. Then I heard the shouting at the wall. Then the shouting was in the street. Then it was in the house. I heard the jars break above, the big jars. The oil came through the boards and ran down the wall onto my hands. I heard men laughing. They said, in the speech of Tiy: where is the silver? Then I heard my master. Then I did not hear my master.

I put my hand over the mouth of the little one. I sang to him in my head. He did not cry. Let it be written that he did not cry.

*[Reverse.]*

The elders asked: How long were you in the cellar?

The servant said: Three days. I counted by the light in the cracks. Light, dark, light, dark, light, dark. We drank oil from my fingers. We drank rain that came down the wall. On the third day it was quiet. I lifted the board. The mat was burned. The house had no roof. I saw the sky of Had over the house, and the smoke going into it.

The elders asked: What did you see in the street?

The servant said: I will not say all of it. Hutlpi below the earth knows it; she has them now. My master was in the court of the house. My master's wife was in the street. I covered their faces with the burned mat. I took the little one to the house of his mother's brother, by the river gate, which was not burned. He wept, and gave me bread.

The elders asked: What did the men of Tiy take?

The servant said: The silver of the house. Twelve [[cur:130|Avish]] in a pot under the hearth, I know, for I swept over it every day. The bronze lamps. The good cloth. All the oil that did not break. What they could not carry they broke.

The elders asked: What do you ask of the elders?

The servant said: I ask that it be written that I kept the son of the house alive. I ask that I be not sold with the house, for the house is ashes. I ask for bread, for barley is two Avish and more the measure, and I have nothing.

The elders said: It is written. The house is ashes and the city must pay the silver of the [[evt:6448|Treaty of Nimid]] to Tiy, and there is little to give. But it is written. Go to the gate of Niw'i at the new moon and ask again.
