In AY 3423 the ruler of a small city-state on Tomzel's border sent a clay letter to [[per:31217|Inemamshe]]. It opens in the courteous form of the age: "To Inömamshö, king of Tomözel, my brother... With you, your wives, your sons, your chariots and your land, may all go very well." Then it comes to the point: "Your herdsmen graze their flocks on my side of the boundary stones. Keep them on your side, or I will drive them off with spears." The formula wished well to sons he did not have. Within three years both the writer and the man addressed were dead, killed in the war that followed. A translator rarely finds a reign's ending so plainly foreshadowed.

## A son of a fallen queen

He was born in 3380, a man of the Lizo, and his name means "oak jar", a vessel for keeping things. His mother, [[per:30823|Eshieliean]], became ruler of [[pol:1212|Tomzel]] in 3385, when he was five. His father, a consort, never ruled. In 3388 his mother opened the [[evt:31477|First Tomzel–Ehaemew War]] against [[pol:1303|Ehaemew]]. In 3391 she led nearly 110,000 men against about 2,300 near a frontier town and won, but she was killed on the field, aged thirty-one.

The record makes her death the cause of the [[evt:31524|Accession of Inemamshe]] in 3392. The new king was eleven or twelve, and [[per:31220|Tuefshi]] governed as regent until 3396. The [[evt:31536|Treaty of Litlitli (AY 3393)]], sworn at [[set:40|Litlitli]] in the boy's name, was concluded with Ehaemew's king, [[per:30837|Nehtlehemem I]], himself a usurper of thirty years' standing. Ehaemew ceded thirteen districts and paid an indemnity. The one town in the cession was the town where his mother had died. The war had cost Tomzel 15,586 men and Ehaemew 3,572.

He had a younger sister and a younger brother. He married a woman four years older than himself, who outlived him by a quarter of a century. The record knows of no children.

## The defender at Iazhash

The first war he fought in person was not of his choosing. In the [[evt:31691|Tsey–Tomzel War]] (3406–3409) [[pol:1317|Tsey]] attacked. At the [[evt:31718|Battle of Iazhash (AY 3409)]] Inemamshe met 20,611 men under [[per:31277|Sha Tle]] with 77,521 and held the field. He lost far fewer men than the invaders, 1,927 against 5,969. The [[evt:31719|Treaty of Iazhash (AY 3409)]], sworn at [[set:206|Iazhash]] with the old king [[per:31029|Sha Losha III]], gave Tomzel nothing but a fifteen-year truce. That year he built a wall at his capital, and the foundation tablet bears his name. Over the war Tomzel had lost 4,621 men to Tsey's 16,845. It was a defence, honestly fought, and the invaders paid more than three times as much.

## A city three years old

The next war was his own. The [[evt:31787|Tomzel–Unushthi War]] (3415–3417) was waged against [[pol:1358|Unushthi]], a city-state that existed from 3414 to 3417, and Tomzel's war took up most of its life. At the [[evt:31807|Battle of Lishimti (AY 3416)]] the king led 68,725 men against 1,658 under [[per:31494|Bahash]]. He lost 113 and killed 160. Unushthi's palace scribe, writing fifty-three years later, claims that his side lost 81 men while the enemy lost 236. His narrative ends: "the gates were opened to the enemy. The gods had turned their faces from us." The king's own stele for the second battle at Lishimti, which a general commanded, claims 4,000 enemy dead; the record counts 531. The [[evt:31822|Conquest of Unushthi]] annexed ten districts. Its ruler, [[per:31493|Shinmatlhimim]], who signed it at [[set:23|Unushti]], was four years old. The war had cost Tomzel 359 men against 691.

By 3420 his brother was dead. Tomzel's law gave the crown to sons, and he had none.

## The ford at Itlyitlinuise

The [[evt:31896|Holy War of Tomzel against Yiotl]] began in 3423, in the year of the letter about the herdsmen. Its enemy, [[pol:1354|Yiotl]], was another small city-state. Each side's chronicle names the other as aggressor. Tomzel's says Yiotl "had gathered an army against Tomzel". Yiotl's says Inemamshe, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Yiotl". The record names Tomzel as the attacker. A general won the first battle in 3424, and a basalt stele credits the king with 2,900 enemy dead against the record's 582.

In 3425 the king took the field himself at the [[evt:31917|Battle of Itlyitlinuise (AY 3425)]], with 56,281 men against 1,602 under [[per:31589|Iimash]]. Tomzel won. It lost 193 men to the enemy's 239, and one of the dead was Inemamshe, aged forty-five. Tomzel's official history of the war, written fifteen years later under his successor, describes this battle as a triumph: the king "held the ford against them", 394 of the enemy fell, "and of ours only 94". It does not mention that the king died. Yiotl's chronicle passes over the battle altogether.

## The usurper's peace

No son stood ready. In 3426 the record marks a crisis, and the [[evt:31920|Usurpation of Shimave]] put on the throne a man two years older than the dead king, with no recorded parentage. [[per:31596|Shimave]] finished the war. Yiotl's ruler, the author of the letter about the herdsmen, was killed in 3426, and in 3428 Yiotl was annexed. The house that had ruled Tomzel since 3280 ended with Inemamshe. The record counts the usurpation that followed his death among the causes of two later wars and of Tomzel's collapse into six successor states in 3466.

Over his reign Tomzel grew from about 390 districts to 410 and from twenty-three and a half million people to over twenty-four. The two small wars he chose, against city-states that could field at most a few thousand men, cost Tomzel fewer than a thousand men; their enemies lost about 1,900. He was a capable defender when attacked and, when not, an aggressor who brought thirty or forty men for every defender and still fell at the ford. His successor's court history hid his death. The jar of his name kept the realm intact until the end, but the line ended with him.
