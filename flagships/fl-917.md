> As for myself, I took a blow in the press near the end of the day. The physicians have bound it and say I must lie still. Let no rumour run through the city that the sovereign is struck down.

In AY 4120 the sovereign of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]] dictated a letter from the field of Linidhagshal to her council at [[set:0|Taulin]]. Bilkulufath, called the Brave, had beaten the army of [[pol:1565|Binge]] that day, and she wanted the bells rung. Her heralds, she wrote, had counted "above twenty thousand of the men of Binge dead", and her own loss was "far less than the enemy's". The letter survives, water-stained and partly illegible, in the palace archive at Taulin (PAL·LT·01670). The record allows us to check it, and the check is sad. Binge lost 16,811 men at Linidhagshal and Yidhe 4,721: the heralds rounded up, but her loss was indeed far less. Yet the rumour Bilkulufath forbade was true. She died of her wound that year. Of the eight battles that followed, Yidhe lost six, and in 4131 it paid Binge 345,612 kilograms of silver for peace.

I have called this chapter "The Arithmetic of Kings" after a satire of 4083 that we shall meet below, because the years from 4000 to 4250 were above all a time of counting. Kings counted victories. Clerks counted the dead. Treasurers counted silver paid out in indemnities larger than any before. Famine-stewards counted the buried. The totals were grim. People living under states numbered 233.6 million in 4000, fell to 224.0 million by 4120 and climbed back only to 235.9 million by 4240. The number of states stood at 32 at every count, a stillness that hides six great collapses and as many great foundings. The cold returned, too: the mean anomaly was −0.28 °C, and the 4220s, after the eruption of Mount Myibi, were the coldest decade of the period.

## Plague years (AY 4000–4075)

The century opened with two of the worst epidemics in this part of the book. [[evt:38362|The Blue Ague]] broke out at [[set:264|Ölye]], the capital of [[pol:1497|Ölie]], in 4012, and went down the merchant roads to nine more cities. It killed about 2.2 million people, a third of those it reached. A litany said in the temples of Ölie in 4019 (PAL·SC·00133) remembers that "in the city of our lord it took a third part of the people. In all the cities, more than two thousand thousand it took," and names the cities one by one with the response "Have mercy." The record bears out both the total and the proportion. Then the land dried. [[evt:38419|The great drought in Ayshi]] began in 4026 over 511 districts and lasted until 4065. Famines followed in Yizamashti in 4026 and in Diebeguvebenga in 4029, where 1.65 million people died or fled.

[[evt:38669|The Grey Sickness]] was worse. It broke out at Khayagu in 4053, reached 36 more cities and killed about 4.4 million people, nearly a third of those it touched. "A Prayer for the City of Ayagu in the Time of the Grey Sickness" (PAL·SC·00134) blames the city's own pride: "We weighed with a heavy hand and sold with a light one." Then comes one of the bleakest sentences in the archive: "The grave-diggers are buried by other grave-diggers." And in 4075 [[evt:38867|famine in Ng'jinaguh]] killed or drove out 1,051,358 people, nearly one in five.

The first great state to break was [[pol:1398|Imshi]], already hungry at the end of the last chapter. It was ruled in succession by three women, [[per:37474|Fayamam I]] (3977–4013), [[per:37526|Imamfaya]] (4013–4026) and [[per:37525|Fayamam II]] the Wise (4026–4036). Binge attacked in 4033, in the third queen's reign, and the [[evt:38559|Binge–Imshi War]] (4033–4043) cost Imshi 105,997 men. Its king, whose name the record gives simply as I, had a rock relief cut claiming 92,200 enemy dead at Vimginge in 4038 (PAL·IN·02935); the record gives 15,779. The city of Imshi was sacked for the third time in 4043, and in 4050 came the [[evt:38687|Collapse of the Imshi State]], into six pieces.

## The king who could not count

Ölie reached its greatest size in this period and fell from it. Its queen [[per:37905|Nitllu Foyatl]], daughter of [[per:37767|Ie Shimin]], reigned from 4022 to 4061 and was mocked in "Verses on Five Reed" (PAL·PO·00033), nailed to the door of the alms-house in 4045. The poet's charge is that she "counts her many victories, and never counts her men". At Kheye, he says, rebels "some forty thousand, by my soul" left seven thousand dead, and "of ours, near seventeen hundred". This sounds like satire's exaggeration. It is the record's arithmetic almost exactly: 41,828 rebels, 6,907 killed; 137,560 royal troops, 1,652 lost.

Her son [[per:38166|Wilau]] (4061–4099) earned a sharper poem, the "Verses on the Arithmetic of King Wilau" (PAL·PO·00034) of 4083. At Nirun, the poet says, the king "marched with thirty men for one", and still "one of ours fell there for every two of theirs"; the record gives 177,920 men against 5,168, and 546 dead against 1,049. At Myixim, "four hundred men and more" held the wall, seventy of them died, and "for each of those he marched two thousand five hundred men". The record: 466 defenders, 68 dead, 170,503 of Ölie's men in the field. Even the poet's price of bread checks out. He gives it as "two-thirds of a Myiu the measure"; the myiu weighed 11.3 grams of silver, so that is 7.5 grams, and the record's price at Ölye in the 4080s is 7.5. I know of no court chronicle of the age as accurate as these two anonymous squibs, and that should make us humble about what courts chose to keep.

The weakness behind the victories lay in the province of Unushti, which seceded in 4049, again in 4092 and again in 4110. When Wilau died in 4099 a usurper, Sitlyitliluisha, took the throne. Ölie peaked in 4110 at 48.1 million people and 609 districts, and in 4114 the [[evt:39263|Collapse of the Ölie State]] split it six ways. The record lists the three secessions and the usurpation as causes, and nothing else. The breakaway state itself, an Unushti empire, lasted a century before it too fell apart ([[evt:40279|Collapse of the Unushti State]], 4214). One heir, the kingdom of [[pol:1656|Ane]], would last until 4834 and one day hold 85.4 million people. The rump [[pol:1655|Ölie]] fought on, lost a war to [[pol:1657|Myizinitie]] and paid 108,006 kilograms of silver for it in 4139, then beat [[pol:1658|Ishwyithie]] in a sixteen-year war that ended in 4199.

The same decade broke [[pol:1523|Yisamashtsi]], whose two long-lived queens, [[per:37780|Yodi]] the Old and [[per:38108|Lashlafoyatl]] the Old, had ruled it between them from 4005 to 4085. In the [[evt:39034|First Wemo–Yisamashtsi War]] the kingdom of [[pol:1461|Wemo]] took 23 districts, four towns and 149,884 kilograms of silver, and sacked Bakh, burning its archives, in 4100. A fever followed in 4106. The [[evt:39302|Collapse of the Yisamashtsi State]] came in 4118, and a new [[pol:1662|Yisamashtsi]] empire rose at [[set:31|Bakh]] under rulers such as [[per:39370|Yi Tlyi]] the Red and [[per:39507|Nitllo Tluie II]] the Conqueror.

## Two indemnities (AY 4043–4245)

Yidhe began the century well. Under [[per:37966|Dipimtin]] the Old (4037–4081) it fought the [[evt:38627|Second Yidhe–Lienunushal War]] (4043–4056) for [[reg:9|Tuvizhinge]] against [[pol:1430|Lienunushal]] and its ally [[pol:1432|Rieney]]. The "Memoir of a Lancer of Yidhe in the War for Tuvizhinge" (PAL·CH·08438) calls it "the war of the fourteen summers", because its author "was nineteen when the banners went out ... and past thirty when they came home". He admits defeats, as official accounts seldom do, and he is right to: the queen of Lienunushal's stele for Zhinontunu in 4045 (PAL·IN·02938) claims 98,800 Yidhe dead, while the record gives 15,434, but Zhinontunu was indeed a Yidhe defeat. Yidhe won the war all the same, and the Treaty of Linnshal brought it 39 districts, five towns and 140,505 kilograms of silver.

Then it turned on Binge, and the arithmetic turned against it. In the first war, 4101–4104, Yidhe lost 68,263 men in one day at Linidhagshal under Bilkulufath's own command, and she set her seal to a peace that cost 336,905 kilograms of silver. In the [[evt:39320|Second Yidhe–Binge War]] (4119–4131), fought for [[reg:8|Diebeguvebenga]], the greatest war of the period, Yidhe lost 125,322 men to Binge's 77,006 and paid 345,612 kilograms more. Its treasury, which held about 2.67 million kilograms of silver in the 4090s, held 1.58 million by the 4130s. Here, for once, a defeat can be seen in the vaults. Yet Yidhe recovered. [[per:38819|Maanishifin]] reigned for 53 years (4121–4174), and by 4240 Yidhe held 43.9 million people, the most populous state in the world. In the [[evt:40396|Third Yidhe–Binge War]] (4224–4228) it won every battle, and the Treaty of Yirantudhu took Linidhagshal itself.

Binge's best years came under [[per:39313|Vithnilòrath]] the Victorious, who came to the throne at nine in 4169 and reigned until 4226. A panegyric spoken in the thirty-fifth year of her reign (PAL·PO·00036) says that she put down the rebels of Tungaruikhrimu in her seventeenth year and "pardoned them, every one, by her own word". Binge reached 20.4 million people in 4210. Her last years brought a new war with Yidhe, and after her death in 4226 came the defeat of 4228, a son who reigned four years, a usurper in 4230, and in 4240 another secession of Tungaruikhrimu, the province she had pardoned. The [[evt:40561|Collapse of the Binge State]] followed in 4245.

The quiet giant of these years was [[pol:1540|Fièvi]], ruled from [[set:169|Ajü]]. On the map of 4120 it and Binge held the largest territories in the world, about 11 per cent of the land each. Its court annals for 4000–4099 (PAL·AN·00710) mention six of the twenty-five battles Fièvi fought in that century, all of them won, in stock formulas such as "the enemy fled" and "the land had rest". Under [[per:38174|Nashathe]] the Victorious (4051–4102) and later [[per:39735|Nambave I]] (4208–4239) it grew steadily, to 30.1 million people and 409 districts by 4240. Fièvi suffered no collapse within this chapter, and so left little drama for the poets; its break-up came in 4255, five years after our closing date.

## The hungry years and the small beginnings

The middle of the period belonged to drought. [[evt:39049|A great drought in Ng'jinaguh]] lasted from 4098 to 4143, and [[evt:39608|another]] began in Mikhukhizhi in 4155 and did not end until 4221. In 4158 [[evt:39611|famine in Mana]] killed or drove out 1,376,121 people, a quarter of the population. "The Steward's Book of the Hungry Year" (PAL·DI·00008), kept on a manor in that country, records grain in the city market at "eight rithtu and more for a single measure", about 46 grams of silver; the record's price in that same market, the town we now call Ontiedzey, is 50.2 grams for the 4150s, six times the usual. The steward watched the Jego take to the roads toward Mikhukhizhi, 1.1 million of them in all, and counted nineteen dead among the 140 souls of his village. At harvest he wrote: "There is no harvest. I write the word, and there is nothing under it." In 4172 [[evt:39822|the Burning Cough]] broke out at Ma'itli and killed another 1.9 million. The almanac of Myizhitl for 4160 (PAL·AL·00440) ends its warning to farmers: "trust no promise of rain."

The kingdom of [[pol:1464|Dagafu]] grew through all this, to 28.1 million people in 4140. Its king [[per:38553|Nabivi I]] the Conqueror took the faith of [[mov:83|I'ashakh]] in 4105 ([[evt:39202|Conversion of Dagafu to I'ashakh]]), and after him came a usurper and a secession. In 4141 the [[evt:39520|Collapse of the Dagafu State]] produced a new [[pol:1670|Dagafu]] empire, later ruled for 54 years by [[per:39411|Nambave]] the Victorious, and also the kingdom of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]]. Mbilu began as one fragment among six. It is still with us, and at its height has held 234.8 million people.

A historian of Khishkhis must now be careful. The old [[pol:1454|Khishkhis]] empire won the [[evt:38511|Ayagu–Khishkhis War]] (4029–4042) against [[pol:1532|Ayagu]], and its king Naguf set up basalt steles in the Luzhe alphabet, the letters in which this book is printed. One claims 45,400 enemy dead at Kabaf (PAL·IN·02933), where the record gives 9,523; we lied in our own script as fluently as anyone. The victory did not save the state. The record traces its fall to a war of conquest against Kumbumbi (4019–4031) that it won at the cost of 33,020 men against 8,597, a heavy defeat at Kabaf in 4030, before Naguf's own victory there, and two lost battles against Fièvi in 4062 and 4063, the first of which Fièvi's annals duly note as a day when "the army of the king prevailed" (PAL·AN·00710). The old empire broke apart in 4067. From it rose the [[pol:1638|Khishkhis]] empire of today, with its capital at Nambi. In 4070 it held 23 districts and 2.7 million people. Its stability was high for the age, and its coin, the shikh, first struck in 4109 at 7.6 grams of silver, kept that weight for nearly seven centuries. In 4120 nobody would have picked it out from the 32 states of the map. It would one day hold 363 million people.

## The One, divided

The faith of the One still held 21 per cent of the land in 4120, ahead of Uthungala at 14 and Mie'to'ieshio and [[mov:69|Kievi]] at 11 each. But it was splitting. After a council at Pikhtunge broke up in 4105 without agreement, [[per:38661|Fispemamshi]] began [[evt:39204|the Pyizhenatlshi Schism]] at Iazhash in Ölie. The "Record of the Disputation at Iazhash" (PAL·CH·08448) has him keep every doctrine of the fathers and add only "that prosperity is a sign of the Light's favour". After another failed council, at Pikhtunge again in 4175, came [[evt:39907|the Ishlish Schism]] in Wemo in 4176, then the Mituth schism in 4186. Three new sects split from the faith of the One in eighty years, and with Fispemamshi the Shamu argument of the last chapter, that wealth is a sign of favour, came back. Other realms changed faiths outright: [[pol:1539|Khanji]] took up [[mov:61|Langikath]] in 4013, [[pol:1606|Nsinakhizi]] in 4040, and [[pol:1521|Ayshi]] turned to Kievi in 4127 ([[evt:39419|Conversion of Ayshi to Kievi]]).

Speech divided as faiths did. Yegem, Birirli and Noshi emerged about 4099; Jiji, Am and Liva about 4199. In Tasemem, about 4155, t became ts before i and e. Trade went on through the hunger: 228 merchant houses were founded, 45 new coinages struck and 13 weight standards introduced. Of 777 documents surviving from these years, besides twenty literary works, 362 are inscriptions, 61 are ledgers and 23 are wills; in these three chapters wills have never come so close to the six commonest kinds of document.

In 4202 [[evt:40116|a great drought in Daadüdhih]] spread over 524 districts; a record kept at Athnambi (PAL·RE·00172) says that "the farmers left the upper fields to the thorns." By 4250 Yidhe was the greatest power in the world, Fièvi held 30.1 million people from Ajü, Binge was in pieces, and a small empire at Nambi was minding its coin. The next chapter follows Yidhe, Ölie and Ayshi toward the war between the last two.
