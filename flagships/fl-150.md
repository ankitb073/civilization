title: Words of Ma'she Yueve in the House of Tablets

Hear me, scribes of the house of tablets. Hear me, priests of [[dei:1|Uelti]], lord of wisdom. Hear me, elders of [[set:3|Shue'ethie]], who sit on the benches by the wall.

I am [[per:476|Ma'she Yueve]], son of a carpenter, a carpenter. I am not a priest. I am not a scribe. My hands are rough. But the king [[per:388|O'e Ada]], great king, king of [[pol:0|Shue'ethie]], has said: "Go to the house of tablets. Tell them what you have made. Let it be written." So I have come. So I speak. Let the scribes write.

Hear what was before.

Before, when a great stone was brought for the house of the gods, how was it brought? Forty men pulled it on a sledge. The sledge went on the earth, and the earth held it back. Men laid logs under the sledge. The sledge went on the logs. When the sledge passed a log, a man ran and took the log from behind and put it in front. So it went, a little and a little. Many men. Many days. Many backs broken.

Hear what I saw.

I was a boy in my father's yard. I saw a log roll down the slope by the river of [[dei:3|Niele'i]]. It rolled and rolled and did not stop. I said: the log wants to roll. The log does not want to be carried. Why do we not let it roll, and carry it with us?

Many years I thought on it. I cut the log short, so that it was a round, like the moon of [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] when it is full. The round rolled. But the round could not carry a load; the load fell off. Then I made a hole in the middle of the round. I put a pole through the hole, a pole of oak. I made two rounds, one at each end of the pole. I set a box upon the pole. I put grease of fat in the hole. Then I pushed the box. The rounds turned. The pole stayed. The box went forward.

This is the wheel. This is what I have made.

A round cut from one log splits. So I made the round from three planks, pegged together with pegs of hard wood, and cut it round with the adze. It does not split.

Hear what it does.

What forty men pulled, now four oxen pull. What went in ten days, now goes in two. The barley of the fields goes to the granary in one load, not in twenty baskets. The stone for the house of the gods comes from the hills on a box with four wheels. I have done it. The king has seen it.

Hear why it is good.

The priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] teach: all things turn on a great wheel; what ends will begin again. The sun of [[dei:0|Atlie]]'s sky is round and turns over the world. The moon is round and turns. The year turns, from sowing to harvest to sowing. So I say: I did not make a new thing. I made a small thing in the likeness of the great thing. The gods turn the great wheel. Men turn the small wheel. Is it not right?

Hear what I ask.

Let it be written on the tablets of the house of Uelti that the wheel was made in Shue'ethie, in the 38th year of O'e Ada the king, by Ma'she Yueve the carpenter. Let no man of another city say: I made it. Let the carpenters of Shue'ethie keep the way of the three planks and the pegs, and teach it to their sons.

The king has given me a field and a house and a weight of [[cur:10|ashfan]] silver. I thank the king. I thank [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth, who gave the oak. I thank Uelti, who gave the thought. May [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of war not take the wheel for the host before the farmer has it for his barley. But if the king wills it, let it be so.

I have spoken. Let the scribes write. Let the elders say: it is so.

So it was spoken in the house of tablets, before the elders, in the 38th year of O'e Ada, king of Shue'ethie, in the time of [[evt:386|the making of the wheel]].
