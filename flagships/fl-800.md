*[Obverse]*

In the 264th year of Yufkoy, in the month of the shearing, at the house of [[dei:25|Novigu]], lord of wisdom, in the land of [[reg:2|Nubi]]:  
a man, grey in the beard, a maker of baskets, came before the priests and laid down a sheaf and a jar of oil, and said:

> Hear me, Novigu, who weighs words. Hear me, Navbuthin, river, who carried me away. I have a thing to put down. I have carried it twelve years. It is heavy.

> In the days of the war over the roads and the tolls, the [[evt:16712|Fourth Nivi Trade War]], I was in the levy of [[pol:625|Yufkoy]]. The king then was [[per:16629|Dyiyulun]], who had taken the seat by force. The criers said: the men of [[pol:649|Ishuvikithye]] take the tolls of Nubi that are ours. We go to take them back. Every village gives ten men.

> We marched under the lord [[per:16672|Dyiypantsin]]. We came to [[set:70|Nibange]]. There the men of Ishuvikithye stood against us, and they were three for every one of us. We fought. We killed many. We killed more of them than they killed of us, and still the field was theirs at evening. The next year we came to Nibange again, fewer, and they were more, ten for every one of us, like the reeds of the river. That was the [[evt:16731|Battle of Nivanga]]. We killed many again. Again the field was theirs.

> I saw it then. You can kill two men for every one of yours and still lose, if the other side has twenty to send and your king has two.

*[Reverse]*

> The next year they called the levy again, for Lunge. The night before we marched, the priest cut the ram, and the liver was black at the edge. An owl sat on the standard and would not go. Men whispered. I got up in the dark and walked to the river and went down the bank. I floated in the reeds of Navbuthin until the camp was far. I did not go to Lunge.

> I heard after: at Lunge the king himself led, and the men of Ishuvikithye broke us. Then came the [[evt:16737|Treaty of Lunge]]. The criers said: Yufkoy pays silver, much silver, to Ishuvikithye. The criers said: the king of Yufkoy bows his head to Ishuvikithye. The criers said: the merchants of Ishuvikithye go free on our roads. That was what we fought for. We fought, and we paid them, and they have the roads.

> I do not say I was right to run. Men of my village died at Lunge, and I was not with them. Their mothers spat when I came home. I said I had been sick in the camp. Now I say the truth. I was not sick. I was afraid. And I had seen Nibange twice.

> Then the king put down another enemy at Lunge, and then another man took his seat, and then, eight years ago, the [[evt:16776|Collapse of the Yufkoy State]]. The great house fell into two. The scribes who had my name in the levy-roll are gone. No one will hang me now. That is why I come. A man who cannot be punished by the king must ask the gods.

> I bring a sheaf. I bring oil. I bring two [[cur:329|Nashay]], and barley is one Nashay the measure, so it is two measures of barley. Let Novigu weigh it.

The priests of Novigu took the sheaf and the oil and the silver.  
They said: the gods do not ask a man to be brave. They ask him to be true.  
They said: go to the mothers of your village who spat, and say there what you said here.  
They said: then weave baskets for the house of Novigu one year, for nothing.
