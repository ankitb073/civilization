In AY 4005 a schoolboy in the capital of [[pol:1398|Imshi]] named Shiish copied out the kings of his country on a clay tablet. He wrote the number of years each had reigned, and corrected his spelling of the seventh name. The list ends with the eighth: "Fayamam, who reigns now". Below it his teacher scratched a note: "Write it again, ten times." For a boy of that age [[per:37474|Fayamam I]] had always been queen. By then she had reigned twenty-eight years, through invasion, famine and rebellion, and she would reign eight more. The tablet is a fitting place to begin, because her reign is best measured by what held together around her.

## An elected crown

She was born in 3944, a woman of the Nishmam, and her name means "reed eagle". Her mother died when she was seven. An elder sister survived into her reign. Imshi's crown went by election, not by birth, and in 3970 the electors chose her father, [[per:37016|Yi Imin]], then sixty-four. She married a man four years her senior. Her daughters [[per:37525|Fayamam II]] and [[per:37526|Imamfaya]] were born in 3967 and 3971, a son in 3975, and a last daughter, ten years later, in 3985.

In 3974 the kingdom of [[pol:1461|Wemo]] invaded, opening the [[evt:38016|Second Wemo–Imshi War]]. Wemo's own historian, writing twenty-two years later, says the opposite: that Yi Imin, "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore, set his hand against Wemo". The record names Wemo as the attacker. When Yi Imin died in 3977, the [[evt:38036|Accession of Fayamam I]] gave the electors' choice to his daughter, aged thirty-three, with an enemy army in the field.

## The war she inherited

She took command at once. At the [[evt:38041|Battle of Bathshi (AY 3977)]] she met Wemo's queen, [[per:37359|Shiinim II]], with 175,972 men against 50,903. Two queens fought that day and one died: Shiinim fell on the field. Imshi lost 10,812 men and Wemo 9,625. Her rock relief at Bathshi claims "76,300 soldiers" killed. The carvers used the formula made for kings, changing only the pronouns, so the stone says that "she" killed them and set up the monument for "her lady", the goddess. Shiinim's death threw Wemo into a usurpation, and [[per:37484|Inimin I]] took its throne.

The next battles were won at a terrible price. At the [[evt:38048|Battle of Bau (AY 3978)]] her 222,596 men beat 27,216 under Mashi Ash and lost 34,972 doing it. At the [[evt:38070|Battle of Nimxi (AY 3980)]] Inimin routed her: 251,589 Imshi soldiers against 62,051, and 66,554 Imshi dead against 6,281. The Wemo historian inflates even this, claiming she brought 719,173 men and lost 169,480. In 3982, at the [[evt:38089|Battle of Bau (AY 3982)]], she won again. Its muster roll survives: 142,964 men from eight towns, from 38,175 out of the capital down to 13,702. A note at the foot reads: "The captains of hundreds answer for the absent."

The [[evt:38090|Treaty of Bau (AY 3982)]] ended the war. Wemo paid an indemnity and accepted a fifteen-year truce. Its last term was a marriage: Fayamam's eldest daughter, then fifteen, was given to the usurper's son. Over the war Imshi, the defender and the victor, lost 165,110 men; Wemo lost 55,335. Wemo's historian says its king made peace "wearied of bloodshed", accepting "the pleas of Imshi". That is false, but the losses suggest why he could believe it.

## Mahim, famine and Talenunuy

Her first war of conquest came next. The [[evt:38163|Imshi–Mahim War]] (3991–3996) was fought against [[pol:1580|Mahim]], a kingdom that could field four to seven thousand soldiers. At the [[evt:38185|Battle of Vimginge (AY 3994)]] she led 206,344 men against 5,175 under Yawni Nim and lost 20,400 to his 820. After that a general fought the war for her, though a stele at Yirantudhu still credits her with a victory of 3995. The [[evt:38205|Treaty of Viradhuthpi (AY 3996)]], sworn with [[per:37543|Ai Lihtla]], gave Imshi nine districts and a truce. Those nine districts cost Imshi 78,397 men.

In 3996 the rains failed. Between 3996 and 4000 about two million people died of famine, one in seven of the region. The record lists the rout at Nimxi, sixteen years before, among its causes: a country that had lost so many working men had little margin left. In the same year [[pol:1608|Talenunuy]] broke away under [[per:37653|Kaayuf]], and the [[evt:38195|Sixth Imshi Civil War]] began. The record traces the revolt to wars of the 3940s and 3950s and, again, to Nimxi. At the [[evt:38212|Battle of Lithi (AY 3997)]] the rebel general Yei Imin faced her 126,253 men with nineteen. Her army lost 12,894 men and the rebels eight. The record does not say what killed so many royal soldiers in a famine year, and I will not pretend to know. The Talenunuy chronicle, written forty years later, accuses her of breaking the peace and boasts that only four of its men fell. The [[evt:38229|Suppression of the Talenunuy Revolt]], sworn at Nidhinge in 3999, returned 136 districts to the crown. Her husband died that year.

## The last conquest, and the electors' choice

The [[evt:38300|Imshi–Viernadhu War]] (4005–4008) repeated the pattern on a smaller scale. Her general's armies of up to 238,699 men faced defenders of a few dozen. Imshi lost 79,104 men and [[pol:1615|Viernadhu]] twenty-nine. A relief at Nunnga claims a hundred enemy dead for her; the record gives fourteen. The [[evt:38343|Conquest of Viernadhu]] was sealed in 4008 with its young queen, [[per:37775|Makhuevkuth]], after the twelfth sack in that city's long history. On the plaster of a temple wall in Virnadhu an Imshi soldier cut his name: "Miiji, son [...] Nim, of Imshi, was here when Virnadhu fell." It is the only single soldier's voice her armies have left us.

She died in 4013, aged sixty-nine, of natural causes. The electors chose her second daughter: the [[evt:38401|Accession of Imamfaya]] passed over the eldest, married long since into Wemo. Fayamam II, later called the Wise, reigned only after her sister's death in 4026. Imshi lasted until 4050, and the record counts the Talenunuy secession among the causes of its end.

Her realm was larger at her death than at her accession, about 338 districts against 306. Its people were fewer, some twenty-five and a half million against nearly twenty-seven, and its internal order, poor when she began, was little better. Half her wars were forced on her. She survived them all, though the cost was very high. She was the kind of ruler whose name schoolboys copied out ten times; the cost was paid by soldiers like Miiji.
