To the priests of the house of the gods in [[set:16|Turamtu]], and to the elders who sit at the gate, say: thus says [[per:17204|Vikhminin]], king of [[pol:670|Turamtu]], great king, beloved of [[dei:136|Dueminu]]. With me it is well. With my chariots, my spearmen and my horses it is well. With you may it be well.

Hear what the gods have done. Hear it, and let it be read out in the court of the temple on the day of the new moon, so that all the people know it.

You know that in the first years of this [[evt:17348|war against Yinnga]] the gods had not yet shown their face. At [[evt:17358|Biniynge]], under the walls of [[set:11|Buneynga]], the men of [[pol:580|Yinnga]] came against us as many as the locusts of the dry year, three of them for every one of mine. Their general [[per:17229|Lukhvelshewen]] would not come out to fight as a man fights; he kept his men behind the ditches and the walls, and only when my men were weary did he send them out. We did not break. We drew off in good order, and of theirs more fell than of mine. Let no one in the city say that Turamtu ran.

At [[evt:17369|Yirantudhu]], by [[set:239|Yirentuvu]], it was the same. [[per:17003|Shevunuvumalen]], the king of Yinnga, the old one, the proud one, whose fathers boasted over Turamtu in the [[evt:16959|second war]], brought against me a host that covered the hills, more than seventy thousand men. The dust of them hid the sun. My men slew them until their arms were heavy, and the field was red on both sides, and at evening we drew back to our camp. The diviners said: the liver is not yet whole. Dueminu has not yet spoken. Wait.

I waited. I made the offerings. I poured out wine for [[dei:137|Shyedartsi]] of the house below, that he might make room for the enemies of Turamtu. I gave grain to Nidikhti. I sent oil to [[dei:138|Tuel]], and on the night before the battle Tuel stood full and white over the camp, and the diviners said: the liver is whole. Go up.

Now hear. In this eighteenth year of my reign, at [[evt:17374|Yivinnimkhu]], by [[set:244|Yiwnunemgu]], I went up against Shevunuvumalen. Again he had more men than the stars; again they were three to one. But Dueminu thundered at their backs. Their horses turned and trampled their own spearmen. My chariots went in among them like the river in flood. We killed and killed until the sun went down. Twenty thousand of them lay on the plain, and more; I could not count them all, for the vultures came before the scribes. Of mine, few fell, and those I have buried with honour and their names are written.

And Shevunuvumalen, king of Yinnga, who came out against me as a lion, lay among his dead. He did not return to his house. His spear is in my tent. His shield I send to you with this tablet, to be hung in the house of Dueminu.

Now the road into [[reg:8|Duegvinga]] is open to me. The towns of that land send messengers. Let them bring their tribute; let them bring their silver by weight, in [[cur:346|yulu]] of good silver, and their grain and their cattle, as is right before the gods of [[mov:21|Khunewmu]], who give the land to the one they love.

Therefore do this: open the storehouses and send me grain for my men, for the harvest here is trampled. Send me fresh horses. Send me the arrows that are made. Let the priests make the offering of thanks, and let the people eat meat on that day at the king's cost.

Let no one in Turamtu speak doubting words. The one who speaks doubting words against the king's war, let his name be written down. The gods have spoken at Yivinnimkhu, and what the gods have begun they will finish.

Written in the 18th year of Vikhminin, in the camp of the king.
