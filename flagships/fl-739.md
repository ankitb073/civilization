*[Obverse.]*

Say to my cousin, the son of my father's brother, who lives by the sea: thus says your cousin, the son of the potter of the lower town of [[set:156|Fil]].

May [[dei:29|Bumbi]], lord of the sea, keep your boat and your nets. May [[dei:31|Kumbi]], lady of the moon, give you light on the water. May your house be whole. Mine is not. Hear why I write, in the 129th year of Fil.

You remember the city. You came to my father's house when we were boys, at the feast of the new barley. We ran on the wall. You were afraid of the height and I laughed at you. Do not be angry. I do not laugh now.

Thirteen years ago, in the [[evt:12708|Fourth Sack of Hil]], the men of [[pol:506|Puvu]] came through the gate. You heard of it. All the coast heard of it. My father was at the wheel when they came into the lower town. He did not get up from the wheel. My mother took me and my sisters through the drain under the wall, where the dogs go. We lay in the river reeds one day and one night. When we came back the kilns were broken and the street was black.

We did not stay in the city. We went up the river to the village of the reed-cutters, an hour's walk from the gate, and built a house of reeds and mud. I cut reeds. My mother wove mats. We sold the mats in the city for bread. When that war ended, Fil paid silver to Puvu every year, and the price of bread went up, and our mats did not.

*[Reverse.]*

Now hear the rest. Three years ago there was trouble in the king's house. A man took the seat who was not born to it; it is called the [[evt:12807|Usurpation of Nakudhüth]]. That same year the other enemy, who had fought us three summers already, the ones whose speech we do not know, broke the army of Fil in the field. Then came the [[evt:12812|Sixth Sack of Hil]]. I stood on the roof of my reed house and watched the smoke go up from the city again. My mother covered her face. She did not watch.

So there is no king of Fil now. That is the [[evt:12813|Conquest of Fil]], and we are under new masters. Their tax-men come to the village. They count the reed-beds. They count the children. A measure of barley is two [[cur:261|Vükh]] and more in the city, and I have not seen a whole Vükh since the spring.

My mother is old. My sisters are married to reed-cutters and have children of their own. I have a wife and two sons. The elder is nine. He has never seen the city whole. He thinks the city is a black place where you go to sell mats.

Cousin, I ask you this. Is there work by the sea? Is there a place on a boat for a man who can cut reeds and make mats and mend a net if he is shown? Is there a corner of your yard for a reed house? I do not ask for silver. I ask for a place.

I swear by Ugash, lady of the sun, that I will work. I swear by Bumbi, your lord of the sea, that I will not shame your house.

Send word with the salt-carrier who brings this. He comes back at the full moon. Tell me yes or tell me no, but tell me. Greet your wife. Greet your mother, my aunt, and kiss her hands for me.
