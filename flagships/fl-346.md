*[Obverse. A temple tablet of the lamentation-singers; each stanza is closed by a ruled line and the sign for "repeat".]*

Woe, my city! Woe, [[set:6|Chinontonu]]! Woe, my house!  
The sheepfold is open; the wind blows through it.  
The cattle-pen is open; the dust blows through it.  
Woe, my city! Who will shut the gate of the sheepfold?  

[[dei:55|Dumamnul]], father of the sky, why did you look away?  
[[dei:56|Nabuthin]], lord of the river, why did you not rise against them?  
[[dei:58|Chithal]], lord of the spear, why did you sleep in your house?  
They looked away. They did not rise. He slept. Woe, my city!  

The men of [[pol:25|Yida]] came down like the storm out of the hills.  
The first time they came, the gate gave way; that was the [[evt:663|Sack of Zhinontunu]], the first.  
They took the bronze. They took the barley. They went away.  
We mended the gate. We set up the doorposts. We said: it is over.  
It was not over. Woe, my city!  

Three summers more the spears of Yida sat in the fields of the river.  
Three summers more the barley was cut by strangers.  
That was the [[evt:656|Second Yida–Chinontonu War]]; it did not end. It did not end.  

Then came the [[evt:673|Battle of Zhinontunu]], before our own walls.  
Our young men went out with bright spears, few, few against many.  
The men of Yida were as the reeds of the river, a forest of them.  
Our young men lay down by the wall. The wall did not stand.  
Woe, my city! The wall did not stand.  

That was the [[evt:674|Sack of Zhinontunu]], the second time, the bitter time.  
They came in at the river gate. They came in at the gate of the sheepfold.  
In the street of the bronze-smiths the hammers are silent.  
In the street of the bakers the ovens are cold.  
In the house of [[dei:57|Bubin]], lady of the moon, the lamps are broken.  
The mother says, "My son!" and no son answers.  
The old man says, "My house!" and no house answers.  
Woe, my city! Woe, my house!  

*[About four lines lost. Legible: "[…] the tablets were not […]", "[…] the house of the scribes stood […]".]*

[[dei:59|Winba]], mother below, you drank the blood of the young men.  
Winba, give back the barley for it. Give back the barley for it.  
[[dei:60|Shineyta]] of the sea, take the tears of the women down the river.  
Take them far away, where the strangers cannot see them.  

*[Reverse.]*

Then the king went out. [[per:789|Dolulrinin]] went out,  
the shepherd of [[pol:12|Chinontonu]] went out of the gate to them.  
He went out not with the spear but with the open hand.  
He sat with them under the sky and they swore; that was the [[evt:675|Treaty of Zhinontunu]].  
Fields by the river that were ours, the strangers hold them.  
The [[cur:8|Yong]] of our storehouses goes up to Yida, year after year.  
So the shepherd bought back the sheepfold. So he saved what could be saved.  
Long live the shepherd! The sheepfold stands, though the wind blows through it.  

Now the swallows nest where the doorposts stood.  
Now the children play where the bronze-smiths sang.  
Now the priests of [[mov:8|Ligay]] say: the wheel turns; what ends will begin again.  
Let it begin again, O Dumamnul. Let it begin again.  
Turn your face back to your city. Turn your face back to your house.  

Sixteen years we have sung this song. Sixteen years we have poured water at the river gate.  
Bubin has grown full and grown thin two hundred times.  
We do not forget. Woe, my city! We do not forget.  

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> For the singing at the river gate, on the night of the dark moon.  
> Written in the 46th year of Dolulrinin, king in Chinontonu.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Winba close her mouth to his grain.
