Only two inscriptions in the record speak in the name of [[per:33011|Nagule]], and neither says much. A limestone slab from the wall of his capital, cut in AY 3579, reads: "Nagule, great king, king of Dagafu, built this wall in Dagafu. May Khuthaze bless the city." A boundary stone from the conquered north asks the same god to curse the enemy. For a king who reigned sixty-three years, the longest of his line, and whom later tradition calls the Conqueror, it is very little. The rest has to be pieced together from the record, from the enemy chroniclers who hated him, and from figures so large that they are hard to take in. No ruler of his age sent more men to war. Few saw more of them die.

## A child in a house of short lives

Nagule was born in 3552 into the Khumbivi house that had ruled [[pol:1279|Dagafu]] since the empire's founding, three centuries before. The house took its name from its founder, Nakathash. His mother, [[per:32940|Navavun]], had come to the throne at seventeen on the death of her father, Navavun the Great. His father has no name in the record. Navavun died in 3557, aged twenty-four. The [[evt:33420|Accession of Nagule]] made a five-year-old the ruler of perhaps twenty-four million people, with [[per:33015|Nambave]] as regent.

The family records of the next decade are a list of burials. His elder sister died at eleven, in 3561. A brother born in the same year as Nagule died the year after. A younger brother died in 3568, aged fourteen. That year his father died too, and the regency ended. Of five children only Nagule and one younger brother reached adulthood. His marriage produced no children who appear in the record. The question of who would follow him was open from his youth, and it hung over the reign to its end.

## The field of Mbambanuduyih

The year he came of age, Dagafu went to war. The [[evt:33514|First Dagafu–Nsinakhizi War]] of 3568–3572 conquered the kingdom of [[pol:1404|Nsinakhizi]]. Its king, [[per:32996|Nambuy]], is remembered as the Last. The [[evt:33576|Conquest of Nsinakhizi]] was signed at Davazhufu after a siege and annexed twenty-five districts. Dagafu paid for them with nearly fifty thousand dead, six times the enemy's losses. That ratio would recur through the reign.

The [[evt:33618|Dagafu–Tambulu War]] began in 3579, the year the wall inscription was cut, and it was the great test of Nagule's life. At the [[evt:33648|Battle of Mbambanuduyih (AY 3580)]] he led nearly three hundred thousand men against forty thousand of [[pol:1386|Tambulu]] under its king, [[per:33152|Nangithvu III]], called the Black, and he was routed. The record counts some 119,000 Dagafu dead in a single day, against about six thousand of the enemy. No Dagafu document mentions it. The wall inscription, cut the year before, is the last thing we have in his name until the war was over.

What followed shows something like stubbornness, or perhaps only the depth of an empire's reserves. In 3581 Nagule won narrowly at Dyizhih. Four years later he came back to the field of his defeat, and at the [[evt:33685|Battle of Mbambanuduyih (AY 3585)]] he won there. In 3586 he beat Nangithvu himself at the [[evt:33691|Battle of Ndulamlol (AY 3586)]]. The [[evt:33701|Treaty of Jyijinjivji (AY 3587)]] gave Dagafu forty-one districts and five towns, Mbambanuduyih among them, along with a large indemnity and a truce of fifteen years. The war had cost Dagafu more than 218,000 dead, four times Tambulu's losses. The boundary stone he set up afterward asks only that the god curse the enemy.

## The defender of Shyih

Five years later Nagule fought the only war of his reign that he did not start. In 3592 [[per:33242|Nalangu]] of [[pol:1277|Puvu]] began the [[evt:33737|Puvu–Dagafu War]]. At the [[evt:33752|Battle of Shyih (AY 3593)]] Nagule, now past forty, met some ninety-six thousand Puvu soldiers with more than a quarter of a million of his own and broke them. Puvu lost about forty thousand and Dagafu sixteen thousand. It was the most complete victory of his life, and the only great battle in which he lost fewer men than the enemy.

The Puvu account of the war, written in 3609 by the historian Nanu, shows how a defeated court consoled itself. It says Nagule had "forgotten the oaths his fathers swore", although Puvu attacked. It says the Puvu army at Shyih "withdrew in good order" after "the river rose and cut the army in two". It gives Dagafu losses at more than double the true figure and its own at half. In 3595 the [[evt:33769|Treaty of Shyih (AY 3595)]] obliged Puvu to pay Dagafu an indemnity of more than a hundred thousand. Nanu wrote only that "the honour of the realm was preserved". His wife died in 3594, in the middle of the peace talks. The record notes the death and nothing more.

## The old king and the empty throne

For eighteen years after Shyih there was peace on the record. The empire grew to some twenty-eight million people, and its army to nearly half a million. Its measures of order, however, fell steadily from the middle of the reign, and his last surviving brother died in 3604. In 3613, at sixty-one, Nagule went to war a last time. The [[evt:33986|Second Dagafu–Nsinakhizi War]] was fought against a [[pol:1433|Nsinakhizi]] revived in 3606 under [[per:33467|Nandumbeni]], whom later ages called the Unlucky. Dagafu's armies of a hundred and two hundred thousand faced a few thousand and won, though other men led them now. The Nsinakhizi account of the war claims seventy-four thousand Dagafu dead in one battle, while the record gives about thirty-four thousand for the whole war. The [[evt:34023|Treaty of Dambulu (AY 3616)]] took ten districts and the town of Khambizhinu, and Dambulu was sacked. The Nsinakhizi scribe wrote that only "some villages of no account" were lost, "which had always been poor". Nandumbeni died in battle against Puvu three years later.

Nagule died a natural death in 3620, aged sixty-eight. He left no child, no brother and no adult heir of the blood that the record names. The [[evt:34081|Usurpation of Nakathash]] followed at once, and the record marks it as a crisis. The new ruler, [[per:33613|Nakathash]], a man of forty-nine with no recorded parents, carried the very name of the house's founder. It was the end of that house. Within a decade the empire had lost nearly half its districts to revolt and secession. It recovered them, and Dagafu lasted another sixty years before breaking into six successor states in 3681.

## A verdict

Nagule's reign, then, was long, victorious and enormously costly. His three wars of conquest added some seventy-six districts to Dagafu, and his soldiers died at four or five times the rate of their enemies to win them. He came back from one of the worst defeats in the record to win on the same field. He did not boast of it, or nothing of the boast survives. The two inscriptions we have speak of a wall and a boundary. Perhaps that is fair. He spent his life building walls around an empire that, when he died, had no one inside them to inherit it.
