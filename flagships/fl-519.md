*Here begins the confession of [[per:24486|Nashinche]], written with her own hand at [[set:146|Teshivi]], in the year 2707 of the Udunkala, for those who would know how she came to the Way.*

To you, the One Power, who were before the gods and of whom the gods are but servants and shadows, I make this confession; not because you do not know what I shall say, but because I do not yet fully know it myself until I have said it.

I was born in [[pol:977|Tadudüvi]], in the reign of the father of [[per:24162|Natadtsud]] our king, and I was raised in the faith of [[mov:24|Udunkala]], as were all the children of my street. I was taught that time runs in a line from the creation to the judgement, as a road runs from one city gate to the other. I was taught that each seeker may find the truth by the inner light and by reason, and I was proud of my light, and prouder still of my reason. And I was taught that prosperity is a sign of the favour of heaven. This last teaching I believed most easily of all, for my father's house was prosperous, and I was glad to think that heaven loved us.

Hear, then, how I was undone.

In my thirtieth year there was a sickness in the lower quarter of [[set:140|Adunambi]], and I went down with the physicians' women to carry water. I had never gone into those streets. I found there a weaver, a widow, who had lost her husband and two children within a month, and who had nothing left but a loom and a debt of forty [[cur:533|Lud]]. By the teaching I had been given, I ought to have concluded that heaven did not love her. I could not conclude it. I sat beside her on the floor of her room, and I knew, with a certainty that no reason had given me, that the One Power was nearer to her in her ruin than to my father in his counting-house.

That was the first crack in my pride. Others followed.

I began to ask what my inner light was worth, if it had shown me nothing of that room in thirty years. I saw that every seeker who trusts only her own light finds, at the end of her seeking, a mirror. I came to believe that truth must be kept, as a fire is kept, by those who are set apart to tend it, and handed down from one keeper to the next, lest each generation light its own small fire and call it the sun.

I began also to doubt the road from gate to gate. When I looked at the fields, I saw the seed go into the ground and come up again. When I looked at the sky, I saw the moon die and be born. When I sat with the widow, I saw a grief that had been her mother's and would be her daughter's. All things turn on a great wheel, I said to myself; what ends will begin again. And I was afraid, for this was not what I had been taught.

This spring I came to Teshivi, to the shrine above the village, to be alone. On the third night I did not sleep. I will not write what I saw, for I am not sure that words can carry it without breaking it. I will say only this: that I understood the good life to be the life of compassion and of release from suffering, and that this message was not for my people only, but for all peoples, and that I must not keep it to myself.

So it was that, in this year, at Teshivi, the Way that men now call [[mov:89|Bankekumgi]] began; and those who keep the records will write it down as the [[evt:24691|Founding of the Bangegumgi Faith]]. I did not intend a founding. I intended only to tell what had happened to me. But others listened, and some stayed.

I do not reject the teachers of Udunkala. They taught me justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and I keep those still. But I have turned, and I confess it.

Let whoever reads this pray for me, that I may tend the fire and not mistake it for my own.
