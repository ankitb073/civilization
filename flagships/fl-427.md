*[Obverse]*

Thus says [[per:386|Piscua'agw]], king of [[pol:17|Wal'a]], the strong one, the chosen of [[dei:8|Nish'il]] of the sky, the beloved of [[dei:11|Nizhashta]] of the spear, he whom [[dei:10|Masto]] the sun saw and did not turn from. Hear it, men of the city. Hear it, men of the villages. Hear it, herdsmen of the hills. Let it be read at the gate. Let it be read at the threshing floor. Let it be read at the well.

In the days of [[per:258|Ilshali'an]] the land was good. I say it; I do not hide it. Ilshali'an sat on the seat for many years. He fed the gods. He fed the priests. He dug the canals. I honour him. I pour water for him. I pour beer for him at his tomb.

But Ilshali'an grew old. His eyes grew dim. His hand grew slack upon the staff. Then the gods were angry, and the rain did not come, and the [[evt:290|Famine in Yimla]] sat upon the land. Four years it sat. The barley died in the field. The ox died in the stall. Of every fourteen mouths, one mouth was shut and did not open again. Where was the strong hand then? The strong hand was in the old house, and it was slack.

In the last year of Ilshali'an the old king went down to his fathers. He went down in his bed, full of days; let no one say otherwise. Then the house quarrelled. The house of the old king quarrelled over the seat as dogs quarrel over a bone. One said: "Me." Another said: "Me." And the seat stood cold, and the gate stood without a keeper, and the gods had no smoke.

Then I stood up. I, Piscua'agw, stood up. I am not of the old house; I do not say that I am. But the gods do not ask a man whose son he is. The gods ask: "Who will feed us? Who will keep the gate?" I said: "I will." The men of the spear said: "He will." The priests looked at the smoke, and the smoke went up straight. So I sat on the seat. That was in the year the old king died. Now it is the second year of Piscua'agw.

*[Reverse]*

Hear the words of the king.

Whoever swears to Piscua'agw by [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river and by [[dei:7|Yub]] of the fire, his house is safe. His field is safe. His ox is safe. Whoever does not swear, let him go out of Wal'a with his sandals in his hand.

The debts of barley that were made in the years of the hunger, I break them. The tablet of the debt is broken. The widow does not pay. The orphan does not pay. Let the moneylender weep; the gods do not weep for him.

The storehouses of [[set:22|Wal'a]] I have filled again. The barley of the king's fields goes into them, not into the mouths of the old king's kinsmen.

To [[dei:9|Shimintlo]] of the storm I have given a bull, so that he sends rain in its season. To Nizhashta I have given a bronze spear, so that she stands before my men. To the priests of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]] I have given bread and oil and wool, so that they keep the order of the gods, and pass it down, as it was passed down to them.

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The house of Ilshali'an ended. The house of Piscua'agw begins. That is the turning of the wheel, and no man can stop the wheel.

*[Lower edge]*

Whoever erases my name from this tablet, may Nizhashta break his spear. May Nili dry his canal. May Yub put out his hearth. May his sons have no sons.

Thus says Piscua'agw, king of Wal'a, in the second year of his seat.
