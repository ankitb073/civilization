[[per:25989|A'initlue'e]], by the favour of the gods lord of [[pol:922|Ane]], to the wardens and elders of the merchants' hall of [[set:7|Ane]]: greeting, though there is little health in the city to wish you.

I have received your petition, sealed with forty seals, and I have read it twice: once as your emperor, and once as what I have always also been, the chief merchant of this empire, whose granaries feed the city and whose ships carry its oil. You complain that the gates are shut, that the caravans stand idle in the outer yards, that the ships ride at anchor with their cargoes rotting, and that each day of this costs you more [[cur:498|Shi'me]] than you can bear. I do not dispute a word of it. I ask you only to hear why it is so.

You know, as all the world knows, whence this [[evt:26793|Long Sickness]] came. It came from [[set:229|Ivo]], in the bales of traders who would not wait, by roads that no one thought to watch. I do not say those traders meant us harm. I say only that the merchant who carries death with his cloth is no less a murderer for having meant to carry only cloth. There were men in this very hall, wardens, who pressed for the Ivo caravans to be admitted in the spring, against the counsel of the physicians, because the season was short and the prices good. I name no one. They know who they are, and the gods know.

Now hear what the couriers tell me, for I hear it every morning before I eat. The sickness is in [[set:30|Namos'ash]]. It is in [[set:205|Pivish]] and in [[set:22|Wal'e]]. It is in [[set:2|Mieshlothie]], our old neighbour. It is in [[set:114|Tamazhidiy]], among those against whom we fought at the [[evt:26764|Battle of Thushashfi]], at the [[evt:26769|Battle of Likha]] and at the [[evt:26778|Battle of Nyinsitlasa]]; and I take no joy in it, for the sickness does not ask a man which banner he followed. Wherever the roads run, it runs. Shall I open the gates of Ane to it, so that the warehouses of your hall may be emptied a month sooner?

I know what is said of me in the wine-shops: that the old usurper is afraid for his own skin. Let them say it. I am an old man, and my skin is worth little. But I have buried two daughters in five years, the younger at the last turning of the year, and I will not open the gates through which a third grief may come to every house in this city.

The priests of [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] tell us that justice in dealings and moderation in all things are what the gods require, and that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I have not always kept that teaching. Few emperors do, and fewer usurpers. But I keep it now, and I ask you to keep it with me. Moderation in the counting-house is a virtue in years of plenty. In years of sickness it is the price of a city.

Therefore I command, and I ask:

That the gates remain shut to all caravans from the stricken lands until the physicians declare the roads clean.

That the imperial granaries be opened to the poor at the price of the last harvest, and that no merchant of this hall sell grain above it, on pain of the forfeit of his stock.

That those of your members who have lost their trade be lent silver from the treasury, without interest, to be repaid when the gates are opened again.

That every warden of the hall, myself included, give a tenth of his gain this year to the houses where the sick are kept.

I do not know when this sickness will end. No one in Ane knows it. Perhaps the gods are angry with us; perhaps they are angry with Ivo, and we are only standing too near. I have poured to them every morning since the first death in the lower town, and they have not yet answered.

When they do, the gates will open, and your caravans will go out again, and you may curse me in the market as much as you like. Until then, keep your doors shut and your hands clean.

Given at the palace of Ane, in the year 3269 from the founding of Ane.
