Hear, O [[pol:153|Waga]], hear, O city of [[set:31|Waga]],  
hear the song of the storm snake, [[per:8884|Simenmovi]], the son of [[per:8804|Lotla]].  

Lotla climbed to the high seat by the back stair, when no one was looking;  
the little snake slid up after him, belly on the steps.  
Etlie saw it from the sky. Etlie said nothing.  
The priests saw it from the temple. The priests said: hail.  

**I.**

The storm snake went down to Tliyiya with eighteen thousand spears.  
Tliyiya came out with a thousand, and a few old men, and a dog.  
The snake struck. The snake won. Count the dead, scribe, count them:  
of Tliyiya, three hundred; of Waga, thirteen hundred.  
Hail the storm snake! Hail [[evt:8887|the great victory at Tliyiya]]!  

He sat down before the wall, [[evt:8888|he sat and sat]],  
and he drank the beer of Tliyiya, and he ate the bread of Tliyiya,  
and by [[evt:8889|the tablet of Tliyiya]] he took their silver for thirty-three years.  
Thirty-three years! The snake counts on his fingers, and runs out of fingers.  
Hail the storm snake!  

**II.**

Then the snake was hungry, and he looked at [[evt:8980|Miezenitie]],  
and he opened his jaw, and he swallowed Miezenitie whole, like a frog.  
The frog did not struggle. The frog is in the snake.  
Ask the frog how it likes the dark. Hail the storm snake!  

**III.**

Then the snake looked at Yimla, and [[evt:8988|the war of Yimla]] began,  
and he went down to [[evt:9007|Niuzafammas]] with sixteen thousand spears.  
Yimla came out with two thousand. The snake struck. The snake won.  
Count them, scribe: of Yimla, five hundred; of Waga, fifteen hundred.  
Hail the storm snake! Hail the great victory!  

Two years, and he went down to [[evt:9026|Niuzafammas]] again.  
Is there no other road in all the land of Yimla? There is no other road.  
Thirteen thousand against two thousand. The snake struck. The snake won.  
Count them, scribe: of Yimla, five hundred and more; of Waga, two thousand and more.  
Hail the storm snake! Hail the great victory!  

Then the snake grew tired, and lay in the sun, and sent his captain.  
At [[evt:9050|Tunavükhini]] the captain struck, and the captain won,  
and the snake was in his bath in Waga, and the water was warm.  
Count them, scribe: of Yimla, two hundred; of Waga, nearly three thousand.  
Hail the storm snake! He did not get wet.  

And this year the captain went down to Niuzafammas a third time;  
[[evt:9055|Niuzafammas]] knows us now; the crows of Niuzafammas know us by name.  
The captain struck. The captain won. The snake sent a letter.  
And by [[evt:9056|the tablet of Niuzafammas]] a strip of Yimla is ours.  

**IV.**

Seven years of the war of Yimla. Eight thousand of our sons  
for seventeen hundred of theirs. That is the count; the granary scribes have it.  
The scribes write: great victory. The widows write nothing.  
The widows cannot write. They pour water at the new moon.  

Barley is a little more than one [[cur:85|i'a]] the measure. Barley is steady.  
Barley is steady because the dead eat none. Hail the storm snake!  

**V.**

[[dei:5|Nieshashta]], goddess of war, count our sons, not the snake's victories.  
[[dei:3|Niele'i]], river, carry this song down to the sea where the snake cannot follow.  
Gods of [[mov:0|Le'im]], keep Waga. Keep Waga. The snake is old; Waga is older.  
The great wheel turns. What ends will begin again. So the priests say.  

Written in the 43rd year of Simenmovi, by no one, and copied by everyone.
