Posterity called [[per:38727|Lòpavbukhin]] "the Old", which is strange, because he died at forty-five. The epithet makes sense only as a measure of his reign. He came to the throne of [[pol:1565|Binge]] at three and held it for forty-two years, from AY 4116 to 4158. His name means "bear sword". His life was shaped from the start by a crime he did not commit. His father had stolen the throne, and the record traces the first two wars of the reign to that theft. His greatest enemy justified her war by it, his own sister rebelled after his defeats, and when he died another man stole the throne in turn.

## The usurper's son

He was born in AY 4113, among the Imu, a people then scarcely a generation old. Two years earlier his father, [[per:38698|Vaayushin]], a man of no recorded ancestry, had seized the throne of Binge in a year the record marks as a crisis. Vaayushin reigned five years and died in 4116, aged thirty-five. His sarcophagus lid (PAL·IN·06149) gives him the title he had taken: "Vaayushin, king of Binge, dwells in this tomb."

The [[evt:39285|Accession of Lòpavbukhin]] put a child of three on a throne whose legitimacy was everywhere in doubt. His mother, [[per:38725|Tivimninath]], governed for thirteen years. In her first years the empire, some 303 districts and seventeen and a half million people, fought the [[evt:39288|First Binge–Myizhitl War]] against [[pol:1660|Myizhitl]], a kingdom born only two years before. By the [[evt:39322|Treaty of Nirun (AY 4119)]], sworn with [[per:38718|Myimeyuive I]] at Nirun, Binge took nineteen districts and three towns. It had cost 66,177 of its soldiers against twenty-four of the enemy.

## "A thief upon the seat of their kings"

In 4119 the Yidhe empire attacked, and the [[evt:39320|Second Yidhe–Binge War]] lasted twelve years. Its reason survives in a remarkable document. After the first battle, at Linidhagshal in 4120, the sovereign of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]], Queen Bilkulufath, dictated a letter to her council (PAL·LT·01670). The land in dispute was Yidhe's "by right of old", she wrote, "and those who hold Binge hold it by no right at all, since the usurpation of Vaayushin put a thief upon the seat of their kings… Those who serve a stolen throne serve a lie." She claimed above sixty thousand of the enemy dead. The record gives 49,815, a lighter exaggeration than most. She added that she "took a blow in the press", and asked the council to let no rumour run that the sovereign was struck down. The record says she died of the battle. A seven-year-old boy in Binga had lost an army to a queen who would not live to profit by it.

The war went on under her son [[per:38819|Maanishifin]]. Binge's generals won more than they lost, and in the last battle the young king took the field. At the [[evt:39449|Battle of Zhidhzhinge (AY 4131)]] Lòpavbukhin, aged eighteen, with 84,869 men, defeated 163,315 under [[per:38933|Kuthnakhlukhnen]], losing 7,486 men to the enemy's 39,354. It was the best day of his life. The [[evt:39450|Treaty of Zhidhzhinge (AY 4131)]] made Yidhe pay a large indemnity. The Yidhe account of the war (PAL·CH·06979) said that its king, "wearied of bloodshed", had "granted peace to Binge". It passes over four of the nine engagements. The scale of the war is almost beyond grasp: 418,039 dead for Yidhe and 171,371 for Binge.

## The war of choice

His next war he chose himself. In 4140 he attacked the kingdom of [[pol:1658|Ishwyithie]], ruled by Queen [[per:38747|Oi Tlyi]], a woman a year older than he, whose land had lately taken up a new faith. The [[evt:39515|Binge–Ishwyithie War]] opened well. At the [[evt:39534|Battle of Tivnu (AY 4141)]] the king beat [[per:38884|Ishuimamshi]] in a hard fight, losing 21,784 men to 26,270. Then the war turned. His generals lost four battles in four years, and at Tivnu in 4144 alone 41,172 men of Binge died. Oi Tlyi set up steles claiming 147,900 and 44,000 enemy dead. The [[evt:39562|Treaty of Tivnu (AY 4145)]] was the first peace of the reign that Binge signed as the vanquished, and Binge paid the indemnity. It had lost 153,709 men.

## The sister's empire

The record traces what followed directly to these defeats. In 4150 his elder sister [[per:38726|Yibilshiwath]], daughter of the same usurper father, proclaimed an empire of her own at [[pol:1679|Tungaruikhrimu]]. Its causes, as the record lists them, are the lost battles of 4120, 4128 and 4144 and the humiliating Treaty of Tivnu. The [[evt:39645|Second Binge Civil War]] was strange even by the standards of this age. At the [[evt:39668|Battle of Nivirathyirshi (AY 4151)]] the king led 186,242 men against a rebel force of twenty-seven under [[per:39153|Taadhen]], and lost 26,758. The next year, at the [[evt:39677|Battle of Nivirathyirshi (AY 4152)]], the armies were much the same and so was the result. The [[evt:39678|Suppression of the Tungaruikhrimu Revolt]] brought back some ninety districts, more than a quarter of the realm. Yibilshiwath signed it herself, and the record lets her live another eighteen years. A later chronicle of the rebel city remembered only that "Lòpavbukhin of Binge, swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Tungaruikhrimu", and that "the gates were opened to the enemy."

In those same years the king lost both his children. His daughter died in 4150 at eight, his son in 4151 at twelve. The record says only that both died of natural causes. By the end of that decade its measure of the realm's order stood at zero, the lowest it can register.

## The end of a stolen house

The last years brought famine. In 4157–4158 hunger in the region of Nivi killed some 124,500 people, a tenth of those it touched. Lòpavbukhin died in office in AY 4158, of natural causes. His funerary stele (PAL·IN·06150) is careful to name his father: "Lòpavbukhin, king of Binge, son of Vaayushin, dwells in this tomb." Even in death the son claimed the usurper's title as his inheritance.

He left no surviving child. The same year the [[evt:39707|Usurpation of Nithdun]], another crisis, gave the throne to [[per:39188|Nithdun]], and the house of Vaayushin ended after forty-seven years. Binge outlived it. The empire lasted until 4245, though Tungaruikhrimu broke away again in 4171. The record shows a reign spent defending a stolen throne against enemies who said so openly. It shows one great victory at eighteen, a costly war of choice in mid-reign and a civil war against his own sister, and it ends with a man burying his children and dying young. "The Old" may be the cruellest of all the epithets in this series. Lòpavbukhin never grew old. He was only made to bear, from the age of three, a burden that his father had left him.
