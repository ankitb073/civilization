To my honoured mother, at home in the village, from your son, apprentice in the house of the bookbinder at [[set:232|Yyitl]], greeting, and the blessing of all the gods who have their place and their due.

Dear mother, I write in haste and with a poor pen, for the master's good pens are locked away with his ledgers, and the master is not able to unlock them. Do not be afraid at that. He lives. I will tell you all in order, as the teachers of the [[mov:25|Myitsiyishio]] say a true account must be told.

You will have heard in the village of the sickness that men here call the [[evt:39137|White Fever]]. It began with us, in this town, after the feast of the new year. First the face goes white as tallow, then the sweating, then the cold that no blanket mends. Some rise on the fourth day. Many do not. The bell of the shrine of the gate rang from morning until night for nine days together, and then the priests forbade it, because the ringing put the whole town in terror.

The master's wife sickened first, and then the master. The journeyman fled to his brother's farm on the second night, taking his tools and, I am sorry to tell you, two of the master's. I stayed, mother. I remembered what you said when you put me to this trade: that a servant who leaves his lord in the hour of need has sold his soul for his skin. I carry the water and the broth, and I burn juniper at the door, and I say the prayers of the hour. The master's wife is risen and walks a little. The master is still in his bed, but he knew me this morning and asked after the binding of the lord bailiff's book, which I took for a good sign.

Bread is dear. The bakers who are not sick will sell only for coin, and a measure of grain is above two [[cur:847|Antli]] and a quarter, and the carters from the country will not come in at the gate, so it will be dearer. I have kept the quarter Antli you sewed in my coat. I have not spent it. I eat what the master's house eats, which is little, and the teachers say the wise withdraw from appetite, so I am, I suppose, becoming wise.

Men say the fever has gone out along the roads, to [[set:30|Namosash]] and to [[set:31|Baga]] and further, and that it is in the lands of the lord bailiff also. I pray it is not in our village. Mother, if it comes, shut the door and let no one in who has come from the town, not even the pedlar, not even your son.

The old people here say this is the second judgement on the town. The first, they say, was six years ago, when the enemy beat our men at the [[evt:39162|Battle of Yyixi]] under these very walls, and then went on to the [[evt:39163|Sack of Bakh]]. I do not know about judgements. I only know the town has not been glad since.

Do not send me anything. Do not come. Say to my sisters that I think of them, and to my uncle that I have not forgotten the debt of the plough-share, which I will pay at the end of my second year, if the gods allow. Light a lamp for me on the day of the household gods. I will light one for you.

May our lord [[per:38477|Fispatluie]] be preserved, and all the realm of [[pol:1523|Yisamashtsi]], and may the fever pass over our house as the wind passes over stubble.

Your obedient son, who kisses your hands,

written at Yyitl, in the year 3582 of the Myitsiyishio.
