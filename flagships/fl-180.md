*THE CONQUEROR'S COUCH; or, ARITHMETIC FOR KINGS. Verses by an unknown hand, sold at the bridge of [[set:0|Tallin]] on the 3rd of Nuw, in the year 2327 of the Vishithmikh. Printed without licence, and the printer not at home.*

**I.**

In twenty-three hundred, by the priests' own count,  
Our [[per:43516|Buyuen]] climbed the throne's high mount;  
His sire [[per:42941|Nivlalin]] had gone to his rest,  
And left the realm to one who loved it best,  
Or loved, at least, the cushions and the wine,  
The painted maps, the fireside, and the line  
Of generals kneeling with the morning's news:  
"Another victory, Majesty." "Which? I choose."  

**II.**

They call him Conqueror. Then let us see.  
At [[evt:44144|Piyikhzhidhe]], who held the field? Not he.  
At [[evt:44164|Binge]], who spurred along the river shore?  
A general. And the Conqueror? Indoors.  
At [[evt:44170|Piyikhzhidhe]] again the trumpets played;  
A general led; the King, I'm told, was weighed.  
Then came the [[evt:44171|Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe]], signed with grace,  
For signing is the battle he will face.  

**III.**

Now, scholars, take your slates; we'll do a sum.  
The [[evt:44233|First Tallin–Nuwkennga War]] has come.  
At [[evt:44244|Nunnga]] we brought a dozen men for one;  
For every one of theirs, four sons of ours were done.  
Their captain fell; we cheered him in the square;  
A hundred thousand of our own lay there.  
The Conqueror, informed, put down his cup,  
And said, "How tiresome. Send more. Fill it up."  

**IV.**

At [[evt:44250|Jerathnu]], hear it, and believe your ears:  
A million marched, the largest host in years,  
Against some thirty-five thousand, tired and few,  
And won, of course. What else were they to do?  
But count the graves, my masters, count them slow:  
For every man of theirs that we laid low,  
Thirteen of ours went down into the clay.  
That is the arithmetic of a Conqueror's day.  

**V.**

At [[evt:44265|Nunnga]] once again, the field was ours;  
Then the [[evt:44266|Treaty of Nunnga]], all wax and flowers:  
A province, silver, twenty years of peace.  
Three hundred thousand of our sons, at least,  
Paid for it, by the clerks' own tally-stick,  
Against not sixty thousand of the enemy. Quick,  
Strike a medal! Let the Conqueror's face  
Be stamped in silver on the widow's grace.  

**VI.**

The priests of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] in their pulpits preach  
Compassion and release; and what they teach  
Is that a final judgement waits for all,  
For kings who sit as well as men who fall.  
Your Majesty, the line of time runs straight;  
It does not turn, it does not stop, or wait.  
The ledger that you never stooped to read  
Is read aloud at last. Take heed. Take heed.  

**VII.**

And you, Prince [[per:43645|Pavbukhshifin]], who are grown,  
And stand already near your father's throne:  
Learn arithmetic. The cost of glory, son,  
Is sums of men, and not a single one  
Comes back when you subtract him. Learn it well.  
Bread is a [[cur:897|rimi]] still; the grain is cheap to sell;  
But men are dear, the dearest thing we grow.  
Your father never learnt it. Now you know.  

**VIII.**

Long live [[pol:1785|Tallin]], the city and the realm!  
Long live the honest pilot at the helm,  
Whoever he may be, when he is found.  
And may the Conqueror sleep long and sound.
