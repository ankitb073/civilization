*[Obverse]*

In the 314th year of Yufkoy, in the month of the first rains,  
before the scribes of the new house, who sit at the gate of [[dei:24|Navbuthin]], lady of the river, to hear the claims of the fields,  
a woman of the lane of the bead-makers came and spoke. She swore by Navbuthin. She swore by [[dei:20|Nothol]], lord of fire. She said:

> I was a servant in the house of the bead-maker by the river wall. I came into that house a girl of eleven years. I was in that house in the year of the [[evt:14142|Second Sack of Yuftuy]]. Twenty and one years are gone since. Now I have sons.

> Before the sack came the [[evt:14141|Battle of Yuftuy]]. The men of [[pol:466|Duramdu]] came with their spears and their carts. Our men went out. Our men were few against them, one against four, the old men said. In the evening our men came back through the gate, and they were fewer. The gate was shut. In the night the men of Duramdu broke it.

> My mistress said to me: take the boy and go down. The boy was her son, four years old. I took him down into the cellar under the floor, where the beads were kept, the blue beads and the red beads in jars. I put the boy in the big jar for the river clay. I sat on the lid. I sang to him with my mouth shut.

> Above us there was fire. Nothol was in the street. The smoke came through the boards. We heard the men of Duramdu in the house. They took the strings of beads from the pegs. They took the bronze tools. They took the master. I heard him speak, then I did not hear him. They did not lift the boards. Navbuthin covered the boards with her hand.

> On the second day it was quiet. I came up with the boy. My mistress was dead in the courtyard. The master was not in the house. We never saw him again.

*[Reverse]*

> The scribes ask: whose is the field by the river wall, the long field with the two date palms? I say: it was the field of the bead-maker. It was his father's field. It was the field of his house. I carried water to it. I gathered the dates of the two palms. The neighbour who sowed it after the sack, he took it because there was no one to say no. Now I say no.

> The scribes ask: where is the boy? The boy is a man. He is the man who stands here beside me. He is the son of the bead-maker. I put him in the jar. I sat on the lid. He is the heir of the field.

> After the sack the king made the [[evt:14143|Treaty of Yuftuy]]. They said silver was paid to Duramdu, much silver. They said lands were given. We paid. The tax-men came every year. Then the king was put down and another sat in his place. Then war again. Then last year the [[evt:14319|Collapse of the Yufkoy State]]. Everyone in the lane was afraid. Then the [[evt:14320|Rise of Yufkoy]], the new house, and you, the scribes, at the gate.

> A measure of barley is one [[cur:250|Noda]] and three parts of another. I have no field. I do not ask for myself. I ask for the boy I sat upon.

So she spoke. So she swore by Navbuthin and by Nothol.  
The man stood beside her. He showed the scribes the mark on his shoulder where the jar cut him.  
The old women of the lane said: it is so, we knew the bead-maker, the field was his.  
The scribes wrote it down. The claim goes before the lord of [[set:55|Yufkoy]].
