**THE PINNGA RELATION OF OCCURRENCES**  
*Printed at [[set:311|Pinnga]], 21 Ushuth 2089 of the Vishithmikh.*

**A TRUE AND PARTICULAR ACCOUNT of the new Engines of Fire lately proved before the Council, set down by the hand of her who devised them, [[per:41235|Vuthshuth]], that the public may be informed of the matter truly and not by report of the taverns.**

Since the trial of the fourteenth of this month, it is said in the market that I have conjured thunder out of a pipe, and that the Council has bought a dragon. I think it best, therefore, to relate what was done, in plain order, and to leave the reader to judge.

**I. Of the trial.** On the morning of the fourteenth, in the presence of [[per:41187|Shimngin]], our lord, and of the Council of [[pol:1754|Pinnga]], there were brought into the field outside the river gate three tubes of cast bronze, each about the length of a man's arm, stopped at one end and open at the other. Into each was put a charge of the black powder whose composition I have communicated to the Council and to no other person; upon the powder, a ball of lead; and at the stopped end, through a small vent, a lighted match. At the touch of the match the powder took fire with a great noise and smoke, and the ball was driven out with such force that it passed clean through a board of seasoned oak set up at forty paces, and buried itself in the bank beyond.

This was done thrice, and thrice the board was pierced. One tube, being ill cast, split along its length at the third firing; no person was hurt, the gunner standing, by my instruction, behind a screen. I record this failure as faithfully as the successes, for an account that omits its failures is not a relation but an advertisement.

**II. Of the cause.** I make no claim to sorcery. The effect proceeds from the nature of the powder, which, being kindled, does not burn slowly as wood burns but all at once, and in burning is turned wholly into a hot and furious air. This air, being shut in the tube, can escape only by the open end, and in escaping drives before it whatever lies there. The principle is no more mysterious than the bursting of a chestnut in the fire; it is only that the chestnut has never before been so well disciplined.

**III. Of the use.** Here I must answer those who say that such an engine is contrary to our faith. The teaching of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] is that the good life is the life of compassion and release from suffering. I hold to it. But I ask the objector: which is more compassionate, a city that can defend its walls with a dozen gunners, or a city that must send out a thousand of its sons to be cut down in the field? Pinnga is young. It is but fourteen years since the [[evt:41512|Founding of Pinnga]], and it has neither the numbers nor the treasure of its neighbours. A small city must have long arms, or it will soon have none.

**IV. Of the cost.** The Lord Treasurer will be glad to know that a tube of good bronze may be cast for a sum in [[cur:967|Kiril]] well below the yearly wage of a company of pikemen. I have offered to establish a powder-house outside the walls, at a safe distance, and to train such founders as the Council may appoint.

**V. A caution.** I do not pretend this engine is harmless. It is not. Whoever possesses it holds a power over other men that no one should hold lightly. I ask the Council, therefore, to keep the composition secret, to keep the tubes under the city's seal, and never to lend them to any private quarrel.

The [[evt:41627|Invention of gunpowder weapons in Pinnga]], as the clerks are already pleased to call it, is not my glory but the city's safety. If it serves that end, I am content; if it serves any other, let the blame fall where it belongs.

*Written by her own hand, and printed by order of the Council.*
