To the house of the king in [[set:79|Talenonoy]], to the elders of the gate and to the keepers of the granary, say: thus says [[per:1568|Nuwanan]], the king, the strong one, the one whom [[dei:136|Duemino]] of the storm set upon the seat.

With me it is well. With my war-carts it is well. With my spearmen, with my asses, with my tents, it is well. With you may it be well. May Duemino thunder for you. May [[dei:141|Nabüdin]] of the river fill your ditches. May Nidiktti fill your granaries. May Winba hold firm under your feet. May Tuel count your months in peace.

Hear the word of the king. Read it at the gate. Read it in the court of the granary. Let no one say it was not read.

In the nineteenth year of Nuwanan the men of [[pol:25|Yida]] shut the road of [[reg:8|Duegowinga]]. They took the toll of the boats. They took the copper. They took the tin. They said: "The trade of Duegowinga is ours." The king said: "Is it yours? It is not yours." Three times before this the road of Duegowinga was fought for; this is the fourth time, the [[evt:1299|Fourth Diebeguvebenga Trade War]], and the king of [[pol:19|Talenonoy]] went out.

In the twentieth year [[per:1569|Kudunin]], grey of head, went out by Bimunga, at the [[evt:1312|Battle of Bimnge]]. [[per:1654|Eshbelin]] of Yida came against him with twice our number. They did not stand in the field as men stand. They hid in the reeds. They struck from behind. Kudunin turned back to the camp. It was not his fault. It was the fault of Yida, who fight like thieves.

In the twenty-third year I, Nuwanan, went out myself. At [[evt:1322|Bimnge]] I met Eshbelin. Duemino roared over my head. The earth shook under my war-cart. I broke them. Six thousand of Yida and more I struck down. Of mine, barely twelve hundred went down to [[dei:137|Shyedartsi]]. The field was mine. The dead of Yida I left for the dogs.

In the twenty-fourth year [[per:1679|Duetibun]], my general, went out at [[evt:1331|Ture]]. The rain of the god was heavy; the ground was mud. The field went to Yida. But hear me: of ours, fewer than eight hundred fell there.

In the twenty-sixth year Duetibun went out at [[evt:1339|Biniynge]], by Buneynga. [[per:1689|Shishyin]] of Yida stood against him. Duetibun broke him. Three thousand of Yida fell; of ours, not fifteen hundred. In that same year Eshbelin, the dog of Yida, went down to Shyedartsi. No spear of ours struck him. The god took him. Who is with Yida now?

Now in the twenty-seventh year Shishyin came out again at [[evt:1342|Biniynge]]. He came with twenty thousand. Twenty thousand! Every man of Yida who can lift a stick, every herdsman, every boy. We were five thousand. We stood. We struck. More than three hundred of Yida we struck down, and those the river took no scribe has counted. Of ours, fewer than fifteen hundred went down. Shishyin holds the bank. Let the elders not say the king is beaten. Is the bull beaten that steps back to lower its horns?

Now hear what the king asks. Send to me grain, a thousand measures. Weigh out silver for it: for each measure one [[cur:6|tyektsi]] and a little more, as it is at the gate. Let the merchants not weigh more; if they weigh more, the king will hear of it. Send bronze for spear-points. Send the young men of the villages, those who have seen fifteen harvests. Send asses for the carts.

And let the priests of [[mov:21|Kunelmu]] slaughter a black bull for Duemino, and pour beer for Nabüdin at the river, and give a ewe to Shyedartsi for the men who went down. Let it be done in order, as the fathers did it.

And hear this also. The king has heard that in the city there is whispering. Who set Nuwanan on the seat? Duemino set him. Who gave him the field at Bimnge? Duemino gave it. Let the whisperer close his mouth, or the king will close it for him when he returns.

Written in the camp before Biniynge, when Tuel was full, in the twenty-seventh year of Nuwanan.
