[[per:44200|Nakhumbu]], by the favour of the One Power King of [[pol:1894|Fuvu]], to the settlers lately landed at [[set:321|Ndulamkhau]] in [[reg:17|Nishu]], and to the captain and council of that place: greeting, and health in a strange air.

The ship that carried your first letters reached [[set:5|Nilu]] on the fourth of this month, and I have read them twice: once as your king and once as an old man who will never cross the sea himself. Let me say at the start what I could not say to you on the quay without the whole court listening. I count myself the first settler of Ndulamkhau, even though I shall never see it. I chose the place on a chart, I argued for it in council, and I answered for the cost of your ships out of my own treasury. If the colony fails, the fault will be mine before it is yours. If it prospers, the credit will be yours before it is mine.

Some of my councillors held that a colony across the sea is a folly: land too far away to defend, and a people too far away to govern. I answered them, and I set down my answer here so that you will understand the purpose you serve. Fuvu is young among the kingdoms. Its fields are full and its sons are many. A kingdom that cannot enlarge itself by labour will in the end try to enlarge itself by war. I would sooner send you to plough an empty shore than send your brothers to burn an inhabited one. The [[evt:45132|Founding of the Colony of Ntulamkhau]] is therefore an act of peace, and I require you to keep it one.

Hear, then, my instructions, which the captain shall read aloud on the first day of each month.

First, the land. You report that the ground about the harbour had no owner when you landed. Take it, and register it in the colony's book, field by field, with the date and the names of the witnesses. You also report a kingdom that holds the greater part of that coast, and villages further inland. Their lands you shall not take. If you want land of theirs, buy it, set down the price in writing, and pay in good [[cur:923|Ngathe]] or in goods valued at the market rate. A title bought fairly will last a hundred years. A title seized will not outlast the first quarrel.

Second, justice. The [[mov:24|Uthungala]] teaches that every person may find the truth by the light within and by reason, and that the One Power asks for justice in our dealings and moderation in all things. Do not compel any native of that country in matters of faith. Show them by your conduct what your faith is worth. A settler who cheats a native in the market cheats the crown as well, and the council shall punish him as it would punish a thief in Nilu.

Third, prudence. Build the stockade before you build the storehouse, and the storehouse before the houses. Dig your wells uphill of your privies. Record every birth, death and sickness, and send me the register with each ship, so that our physicians can learn what the air of Nishu does to people born on our side of the water.

Fourth, commerce. Send samples of the timber, the clay, the stone and whatever the natives will trade, each with a label giving where it was found and what was paid for it. Grain costs about a Ngathe and a sixth the measure in Nilu this season, and I do not expect it to grow cheaper. If your fields bear well, you will feed more than yourselves.

The teachers of our faith say that prosperity is a sign of the One Power's favour. I believe them, but I would add that favour has to be earned every year and can be lost in a single one. The day you landed is entered in our annals as the [[evt:45133|Founding of Ntulamkhau]]. Make the entry for the second year a better one than the first.

I commend you to the One Power, which made the sea as it made the land, and which sees both shores at once.

Given at Nilu, 8 Vande 4587 of the Uthungala.

**NAKHUMBU, King**
