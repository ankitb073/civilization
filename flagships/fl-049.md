*Printed on the front page of the evening edition, 13 Vande 4912 of the Uthungala, beneath the national flag and the notice of the two minutes' silence. Unsigned.*

**THE NAMES CAME DOWN THE WIRE**  
*Verses for the Fallen of the War in Miuishu*

I.

They went out from the stations of [[set:136|Mbilu]] in the grey of the morning,  
twelve carriages to a train, and the trains one behind another,  
and the women stood on the platforms with bread wrapped in newspaper,  
and the bands played, and the steam went up like a prayer that has no words.

They went to [[reg:10|Miuishu]], where the rivers run brown in spring,  
as the fathers of our fathers went out in the [[evt:46685|first war]] and left their bones,  
where the people of [[pol:1968|Iluye]] had put away the old faith  
and [[evt:48083|turned to a stranger's teaching]], and closed the road to us.

II.

The names came down the wire.  
They came down the wire at night to the post-office in every town,  
and the clerk with the green eyeshade wrote them out in a fair hand,  
and in the morning they were nailed to the board beside the door.

At [[evt:48307|Lunge]], the first year, the board was full,  
and they put up a second board, and a third.  
At [[evt:48323|Lunge]] again, two years after, there were not boards enough in the town,  
and the names were printed in the newspaper in letters so small  
the old men read them with a glass, and the old women did not read them.

III.

General [[per:47102|Nazhige]] led them twice at Lunge, by the walls of [[set:184|Luna]];  
he did not live to see the end; he died in his bed, and not by the enemy.  
General [[per:47141|Nakhathash]] led them at [[evt:48358|Unushti]], by [[set:23|Unushsi]],  
where the guns spoke all day and all night and the earth was turned like a ploughed field,  
and led them at [[evt:48370|Lishimti]] at the last,  
and the flag went up over [[set:249|Lishimdi]], and the wire said: *it is done.*

IV.

The Ministry has published the figures, and we honour the Ministry.  
Fifteen millions, and forty-two thousands, and some hundreds more,  
of the sons of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]], fallen, missing, dead of wounds and fever.  
A number is a closed door. Behind each door, a kitchen,  
a chair pushed back from the table, a coat on a hook,  
a ration-book with one name struck through.

V.

Do not say they died for nothing; the map says otherwise.  
Luna is ours, and the provinces are ours, by the [[evt:48371|Treaty of Lishimti]],  
and the children of Iluye learn our songs in their schools.  
Do not say they died for everything; the widow says otherwise,  
counting her pension in [[cur:1094|dathu]] at the baker's counter,  
where the price of a loaf is a week's remembrance.

VI.

O Judge at the end of time, to whom all lines run,  
who keeps the ledger that the Ministry does not keep,  
read these names slowly. Read them in their own voices.  
We have had them printed so small. Make them large.

Chairman [[per:47096|Nazhige]] will lay the wreath at noon;  
the factory whistles will sound together across the city;  
the trams will stop where they stand, and the conductors take off their caps.  
For two minutes the whole of Mbilu will be as quiet as a post-office at night,  
before the wire begins.

VII.

Sleep, soldiers of the [[evt:48290|second war]], in the brown fields of Miuishu.  
The trains have come home empty and been painted another colour.  
We who are left will keep the moderation that the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] asks,  
justice in our dealings, and your names in our mouths,  
until the line of time is drawn to its end, and the Judge reads, and you answer.
