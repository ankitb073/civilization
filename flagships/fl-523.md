[[per:26214|Anodhon]], a servant of the One who judges, to those in [[set:11|Vieneynga]] who have heard the word and kept it, and to all in every province of [[pol:1013|Diebegovebenga]] who will hear it hereafter: clear sight, and a steady heart.

Brothers and sisters, you asked me before I left the city to write down what I had said to you in the gardens, so that it would not change in the mouth as it passed from one to another. I do so now, in the year 269 of Diebegovebenga, in the season when the grain is cheap and the granaries full, which is to say in a season when men are least inclined to think about the end of things. That is precisely why we must think about it.

**On time.** You were taught as children, as I was, that all things turn upon a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. It is a comfortable doctrine, and a lazy one. If every harvest returns, no harvest matters; if every reign returns, no deed is final. I say to you that time is not a wheel but a road. It runs from the making of the world to a final judgement, and every step upon it is taken once. What you do this year will not be undone by the turning of some later year. It will be weighed.

**On the gods.** I do not ask you to throw down the images of [[dei:16|Duemamnol]] of the sky or [[dei:15|Bienva]] of the earth, nor to stop the offerings to [[dei:17|Niedhekhte]] at the harvest. I ask you to understand them. There is one divine power behind all things, and the gods our mothers taught us are its servants, or the shadows it casts upon the world. Honour the servant for the master's sake. But do not mistake the shadow for the one who casts it.

**On the priests.** The priests of [[mov:2|Zhienro]] say that truth is kept by their order and handed down from one ordained hand to the next. I say that each of you carries a lamp within, and reason is its oil. A truth that must be guarded from the people is not a truth but a property. You need no ordination to see what is in front of you; you need only to look honestly, and to argue honestly with those who see otherwise.

**On duty.** Do not think, because you need no priest, that you need no discipline. The good life is the life of duty, order and right sacrifice. Pay your taxes. Keep your word. Serve the Queen, [[per:26164|Makhsheshienmomath]], loyally, for an orderly kingdom is a road on which the righteous may walk safely. Sacrifice, not the blood of animals only, but your own ease, your own appetite, your own pride, for the good of the city.

**On the nations.** When I was a boy, the armies of the kingdom won at the [[evt:26387|Battle of Yiranunyirin]], and won again the next year at the second [[evt:26396|Battle of Yiranunyirin]], and by the [[evt:26397|Treaty of Yiranunyirin]] a whole province and its people were joined to the crown. The old worship belongs to one people and seeks no converts; what then shall it say to these new subjects, whose fathers fought ours? It has nothing to say to them but "obey." We have more to say. The One judges all peoples, and the word is for all peoples. Go to them. Speak their tongue if you can, and ours if you must. Do not ask them to become us; ask them to become just.

**On the name.** Some of you have begun to call yourselves [[mov:101|Taletuetpoth]]. I neither command the name nor forbid it. Names are for the convenience of neighbours. What matters is the road.

Thus has the [[evt:26537|Founding of the Taletuetpoth Faith]] begun, not with a temple, but with a garden and a few dozen friends who were willing to think. Keep it so as long as you can. If ever you build temples, build them with doors that face every direction.

I will return before the rains. Read this aloud when you meet. Copy it faithfully. Correct it only where it is wrong, and tell me where.
