On a limestone stele from Rurin, its last lines worn away, a king of [[pol:1323|Lienunushal]] remembers one day in AY 3495:

> Tafutuevan, great king, king of Lienunushal, defeated Yiedha in Rueren. He killed 119,700 soldiers.

[[per:32119|Tafutuevan]] (AY 3443–3502) did win at Rurin that year, and it was the best day of his reign. But the record counts 23,269 dead of Yidha, from an army of 52,398. The stele kills more than twice the men who took the field. A life of him has to be written in that gap, because his stones are most of what he left.

## A throne without heirs

Of the man before 3473 the record says almost nothing. He was a Jinru, born in 3443, and his name means *bull iron*. It names no father or mother and gives him no office or command before he took the throne at thirty.

It does show why the throne was empty. [[per:31571|Khuetakhshalath]] the Old had reigned for fifty-two years. She had three daughters. One died at fourteen, one at thirty-two, and the last in 3473, aged thirty-five, the same year as the queen herself, who was sixty-six. The house that had ruled since the empire's founding in 3320 ended with them. The law was primogeniture, and no one was left to inherit. The record marks the [[evt:32409|Usurpation of Tafutuevan]] as a crisis, but it does not say how he took power, who backed him, or whether anyone died for it. The survey at the end of the 3470s found the empire less stable than a decade before.

He married a Jinru woman five years younger than himself, and their sons were born in 3478 and 3485. For the first eleven years of his reign the archive holds no Lienunushal document.

## Fourteen years of war

Under 3483, Yidha's chronicler wrote, "there came word that Tafutuivan of Linunushal had gathered an army against Yidhe." The record traces the [[evt:32507|Lienunushal–Yidha War]] to his usurpation, and the prize was land in Diebeguvebenga. [[pol:1324|Yidha]], like Lienunushal, was a kingdom of the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] faith, and by the end of the 3480s it had more people (13.9 million against 13.3). In 3484 a merchant's day-book at Yinnge noted in its margin: "Caravans are held back on account of the Lienunushal–Yidha War."

At first his generals did the fighting. They won at Rurin in 3484, then lost at Luunge in 3485 and at Nilu in 3486, where Ununushiefen lost 15,659 men. Then the king took the field himself at the [[evt:32542|Battle of Nilu (AY 3487)]], with 29,756 men against the 28,579 of [[per:32114|Talminin]]. He was narrowly beaten, losing 1,881 men to Yidha's 672. The chronicle gives him 68,006 men and says "Talminin held the ford against them, and the faithful of the Vishithmikh prevailed."

In 3489 [[pol:1328|Pienpedhzhe]] attacked from the other side. At the [[evt:32603|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 3490)]] its queen, the twenty-four-year-old [[per:32148|Khuevtakhshalath]], brought 39,086 men against his 32,840, and lost 5,375 to his 976. In 3492 he attacked [[per:32233|Baldun II]], 49,549 men against 57,961, and narrowly won the [[evt:32614|Battle of Himi (AY 3492)]]. In 3493 he lost the [[evt:32623|Sixth Battle of Luunge]] to [[per:32215|Kukhwutken]], with 7,897 dead to Yidha's 2,147. That year, on the other front, he broke [[per:32161|Tuentulen]] at Virnadhu, losing 2,370 men to Pienpedhzhe's 9,200. The [[evt:32627|Treaty of Virnadhu (AY 3493)]] gave him no land, only a fifteen-year truce, but it left him with one war instead of two.

## Three accounts of one afternoon

Two of his four surviving victory steles celebrate battles he did not fight. Both the Fifth Battle of Luunge in 3489 and Rurin in 3494 were won by his general Duenatdan, who was born the same year as the king. Royal stones give the crown the credit; the numbers are another matter. For Luunge in 3489 there are four witnesses. Tafutuevan's stele says: "He killed 72,700 soldiers." Yidha's muster roll for the battle counts the whole host at 36,645 and gives a plain order: "Each man to bring bread for ten days." Yidha's chronicler blames the weather for the defeat, "because the river rose and cut the army in two," and puts his own dead at 5,207 and the enemy's at 8,947. The record has 10,517 dead of Yidha and 2,424 of Lienunushal. So the stele killed nearly twice the army that came. The chronicle halved its own dead and more than tripled the enemy's.

The chronicle inflates too. It gives Tafutuevan 116,818 men at the Sixth Battle of Luunge, more than the whole army Lienunushal kept at the end of the decade (96,104). He had 51,114. Both kings dedicated their steles to the same war goddess of the old Jinru gods. Tafutuevan's read: "He erected this stele for Buevme, his lady. May Buevme destroy the name of the enemy." Baldun's stele for the Sixth Battle of Luunge names her Vulmi and claims 28,800 dead, against the record's 7,897. Yet Yidha's chronicler thanked "the One whom the Vishithmikh teach," and the record counts both kingdoms as Veshethmekh throughout the war. A state's official faith and the faith of its war stones were not the same thing.

## Rurin and Himi

In 3495 Tafutuevan attacked near Rurin with 23,530 men. Talminin, who had beaten him at Nilu eight years before and was now sixty-five, waited with 52,398. Outnumbered more than two to one, the king won the [[evt:32633|Battle of Rurin (AY 3495)]]. Yidha lost 23,269 men, the heaviest loss either side suffered in any battle of the war, and Lienunushal lost 5,883. An Iethithi soldier's memoir of 3536 recalled that Yidha "had been brought low at Rurin by other hands, and its strength lay broken like a reed."

The war ended in 3497. By the [[evt:32645|Treaty of Himi (AY 3497)]], which Tafutuevan and Baldun II signed at [[set:17|Himi]], Yidha ceded twelve districts across three regions, Himi among them, and swore to a fifteen-year truce. Yidha's chronicler gave this one sentence: "Some villages of no account were given up, which had always been poor." Around 3500 Himi alone held some 48,000 people, nearly half as many as Linnshal, the capital. In 3497 the king also set up a boundary stone in Baan, the plainest text he left: "Tafutuevan, king of Lienunushal, erected this boundary stone in Vane. May Buevme curse the enemy." Here he is king, not great king.

The war was won, and it was paid for. Lienunushal lost 80,302 men and Yidha 78,309, so the victor buried more. Still, the king fought better than the men he sent. In his four battles against Yidha his side lost about 20,000 men and Yidha about 32,000. In the eight his generals fought, Lienunushal lost some 60,000 and Yidha 46,000.

## Rows like sheaves

He died of natural causes in 3502, aged fifty-nine, after twenty-nine years on the throne he had taken. His elder son [[per:32333|Tuethen]], then twenty-four, succeeded him. The survey at the end of the 3490s, his last, found Lienunushal at 211 districts against the 191 he inherited, with 14.6 million people, and steadier than in any survey since his birth.

In 3504 a lament for the war's dead was written at Linnshal, to be sung "on the day of the dead in every house of the faith, at the command of our lord." It remembers "Our late lord Tafutuevan, who now sleeps with the faithful," who "broke the Yiedha at Rurin, so that their dead lay in rows like sheaves". Unlike any stele, it also counts: "Eighty thousand and more of ours lie in that country," and the record agrees. It tells those who celebrate to "count the empty hearths." It even asks prayers for the dead of Yidha, "for they too lie in the hand of the Most High, though they did not know his name." That is generous, and it is wrong, because Yidha's chronicler thanked the same One.

The man himself stays hidden behind his stones. We do not know what he looked like, whom he trusted, or what he believed. The numbers show a man with no recorded claim who took an empty throne and held it for twenty-nine years. He won four of the six battles he fought himself, and he left his son truces with both his enemies. At Rurin he carved some ninety-six thousand deaths that never happened. The twenty-three thousand men who really died there have no stone. The nearest thing to one is a lament written by their enemies, which asked prayers for them too.
