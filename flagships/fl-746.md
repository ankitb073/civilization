*Testimony given before the magistrates in the dispute between the heirs of a merchant of the street of cisterns, concerning the goods of his house. The witness served in that house fourteen years. Her words are set down as she spoke them.*

You ask me, sirs, what I saw on the day [[set:28|Tlu]] was taken, and what became of my master's goods. I will tell you what I saw, and not what I have heard. A servant learns early that a house has many rooms, and that she stands in only one of them.

It was ten years ago, in the last year of the kingdom of [[pol:995|Tlu]]. For two years the news had been bad. Our soldiers had been beaten at the [[evt:23693|Battle of Ashash]], and beaten there again, and then at the [[evt:23711|Battle of Tunavükhini]], and after that there was no army between us and the men of [[pol:994|Tamdal]]. Grain doubled in a month. My master bought oil, because oil keeps, and filled the cellar with it. That is why I am alive.

When the shouting came over the wall, my mistress was already gone to her sister's in the hills with the two elder children. The youngest boy had a fever and could not be moved. My master told me to take him down among the oil jars and to stay there until he called. He did not call.

We were in the cellar three days and two nights. On the first day I heard men in the courtyard, and the noise of the household gods being pulled from their niche. On the second day I smelled smoke, and the boy coughed, and I put my hand over his mouth and prayed he would not die under it. He did not. He is the young man who sits there now, sirs, and he is taller than I am.

On the third day it was quiet. I came up. My master lay in the courtyard by the cistern. I covered him with the reed mat from the kitchen, for there was nothing else. The rooms on the street side were burned. The rooms on the garden side were whole, but empty.

Now to the question you have put to me. The strongbox of bronze, with the bands of iron, did not go with the steward. I saw it go. It went on the shoulders of two soldiers of Tamdal, out of the gate, on the first day, while I watched through the grating of the cellar. The steward was not in the house that day. Where he was I do not know, and guessing is not testimony.

The teachers of the [[mov:25|Miyituyishiu]] say that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I learned that lesson in a cellar: one mouthful of oil-cake in the morning, one at night. I have been moderate ever since, and not by choice.

Since then, sirs, as you know better than I, the [[evt:23713|Conquest of Tlu]] was sealed, and we have other masters, and the courts sit as before. I am paid now a [[cur:560|Mina]] a month and my keep, in the house of a cloth-seller. Bread is cheap again; a measure of grain is a little under a mina. The city has been rebuilt, mostly. The smell of the smoke has not.

Men say a city is its walls, or its laws, or its citizens. I say that on the day of the [[evt:23712|Sack of Zhükhi]] it was its cellars, and those who had none are not here to give testimony.

This is what I saw. I swear it by the gods of this city, which are the gods of my master and my own.
