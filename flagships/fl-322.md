*[Obverse. A ruled tablet from the temple archive at Tallin; the opening invocation is written in red-filled signs.]*

Praise, praise to [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear, whose feet are in the dust of the field!  
Praise to [[dei:16|Dumamnul]], father of the high sky, who sees the field from above!  
Praise to the queen, praise to the queen of [[pol:25|Yida]], the strong-armed, the lion of [[set:0|Tallin]]!  
Lift the drum, lift the reed pipe, lift the voice. The field was Yida's.  

The men of [[pol:12|Jinontonu]] shut their hands.  
They shut their hands on the silver, they shut their hands on the grain.  
They did not bring the gift to Tallin, they did not bow at the gate of Tallin.  
So the queen lifted her hand to Vulme, and Vulme heard,  
and the spears went down into [[reg:9|Tupujinga]].  
Four summers, five summers the spears went down; that was the [[evt:738|Third Yida–Chinontonu War]].  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

The first field was the [[evt:748|Battle of Zhinontunu]], by the walls of [[set:6|Jinontonu]].  
[[per:957|Shenshetkuth]] the queen stood in the front, bronze on her breast,  
she stood against [[per:1049|Pimlomin]], the general of Jinontonu.  
Our spears were as the barley of the plain; theirs were as the stones of the threshing floor, few.  
Pimlomin turned his back. The field was Yida's.  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

The second field was the [[evt:756|Battle of Linnshal]], by the walls of [[set:18|Lenunushal]].  
[[per:1057|Puftovan]], the queen's general, the swift one, went out,  
he went out against [[per:1058|Puyedujinin]], the general of Jinontonu.  
Twelve thousand spears went with Puftovan; the dust of them hid the sun.  
Puyedujinin ran. The field was Yida's.  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

The third field was the [[evt:761|Battle of Puthnimdu]], by the walls of [[set:90|Putnimdo]].  
Puftovan went out a second time,  
he went out against [[per:789|Dolulrinin]], the king of Jinontonu himself.  
The king of Jinontonu put on his bronze. The king of Jinontonu took off his bronze.  
He went in at the gate of Putnimdo and shut the gate. The field was Yida's.  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

The fourth field was the [[evt:764|Battle of Puthnimdu]], the second time, by the walls of Putnimdo.  
The queen went out herself a second time, Shenshetkuth went out,  
she poured oil to Vulme at dawn, she poured beer to Dumamnul at noon,  
she went against Puyedujinin a second time, and a second time he ran.  
The field was Yida's. Four fields, and four times the field was Yida's.  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

*[About six lines broken. Legible: "[…] our brothers […] Vinba has taken them […]".]*

Then the king of Jinontonu came out of the gate of Putnimdo.  
Dolulrinin came out, and he bowed his face to the dust before the queen.  
They swore by the gods of the sky, they swore by the gods of the earth,  
and that was the [[evt:765|Treaty of Puthnimdu]].  
The land of Tupujinga which they held, it is Yida's.  
The silver of Jinontonu, every year it comes to Tallin, fifteen years it comes to Tallin.  
The king of Jinontonu is the servant of the queen. His road is open to her merchants.  
Ten years the spears will sleep. The wheel turns.  
Lift the drum. The field was Yida's.  

*[Reverse.]*

O Vulme, red-footed, take the ram, take the oil, take the bronze we hang in your house!  
O Dumamnul, take the smoke, take the song!  
O Nidhikhti, lady of the barley, make the fields of Tupujinga fat for Yida!  
O Yenan of the night, lord of the moon, light the road of the queen!  

Who is like the queen? Who stands in front with the bronze on her breast?  
Who goes out twice and twice comes home? Who is like the queen of Yida?  
Praise, praise to Shenshetkuth. The field was Yida's.  

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Sung in the house of Vulme at Tallin, in the 13th year of Shenshetkuth, at the hanging of the spears.  
> Whoever erases the queen's name from this tablet, may Vulme stand against him in the field.
