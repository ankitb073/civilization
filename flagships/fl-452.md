**REPUBLIC OF MANA.**

**REPORT OF THE GOVERNOR FOR THE LODE TERRITORIES ON THE LATE OPERATIONS AND THE WITHDRAWAL OF THE ESTABLISHMENT.**

*Presented to the Ministry, [[set:13|Tzeventoa]], and ordered to be printed. 10 Mua 2601 of the Eheshio.*

---

Sir,

I have the honour to submit, as required by the regulations, my report upon the conduct and conclusion of the [[evt:46030|Mana Colonial War in Luthe]], together with the returns of strength and casualties, and such observations as my office permits.

**1. Origins.** The Ministry will not need reminding that the operations arose from the [[evt:46029|Mana Claim against Luthe]], which the Republic advanced in good faith and on sound title, and which the chiefs of [[reg:27|Lode]] refused to acknowledge. The decision to enforce that claim by arms was taken at Tzeventoa. It is not for me to review it.

**2. Strength.** I must place on record, with all respect, the following figures. The column of the Republic under Colonel [[per:45323|Voshkheyof]] at no time during three campaigning seasons fell below sixty-nine thousand officers and men. Its strength at the three principal engagements was 86,646, 70,857 and 69,275. Opposed to it, according to the estimates of my intelligence officers, which I consider moderate, were bodies of 24,371, 28,747 and 19,379 respectively. I repeatedly applied by despatch for ammunition and transport. The replies are on file.

**3. The engagements.** All three were fought before the native town of [[set:25|Khavo]] and are officially styled as follows:

- The [[evt:46035|Battle of Kavu]], first season. Enemy commanded by [[per:45259|Navavon]]. Our losses, 10,923; the enemy's, 2,701.
- The [[evt:46042|Battle of Kavu]], second season. Enemy commanded by [[per:45185|Nalale]]. Our losses, 10,551; the enemy's, 6,471.
- The [[evt:46055|Battle of Kavu]], third season. Enemy again under Navavon. Our losses, 26,437; the enemy's, 4,845.

The total of our dead for the war is therefore 47,911. The enemy's, by careful count of the burial parties, is 14,017. I do not pretend that any column in the history of the Republic has paid so dearly for so little; I ask only that the Ministry weigh the despatches on file when the conduct of Colonel Voshkheyof and his men comes, as I understand it will, to be discussed in the Chamber.

**4. The result.** It would nevertheless be dishonest to describe the outcome otherwise than as it was. On each of the three occasions the column, having exhausted its ammunition against an enemy who did not cease to come on, was obliged to retire upon its base, and the field remained with [[pol:1826|Lode]]. After the third season it was clear to me, and I so reported by despatch, that the position could not be held at any cost the Republic was prepared to pay. The Government of the Republic drew the same conclusion.

**5. The peace.** The [[evt:46056|Treaty of Kavu]] was accordingly concluded at Khavo between His Excellency [[per:45307|Melianuo]], for the Republic, and Nalale for the chiefs of Lode. Its terms are known to the Ministry: the Republic renounces its claim, pays an indemnity, and observes a truce of twenty years. I signed nothing and was consulted on nothing, but I carried out the withdrawal in good order. The last of the establishment's stores were embarked within the period stipulated.

**6. Observations.** I offer three:

(a) The enemy are few, but brave and well led. Navavon and Nalale handled bodies of men a third the size of ours with a skill that I should be glad to see in our own staff college.

(b) A colonial policy that is willing to claim a country but not to supply the force it sends there is not a policy but a wager. The Republic has lost this wager.

(c) I would ask that our dead be honoured by name in the rolls, though the rolls run to forty-seven thousand names. They were sent to do a hard thing without the means to do it.

**7. Religious.** The chaplain of the [[mov:57|Eheshio]] attached to the column read the funeral office over our dead and, at my order, over theirs. He reminded us that the wheel turns, and that what has ended may begin again. I record his view without endorsing it as policy.

I have the honour to be, Sir, your obedient servant,

THE GOVERNOR.
