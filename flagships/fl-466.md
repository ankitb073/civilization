*[Obverse. A large proclamation tablet; the opening lines are cut in signs twice the ordinary height. Copies were to be set up at the gates of each town.]*

**Hear it, all who pass through this gate.**

By [[dei:83|Hath]] of the sky, who sees all oaths. By Mahtu of the sun, who shows all deeds. By Niwi of the river, who carries the boundary. By [[dei:87|Hutlhi]] below, who keeps the oath-breakers.

The elders of [[pol:808|Tainakhath]], the heads of the houses, the priests of [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]] and all the people of the city and the fields proclaim this, in the 6th year of [[per:18026|Uhe]], our king.

Hear first what was done to us.

For many years the hand of [[pol:723|Mane]] lay on [[set:42|Tainakhath]]. Every harvest the officers of Mane came up the high road with their scales. They weighed our barley and took the best of it. They weighed our silver and took the most of it. They took our young men to carry spears in the wars of Mane, and the young men did not come back. They set their own judges in our gate, and the judges of Mane judged for Mane. We sent gifts. We sent more gifts. We bowed. It was not enough.

Then in the year of the omen, when [[dei:84|Lashli]] of the moon rose red over the river three nights together, Uhe stood up in the gate and said: "Enough." That was the [[evt:18136|Revolt of Tainakhath]]. The officers of Mane were sent back down the high road with empty scales. So began the [[evt:18137|Tainakhath War of Independence]].

Hear now how the gods judged between us.

In the year after the revolt, at the [[evt:18178|Battle of Lishimti]], our general [[per:18058|Nihtluk]] went out against [[per:17834|Katlidifath]], queen of Mane, before the walls of [[set:249|Lashimti]]. The men of Mane were broken, and the field was ours.

In the next year Uhe himself went out against the queen at Lashimti, and Lashli hid his face, and the field was hers. We do not hide it. We buried our dead and sharpened the spears.

In the year after that Uhe went out again, against [[per:18090|Tlamhin Yuf]], the general of Mane, at Lashimti, and the field was ours.

In the year after, Uhe carried the spears far from home. That was the [[evt:18211|Battle of Mane]], by the walls of [[set:171|Mahne]], and again Tlamhin Yuf was broken.

In this year the queen came out a third time to Lashimti, and a third time we met her; that was the [[evt:18226|Battle of Lishimti]], the last. Her spears went down. The field was ours.

*[Reverse.]*

Then the queen of Mane sat down with our king at Lashimti, under the sky of Hath, and they swore. That was the [[evt:18227|Treaty of Lishimti]]. Katlidifath set her seal; Uhe set his seal. Mane has let Tainakhath go. Ten years no spear shall pass between us.

Therefore hear the word of the elders and the people:

Tainakhath is its own house. Tainakhath has its own king.  
Tainakhath pays no tribute to Mane, not a measure of barley, not a ring of silver.  
No judge of Mane sits in our gate. No officer of Mane weighs in our market.  
Our young men carry spears for Tainakhath alone.  
Our gods are fed at our own altars, from our own fields, by our own priests.  
[[dei:86|Shu]] of the grain, the barley is yours and ours. Mane shall not touch it.

Whoever brings the scales of Mane back up the high road, whoever bows the gate of Tainakhath to a stranger, whoever breaks this tablet or turns it to the wall: may Hath shut the sky on him, may Niwi dry his canal, may Hutlhi open her mouth for him before his time.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Set up at the gate of Tainakhath in the 6th year of Uhe, king, in the month of the first threshing, when the spears came home.
