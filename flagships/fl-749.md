*Sung by those who survived, in the year 866 of the [[mov:62|Nidunde]].*

Tell it plainly, for ornament belongs to the fortunate:  
the city of [[set:146|Teshivi]] was taken, and the city burned.  
Not by a thunderbolt, not by a judgement out of heaven,  
but by men who were hungry for land, and who were many.  

Seven summers ago we stood at the [[evt:28715|battle of Dyizhih]]  
against other strangers, and lost, and lost our captain;  
we gave the southern fields away at the [[evt:28716|Treaty of Dyizhih]],  
and told ourselves: a smaller city is still a city.  

Two summers ago the men of [[pol:1078|Lud]] came and beat us.  
This summer they came as the tide comes over a sandbar,  
not wave by wave, but all the sea at once:  
a hundred of theirs and more for every one of ours.  

At the [[evt:28792|battle of Dyizhih]] two thousand stood at the gate.  
Our king stood with them. The people call him unlucky;  
let them call him so, but let no one say he did not stand.  
[[per:27911|Nabul]] lives. Many who stood beside him do not.  

Then came the [[evt:28793|Fourth Sack of Dyizhih]], as the clerks will write it,  
counting as clerks count, as if the fourth were lighter than the first.  
Fire in the street of the rope-makers, fire in the cloth-hall,  
the roof of the house of prayer fallen in on its own lamps.  

I saw a baker of our street give bread to a wounded stranger,  
one of the men of Lud, who lay by the well with his leg broken.  
"Why feed him?" a woman cried. The baker said:  
"The teaching is compassion. It does not ask whose son he is."  

This is the whole of philosophy, as far as I can learn it:  
walls are taken, and walls are built again;  
three times before, the old histories say, this city was taken,  
and three times the grandchildren swept the ash from the doorsteps.  

The teachers say the city is not its walls but its citizens.  
Then Teshivi lives, for we are living, a few of us,  
under the scorched plane trees, counting the children,  
sharing out the barley that was buried in the cellars.  

Time runs in a line, the preachers say, and not in a circle;  
it runs to a final judgement, where every deed is weighed.  
On that day let the men of Lud answer for this burning,  
and let us answer too, for whatever we have burned.  

The war is not finished. Their camps are in our fields.  
Their horses graze where the barley should be standing.  
What the next year brings, no singer of this song can tell you;  
we sing only what we saw, and what we lost, and that we live.  

Release from suffering, the teaching promises:  
not that suffering ends, but that it need not own us.  
So we sing, and set the stones back one upon another,  
and the plane trees, scorched on one side, put out leaves on the other.
