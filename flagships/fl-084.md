title: Litany for the Gate of Unushuki

*To be sung at the gate of Unushuki on the day of the dark moon. The lamenter strikes the drum seven times. The women answer at each "Ah".*

Ah, [[pol:275|Mikhuthitsu]]! Ah, the house of the great kings!  
Ah, the gate of [[set:23|Unushuki]], where the kings went in and out!  
The gate stands. The kings do not go in and out.  
The king of the name sits in another city.  
Unushuki is a town among towns. Ah, Unushuki!  

*The lamenter strikes the drum.*

Weep for Khungumnu. Weep for Khungumnu three times.  
Weep once for [[evt:7938|Khungumnu]], where our spearmen went against the stranger.  
The field was not ours that day, but [[dei:5|Nyishashuda]] of war remembered us,  
and in the end the stranger bowed, and sent tribute to the house.  
Weep twice for [[evt:8143|Khungumnu]], where the stranger came against us.  
The field was not ours. Our sons lay in the furrows like dropped sheaves.  
Weep three times for [[evt:8154|Khungumnu]], where the stranger came again.  
The field was not ours. Nyishashuda turned her face away.  
Then at the [[evt:8155|Treaty of Khungumnu]] we gave Khungumnu itself into his hand.  
We gave him the town. We gave him the fields. We weighed out silver to him.  
Ah, Khungumnu! Ah, the town that was given away!  

*The lamenter strikes the drum.*

Weep for Ontiedzey, the field of the long day.  
At [[evt:8251|Ontiedzey]] our host went out, and the field was not ours.  
Yet the gods are strange. Yet the wheel turns.  
At the [[evt:8272|Treaty of Akhtse]] the enemy bowed to the house.  
He gave us towns. He gave us the town of the oath.  
Five years the house held them. Five years only.  

*The lamenter strikes the drum.*

Then came the year of the breaking, the [[evt:8324|Collapse of the Mikhuthitsu State]].  
Then the one house became four houses.  
The first keeps the name: [[pol:361|Mikhuthitsu]], the son that wears his father's cloak.  
The second is [[pol:362|Kiya]]; it set up its own gate and its own priests.  
The third is [[pol:364|Kishuyitsuki]]; it holds Akhtse, the town of the oath.  
The fourth was [[pol:363|Punikyithdiy]]; it sat in a town that Akhtse gave us.  
It stood six years, and it is gone already. Strangers sit in its town.  
Ah, Punikyithdiy! The child that died before it was weaned!  

*The lamenter strikes the drum.*

[[dei:0|Katsuye]] of the sky, you saw it, and you did not send the hail upon the stranger.  
[[dei:3|Nyiliki]] of the river, you saw it, and you did not drown the stranger at the ford.  
[[dei:4|Lashulake]] of the moon, you counted the months of the breaking.  
Count them now: sixteen years. Sixteen years the house has been four houses.  
In the market the silver is weighed by the [[cur:168|kafbaf]] of the house,  
but the hand that weighs it does not bow to the house.  
Ah, the true weight! Ah, the scales of the great kings!  

*The lamenter lays down the drum. The priests answer.*

Hear the teaching of [[mov:0|Lakimu]], which our fathers kept in order:  
All things turn on the great wheel. What ends will begin again.  
The house that was broken will be bound again.  
The gate that is empty will be filled again.  
Kings will go in and out at Unushuki again.  
Turn, wheel. Turn, and do not stop.  

*The women answer:* Turn, wheel. Ah, Mikhuthitsu! Turn, wheel.

*Written in the two hundred and twenty-third year of Mikhuthitsu, on the day of the dark moon, at the gate.*
