*Before the scribes of the house of the king, in the 2nd year of [[per:15552|Ia Imam]], king of [[pol:575|Nizh]]. The servant of the house of the bronze-smith speaks, for the widow of the house, who asks that her husband be written among the dead and his seal be given to his son.*

I am the servant of the house of the bronze-smith by the gate of the moon. Twelve years I have served the house. I swear by [[dei:4|Lashla]] of the moon, whose gate it is: I saw what I say.

Two summers ago the old king fell in the [[evt:15643|battle of Litlitli]], before the walls. The boy his son sat on the seat. The boy is our king. The men of [[pol:555|Tlaiy]] came the next summer and fought before the walls. They came again in this year, many, many, like ants on spilled honey.

Our men went out to the [[evt:15665|battle of Litlitli]], before the walls, the third time. They were few, a handful, a handful against a field. Their captain fell. My master did not go out. My master was at the furnace. He said: the city will need spearheads. He made spearheads. He made spearheads all the night.

In the morning there was shouting at the gate of the moon. The gate broke. My master said to me: go down. Take the boy. Take the seal. Go down into the cellar where the tin is kept, and put the tin-baskets over the door.

I went down. I took the boy, the son of my master, seven years old. I took the seal of the house, the seal of green stone, on its cord. I put the tin-baskets over the door. My master's wife was at her mother's house in the upper city. My master stood in the workshop with a hammer in his hand.

I heard the men of Tlaiy in the workshop. I heard them take the bronze. I heard them take the ingots, the bowls, the spearheads he had made. I heard my master speak. I heard him speak once. Then I did not hear him.

Two nights we were in the cellar. The boy slept on my knees. I gave him water from the quenching jar. On the third day the city was quiet, and a voice cried in the street in our own tongue. I went up.

My master lay in the workshop by the furnace. The furnace was cold. The ingots were gone, the bowls were gone. The anvil they did not take; it was too heavy. I closed his eyes. I put his hammer in his hand. The boy saw. I could not stop the boy from seeing.

This is what I say to the scribes. The bronze-smith is dead in the [[evt:15666|Third Sack of Litlitli]], by the hand of the men of Tlaiy. I saw him dead. Here is the seal of the house. I give it into the hand of his son, before you.

The scribes say: the king has sworn at the [[evt:15667|Treaty of Litlitli]], and the city will give silver to Tlaiy for years. I say: the house of the bronze-smith has an anvil, a cold furnace, a widow, a boy, a servant. It had forty [[cur:302|Iwal]] of bronze on the shelves. Now it has none.

The widow asks: let her husband be written among the dead. Let the seal be the boy's. Let the anvil be the boy's. I will work the bellows until the boy can lift the hammer.

[[dei:3|Niyl'i]] of the river, carry my master to the house below. [[dei:5|Niyshashta]] of war, you have eaten in [[set:40|Litlatlchi]]. Be full now. Be full.

I have spoken. Let the scribes write.
