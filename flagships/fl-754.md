**To our most reverend lord the abbot, and to the noble lord of this manor, the humble complaint of the farmers and freeholders of the parish, in the year 4708 from the founding of [[set:0|Tallin]].**

Most reverend and noble lords,

We are your obedient people, who pay the tithe at the abbey barn and do the boon-work on the lord's demesne, as our fathers did. We set down our grievances in order, that you may judge them, as the Book bids rulers judge between the poor of the land.

**First.** Thirteen years ago the first of the [[cul:55|Zhigu]] came down out of [[reg:2|Nivi]], in the time of the [[evt:36592|Famine in Nivi]], with their carts and their goats and their children. We gave them bread at the church door, as is commanded. We thought they would pass on. They did not. The friars say a thousand thousand of them have come into the land of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], and we believe it, for in this parish there are now nearly as many Zhigu hearths as old ones. Every year more came, until two years past they stopped, because, they say, there is no one left in Nivi to come. The clerks write it in their rolls as the [[evt:36593|Migration of the Jego into Yidhe]]; in this parish we call it the coming of the goats.

**Second.** They graze their goats on the common, and the goats strip the hedges and the young ash. They cut the lord's wood and say they did not know it was his.

**Third.** At harvest they hire themselves out for bread alone. Before they came, a reaper had a [[cur:778|Zhiflun]] a week and his dinner. Now the bailiff says, Why should I pay you a zhiflun, when the Zhigu will do it for a loaf? So our sons stand idle while the newcomers reap.

**Fourth.** They have cleared the waste beyond the river, and now there is so much grain at market that the price is fallen to little more than half a zhiflun the measure. This is good for him who buys, and ruin for him who sells. But the tithe is the same, and the rent is the same.

**Fifth.** Our sons have gone to the king's war beyond the border, and it is said that our lord [[per:36196|Torinin]] has twice beaten the enemy at Nuvun, at the [[evt:36775|Battle of Nuvun]] this very year. But our sons are there, and the newcomers' sons are here.

**Sixth.** Some among us say the Zhigu brought the trouble of the rebels with them, and that the rising which was put down at the [[evt:36679|Suppression of the Lininadhe Revolt]] was stirred by their kind. We do not know this to be true. We set it down only because it is said.

We do not ask that they be driven out. At the Judgement every soul shall be asked what it did for the stranger, and we fear that Judgement. And it must be said that when our neighbour's wife died in childbed last winter, a Zhigu woman nursed the infant at her own breast and asked nothing. Their children are thin, as ours were.

But we ask, humbly:

That the newcomers be given land of their own beyond the river, under a reeve of their own, and kept to it.

That goats be forbidden on the common.

That the reaper's wage be fixed by the lord's court, for Zhigu and for us alike.

That the tithe be lightened while grain is cheap, as it was raised when grain was dear.

And we pray that the priests preach to them in their own tongue, so that they may hear the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] as we hear it, and be one people with us before the Judgement, if not before the bailiff.
