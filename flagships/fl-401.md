**First month, the fourth day.** I begin this book on the advice of my father, who kept one in a hard year of his own and said that whoever writes down troubles counts them only once. The granary of our district stands half full; the tax-gatherers took the other half in the autumn, for the silver owed to Tomözel must come from somewhere, and it has come, as such things always come, from the barley of [[reg:19|Mankhe]]. Grain at the market today: five [[cur:483|bedu]] the measure. My mother says that when she was a bride it was not two.

**Second month, the ninth day.** I went to the temple of [[dei:17|Nithikhchi]] with a jar of oil, which I could ill spare. The priest of [[mov:2|Tsinru]] told the people that prosperity is the sign of divine favour, as the fathers taught. I do not doubt it. But I stood in the court and looked at the faces around me, and I could not find the sinner among them whose wickedness had dried the sky. If want is the sign of disfavour, then the gods are displeased with the whole province at once, and with the children first. I set down this thought here and will not say it aloud.

**Third month, the second day.** It has not rained. The seed in the low fields has come up thin and yellow. A neighbour says the ground has never recovered since the armies went over it at the [[evt:21588|battle of Tivintukhe]]; that was five years ago and the fields bore after it; but an army is easier to blame than the sky.

**Fourth month, the fifteenth day.** I have been thinking of the years of my youth, when the news came of the [[evt:21493|Treaty of Tivnu]], and the whole of [[set:10|Navnge]] lit lamps, and silver came in from the defeated by the cartload, and districts were added to the empire as a man adds rooms to his house. We thought then that [[pol:866|Yide]] would never be poor again. Then came the [[evt:21581|Yide–Tomözel War]]. Our young men went to the [[evt:21599|battle of Kheye]], and fewer came home. They went again to the [[evt:21638|battle of Kheye]], and the old king fell there, and fewer came home again. Then the [[evt:21639|Treaty of Kheye]], and the silver went out by the cartload to the victors as it had once come in. All things turn on the great wheel, the priests say; what rises will be brought low, and what is low will rise. I have now seen the first half of that teaching with my own eyes.

**Fifth month, the eleventh day.** Grain at six bedu, and the merchants are ashamed to say it. The families from the upland villages are on the roads. They carry their pots. Of every five houses in the street of the dyers, one stands empty; I counted them.

**Sixth month, the third day.** Our king, [[per:21468|Shuyalun]], is a youth of sixteen, and it is said the treasury is empty. May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky give him wisdom beyond his years, for he has inherited his father's debts before his father's beard. I pray for him daily. A city owes its king its prayers when it can no longer pay him its taxes.

**Seventh month, the first day.** The priests carried the image of [[dei:15|Vinbe]] of the earth through the fields, with the drums and the white goats. The whole town walked behind. Even those who mocked the priests in the spring walked behind. Then I gave a handful of the last old barley to the widow's child next door, the only sacrifice I am sure was heard.

**Ninth month, the sixth day.** Two burials in our street today, both of the old. The young go away; the old stay and die. Yenan stood full over the graveyard this night, and I thought how many times he has looked down on hungry years and on fat ones, and how little he seems to choose between them.

**Tenth month, the fourteenth day.** The harvest is in, such as it is. It will not last the winter. I do not know what the spring will bring, and I will not pretend to. I write it here so that, if I live to read it, I shall remember what it was not to know.

*Written in the 2nd year of Shuyalun.*
