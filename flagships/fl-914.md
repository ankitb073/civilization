In AY 3448 a candle-maker who had lost everything wrote to his cousin in the hill country. Six years earlier the armies of Iethithi had sacked his city, Tungaruikhrimu. His father's shop in the street of the chandlers had burned, and the family had left through the river gate "with the children and one mule and the clothes we stood in." Since then they had lived below the walls of the same city "like sparrows in a barn." But his letter (PAL·LT·01816, *Letter of a Dispossessed Chandler to His Cousin*) is not mainly about the sack. It is about a lawsuit. The invaders had also burned the hall of records, and with it the deed to the family house. Now a stranger had built a stable on the plot and brought two witnesses, both his cousins.

> The magistrate is not unkind. He says: bring me a paper. I say: the paper is in the same place as my father. He shrugs. It is the will of the queen's law, he says, and the queen's law needs paper.

The record confirms the detail that makes his case hopeless. The sack of Tungaruikhrimu in 3442 was one of nineteen sacks in this period in which the archives burned. I begin with this letter because it shows two things at once about the quarter-millennium covered here. The first is how far writing had reached. Rag paper, a novelty at Shityi three centuries earlier, had become something a provincial magistrate demanded before he would recognize a man's property. The second is how thin the margins had grown. Grain, the chandler writes, was "two Ödon the measure and the bakers make the loaves small." The world was getting colder, and it had nearly stopped growing.

## A world that stopped growing

In each of the last two chapters the number of people living under states rose by tens of millions, some 39 million and then 27. In this one it nearly stalled: 171.8 million in 3250, 180.3 million in 3370, 182.9 million in 3490. That is a gain of 11 million in 240 years. The climate is the likeliest suspect. The average temperature of the period was 0.34 °C below the long-run mean, against 0.13 °C in the last chapter. There were three great eruptions, of Mount Nigitlhi in 3287, Mount Nvideyo in 3437 and Mount Vininvukhnir in 3452, and a long cold spell that the record dates from 3410 to 3469. Every decade from the 3410s to the 3460s was at least half a degree colder than normal. The 3430s and 3450s, each marked by one of the eruptions, came close to a full degree, the coldest years of the chapter. Large droughts followed: the [[evt:30094|Onset of the Great Drought in Daadüdhih (AY 3271)]] across 310 districts, and the [[evt:31292|Onset of the Great Drought in Baan (AY 3379)]] across 280.

There was no great plague in the period; the record lists none. Famine did the work instead. A famine in Tunnge in 3290 killed 699,119 people in the Lienonoshal Empire. In 3378 a famine in Yiutl killed 1,110,612 people in the Tomzel Empire, the deadliest of these years. In 3460, in the depth of the cold spell, a famine in Mii killed another 592,248 Tomzel subjects. Each struck about one person in eleven in the lands it reached. Two of these three famines are recorded among the causes of the collapses that follow. In the decade of the second, grain at Thimisitsiy, the old Yiotl capital, cost nearly three times what it had a century before.

## The great empire breaks

The chapter opened with [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]] at the height described at the end of the last one. It grew further, to 35.9 million people and 448 districts in 3310, the most populous state the world had yet seen. Its rulers in these years were [[per:29468|Tuemagonen]], the queen [[per:29714|Luravievshevath]] and [[per:29858|Lavan]], who reigned from 3284 to 3315. But the decades of its greatest size were also the decades of its first real hunger. At [[set:18|Linnshal]] a measure of grain cost 7.7 grams of silver in the 3250s, 14.9 in the 3270s, the decade the Daadüdhih drought began, 16.5 in the 3280s and 18.3 in the 3300s. The famine of 3290 followed. The record's measure of order in the empire fell from fair to poor, and its treasury shrank by more than a quarter between 3280 and 3300. Then the province of Evun broke away under the claimant Vievnan, first in the [[evt:30409|Secession of Evun]] of 3304 and again in the [[evt:30519|Secession of Evun]] of 3312. The [[evt:30623|Collapse of the Lienonoshal State]] came in 3320. The record lists four causes: both secessions, a lost battle at Taalinnuy in 3286, and the famine of 3290. For a historian who reads prices, that list is more satisfying than most.

The heirs are a lesson in how names survive. One heir, [[pol:1323|Lienunushal]], kept Linnshal as its capital. Its name is the old one, worn down by four centuries of speech. A kingdom of [[pol:1324|Yidha]] took [[set:0|Taulin]], and its name is almost certainly Yeda's in a later sound. A third heir, [[pol:1328|Pienpedhzhe]], made [[set:164|Pinpidhzhi]] its capital and would last until 3704. Lienunushal was ruled for fifty-five years by the queen [[per:30315|Duedhuvvalath]], called the Pious, and later for half a century by another queen, [[per:31571|Khuetakhshalath]], called the Old.

Lienonoshal was not alone. The [[evt:30075|Collapse of the Miuhitl State]] in 3268 finished what the Treaty of Listlitl and the [[evt:29972|Secession of Shathiy]] of 3254 had begun, and [[per:29291|I Tli]], its last queen, outlived her state by two years. The war that broke Miuhitl survives in two rival chronicles, and they are worth reading side by side. The Tomzel account (PAL·CH·05393), written at Ölye in 3255, claims that at Listlitl in 3240 the queen of Miuhitl brought 330,364 men and lost 116,808. The record gives 125,051 men and 40,551 dead. The Miuhitl account (PAL·CH·05394), written at Kadhdanul in 3272, claims that the next year the enemy came with 234,412 and lost 91,019. The record gives 90,172 and 36,695. Each side nearly trebled the other's army and its losses. The Miuhitl chronicler passes over five of the eleven battles. He ends the war with the words "the border was settled," written four years after the empire he wrote for had broken apart. Within a generation the [[evt:30378|Collapse of the Kambambül State]] in 3300 under [[per:29636|Nadigi]] the Last and the [[evt:30522|Collapse of the Nemeth State]] in 3312 had added two more empires to the list. Nemeth had won the [[evt:30139|Nemeth–Navnga War]] only twenty-five years before. At Ene, the scene of five of that war's battles, both kings set up victory stelae. Navnga's Uludun claimed 38,200 enemy dead for 3276 (PAL·IN·02258) and Nemeth's Meshe 35,200 for 3278 (PAL·IN·02260). The record gives 15,432 and 10,431.

## Tomzel's century

From the ruins of Lienonoshal's world, [[pol:1212|Tomzel]], the heir of Ana that the last chapter watched rise at [[set:264|Ölye]], became the largest state on the map. In 3370 it held 12 per cent of the land, ahead of Iethithi with 11. Under [[per:29941|Yi Mish II]] and his successor it won the [[evt:30642|Second Tomzel–Mii War]] of 3321–3332. The peace was sworn at Ane, Ana's old capital, now a city of the kingdom of [[pol:1211|Mii]]. The stelae of that war contradict each other almost comically. Yeskhi of Mii claimed 183,000 Tomzel dead for his victory at Ienithie in 3322 (PAL·IN·02293), against 24,242 in the record, and he was killed in the next year's battle. Under [[per:30723|Lueshash]] Tomzel won the [[evt:31066|First Tomzel–Yiotl War]] of 3352–3361 and made the kingdom of [[pol:1215|Yiotl]] a vassal, taking Pivithie and Likha in the country of [[reg:5|Yiutl]]. Old battlefields of the Ana wars had become Tomzel towns.

Then the weather turned against it. The great famine of 3378 struck Yiutl, its new province. At Thimisitsiy, the old Yiotl capital, grain cost 28.3 grams in the 3370s against 8.5 in the 3250s. Tomzel kept growing all the same. Under [[per:31217|Inemamshe]], who reigned from 3392 to 3426, and his successors it reached 28.7 million people and 466 districts in 3450. But the record's measure of its internal order fell to about a quarter of its old level by the 3420s, and to nothing by the 3460s. In 3426 a man named Shimave seized the throne. The usurpation is listed among the causes of the empire's end.

The decisive blow came in the [[evt:32112|Second Tomzel–Lishe War]] of 3445–3451. At Ishwyithie in 3448 Tomzel put 211,665 men into the field against 21,513 of [[pol:1342|Lishe]] and lost 92,598 of them. The defenders lost 2,064. King Isuie of Lishe raised a basalt stele near the town (PAL·IN·02418) claiming 661,700 enemy dead. That is seven times the true figure, and it is the largest such claim anywhere in the archive. For once the exaggeration matches the scale of the disaster, if not its numbers. Tomzel won the war anyway and annexed Lishe in 3451. Then came the cold. At Ölye grain had cost 7.2 grams in the 3340s. In the 3450s and 3460s it cost over 13. The Mii famine of 3460 followed, and in 3466 the [[evt:32324|Collapse of the Tomzel State]] split the empire six ways. The record lists three causes: the disaster at Ishwyithie, the usurpation and the famine. One heir kept the name, and a new [[pol:1371|Tomzel]] began at Ölye that would grow larger still. Another, the [[pol:1373|Ishuiithie]] Empire, took as its capital Ishwyithie itself, the town where the old empire had lost 92,598 men eighteen years before.

## Fourteen persecutions

The persecutions that began in the last chapter became routine in this one. The record lists fourteen, almost three times as many as in the previous quarter-millennium. The Veshethmekh establishment was behind eight of them, the Dankhüdhil behind four and the I'ishiu behind two. The Jinru, the old faith of Taulin, were persecuted six times. The thread runs back to the prisoner under the wall in 3008. In 3321, a year after its founding, the kingdom of Yidha at Taulin underwent the [[evt:30752|Conversion of Yidha to Veshethmekh]] under the queen [[per:30279|Kultulorath]], remembered as the Convert. Within seven years it was persecuting the Jinru again, and it did so once more in 3366. Faith shares moved slowly but in one direction. By 3370 [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] held 9 per cent of the land, up from 5 per cent in 3120, while Mie'to'ieshio fell to 21 per cent. The [[evt:30093|Conversion of Munu to Dankhüdhil]] in 3263 was followed within eight years by a persecution of Mie'to'ieshio in Munu. Some conversions echoed older ones. In 3322 the kingdom of [[pol:1314|Khanji]] took up [[mov:61|Langikath]], and in the same year a new kingdom of [[pol:1318|Mane]], one of the heirs of Nemeth, went through the [[evt:30751|Conversion of Mane to I'ishiu]], almost exactly five centuries after an older Mane had taken the same faith in 2823. Names, faiths and quarrels in this world often outlived the states that first carried them.

The one religious voice from inside these years that is not a prayer from a cellar is a defence. In 3304 [[per:30128|Lamfin Mu'a]], a teacher at Los, answered the elders of the Kievi faith at Iethithi before King Ish'o Fay (PAL·SC·00130). He claimed to have changed "nothing of the root" of Veshethmekh, only "cleared away the weeds". The weeds were the idea that judgement was far off, the idea that the rich were damned, and the habit of keeping the word "in their own houses, among their own kin". "I did not choose the name," he wrote. "I chose only the road." The clerks named it [[evt:30485|The I'ashakh Schism]], and the [[mov:116|I'ashakh]] went on as a movement. His plea for toleration — "let those who choose the road walk it, and let those who choose the wheel turn with it" — is the most generous sentence in a harsh century. The record does not say whether he was granted it. New faiths still appeared: [[mov:115|Ndule]], founded by [[per:29897|Nanzhizhim]] at Azhi in 3282, Zhibingümi at the same Azhi in 3369, and Pyishawiwtsi at Itlyitlinuise in 3421. Three schools of philosophy date from the period, the last founded in 3476.

## Queens of a cold age

The sources for the rulers of this age can often be tested against the record, and a surprising number pass. The first is a king's dispatch. In 3358 the secretary of King Nabingu of [[pol:1279|Dagafu]] wrote to the council from the field of Kanindu, in the [[evt:31128|Second Dagafu–Luthe War]] (PAL·LT·01669). He reported a victory and refused to hide its price: "some fifteen thousand men, and the enemy scarcely fewer." The record gives 15,450 and 14,134. He asked that the legions be paid "in Shiègi of full weight, and not in the clipped coin of the frontier markets," and that no arch be raised "until the war is ended and its end is known." Under [[per:30881|Navande]] the war ended in 3370 with land and an indemnity from the kingdom of [[pol:1276|Luthe]].

The queen [[per:31732|Nakange]] of Dagafu came to the throne in 3437 at the age of twelve, with her mother as regent, and reigned for sixty-three years. Her testament, sealed in 3499 (PAL·DE·00930), claims that "on every field where I rode in person, the One gave me the victory. Twice in my youth and twice in my middle years." The record lists exactly four battles under her command: Bige in 3450 and Dadavli in 3451, Shimbayi in 3476 and Lamligul in 3480. She won all four. She is the rare ruler whose boast survives an audit. Her captains were less lucky. At Aguga in 3474 they lost 41,828 men, and the Kabav king's stele (PAL·IN·02437) inflates that to 173,200. Still, the [[evt:32400|Dagafu–Kabav War]] ended in 3480 with thirty-four districts and an indemnity for Dagafu, which grew from 187 districts in 3440 to 281 in 3500. Her last acts were to free every prisoner held only for debt and to ask for burial "without gold or show, in a plain robe."

It was an age of long reigns by women. In Pienpedhzhe [[per:31084|Vanath]], called the Old, ruled from 3380 to 3426, and [[per:32148|Khuevtakhshalath]], also called the Old, from 3478. In Kambambül the queen [[per:29504|Nakagum]] reigned from 3250 to 3281, nineteen years before its collapse, and in Iethithi [[per:31020|Ozhon]] reigned from 3401 to 3435.

Nakange's contemporary [[per:31931|Lamfin Li]], empress of [[pol:1177|Iethithi]] from 3456 to 3502, had a court poet less careful with figures. The *Panegyric on the Empress Lamfin Li, Called the Victorious* (PAL·PO·00037), sung in 3483, admits the disaster at Nidhinge in the [[evt:32399|Iethithi–Zhidhzhinga War]], where a trusted captain "went proudly where he should have gone humbly." The record says what that cost: 57,484 of 273,018 men lost, against a city-state of [[pol:1269|Zhidhzhinga]] that lost 1,968. The poem is right that she then took command herself and won at Taalinnuy, Nidhinge and Diviminnge. Her own stele for Nidhinge claims 57,400 enemy dead; the record has 11,976. In the poem a measure of grain sells for "less than one isa". Iethithi was larger than ever, with 25.8 million people and 452 districts in 3490, but the record's measure of its internal order fell to nothing within a few years of her death.

The last war of the period was fought between two heirs of Lienonoshal. The [[evt:32507|Lienunushal–Yidha War]] of 3483–3497 began after [[per:32119|Tafutuevan]] seized power in Lienunushal in 3473. It ended with the Treaty of Himi, by which [[per:32233|Baldun II]] of Yidha gave up Himi and twelve districts. *The Planctus for the Fallen of Diebeguvebenga* (PAL·PO·00013), written for the victors in 3504, puts their dead at "a hundred and seventy thousand and more". The record gives 175,088, and 171,789 for Yidha. The war cost the winners as much as the losers.

Under all this, trade went on. Merchants founded 210 houses in the period, and rulers introduced 52 coinages and weight standards. Paper was invented a second time, independently, in Puvu in 3367. Ledgers survive in greater numbers: forty, against nineteen in the last chapter. Only one coin was debased. The Nivba of Navnga lost about a sixth of its silver in 3469, falling from 6.8 to 5.617 grams, and the record ties the cut to two sacks of the city of Navnge, in 3449 and 3451. Five new languages were recognized: Yinsam, Kutyiyi and Khubuge by 3299, and Tyimbu and Rudun by 3399. In Tasemem the record follows vowels rising around 3421 and falling around 3463.

In 3500 Nakange died, and her son came to the throne of a Dagafu still growing. Iethithi was near its largest and close to breaking, Yidha's king would be remembered as the Last, and the cold had not lifted. The next chapter begins in that colder world.
