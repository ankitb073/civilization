[[per:19476|Nakhijvo]], king of [[pol:854|Adhgomgi]], first servant of the altars of his fathers, to the priests of the old gods who keep the hearths in the city and in the hill shrines: greeting, and the peace that is owed between those who have served the same fire.

You will have heard it from the market before you read it from my hand, and that is as it should be, for the market decided it before I did. Nevertheless, a thing so great ought to be said plainly by the one who must answer for it. Therefore hear it from me.

From this year the kingdom gives its public worship to the [[mov:61|Langikaf]]. The sacrifices of the state, the oaths of the courts, the blessings of the harvest and the rites of the gate will be performed according to its teaching, and not according to the order of the [[mov:4|Khopande]], which you and I have served since we were boys.

I know what you will say, for I have said it myself in the council, and more than once. You will say that the gods of our fathers gave this land its walls, its rivers and its kings; that their priesthood has kept the truth in order, hand to hand, since before there was a city here; that a people which forsakes its altars forsakes its own name. These are not small arguments. I have honoured them for fifty years and more, and I do not despise them now.

But consider, fathers, what a king is for. He is not the owner of the people's conscience; he is its steward. When I came to this seat as a young man, the new teaching was a rumour among the boatmen. When my hair began to whiten, it was the speech of the artisans and the scribes. Now it is the faith of the greater part of the households of [[set:49|Adhgomgi]], of the officers, of the soldiers in the forts, of the very servants who sweep your courts. I have had it counted, street by street, and the count is not doubtful. A king who sets the worship of the state against the worship of the many does not preserve religion; he makes it a garrison in a hostile country.

Nor is the new teaching so hostile to you as the fearful say. Its teachers hold that there is one divine power, and that the old gods are its servants and its shadows. Very well: then the gods you serve are not cast out, but given a master. Their images may stand. Their festivals may be kept by those who love them. What changes is the order of honour, not the fact of it. And if its teachers say that each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason, you who have reasoned all your lives need not fear the light.

I will add one thing more, as an old man to old men. The Langikaf teaches that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. If that teaching were practised in this palace as diligently as it is preached in the streets, I should have fewer complaints to answer. Let us see whether its followers keep it better than we kept ours.

Now hear my provisions, which are just. No shrine of the old gods shall be pulled down by order of the crown. No priest shall be driven from his house. The stipends now paid to the hearths from the treasury shall continue for the lives of those who now receive them, in good [[cur:451|Shonjif]] of full weight, and shall not be diminished. Those of you who wish to learn the new teaching will be received by its teachers with honour, for they seek converts and will be glad of learned ones. Those who do not wish it will be left in peace.

Let it be written in the annals that the [[evt:20276|Conversion of Adhgomgi to Langikath]] was made by the voice of the people, received by the king, and accomplished without the burning of a single altar.

Given at the palace, in the year 134 of the Langikaf, in the fifty-second year of my reign. Farewell, and do not think too hardly of your old companion.
