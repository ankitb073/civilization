*In the fourth year of [[per:13796|Nakagum]], king of [[pol:506|Puvu]]. The tablets of the keeper of the crossroads well.*

**New moon, month of sowing.** The water stands at the ninth knot of the rope. Good water. Sweet water. I poured the first jar on the ground for [[dei:51|Talu]], earth, who holds the water in her belly.

**Third day.** Ninth knot. A salt caravan. Forty jars drawn for men and beasts. The salt-men paid a measure of barley for all. Barley is one [[cur:269|Mbumbi]] the measure in the market, and a little more.

**Fifth day.** Rain in the night. [[dei:48|Kudze]], sky, opened his hand a little. The ninth knot and a hand.

**Sixth day.** On the stone of the well-head are the marks. My father cut marks. I cut marks. A mark is cut only in a bad year, at the lowest water. I counted them today, my grandson asking. Twenty-three marks, one under another, going down. They are the years of [[evt:13311|the Great Drought in Daadüdhih]].

I was a boy when the first mark was cut. I was a man with sons when the last was cut. In those years the water went down past the twentieth knot, past the twenty-fifth. My father went down the shaft on the rope with a basket and scraped mud, and we strained the mud through cloth for the water in it. Men came up the road out of Baan, from the [[evt:13312|Famine in Baan]], with children on their backs. We gave them a cup each. Only a cup. The headman stood at the well with a stick.

In the last years came word of the [[evt:13435|Famine in Anel]]. Then the rains came back. No mark has been cut since. Twenty-two years, no mark.

**Ninth day.** Ninth knot. My grandson asks why I write the knot every day when it is always the ninth. I say: so that in a bad year we will know the day it changed.

**Eleventh day.** Soldiers of the king came down the road, many. They drew two hundred jars and paid nothing. One knocked a stone from the well-head. I said nothing. They are going to the border. Men say the men of Khishkhizi have come over the border into Daadüdhih with spears. This is the [[evt:13803|First Khishkhizi–Puvu War]]. I am too old to know why.

**Twelfth day.** Eighth knot and a hand. Two hundred jars is a great deal of water.

**Fourteenth day.** More soldiers. Their captain said the king beat Khanji at Adhgumgi three years ago, at the [[evt:13781|Battle of Adhgumgi]], and that he will beat these as well. He said it loudly, for his men. He drank from my bucket with both hands. I gave him the bucket. What else can a well-keeper give?

**Sixteenth day.** Ninth knot again. The rain has fed it. I gave thanks to Talu and to Kudze. I gave a little barley-cake to [[dei:49|Tuve]], down below, who is near to every well.

**Eighteenth day.** My father said: if enemies come to the crossroads, throw the rope down the shaft and the bucket after it, and cover the mouth with brush, and go up into the hills. A well-keeper who gives water to enemies is a fool; a well-keeper who poisons his own well is worse. I have put the brush ready by the wall. My grandson asked what it was for. I said, for the fire.

**Twentieth day.** Ninth knot. I cut no mark. May I cut no mark.
