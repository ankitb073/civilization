**[[per:44845|NANGZHINANGE]]**, by the favour of the One Power King of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]], Lord of the Ceded Provinces, to all who shall see or hear these Letters, greeting.

**WHEREAS** in the years 4627 and 4628 of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] it pleased the One Power to give Our arms the victory at [[evt:45509|Dadunde]] and again at [[evt:45515|Dadifi]], and by the [[evt:45516|Treaty of Dadifi]] the Realm was enlarged by four provinces and Our treasury restored by the indemnity then paid; and

**WHEREAS**, the said treasury being so restored, We were moved in the year 4631 to send ships across the sea to the coast of [[reg:30|Shigimu]], there to plant a settlement of Our subjects, which was done, and is called the [[evt:45540|Founding of the Colony of Khambave]]; and

**WHEREAS** the said settlement, laid out in the same year and by the [[evt:45541|Founding of Khambave]] given the form of a town, has now stood two years, and its people have petitioned Us that its laws be set down in writing, lest the distance of the sea make every governor a king;

**NOW KNOW YE** that We, of Our certain knowledge and mere motion, do grant, ordain and establish as follows.

**I. Of the Land.** The town of [[set:325|Khambave]], with all lands within one day's march of its walls not lawfully held by others, is annexed to the Crown of Mbilu for ever, and shall be governed under these Letters and no others.

**II. Of the Governor.** There shall be a Governor, named by Us and removable at Our pleasure, who shall hold office for five years and no longer without renewal. He shall be assisted by a Council of seven, of whom four shall be chosen each year by the householders of Khambave, and three named by the Governor. No tax shall be laid upon the colony without the assent of the Council.

**III. Of Justice.** Every subject in Khambave shall have the same law as a subject in [[set:136|Mbilu]], and no man shall be imprisoned more than three days without being brought before a magistrate. Appeal shall lie from the Governor's court to Our own.

**IV. Of Dealings with the People of the Country.** Since the One Power asks justice in dealings and moderation in all things, no land shall be taken from the people of Shigimu except by purchase fairly made and recorded before the Council; and any subject who cheats them in weight or measure shall pay double, half to the injured party and half to the town.

**V. Of Conscience.** Since each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason, no inhabitant of Khambave, of whatever nation, shall be compelled in matters of belief, provided he keeps the peace and pays his dues. Yet the public worship of the town shall be that of the Realm, and the Governor shall maintain it.

**VI. Of Trade and Coin.** All accounts, rents and dues in the colony shall be reckoned in the [[cur:926|dathu]] of Mbilu, of the standard weight. Ships of the Realm shall enter and leave without hindrance. A twentieth part of all goods shipped from Khambave to Mbilu shall be paid to Our treasury, and no more, for the first ten years; We judge that a young tree must be watered before it is cut.

**VII. Of Defence.** The householders shall keep arms, and shall muster four times a year. The Governor may raise a wall and a battery at the harbour, at the charge of the Crown.

**VIII. Of the Final Judgement.** Every officer under these Letters shall swear, before taking office, that he will deal justly, as one who knows that time runs in a line to a final judgement, where his accounts will be read aloud. For as prosperity is a sign of divine favour, so is a well-governed colony a sign that the favour has not been squandered.

**IN WITNESS WHEREOF** We have caused these Letters to be made patent and Our great seal to be affixed. Given at Our palace in Mbilu, the 25th day of Vande, in the year 4633 of the Uthungala, and of Our reign the twelfth.
