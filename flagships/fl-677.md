## A name in other men's books

The record keeps almost none of what a biographer wants first about [[per:42600|Aüyuf]], general of [[pol:1772|Tushi]]. It gives his birth in AY 4436 and his natural death in AY 4515, at seventy-nine. It names his people, the Nishmam, and the meaning of his name, *spear fire*. It knows no parent, wife or child, and nothing of how he came to command. What it keeps is sixteen battles over forty-one years. Only one commander in the whole record, Natsunga the Unfortunate, is listed at more.

His own country hardly remembered him. The Annals of Tushi for AY 4400–4499 (PAL·AN·00802), kept at [[set:27|Tuzhi]] by the scribe Ishil Mish, mention three battles in a hundred years: "There was a battle at Miishim; the enemy fled"; "The host fought at Shathi; the army of the queen prevailed"; "There was a battle at Ash; the gods gave the victory." All three were Aüyuf's victories, and he is named in none of them. The fullest description of him that survives was written by an enemy king.

## Three towns, won twice

Aüyuf's first recorded command came at twenty-six, in the [[evt:43055|Tushi–Yiixi War]], which Queen Lamfin Maf I opened in AY 4461. The enemy was already failing. Wemu had sacked the [[pol:1777|Yiixi]] capital in AY 4454, the sack the record names as the war's cause, and Yiixi's surveys of the 4450s and 4460s put its stability at zero. At the [[evt:43067|Battle of Myixim]] in AY 4462 Aüyuf brought 22,985 men against 9,352 under [[per:42526|Yeiümi]], and won with losses of 340 to 570, a narrow margin for such odds. He beat Yeiümi again at Yimidiyi in AY 4467 and at Xath in AY 4468. Then Yiixi collapsed in AY 4470, the war ended without a settlement, and the territory log gives all three towns to a successor kingdom, Nisxuti. His victories had won nothing.

Six years later he went back for them. In the [[evt:43215|13th Yimla Trade War]], begun in AY 4476 under Queen [[per:42627|Lamfin Maf II]], he beat [[pol:1830|Nisxuti]]'s general [[per:42770|Lamfin Mua]] at Xath in AY 4477, at the [[evt:43248|Battle of Xath]] in AY 4479, with 35,906 men against 6,355, and at Yimidiyi in AY 4480. The Treaty of Yimidiyi (PAL·TR·02692) then gave Tushi "the towns of Shathi, Yimitiyi and Miishim (9 districts)", the three places where he had fought since AY 4462.

Khiasi's Tushi account of the war (PAL·CH·07615, AY 4481) is the only surviving document from his own country that names him. Twice Aüyuf "brought up the guns at the decisive moment"; once he "held the heights against them". Khiasi's arithmetic is regular enough to undo. In every entry his own dead are a little over half the record's figure, and the enemy's dead a little over double. Nisxuti's muster roll for Xath in AY 4479 (PAL·MU·01358) totals 6,355 men where Khiasi has 9,203. Among them were gunners and pikemen from Xathi, Yimitiyi and Miixim, the very towns in dispute: "Each man to bring bread for ten days." The Nisxuti chronicler Nisliluexa (PAL·CH·07616, AY 4484) passes over both battles at Xath and never names Aüyuf. He blames Yimidiyi on "the failure of the supply trains" and claims 450 of his men lost against 1,038 of the enemy; the record has 1,435 and 338.

In Yimla Aüyuf always had the larger army, by one and a half to more than five to one. Those victories show a competent general beating weak enemies. Ash shows more.

## Nine times at Ash

In AY 4491 [[pol:1769|Wemu]] crossed the frontier. By the end of the 4480s Wemu counted some thirteen million people and 155,963 soldiers; Tushi had 5.3 million and 60,472. Wemu wanted the province of Iith, and with it Ash, which Tushi had held since AY 4383. Aüyuf, now fifty-five, commanded in every battle of the [[evt:43383|First Wemu–Tushi War]]: nine at Ash and one at Bami. No other commander in the record fought so often at one place. He was outnumbered in all ten battles, at odds of between two and a third and four and a half to one.

At the [[evt:43387|Battle of Ash]] in AY 4492, 81,622 Wemu troops under [[per:42903|Yeüji]] attacked his 33,700. Wemu lost 27,296 men, a third of the army it brought, Yeüji among them at twenty-nine. Tushi lost 4,686. King Nilu Mix wrote to his council from the camp (PAL·LT·01672). In his letter Aüyuf is "an old soldier who has grown grey in the business of avoiding a fair fight", dug in "behind earthworks, marsh on one flank and the river on the other". The record says nothing about the ground, and the king's "some ten thousand men" of enemy loss is more than twice its figure. Still, he admitted "Our loss is heavy".

Wemu kept coming, sending seven different generals against him in ten battles. Tushi had three rulers in the same war: King Yeümaf died in AY 4495, Naual seized the throne that year, and Yei Ta seized it in AY 4499. Aüyuf kept his command through both usurpations; the record does not say how. At Ash in AY 4494 he lost so narrowly that Wemu lost more men than he did, 1,733 to 1,285. In AY 4496 he was badly beaten, losing 9,455 men to Wemu's 1,914. At the [[evt:43413|Battle of Bami]] in AY 4497 [[per:42934|Wemaflu]] fell, and Wemu lost 12,789 men to his 2,125. After another defeat came the [[evt:43426|Battle of Ash]] of AY 4499, where Aüyuf, at sixty-three, faced [[per:42959|Yeüth]]'s 66,164 men with 14,711. Wemu lost 17,213; Tushi lost 2,482. Four defeats followed, none as costly as AY 4496. The last was against [[per:43037|Liawi]], born fourteen years after Aüyuf's first battle. Ash never fell to assault. Over the whole war Wemu lost 66,981 men and Tushi 33,772.

Wemu's chronicler Nislimin wrote in AY 4540, thirty-seven years after the war (PAL·CH·07639). He too blames the first Ash on "the failure of the supply trains", and he makes it an even exchange: "It cost us 16,132 men, and them 16,581." Twice he has Wemu "outnumbered three to one". One of those times is Bami, where Wemu's own muster roll (PAL·MU·01370) lists 52,735 men against the record's 21,587 for Aüyuf. Added up, Nislimin's Tushi dead come to nearly 120,000, about twice the whole army Tushi kept.

## What the peace cost

The Treaty of Ash was signed in AY 4503 by Yei Ta and Milak the Devout, who had succeeded Nilu Mix the year before. Tushi gave up nine districts, Ash and Veümin among them, paid 55,369 kilograms of silver, granted Wemu trading terms and accepted a twenty-year truce. Nislimin made the nine districts nineteen, as Khiasi had with the earlier peace. The next Tushi annals (PAL·AN·00803) begin: "Peace was sworn: the Treaty of Ash." By the end of the 4500s Tushi's treasury had shrunk by more than a third, and its stability, 0.57 in the survey of the 4480s, stood at 0.09.

Ordinary people paid for his victories as well. In AY 4490 the court at Yimidiyi heard Mal, son of Shiskha, "formerly a subject of Nisshuti", ask for his father's thirty acres, taken when Yimla passed to Tushi. Counsel for the Crown answered: "No private claim survives the treaty." Mal received 82.5 [[cur:975|nini]], about 1.2 kilograms of silver (PAL·LS·00724). The scribe was Ishil Mish, the man who later kept the annals that never name Aüyuf. Account books at Myixim in AY 4479 and Tuzhi in AY 4494 note in the margin that the roads were unsafe because of the war.

Aüyuf died in AY 4515, the year Zhigu rose in revolt and began the [[evt:43612|Fourth Zhigu War of Independence]]. Other generals put it down. The record gives only the year, so we cannot know whether he heard of it.

His court gave his victories to the queen and the gods; his enemies called him a coward and multiplied his dead. The record is plainer. When he had the numbers he won, which proves little. When he did not, he faced a kingdom more than twice the size of his own for twelve years. He cost it nearly two men for every one he lost, and he never gave up Ash on the field; the peace did that. Nearly every general in these chronicles is victorious. Aüyuf is rarer: a general whose best work was a defeat slow enough for his country to survive it.
