title: Memoir of Lili, Late President of Iith, on the Revolution of 2273

**To the reader.** I write these pages at my house outside [[set:131|Shiüzhi]], on 25 Ash 2279 of the Kievi, some weeks after I laid down the seal of the republic and placed it in the hands of my successor, [[per:44439|Izhil Ath]], whom the citizens chose and whom I congratulate. Since then a great many pamphlets have been printed about me. Some say I was a usurper, some that I was a coward, and one, which I keep on my desk for the pleasure of it, that I was both at once. I do not propose to answer them line by line. I propose to set down, with dates, what was done and why, and let the reader weigh it by his own light, as our faith teaches every seeker to do.

**I. Of the causes.** Every child in Shiüzhi knows the numbers eight and nine. In 2225 of the Kievi the city was taken and plundered for the eighth time in its long history; that was [[evt:44583|the eighth sack]]. In 2226 it was taken again, [[evt:44596|the ninth]]. And in that same year the crown of [[pol:1773|Iith]] put its seal to [[evt:44597|the treaty of Shiüzhi]], by which it gave up a city and a province, bent the knee as another's vassal, and called it peace. I was a boy of thirteen that year. I remember the bells being rung for the treaty, and I remember my father weeping to hear them.

I do not say that a single treaty made a revolution half a century later. I say that a dynasty which could twice lose its capital, and then sign away its honour to keep its crown, had taught us what it valued. The doctrine of [[mov:69|Kievi]] holds that each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason. If that is so, I asked, and I asked it in print long before I asked it in the street, by what right does one family claim to see for all of us?

**II. Of the events of 2273.** Of [[evt:45019|the revolution itself]] I will say less than my enemies would like. The guilds and the assembly sent their petition to the palace; the petition was refused; the assembly declared the throne vacant; and I, who had drafted the petition, was asked to carry the declaration to [[per:44136|Lamfin Lizil]]. I bear her no hatred. She had reigned five years and was not the worst of her line. I gave orders that no hand be raised against her person, and so far as I know none was. She lives yet, and if she reads this, I wish her a quiet old age.

**III. Of the war.** It is said that the revolution invited [[evt:45022|the war with the Bathxi]]. I answer that the Bathxi needed no invitation. They needed only to see a weak army, and the army I inherited from the crown was weak, unpaid and badly led. At [[evt:45039|Khasnin]] in 2275 our men left the field, and at [[evt:45044|Khasnin]] again in 2276 they left it again; and on both days, I will not hide it, we lost more than twice as many men as the Bathxi. In 2277 they came to [[evt:45049|the walls of the capital]] itself, and there, I confess, we lost near four men to their one. [[evt:45050|The eighth siege of Shiüzhi]] followed.

I signed [[evt:45051|the peace of Shiüzhi]]. I do not hide my name on it. We gave up three frontier districts and opened our markets to their merchants on their terms, and we bought twenty years of truce. In exchange, Shiüzhi was not sacked a tenth time. I would sign it again tomorrow. The men who now call it shameful were, for the most part, safely inside the walls I was defending.

**IV. Of laying down the office.** Bread is dear, nearly two [[cur:1046|il]] the measure, and I know that the people blame me for it. Perhaps they are right; a president who could fix the price of bread by decree would be a king, and we had done with kings. When my term ended, as the constitution says it must, I gave up the seal. Our faith asks for justice in dealings and moderation in all things. Moderation in power is the hardest, and therefore the most necessary.

I have written this as [[per:44373|Lili]], a private citizen. The reader may judge it by his own light.
