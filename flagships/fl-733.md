*Before the elders at the gate of the house of [[dei:62|Novigu]], in the 19th year of [[per:4120|Lulutvivan]], lord of [[pol:113|Mikhushitsu]]. The servant of the house of the oil-seller of the lower street speaks. The scribe writes.*

I swear by Novigu of wisdom, I swear by [[dei:66|Shyidartsi]] of the house below: what I say is true. If I lie, let Shyidartsi take my name.

The men of our city went out to the river to the [[evt:3902|battle of Lininadhe]]. My master's son went out. The sons of the street went out. They were few. The men of [[pol:40|Navunga]] were many, many like the reeds, many like the stars of [[dei:65|Tono]]'s night. Few came back. My master's son did not come back.

On the day after, the men of Navunga came to the walls. They came with ladders. They came with fire. The gate of the river broke. The gate of the river broke, and they came in.

My master said: go down. Go down with the oil, hide among the jars. I went down. I went down to the cellar with the little daughter of the house. My master did not come down. My master stood at the door. My master's wife stood at the door.

Three days I was in the cellar. Three days the girl was with me. We drank oil from the small jar, we ate the dried figs. Above us feet, many feet. Above us the voices of Navunga, loud, laughing. Above us the sound of jars breaking, the good jars, the jars of the year's pressing. The oil ran down between the boards. It ran on my head. The girl wept, and I put my hand on her mouth. I put my hand on her mouth, and I said in her ear: Novigu sees. Be silent.

On the third day it was quiet. I went up. The door was open. My master was at the door. My master's wife was at the door. I covered them with the red cloth of the shop. I did not look again.

This is what was taken from the house, as the elders ask:  
Of oil, forty jars of the year's pressing, taken or broken.  
Of silver, the box of the counting-table, eleven [[cur:70|Rvidin]] and pieces, taken.  
Of bronze, the lamp of the house-god and two bowls, taken.  
Of cloth, the bolts of the shop, taken, except the red cloth.  
Of the household: the master, dead; the master's wife, dead; the son, dead at the river.  
The little daughter of the house lives. She is with me. She does not speak yet.

This is what remains to the house: the cellar, nine jars of oil sealed under the floor, the press, the girl.

The elders say: the city must pay silver to Navunga every year, as was sworn at the [[evt:3904|Treaty of Lininadhe]]. Every house must give its part. I say: the house of the oil-seller has nine jars and a girl. Let the elders take the jars if they must. Let them not take the girl. I will keep her. I will press the oil.

The elders say: the lord Lulutvivan lives, the city lives, [[set:47|Lininadä]] stands. I say: it stands. I went through the lower street. Half the doors are burned. The well is full of ash. The dogs eat in the street. Barley is three quarters of a Rvidin the measure, as before; but who has the Rvidin?

The [[evt:3903|Second Sack of Lininadhe]], the scribes call this. Second. The old songs tell of the first. They say the city stood after the first. I say: let it stand.

I have spoken. Novigu heard. Let the scribe set the seal.
