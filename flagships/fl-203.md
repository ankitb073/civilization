The [[evt:1416|Second Virnado–Talenonoy War]] (AY 455–458) is short, one-sided and well attested, which makes it a useful test of method. Both palace accounts are clay-tablet copies recovered by Nambili's campaigns. The Virnado account (PAL·CH·00165, Radape) was excavated at Virnadhu in AY 4931. The Talenonoy account (PAL·CH·00166, Nunmula) came from Taalinnuy in AY 4908. Both were written within four years of the peace: Matatren's in AY 459, Nyebin's in AY 462. Distance in time excuses neither. My argument is that the two scribes distorted different things for one reason. Each court had something of its own to explain away, and only one of them had actually lost the war.

## Field by field

*Who began it.* The record names [[pol:22|Virnado]], which went to war to exact tribute. Nyebin says so plainly: "Nginulin of Birnado, forgetting the oaths his fathers swore, set his hand against Talenonoy." Matatren reverses it with the stock formula of victors' annals: "Duvemitun of Talenonoy broke the peace and sent raiders across the border of Virnatho. So the king went out to punish him."

*The battles.* The record has three engagements in [[reg:7|Tunnge]], all won by Virnado: the [[evt:1431|Battle of Jerathnu (AY 456)]], the [[evt:1439|Fourth Battle of Tunnge]] in AY 457 and the [[evt:1449|Battle of Nuwkennga (AY 458)]]. Matatren has all three, with the right years and commanders. Nyebin has two. Jerathnu is simply absent, and the two defeats he admits come with excuses: treachery at Tunnge, the elements at Nuwkennga.

*The dead.* The record gives Virnado 465 dead (106, 156 and 203) and the [[pol:54|Talenonoy]] forces 2,460 (803, 460 and 1,197). Matatren gives the enemy 1,793, 1,027 and 2,673 dead, 5,493 in all, each roughly two and a quarter times the record's figure, and he admits only 45, 66 and 86 of Virnado's own: 197 dead, where the record has 465. The enemy musters are also scaled: 6,359 men at Jerathnu, 9,114 at Tunnge and 10,897 at Nuwkennga, against the record's 3,410, 4,887 and 5,843. Nyebin gives Virnado 299 and 389 dead in the two battles he admits, roughly 1.9 times the record's figures, and his own side 252 and 656. Ironically, the defeated scribe's total for Virnado's dead, 688, lies closer to the record's 465 than the victor's own figure does. He got there by inflation, not accuracy.

(The record's own proportions need no special pleading: Virnado was outnumbered in every battle and still lost one man for every five of Talenonoy's.)

*Commanders.* No commander was killed. Neither tablet says otherwise.

*Outcome and land.* Virnado won. The [[evt:1450|Treaty of Nuwkennga (AY 458)]] ceded nineteen districts of Tunnge and imposed 1,004 kg of silver a year for 26 years, with a ten-year truce. Matatren has Talenonoy coming "to beg for peace", and he counts forty-five districts won, presented as land "returned". Nyebin calls it a stalemate, ended by the king's grace: "The border was settled." He admits no loss of land, and no tribute at all.

## What each court needed

Matatren wrote in the fourth year of Takaelit, the commander who had become queen in the middle of the war. Her victories were the reign's credentials, so the cost had to be small and the prize large. A war launched for silver had to look like the punishment of a raider.

Nyebin's problem was worse. He wrote for Duemitun, king of the realm we know as the Talenonoy Empire (it took the imperial style only in AY 535), and that future empire was now paying yearly tribute to a neighbouring kingdom. Denying the whole war was impossible within living memory. But Duemitun had commanded at Jerathnu and Tunnge himself, so the first defeat was forgotten and the second became treachery. A dictated treaty became a royal concession. His one concession, that Virnado began the war, cost him nothing: a king who is attacked loses no face by saying so.

An archaeologist's caveat: both tablets are copies, and a copyist may have touched the figures. But each text follows one consistent rule, which points to its author rather than to accidents of transmission.
