*The speech of the President, [[per:45105|Ithdun]], to the people assembled in the great square of [[set:100|Khuele]], on the 4th of Duemi in the year 2570 of the Gigishyu, as taken down by the clerks of the Assembly and printed by its order.*

Citizens of [[pol:1850|Bene]]!

A year ago many of you stood in this square, in this same cold, and some of you, I see, are wearing the same coats. You asked me then what a republic would give you. I said I could not promise you bread; I could promise you only that no one would again spend your sons and your provinces without asking you. I have come to give you an account of that promise, and I ask you to hear it with patience, as reasonable men and women hear an argument, and not as a crowd hears a drum.

First let us be honest about what we overthrew, for there are already pamphleteers in the coffee-houses who weep for the crown and say that the old order was a golden one. Was it? Let us take the record year by year, as a clerk takes an inventory.

In the year 2534 of the Gigishyu the royal army, ill-led and ill-paid, met the army of Wemu at [[evt:45445|the sixth battle of Linririnu]], and it was broken, and its general was left dead upon the field. Who answered to you for that? No one.

In the year 2544, by [[evt:45543|the treaty signed at Lininadhe]], the crown gave away eleven districts and the town of Lininadhe itself to the Kingdom of Ane, and the hand that signed it was a usurper's hand. Who asked the people of those districts whether they wished to be sold? No one.

In the year 2555 the province of Muethka declared itself a kingdom. Why? Because it had been taxed and ignored and taxed again. [[evt:45672|The secession of Muethka]] was not a rebellion against Bene; it was a verdict upon the court. And what was the court's answer? War. At [[evt:45695|Nueddansho]] in 2559 and at [[evt:45724|Nueddansho]] in 2563, Bene fought Bene. Muethka was brought back in [[evt:45725|the suppression of the revolt]], and an amnesty proclaimed, and the court called it a triumph. I call it a family burying its own sons and calling the funeral a feast.

Then the crown passed to a child. I wish to say plainly, because lies are spoken about it: no hand has been raised against [[per:45098|Magunkaleth]]. She was eleven years old when they set the crown upon her head, and she was not the author of our misfortunes. We did not make [[evt:45773|the revolution of 2569]] against a girl. We made it against a system that could put a girl on the throne and govern in her name without asking any of you anything.

Now to the account. What has the republic done in a year?

The Assembly sits, and it is chosen by you. Its debates are printed and sold for a few coppers, so that any citizen may read what was said in his name. No tax has been laid without a vote. The royal granaries have been opened and their contents counted, and I tell you frankly that they were emptier than we were told.

And bread. I will not insult you. The measure of grain stands near five [[cur:1000|khürew]] in the market of Khuele, and that is too much for a labouring family. The republic did not make that price; the old debts and the old wars made it. But the republic must unmake it, and I will be judged by whether it does.

You know the teaching of [[mov:57|Gigishyu]]: that the good life is the life of compassion, and that all things turn upon the wheel. The wheel has turned once in this square. Some of the gentlemen in the coffee-houses hope it will turn back. It will not, citizens, if you do not let it. Compassion is not weakness; it is the refusal to let any one man spend the lives of the many as if they were his own coin.

Go home. Keep warm. Read what your Assembly says. And when it errs, come back to this square and tell it so. That is what a republic is.

Long live [[pol:1850|Bene]], and long live its people!
