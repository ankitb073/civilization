*To the most serene and most mighty lord [[per:29633|Ish'o Fay]], king of [[pol:1177|Iethithi]], defender of the faithful, and to the reverend elders of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] who sit in judgement at [[set:104|Iethithi]]: the humble defence of [[per:30128|Lamfin Mu'a]], teacher at [[set:127|Los]], written in the year 3164 from the founding of Iethithi.*

My lord king, reverend fathers,

It has been told to you that I am a breaker of order, a gatherer of crowds, and the author of a new error. I come before you not as one who disputes with his masters, but as a vassal who brings his account to his lord at the season of reckoning. Hear my account, and then judge.

First, as to my obedience. I hold my house and my field of the king, as my father held them before me. I have paid every tax that the king's stewards demanded of me, in [[cur:675|isa]] of good weight, and I have never cut a coin. When the king's banner went out in the [[evt:30404|holy war against Viraduthi]], the men who sit at my feet went with it, and they go with it still. I have never spoken one word against the king's peace, nor against the [[evt:30314|peace of Nisshudi]] by which his majesty enlarged the realm. Let any man stand up who heard me speak otherwise. As the servant in the old saying returned to his master the full measure and more, so I return to you my whole duty.

Second, as to my teaching. You say it is new. It is not new. It is the teaching of [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]], which came down from the gate of Jenontono a thousand years ago and more, from Tonofmalen the first teacher, who said: *Go out from here, and give water.* I received it from my teachers by the laying on of hands, as they received it from theirs, in unbroken order. I have changed nothing of the root. I have only cleared away the weeds that grew about it.

And what are those weeds? Hear them, and judge whether I did ill to pull them up.

The brethren of Veshethmekh, in our days, have said that the judgement is far off, so far that no man need trouble himself about it. I say: the road runs forward, and it does not run forever. The judgement stands at the end of it, and every day we come nearer. The One who sits there will ask each soul one question only: whom did you release from suffering? Is this an error? Then the first teacher erred, for he said it before me.

They have said, some of them, that wealth is a snare and the rich man is lost. I say: the One gives. When the harvest is good, it is His sign; when the merchant prospers in honest dealing, it is His sign. But a sign is given to be read, and the reading of plenty is this: *give.* The full storehouse is not the reward of mercy. It is the tool of mercy. Is this an error? Then let the king, who is the first of all givers in this realm, tell me so.

They say, as the first teacher said, that the word is for all peoples; but they have kept it in their own houses, among their own kin. I say: carry it out. For the men of Viraduthi also. For the men beyond the sea. There is no people the One did not make, and none He will not judge.

Third, as to the elders of the Kievi. I know that you teach the wheel, and the lamp within. I do not mock your wheel. I say only that I see a road. Your faith asks moderation of all men; then show moderation to me. Let me teach at Los in peace. Let those who choose the road walk it, and let those who choose the wheel turn with it, and let the One judge between us at the end.

This is what men have begun to call [[mov:116|I'ashakh]], and what the clerks of your court have written down as [[evt:30485|a schism]]. I did not choose the name. I chose only the road.

I commend myself, my house and my disciples to the mercy of my lord the king, under whose shadow we live.

Your servant, Lamfin Mu'a.
