title: The Light If: A Street Song of Ihaemiw
---
*As sung at the corners of the bread market of [[set:52|Ihaemiw]], in the year 2672 from the founding of the city.*

Here's the new [[cur:491|If]], hot from the mint,  
bright as a minnow and thin as a hint;  
the king's head on it, handsome and grand,  
and the weight of a feather laid in your hand.  

*Ring it on the counter, hear what it'll say:*  
*"I'm lighter, I'm lighter than yesterday."*  

Eight parts of silver last year's coin knew;  
now count them, neighbour, and see if it's true:  
one part's gone off to the moneyers' tray,  
and seven stay home for the work of the day.  
Sixteen years back they had clipped it before,  
and now they've come back and they've clipped it some more.  

The baker has counted. The baker is wise.  
He weighed the new If with his own two eyes.  
A loaf that was eight-and-a-half to the coin  
is seven and a half now, and burned at the join.  

*Ring it on the counter, hear what it'll say:*  
*"I'm lighter, I'm lighter than yesterday."*  

A measure of barley, last spring at the mill,  
was an If and three quarters, and sold with good will;  
now it's two Ifs, or near it, my dear,  
and the miller says, "Silver is scarce this year."  

Oh, [[dei:84|Lashli]] the moon, she was round in her prime;  
they've cut her like the If, a slice at a time.  
And [[dei:86|Shu]] of the barley looks down at the stall,  
and won't take the new coin for his offering at all.  

Who took the silver? The philosophers say  
that a coin is a promise that's minted to stay;  
a king who clips promises teaches the town  
to clip its own measures, and water them down.  

Who took the silver? Ask Movosh, who won  
at the [[evt:23136|battle of Ashmath]] when our day was done;  
for twenty-three years we sent silver their way,  
as was sworn at the [[evt:23138|Treaty of Izimue]], they say.  

The tribute is paid and the tribute is through,  
so where is the silver? I'm asking you.  
Was it spent on the [[evt:23237|Ihamiw–Kizhyitli War]],  
that came to nothing, and nothing, and nothing more?  

*Ring it on the counter, hear what it'll say:*  
*"I'm lighter, I'm lighter than yesterday."*  

Our [[per:22898|Ikhil Mukha]], long may he reign,  
near thirty years crowned through the sun and the rain;  
he lost us the fields and he lightened the purse;  
the gods keep him, neighbours, it might have been worse.  

The heralds read out the [[evt:23333|Debasement of the If]]  
in words so long that they bored us stiff;  
but the fishwife knew it before they were through:  
"It's the same old coin with less silver in, too."  

So pay the tax-man in the new light If,  
count it out slowly and give it a sniff;  
he'll weigh it and frown, and the tax-man will say:  
"It's lighter, it's lighter than yesterday!"  

The dead in [[dei:87|Hutlhi]]'s house are the luckiest yet:  
they have no bread to buy and they owe no debt.  
But we who are living must haggle and stand  
with the weight of a feather laid in our hand.  

*Ring it on the counter, hear what it'll say:*  
*"I'm lighter, I'm lighter than yesterday."*
