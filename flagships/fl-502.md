*[Obverse. A rough tablet of river clay, poorly fired; the signs are crowded, as if the writer had little clay to spare.]*

To every man who still holds to the house of [[pol:349|Ana]], to every priest who still keeps the lamp, to every woman who still weeps at the name of the city, say: thus says [[per:10891|Ma'sha Ash]], who was queen in Ana, who sat on the seat thirty-six years, who now sits on a stool in a house that is not hers.

With me it is not well. I will not say it is well. Let the scribes who come after hear that it was not well.

Hear what has been done to Ana. Hear it, [[dei:0|Atlia]] of the high sky, who saw it. Hear it, [[dei:2|Muevas'a]] of the black earth, who holds my fathers. Hear it, [[dei:4|Lashla'a]] of the moon, who counted the months while it was done.

The men who ate my bread have broken my house. The men who swore to me by the river have broken the oath of the river. Each has taken a piece, as dogs take pieces of a dead ox.

In [[set:7|Ana]] itself, in my city, in the house of my seat, they have set up [[pol:491|Mii]]. In the other cities they have set up [[pol:492|Tomzel]]. They have set up [[pol:493|Mobosh]]. They have set up [[pol:494|Mi'ozhitl]]. They have set up [[pol:495|To'i'io'a]]. They have set up [[pol:496|Tlayiyan]]. Six seats where there was one seat. Six lords where there was one lady. Six tolls on the road where there was one toll. Is that better? Ask the merchant. Ask the donkey.

Who did this? The gods did not do it alone. Men did it. Men who were my servants, who carried my seal, who weighed the [[cur:188|Mue'a]] in my storehouses and kept back a little each year, and then a little more, until the storehouse was theirs and not mine. When the day came, they did not need spears. They only needed to shut their gates.

And why were their gates strong and mine weak? Hear it. When I was a girl, newly come out from under the hands of my elders, the spears of Ana went out to [[evt:10808|Listlitl]]. They were broken. They went out again to [[evt:10829|Listlitl]]. They were broken again. They went out a third time to [[evt:10846|Listlitl]]. A third time they were broken. Three times the young men of Ana went down into the earth at that one place. After that the villages would not send their sons. After that the lords said, "The queen's spears break; let us keep our own." I was young; I listened to old captains, and the old captains said, "Again." I paid for Listlitl every year that followed.

I gave the gods their due. Hear it, you who say I did not. I gave the bull to Atlia at the new year. I gave the ram to [[dei:5|Niezhashta]] when the spears went out. I poured to [[dei:3|Niele]] when the river rose. Every year, in order, as the priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] teach. Prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour; then why did the gods take the prosperity from me and give it to thieves? I do not know. I am not a priest. I ask Uelti of wisdom, and Uelti does not answer.

*[Reverse.]*

Now hear what I ask.

Do not bow to the six seats in your hearts, even if you bow with your necks. Keep the old names. Keep the old tablets. Keep the count of the years of Ana, so that the scribes do not forget that there was one seat before there were six.

The priests say: all things turn on a great wheel; what ends will begin again. Ana has ended. I have seen it end. Then Ana will begin again. I will not see it. Perhaps your sons will see it. When it begins, let them say: this is the Ana of Ma'sha Ash, who was betrayed, who did not betray.

Do not send me silver; they will take it on the road. Send me a word, by a man who can be trusted, if there is still such a man.

> Pressed by my own hand, in the 36th year of Ma'sha Ash, which is the year of [[evt:11014|the collapse]].
