*[Obverse.]*

Praise to [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the storm, who goes before the host of Egu!  
Praise to [[dei:33|Nabudzin]], the river, who waters the road of the king!  
Praise to Tsevni, the moon, who lights the camp at night!  
Hear, O gods, the song of [[pol:14|Egu]], hear it and be glad.

The king of Egu is [[per:1026|Patun]], the strong bull, the high wall,  
Patun of the long spear, Patun of the bright shield,  
in his fifteenth year he lifted his hand toward [[reg:8|Duvegupuinga]],  
toward the fields of barley, toward the roads of the river.  
He said: "Let the men of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]] know the spear of Egu."  
And Dumnu heard, and the host of Egu went out.

So began the [[evt:794|Egu–Lenunushal War]], the war of ten summers.  
Ten summers the men of Egu went out, ten winters they came home.  
The fathers went out, and after the fathers the sons went out.

**The song of Sheney.**  
At [[set:75|Sheney]] by the river the hosts met, and met, and met again.  
In the first year the dust was thick, and the storm turned away from us, and Egu wept.  
But the hand of Egu did not fail. The hand of Egu went out again.  
[[per:1044|Shababan]], the old lion, the servant of the king,  
he led the host of Egu to Sheney, and the men of Lenunushal fell down.  
At the [[evt:810|Battle of Shiniy]] seven hundreds and more of them fell down,  
and the reeds of the river were red, and the crows were fat.  
In the next year Shababan went out again, and again they fell down.  
At the [[evt:813|Battle of Shiniy]] four hundreds of them fell down.  
Twice the old lion went down to Sheney, twice the old lion came home with spoil.

*[Reverse.]*

**The song of Linnshal.**  
Then Patun himself went up, the strong bull went up to Linnshal,  
to the walled city, to the house of their gods.  
At the [[evt:823|Battle of Linnshal]] he struck, and their host gave way,  
they were near as many as we, and yet they gave way,  
three hundreds of them lay in the dust before the gate.  
Then came the [[evt:824|Second Sack of Linnshal]].  
The gate was opened, the houses were opened, the storehouses were opened,  
the silver came out, the bronze came out, the oxen were driven home to Egu.  
The women of Egu sang on the roofs. The children ran to the river to see.

What is a war? A war is a field of barley. Some years the hail falls.  
Some years the grain stands tall. The farmer does not curse the field.  
So in the later years the hail fell on Egu, and the hand of Egu was tired.  
But the hand of Egu did not fail. The hand of Egu did not fail.

**The song of the oath.**  
In the twenty-fourth year of Patun, the king went up again to Linnshal.  
He stood before the gate with his spearmen, and the lady of Lenunushal came out,  
[[per:1072|Dzivialmagunathu]], who holds the throne of Lenunushal,  
she came out of her gate, she stood before the strong bull of Egu.  
There before the gods the [[evt:844|Treaty of Linnshal]] was sworn,  
Patun swore by Dumnu and by Nabudzin, the lady swore by her gods.  
Let each keep what is his. Let the spears rest for ten years.  
Let the river be a road and not a wall.

Now the men of Egu come home. Now the oxen pull the plough.  
Now the silver of Linnshal is in the house of the gods,  
now the [[cur:14|Niba]] goes from hand to hand in the market of Egu.

Who gave the strength? Dumnu of the storm gave the strength.  
Who gave the bread? [[dei:36|Nidziktti]] of the grain gave it.  
Who gave the king counsel to swear? [[dei:37|Novku]] of wisdom gave it.  
Who keeps our dead? [[dei:38|Shedartsi]] keeps them in the house below.  
For the dead of Sheney, water. For the dead before Linnshal, water and bread.

As the wheel turns, so the war turns to peace and the peace turns to plenty.  
Let the priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]] sing this song in the house of the gods.  
Let them sing it at the new moon, let them sing it at the full moon.  
Long life to Patun, the strong bull! Long life to the king of Egu!

*[Colophon.]* Written in the 24th year of Patun.
