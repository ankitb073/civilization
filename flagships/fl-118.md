*Daybook of the physician of the Lower Ward of [[set:14|Muvush]], in the year 4355 from the founding of the city. The One have mercy on all who are written here.*

**At the end of winter.** A wool-merchant came to me with a sore throat and a tale. He says there is a sickness at [[set:297|Ma'itli]] which the people there call the burning cough: it begins in the throat like a coal, goes down into the breast, and in some it brings up blood by the third day. He laughed when he told it. I gave him honey and vinegar and charged him two [[cur:799|yinu]].

**In the first month of spring.** The same tale from carters on the west road. They say it is at [[set:134|Listlitl]] now, and that the inns there are shut. I have told my apprentice to keep a basin of vinegar by the door and wash his hands after each patient.

**The same month, later.** The first in our ward. A carter's wife, lodged by the tanneries. Fever, cough, a burning she likened to swallowed lye. I bled her a little and gave syrup of poppy. Her husband came in from Listlitl a week ago.

**Three days after.** She is dead. Two children in the next house are coughing. I have sent word to the warden of the ward.

**In the second month of spring.** Now it is everywhere in the Lower Ward. I count in my own book nineteen sick and six dead since the carter's wife. The priests of [[mov:108|Tothiyi]] go from door to door with bread and with the prayer for release from suffering, and I will say for them that they do not stop at the doors of the poor. The bells ring for the dead every hour of the day.

**The same month.** A meeting of the physicians at the guild hall. Some say the sickness rides on bad air from the marshes and bid us burn juniper in the streets. Some say it passes from breath to breath, as I believe. Some say it is a judgement and that physic is useless. I said: if it is a judgement, then let us be judged at work. They did not like it.

**At midsummer.** It is said at the palace that the king, [[per:39280|Tuinatluie]], has ordered alms to be given at the gate, and that he goes to the chapel each morning. I do not know if it is true. The price of bread has risen, for the carters will not come into the city, and the wardens look at every stranger as at a murderer.

**The same.** News comes that the cough is at [[set:52|Izimue]], at [[set:7|Ane]], at [[set:236|Ashmatl]], at [[set:260|Ove]]. A merchant of [[pol:1655|Ölie]] tells me his country has it as badly as ours. So it is not a judgement upon [[pol:1446|Mii]] alone, unless all the world is being judged together. Perhaps it is.

**After midsummer.** My apprentice coughs. I have put him in my own bed and sit by him at night with the lamp. The burning is in his breast. He asks me whether the wheel will turn and bring him back. I told him what the priests say: that all things turn, and that what ends will begin again. I did not tell him what I think, which is that I do not want him to begin again; I want him to stay.

**Four days after.** He lives. The fever broke at dawn. I have never been so glad of anything. Of the others in my book this month, eleven sick, five dead.

**At the turning of the year toward autumn.** It is not over. In the Upper Ward it has only now begun in earnest, and they say at the gates that it is at [[set:78|Biyitl]] and moving still. No one knows how long it will last. Some say until the frosts; some say it will come back each spring. I have stopped guessing.

I write this so that, if I die of it, whoever finds my book will know what we tried: vinegar, honey, poppy, clean hands, and staying with the sick. Most of it did little. The last of it did something. The One who sees the sufferer sees also who stays with him.

Written at [[set:14|Muvush]], in the year 4355 from the founding of the city, in the time of [[evt:39822|the Burning Cough]].
