*[Obverse.]*

To my son, who travels with the donkeys on the road of the river, say:  
thus says [[per:477|Min'a'ash]], your father, the merchant of [[set:13|Tivintusa]].

May [[dei:83|Sada]] of the high sky keep you on the road.  
May [[dei:85|Nil'i]] of the river give you good fords.  
May [[dei:88|Mastu]] the sun dry your bales after the rain.  
May [[dei:86|Shu'i]] of the grain fill your bowl at every inn.

Hear the word of your father. It is a great word. Do not put this tablet in the bottom of the bag. Read it, and read it again.

In this year, in the 8th year of [[per:481|Yucua'agwi]], king of [[pol:13|Tivintusa]], I have set up a house. Not a house of mud and reeds, my son, though I have built that too, by the gate of the potters. I have set up a house of name. From this day we are the House of [[hou:5|Nadi]]. The priests have poured the oil. The elders of the market have witnessed it. The scribe has written it. That is the [[evt:388|Founding of the House of Nadi]], and you are its first son.

Hear why I did it.

When I was a boy, my father carried oil on his back. One jar on his back, one jar in his hand. He sold it at the gate for a handful of grain. When he died, he left me the jar and the road. Nothing more.

I took the jar and the road. I went down the river with oil. I came back up the river with wool. I went down again with woollen cloth, and I came back with spices from the far markets, a little in a pouch, worth more than the donkey that carried it. Twenty years I went up and down. Twenty years my feet were sore. Now there are ten donkeys in my yard, and four men who carry for me, and a storehouse with a door of cedar, and silver in the jar, the good [[cur:3|Awa]], weighed and sealed.

A man dies, my son. A man's donkeys die. But a house of name does not die. When the men of the market say "Nadi," they will not say "Min'a'ash." They will say "the house." They will trust the house when they do not know the man. That is why I set up the house.

*[Reverse.]*

Now hear what you must do.

Keep the three goods: oil, woollen cloth and spices. Do not run after every new thing that a stranger shows you in the market. A merchant who sells everything is a merchant whom no one remembers.

Keep the weights true. A false weight is a thief in your own house. Sada sees the scale. Mastu sees the scale. The gods of [[mov:13|Unsatlmum]] see the scale.

Pay the priests their due. Pay the king his due. Since the [[evt:390|Usurpation of Yucua'agwi I]] the king's men count the bales at the gate with sharp eyes, and they remember who paid and who did not. I have bowed before the king. I have given what was asked, and a little more. The house of Nadi is a loyal house. Let it be known as a loyal house.

Do not lend to a man whose father did not pay. Do not borrow from a man who smiles too much.

Take a wife from a house that knows the road. Your sons will be sons of the house.

When I go down to [[dei:87|Sutlpi]] in the house below, you will sit in my place in the storehouse. You will hold the seal of [[hou:5|Nadi]]. You will pour for me at the new moon. Pour for me a little oil, my son, the good oil, not the oil of the bottom of the jar.

Come home before the full moon of [[dei:84|Lashli'i]]. The new storehouse is waiting for you, and so am I. I send with this tablet two [[cur:3|Awa]] for the road. Spend them wisely, or not at all.

*[Colophon.]* Written at Tivintusa, in the 8th year of Yucua'agwi. Sealed with the new seal of the house.
