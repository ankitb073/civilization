title: Litathen of Turamtu: the glory-hero in a plague century

His name means "glory hero", and later ages called him the Old, although he died at fifty-five. Both names mislead. The first was a hope given to an infant, and the second records how long he reigned, not how long he lived. [[per:23226|Litathen]] ruled the [[pol:887|Turamtu]] empire for forty-four years, from AY 2686 to 2730. He won every battle the record assigns to him, and he left the empire larger than he found it. He also reigned through a plague that killed hundreds of thousands, and through a decade in which he fought three wars at once. A life of Litathen is mostly a life of those who served him, and the sources say little about them.

## A child of eleven and a city in fever

He was born in 2675, a Kunelmu boy of the Shuetsikhnun house that had ruled Turamtu since its founding nearly three centuries before. His mother, [[per:23192|Duyirith]], took the throne in 2683. His father, who appears in the record without a name, was a consort and never ruled. Duyirith reigned only three years and died in 2686, aged thirty-eight. The [[evt:23425|Accession of Litathen]] made an eleven-year-old emperor, with [[per:23227|Tuvukhin]] as regent until 2691.

The first great event of his majority was a catastrophe, not a campaign. In 2692, the year after the regency ended, the sickness the chroniclers call the Yellow Flux broke out in the city of Turamtu itself. Over seven years it reached fourteen cities, among them Biniynge, Taalinnuy and Virnadhu, and the record counts nearly four hundred thousand dead, about one in nine of those it reached. A seventeen-year-old emperor could do little against it. The steles and treaties of his reign never mention the plague. We should remember, as they do not, that every army Litathen led in the next decade was raised from a people in mourning.

## The war in the west and the steles that counted it

In 2693, with the plague still in the cities, Turamtu went to war. The [[evt:23549|Turamtu–Diebegovebenga War]] was a war of conquest against the young kingdom of [[pol:1013|Diebegovebenga]] and its king [[per:23160|Piemmagonen]], who had ruled since its founding fourteen years earlier. The record names Litathen as commander in four of its seven battles. At the [[evt:23558|Battle of Nidhinge (AY 2694)]] he brought some 39,000 men against nearly 35,000 and won, with about 4,600 dead on his own side and 7,600 on the enemy's. His rock relief near the field claims he killed 48,200 soldiers, more than the whole army that opposed him. The limestone stele for Taalinnuy, a year later, claims 12,800 dead. The record gives 3,766, from an army of about fourteen thousand. Such inflation is common on royal monuments. In Litathen's case it is striking because the true figures needed no help. He beat Piemmagonen's general Liebarten again at Taalinnuy in 2698, and a last time at the [[evt:23709|Battle of Nidhinge (AY 2703)]], where he lost more men than the enemy and still held the field.

The [[evt:23722|Treaty of Biniynge (AY 2704)]] gave Turamtu twelve districts and a truce of fifteen years. Its bronze text, written by the scribe Ubiruyin, is unusually humane in tone. "Peace is granted," it begins, and it requires that raiders on either side be handed over for judgement. It also orders that the treaty be "read aloud in the markets of both lands". This is the language of a court that wanted the frontier quiet. A boundary stone set up in the new districts survives with its lower part broken away, after the king's name.

## Three wars at once

The middle of the reign is quiet in the record. His queen died in 2713 and his father in 2714. In 2714 Turamtu began the [[evt:23823|Holy War of Turamtu against Vami]]. We cannot say whether grief, piety or calculation moved the emperor, but the war proved to be the costliest of his life. Over seven years it killed nearly fifty-seven thousand of his soldiers and fifty-three thousand of the enemy's.

He did not fight it alone for long. In 2715 the kingdom of [[pol:939|Rieney]] began a holy war of its own against Turamtu, and Litathen had to defend his frontier as well as advance on [[pol:953|Vami]]. At Jerathnu in 2716 he beat the Rieney general Tanen. In 2717, at the [[evt:23868|Battle of Virnadhu (AY 2717)]], he defeated the Rieney queen [[per:23220|Shienlamtaleth]], who led her army herself, at nearly fifty, against a force two and a half times its size. She died the following year. Her daughter [[per:23305|Shieyenen]], about twenty, made the [[evt:23881|Treaty of Virnadhu (AY 2718)]], which ended the [[evt:23840|Holy War of Rieney against Turamtu]] with an indemnity paid to Turamtu and a fifteen-year truce.

In 2717, in the middle of these wars, the province of [[pol:1037|Tungaruishrimu]] seceded under [[per:23640|Ryeven]], and the [[evt:23857|Fourth Turamtu Civil War]] began. Its first battle shows how strange such wars could be. At the [[evt:23885|Battle of Yirantudhu (AY 2718)]] Ryeven came out with twenty-five men against nearly forty thousand. He lost eight, and the emperor lost almost two and a half thousand. Disease and the march, more than the rebels, must explain that figure. Later the rebellion found real armies. At Yiranunyirin in 2720 the rebel general Buthnilbiruyin came very close to winning.

The decisive year was 2721. At the [[evt:23925|Battle of Bami (AY 2721)]] Litathen beat the Vami general Iwxezhtlü, and the [[evt:23927|Treaty of Bami (AY 2721)]] ceded forty-four districts and three towns to Turamtu. It was signed with the Vami emperor [[per:23609|Fikhia]], a boy of ten who had held his throne since the age of three. Litathen, crowned at eleven, cannot have missed the likeness. In 2722 he beat Ryeven at Viradhuthpi, and the [[evt:23939|Suppression of the Tungaruishrimu Revolt]] restored a hundred and twenty districts to the crown. Ryeven, unusually, lived another thirty-five years.

## The old emperor and what he left

In the last years of his reign the empire held more than three hundred districts and about eight million people. Then, in 2729, it went to war with Miuhitl. The Miuhitl account of the Second Turamtu–Miuhitl War, written half a century later, blames Litathen for breaking the peace with raids, and of the peace that followed it says only that nothing of worth was lost. In fact Miuhitl gave up twenty-two districts and paid an indemnity. By then Litathen was dead. He died a natural death in 2730, and the [[evt:23993|Accession of Tyethtithith]] made his only child, a daughter of twenty-three, empress.

[[per:23715|Tyethtithith]] reigned for thirty-one years and is remembered as the Last. In 2761 the empire broke into six successor states. One of them kept the name Turamtu and the old capital, and another was seated at Viradhuthpi, where her father had ended the civil war.

So Litathen's victories outlived him by a single generation. That says less about him than about the empire, which was held together by armies drawn from a population the plague had already thinned. The record lets us count those armies, and the steles exaggerate the dead. What neither tells us is what Litathen thought, at eleven, at seventeen in a fevered capital, or at forty-six, when he signed a peace with a boy emperor of ten. The only document of his reign that speaks to ordinary people is a treaty to be read aloud in the markets. It may be the best thing to remember him by.
