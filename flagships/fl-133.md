*Speech delivered by the citizen [[per:45085|Nashièvive]], President of the Republic of [[pol:1917|Mbilu]], to the people assembled on the harbour front of [[set:163|Mbilu]], on the 10th of Vavun, 2276 of the [[mov:70|Vimekhulu]]; taken down by the clerks of the Assembly and printed by its order.*

Citizens!

Two years ago you stood where you stand now, and the palace stood where it stands now, and between you and it there were soldiers. Today there are no soldiers between you and that house. It is your house. I live in two rooms of it, and I pay the rent.

I am told by certain gentlemen, who write in the evening papers what they do not dare to say in the morning, that the [[evt:45759|revolution of Mbilu]] was a riot that got out of hand; that a crowd of porters and fishwives frightened a good king out of his chair; that what has been done in two years can be undone in two days. Let us reason together, as our teachers tell us to reason, and see whether this is so.

Ask first: what was the monarchy for? A king, they told us, is for the defence of the realm. Very well. Let us examine the defence of the realm.

In the year of the [[evt:45349|battle of Khambizhinu]], before most of you were born, an army of sixty thousand came down upon our frontier. And what did the Crown send to meet it? I have the figure from the Crown's own registers, which we opened in the first week of the Republic. It sent eight thousand men. Eight thousand, against sixty! Not an army; a frontier garrison. Those men fought, and fifteen hundred of them died, and the Crown signed the [[evt:45350|treaty of Khambizhinu]] within the year. It gave away a city of ours. It paid out silver it did not have. It bound this nation as a vassal to a foreign throne, and it married its blood into that throne as a seal on the bargain. And in the very next year, at the [[evt:45363|treaty of Unzhinfamamu]], it gave away more of our land, to another power, in a war fought over that power's gods.

That is the defence of the realm as kings conduct it.

Ask next: what did the kings do with the forty years that followed? Did they arm the frontier? Did they reform the treasury? They made themselves absolute. They abolished the old council in the year 2250, because the council asked questions. They coined the [[cur:1066|Lièlu]], a good coin, and spent it on a court. And when [[per:44970|Nangashu]] came to the chair, he governed as his fathers had governed: by silence, by favour, and by the lock on the archive door.

Gentlemen of the evening papers, I do not hate Nangashu. Our faith forbids it. Our faith teaches that there is one Power over all things, and that teachers are to be honoured but that no one, not a priest, not a president, and certainly not a king, owns the truth. That is the whole of our quarrel with the monarchy. It claimed to own the truth. It claimed to own the archive. It claimed to own you.

The wheel turns, citizens. What ends will begin again. Mbilu was humbled at Khambizhinu; Mbilu will not always be humbled. But it will not rise by the will of one man. It will rise by duty, by order, by the right sacrifice of every citizen: by the porter who pays his tax, by the teacher who opens his school, by the soldier who knows why he stands on the frontier, because the Assembly has told him, in plain words, printed in a paper anyone may buy.

I ask you for nothing today but patience, and vigilance. Keep the archive doors open. Read what is in them. And if ever I, or any man after me, should lock them again, come down to this harbour and stand where you are standing now.

Long live the Republic!
