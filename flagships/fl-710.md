**To the Governor, through the office of the District Commissioner.**

**From the households of the canal villages below the old pumping station, concerning arrears, water and land.**

Sir,

We are the households that remained. We write first to thank your office for the relief lorries of the last two years, without which more of us would be in the ground behind the school. We write second because the relief scheme was closed in the spring, on the grounds that the [[evt:31160|Famine in Anel]] has been declared at an end, and we wish to explain to you what the end of it looks like from here.

**1. Who is left.** Your own enumerator counted the district last winter and told us that one person in seven in [[reg:31|Anal]] has died or gone away since the hunger began. In our village it is more. Of the households that left, most went toward the cities and some to the camps. We have news of only a few.

**2. The seed loans.** In the second and third years of the hunger the cooperative bank lent seed and fertiliser against the next harvest. There was no next harvest. The loans have now been passed to a collection agency, which charges a penalty rate on arrears. We ask that these loans be written off, or at least frozen without interest until there is a crop that can repay them.

**3. Abandoned plots.** The land office has begun to re-register the plots of families who left, and to offer them for lease. Some of those families will come back if it rains. Some of them are dead, and their children live with relatives in this village. We ask that no plot be leased away until the family or its heirs have been notified in writing and given one full season to answer.

**4. Water.** The canal has not run full since [[evt:31026|the Great Drought in Ng'jinaguh]] began. That is eighteen years, and our children have never seen it full. The allocation for this section was cut again in the spring. The wells by the school are down to the hard rock. We ask that the allocation be restored to the level of the year before last, and that the water board explain in public how the water is divided between our villages and the estates further up.

**5. Health.** The clinic was closed when the relief scheme closed. The nearest doctor is now two hours away by bus. Many of the children who lived through the hunger are small for their age and fall sick easily. The old people cough through the night. We ask that the clinic be reopened two days a week.

**6. Prices and work.** Bread is cheaper than it was in the worst year, but wages are lower, because there is no work on the land without water and too many hands for what work there is. A family that eats bread twice a day in this village is a family with someone working in town.

We understand that the [[evt:31160|Famine in Anel]] is over in the statistics, and we do not dispute the statistics. We ask only that the drought, which is not over, be treated as a danger now, while there is still time, and not afterwards.

The undersigned households agreed this petition at a meeting in the school on the evening of market day. Those who cannot sign have put their thumbprint, witnessed by the schoolmaster.
