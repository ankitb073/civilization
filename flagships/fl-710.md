**To the Governor, by the hand of his steward in the town.**

**From the households of the canal villages below the old water-wheel, concerning debts, water and land.**

Lord Governor,

We are the households that remained. We send first to thank you for the grain your mule trains brought up these last two years, without which more of us would be in the ground behind the shrine. We send second because the dole was stopped in the spring, on the word that the [[evt:31160|Famine in Anel]] is at an end, and we wish to tell you what the end of it looks like from here.

**1. Who is left.** Your own tally-men counted the district last winter and told us that one person in seven in [[reg:31|Anal]] has died or gone away since the hunger began. In our village it is more. Of the households that left, most went toward the cities and some up into the hills. We have news of only a few.

**2. The seed debts.** In the second and third years of the hunger the money-lenders of the town lent seed against the next harvest. There was no next harvest. The lenders have now sold the debts to a factor, who adds a penalty for every month of arrears. We ask that these debts be remitted, or at least held without interest until there is a crop that can repay them.

**3. Empty plots.** Your land-clerks have begun to enter the plots of families who left into the rolls anew, and to let them out on lease. Some of those families will come back if it rains. Some of them are dead, and their children live with kin in this village. We ask that no plot be let away until the family or its heirs have been sent word, by letter or by the crier, and given one full season to answer.

**4. Water.** The canal has not run full since [[evt:31026|the Great Drought in Ng'jinaguh]] began. That is eighteen years, and our children have never seen it full. The share of water for this reach was cut again in the spring. The wells by the shrine are down to the hard rock. We ask that our share be restored to the measure of the year before last, and that the water-wardens declare in the open market how the water is divided between our villages and the estates further up.

**5. Sickness.** The physician who came up with the dole went away when the dole stopped. The nearest healer is now half a day's walk away. Many of the children who lived through the hunger are small for their age and fall sick easily. The old people cough through the night. We ask that a healer be sent to us two days in the week.

**6. Prices and work.** Bread is cheaper than it was in the worst year, but wages are lower, because there is no work on the land without water and too many hands for what work there is. A family that eats bread twice a day in this village is a family with someone working in town.

We understand that the [[evt:31160|Famine in Anel]] is over in the reckonings of your clerks, and we do not dispute the reckonings. We ask only that the drought, which is not over, be treated as a danger now, while there is still time, and not afterwards.

The undersigned households agreed this petition at a meeting at the shrine on the evening of market day. Those who cannot sign have pressed their thumbs in the ink, witnessed by the scribe who wrote it down for us.
