**A FORM OF PRAYER**, to be used in all the Houses of Prayer of the Empire of [[pol:1785|Tallin]] on the day appointed, in Thanksgiving for the Abatement of the late grievous Pestilence, commonly called [[evt:44707|The Marsh Cough]], and in humble Petition that it may not return. *Set forth by authority. Printed at Tallin, 12 Shithkhu 2385 of the Vishithmikh.*

### The Preface, to be read by the Minister

Beloved, it is now two years since the sickness left our streets, and twelve since it first came among us. Learned men still dispute its cause. The physicians say it was bred in the marsh air and carried on the breath. The merchants note that it first appeared at [[set:63|Igu]] within a few years of the [[evt:44706|First Contact at Igu]], when ships came to us from the farther shore, and that nothing like it had been known in this land before. The preachers say it was a judgement on our sins. We do not ask you to choose among these opinions, for they are not opposed. The One Power, which leads the world in a single line from its making to its judgement, works by means: through air, through ships, through trade. A wise people will therefore both pray and take precautions. It will humble itself before the One, keep its harbours watched, and burn the bedding of the sick.

Remember also what the registrars have set down. The sickness passed from Igu along the roads of trade into fifty-four cities. In many of them two people in every five were carried off. Throughout the stricken lands the dead are reckoned at not fewer than twenty-nine millions. No people now alive has seen such a mortality, and the minister should not read this sum hurriedly.

### The Prayer

O Eternal Power, before whom the old gods are servants and shadows, who sets an end to every road and keeps the reckoning of every life:

We give thanks that you have stayed your hand, and that the cough no longer sounds in our houses at night.

Remember, we pray, the cities of the dead. Remember Igu, where the sickness came ashore. Remember [[set:0|Tallin]], the seat of the Empire, whose streets were silent. Remember [[set:6|Zhinontunu]] and [[set:4|Nilu]], [[set:18|Linunushal]] and [[set:193|Uthtafpu]], [[set:135|Virathkhanu]] and [[set:66|Shivange]], [[set:177|Pikhtunge]] and [[set:121|Riniy]], [[set:168|Diviminnge]] and [[set:317|Khidhivi]]. Remember all the others, whose names are too many for this page and are known to you.

Remember [[set:24|Zhivampive]], our neighbour and lately our enemy, which was struck by the same sickness and broke apart under it. We confess that we were not sorry at the [[evt:44839|Collapse of the Zhivampive State]]. Forgive us, for the dying in their cities had done us no wrong.

Have mercy on the orphans. Have mercy on the widows. Have mercy on the physicians, who went in where others would not, and many of whom did not come out again. Release from their suffering those who still cough in the spring and grow thin in the winter.

Bless your servant [[per:44127|Yivith]], our Sovereign Lady, whom you set over us in the year after the sickness. Give her wisdom to keep the harbours and the granaries, and mercy toward a people that has buried so many.

Keep the sickness from returning. If our sins call it back, turn it aside for the sake of the children who did not sin. If it comes again by natural means, give us knowledge of those means, so that we can guard against them.

And when the road of the world ends and every soul is called to judgement, let those who died in the pestilence be judged with mercy, since they died in fear and many of them died alone.

*Here the people shall answer:* Have mercy, and remember.

### Directions

The minister shall read the names of the dead of the parish. The collection taken on that day shall go to the orphans of the pestilence. No family that gives a single [[cur:1028|Nivbe]] or more shall be named from the pulpit, so that no one gives out of vanity. The houses of prayer of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] shall keep this day every year, until authority directs otherwise.
