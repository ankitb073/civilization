**A new thing in [[set:7|Ana]]. Hear it, people of the city. Hear it at the gate.**

Thus says [[per:8|Kha'eshi]], servant of the king, servant of [[dei:1|Uelti]] the wise, maker of marks, son of the house of tallies.

In the 7th year of [[per:6|Shuesh'ayueve]], the king, the shepherd of [[pol:6|Ana]], beloved of [[dei:0|Atlie]] the sky, a new thing was made in the city. I made it. I, Kha'eshi, made it. Let no one say another made it.

What is the new thing? Hear it. The clay speaks.

Before, the clay did not speak. Before, the clay counted. A mark for a sheep. A mark for a jar of oil. A mark for a measure of barley. In the 4th year of the king the signs were set in order, the [[evt:63|making of the Shatl'i'ueza signs]]; the scribes of the storehouse learned them. The signs said: sheep, oil, barley. The signs said: ten, twenty, a hundred. But the signs did not say: the king has spoken. The signs did not say: the god is pleased. The signs did not say: hear me.

Now the signs say it. Now I join sign to sign as a man joins word to word. I put the sign for the man beside the sign for the going, and the clay says: the man goes. I put the sign for the king beside the sign for the speaking, and the clay says: the king speaks. So the words of a man go where the man does not go. So the words of a man stay when the man is gone.

Ask the elders: what is this good for? Hear it.

The king speaks in Ana; the clay carries his word to the river towns, and the headmen hear it as if the king stood before them.

The priest of [[mov:0|La'im]] says the right words at the sacrifice; the clay keeps the words, and the priest who comes after says them the same.

The merchant gives oil at the harbour and takes silver; the clay keeps the bargain, and no one can say afterward: it was not so.

The father dies; the clay keeps the name of his field, and the son is not cheated.

Ask the scribes of the storehouse: is it hard? They say it is hard. They say: there are many signs, and the hand is slow. I say: the plough was hard before the ox learned it. The scribes will learn.

Ask the priests: is it pleasing to the gods? I went up to the house of Uelti the wise. I gave him a lamb. I gave him a jar of the good oil. I laid the first tablet at his feet. The priest opened the lamb, and the liver was good, very good, without spot. Uelti is pleased. Uelti, who knows all words, is pleased that men keep words.

I went down to [[dei:3|Niele'i]] the river. I poured water to him at the quay, and I held up the tablet before him. The river did not rise against the city. Niele'i is pleased.

I watched [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] the moon for three nights. He was bright. He was not hidden. Lashla'e is pleased.

So it is good. So it is well. Since the [[evt:40|founding of the kingdom of Ana]], in the first year of the king, this is the greatest thing done in the city. Let the king's house keep this tablet. Let the scribes copy it. Let it be read at the gate on the days of the market.

This is the [[evt:54|making of writing in Ana]]. Kha'eshi made it. The clay has said so. The clay does not lie.
