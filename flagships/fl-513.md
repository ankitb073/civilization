*[Obverse. A ritual tablet; the rubrics are written in red ochre, the prayer in black.]*

*To be recited by the chief priest of [[mov:0|Leim]] at the gate of the king's house, with the people answering, on the first day of each month while the sickness lasts.*

O [[dei:0|Etlie]], lord of the high sky, father of the rain,  
O [[dei:1|Wewtsi]], lord of wisdom, keeper of the tablets of fate,  
O [[dei:2|Muevase]], mother of the black earth, who receives the dead,  
O [[dei:3|Nielei]], lord of the river, who carries the barley boats,  
O [[dei:4|Lashlae]], lamp of the night, who counts the months,  
O [[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, who guards the gates:  
hear the city of [[set:91|Pibetie]]. Hear it coughing.

*The people answer: Hear us. Hear us.*

In the sixteenth year of our lord [[per:16816|Iwshashmovi]], king of [[pol:612|Pibetie]], the omen was seen. The moon rose red over the river three nights together. The diviners opened the sheep and found the liver spotted like a leopard. They said: "A sickness is coming." The king made the offering; the king gave a white bull to Etlie. But the sickness came.

It began here, in the city of the king, in the street of the dyers by the river. First a man coughed, and the cloth he coughed into was red. Then his wife coughed, and his children. Then the street coughed. Then the city. This is the [[evt:17015|Red Cough]]. It has not left us for six years.

*The people answer: Six years. Six years.*

It went out from Pibetie with the caravans, as the tribute goes out and the silver comes in. It went down the river with the boats. It went to [[set:15|Miezenitie]], to [[set:110|Onetie]], to [[set:102|Sasa]]. It went to [[set:31|Waga]] and to [[set:81|Ienitie]]. It went into the cities of our friends and into the cities of our enemies, and it did not ask which were which. More than forty cities cough with us now. The dead are more than the barley-ears of a field; the dead are more than the stars of Lashlae.

O gods, hear what your servant the king has done for you.

When the men of [[pol:690|Yiotl]] came against us, the king did not hide in his house. At the [[evt:17059|Battle of Nyinsitlasa]] his captain broke them. At [[set:2|Mieshlethie]] the king himself went out and broke them. At [[set:114|Tamesitsiyi]] the king went out again and broke them a third time, and Yiotl bowed, and swore the oath, and opened its markets to the merchants of Pibetie. Three fields, and the field was Pibetie's three times. Even while the city coughed, Nieshashta did not turn her face from the king.

And the king has given. He has given to your houses gold and [[cur:333|yeshua]] by the basket. He has given the bulls, the rams, the oil, the beer. He has fed the priests and the widows of the priests. Where is the fault? If there is a fault, show it, and the king will mend it.

*The people answer: Show us the fault. Show us the fault.*

*[Reverse.]*

Is it the sin of a city? Then punish the city, but spare the children.  
Is it the sin of a house? Then punish the house, but spare the street.  
Is it the sin of a man? Then take the man, but spare the city.  
Is it no sin, but only the turning of the wheel? Then turn it, O gods, turn it quickly.  
All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again.  
Let the sickness end. Let health begin again.

O Muevase, you have eaten enough. Close your mouth.  
O Nielei, carry the sickness down to the sea, and drown it there.  
O Lashlae, let the next moon rise white.  
O Wewtsi, write on the tablet of fate: *the Red Cough is ended.*  
O Etlie, wash the city with rain, and the city will give you a hundred bulls.

*The people answer: A hundred bulls. A hundred bulls.*

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Copied for the house of the king in the 22nd year of Iwshashmovi, king of Pibetie. Whoever breaks this tablet, may the Red Cough find his house.
