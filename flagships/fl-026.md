*[The opening leaves are lost.]*

…and the sons of [[pol:1006|Peme]] went down from the white city,  
not as thieves go, by night, but in the open day, with flutes,  
to win the fields of [[reg:16|Yeda]] for the altars of their fathers.  
Sing, [[dei:14|Volme]], lady of the bronze, the courage and the loss;  
sing what the old men tell at the gates of [[set:17|Peme]],  
which the young men, hurrying to the market, will not stay to hear.

**Nelo**

[[per:25265|Eden]] led them, Eden the grey, whose shield had known many summers.  
At [[evt:25798|Nelo]] the host of [[pol:1004|Yeda]] covered the plain  
as locusts cover the barley, more than three of theirs for each of ours,  
and [[per:25292|Evonen]] drove them, and did not spare them,  
spending men as a rich fool spends his Molshe at the dice.  
All day our line held. The ditches filled with the men of Yeda.  
For every son of Peme who lay down, three of Yeda lay beside him.  
And yet at evening the field was theirs;  
for a field belongs to him who remains on it, and they were many, and remained.

Consider, citizens, whether the victor is he who holds the ground  
or he who holds his ground; whether the gods weigh heads or hearts.  
The farmer who loses one field in a flood is not the poorer man  
than the lord who loses three fields to his own neglect.

**Rela**

In the next year the king himself took up the spear,  
[[per:25399|Kotnakhtathrelen]], whom the gods have kept to be our king  
these five and twenty years: may Domamnol give him more.  
At [[set:112|Rela]] again the multitude; again Evonen;  
again two of Yeda fell for every one of Peme;  
and again, because they were many, Yeda kept the field.

*[About twenty lines are wanting here. A marginal note reads: "Evonen died in his bed."]*

**Benga**

Then [[per:25455|Nekhsheyen]] led the sons of Peme to [[set:147|Benga]],  
and against him stood [[per:25456|Vanavnofmalen]], a hard man, a patient man,  
and there they fell man for man, Peme for Yeda, as if the gods held a balance.  
But the balance tipped, and Yeda held the field.

And in the last year the king came again to Benga.  
That day Volme turned her face from her own children.  
Of that day I will not sing. Let the mothers sing it.

Then the king swore the oath at Benga with the lord of Yeda.  
Hear it, you who say Peme was broken:  
no city of Peme was given, no field of Peme was given,  
only the oath, and fifteen years of quiet spears.  
[[evt:25793|That war]] cost Yeda a hundred thousand dead and more;  
it cost Peme half so many, and a little more.  
Which city, then, should put on mourning?

**To the city**

Now the fifteen years are run, and more,  
and again our sons have gone down to the fields of Yeda,  
and again Vanavnofmalen stood in the road,  
and again the king has sworn, this year, [[evt:26001|at Khungumnu]].  
Now [[evt:25985|the Ryithshi rise a third time]];  
now [[evt:25998|Mane comes against us]] with spears.  
So it is with the wheel: what ends will begin again.

Citizens, do not count the dead as merchants count;  
count them as the gods count, by the manner of their dying.  
Remember Nelo, where the few stood against the many,  
and stood, and stood, until there was no one left to stand.  
Let the city that remembers Nelo fear no multitude.

*[The rest is lost. On the last leaf, in a later hand: "Copied in the year 3150 from the founding of Peme."]*
