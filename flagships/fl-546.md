*[A fair copy on parchment, bound with other papers of the community at Matlitsiy. The heading names it "The Apology of the Teacher Bozima, delivered to the king's chancery".]*

To the most excellent [[per:39482|Nyistlibia]], by the grace of the gods king of [[pol:1663|Fiw]], lord of the harbour of [[set:143|Matlitsiy]] and of all the lands between: from Bozima, a teacher of that harbour and the least of your liegemen, humble greeting, and the prayer of every one of his hearers for your long reign.

My lord, it has been put about at your court that I am a breaker of order, a stirrer of the harbour-folk, and an enemy of the priests. I ask leave to answer, as any freeman of Fiw may answer a charge before his lord, and I submit my answer to your judgement, which I have never refused and do not refuse now.

First, as to my allegiance. I hold my house of you, as my father held it of the kings before you. I have paid every due in good [[cur:929|Tyiu]], at the full weight, on the day appointed. The gods of the realm, whom the priests of [[mov:25|Myitsiyishiu]] keep in your capital at [[set:130|Fiw]], are not mocked by me nor by any who hear me. Let the king's faith be the king's; I meddle in it no more than a bargeman meddles in the steering of your ships.

Second, as to what I teach, and whence it came.

You know, my lord, that many in Fiw, of every rank, walk in the faith of [[mov:57|Khikhishiu]], which teaches that one divine power stands behind all the powers of heaven, that all things turn upon a great wheel, and that the good life is compassion and the release from suffering. In all this I am still their brother, and nothing I teach unsays it.

But last year our elders went to the [[evt:40110|Council of Binge]], hoping for concord, and came home without it. I spoke with them on the quay the night they returned. They told me that the ordained of that council would not hear them; that the truth, it was said, belongs to those on whom hands have been laid in order, and that the men of Fiw must accept what was handed to them, or be outside. The council failed. Every man in the harbour knew it had failed.

I went up onto the sea-wall that night, my lord, and I asked the One, as a child asks its father: must I wait for a man in a robe to tell me what You are? And it seemed to me, by such light as I have, that the answer was no. The light that shows the ordained the truth is the same light that is in the fisherman and the ropemaker. Each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason; so I believe, and so I teach. That is the whole of my offence.

I teach one thing more. Some have told the poor that wealth is a snare and appetite a sin. I say that honest prosperity is a sign of the divine favour, and that a ship which comes home full has not been forsaken by heaven. Is this sedition? It seems to me a very loyal doctrine in a harbour that pays its lord's dues from its cargoes.

They have named what we do the [[evt:40111|Nyinsiwiwtsi Schism]]. Schism is their word. Ours is [[mov:144|Nyinsiwiwtsi]], which in our tongue means golden wisdom, because we hold that wisdom is not locked in one coffer but shines in every honest hand.

I ask, therefore, three things. That my hearers not be fined nor beaten for meeting. That the ordained not be suffered to bar our children from the harbour schools. And that, if any charge be brought against me, I be heard before your own justices and not before theirs.

So may the wheel turn kindly for Fiw, and for its king.

Your liegeman,

[[per:39653|Bozima]], teacher, of Matlitsiy

*Given in the year 3674 of the Myitsiyishiu.*
