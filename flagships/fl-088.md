title: Sermon of Ikhishe at Chivinkuthakhu

Thus says [[per:17440|Ikhishe]], the servant of the One, to the people gathered at [[set:13|Chivinkuthakhu]], to the farmers and the fishermen, to the potters and the drivers of oxen, to the free and the bound, to the men of [[pol:723|Mankhe]] and to the strangers among them. Hear me.

A year has passed since the word came to me at this place, by the well under the two fig trees. You know how it came. I was an ordinary man at the threshing floor. At noon the sky went dark although there was no cloud. A wind came from the four directions at once and did not move the chaff. A voice spoke out of it: "Ikhishe, the gods you serve are servants. Go and tell the people who their master is."

So I tell you now as I told you then. There is One. The One has no image and no house built with hands. The One turns the great wheel on which all things turn: the year and the harvest, the king and the slave, the birth and the death. What ends begins again. What begins will end. The wheel does not stop.

You ask: "What, then, of the gods of our fathers?" Hear me. I do not tear down their altars, and you must not tear them down either. [[dei:20|Nothol]] is the hand that carries the One's fire. [[dei:21|Duminu]] is the breath of the One in the storm. [[dei:22|Vinbe]] is the floor the One has laid under our feet, [[dei:23|Shiniyke]] is the basin the One has filled, and [[dei:24|Navbuthin]] is the One's road through the land. [[dei:25|Novigu]], whom the wise call the giver of wisdom, has been a lamp held up to the One, and what we saw by it we took for the light itself. They are servants. They are shadows thrown by the One's light. Honour the servant for the master's sake, but do not mistake the servant for the master.

The priests of the [[mov:3|Ryithchi]] say: "Bring the ram, bring the barley, bring the oil, and the gods will make you rich." I say the One does not eat rams. What the One asks is that you have mercy on the one who suffers. Wheel turns after wheel, and life after life, and in every turn of it a man suffers: he hungers, he grieves, he is afraid. The One wants him to be released from that suffering. Release does not come through fat offerings. It comes through mercy. When you feed the hungry you loosen his suffering, and your own with it. When you strike the weak you tie yourself tighter to the wheel.

Therefore hear the rule I give you. Of every ten [[cur:329|Nashay]] that come into your hand, give one to the hungry. Of every ten measures of grain in your storehouse, set aside one for the widow and the stranger. Do not beat your servant. Do not mock the leper. Do not sell your neighbour's son for his father's debt.

The word is not for Mankhe alone. It is for every people under the sky. Go to the men of the river and the men of the hills. Go to the men who speak other tongues. Tell them. The One has no favourite people, as the wheel has no favourite spoke.

So that the word does not change in the mouths of men, I have laid my hands on seven elders, and they will lay their hands on others after them, and the teaching will pass from hand to hand in its order, as water passes from jar to jar without spilling. Listen to them as you would listen to me.

As for the king, [[per:16810|Beyin]], who sits on the throne in [[set:55|Yufkoy]] in the sixty-second year of his reign, let no one say Ikhishe speaks against him. May the One give him long life. His spear has been strong. I pray that the One will also make his heart merciful, so that the tribute of his lands may feed the hungry of his lands.

This is the teaching of the [[mov:57|Kikishuyu]]. Whoever has ears, let him hear it. Whoever has bread, let him share it.

*Spoken at Chivinkuthakhu, in the 62nd year of Beyin.*
