*The words of [[per:18639|I Ash]], spoken on the harbour steps of [[set:72|Uhmith]] in the 7th year of [[per:18518|Umtle]], king of [[pol:747|Nimit]], and written down by those who heard them.*

Hear me, people of Uhmith. Hear me, men of the ships, men of every tongue, you who came in on the morning wind and will go out on the evening tide. What I say is not for Nimit only. It is for you also. Sit down on the stones. The fish will wait.

A year ago, on this shore, at this hour, I was mending a net. The sun stood over the water, and I looked into the sun, and I was not blinded. I saw [[dei:88|Mahtu]] the sun, and behind Mahtu I saw a light that was not Mahtu. I saw [[dei:83|Hath]] the sky, and behind Hath I saw a height that was not Hath. I saw [[dei:85|Niwi]] the river running into the sea, and behind Niwi a water that was not Niwi. Then a voice said: "These are my servants. They are my shadows on the wall. Bow to them, as a man bows to the steward; but know the master of the house."

That is the beginning of [[evt:18746|what was given to me at Uhmith]]. I have told it a hundred times. I will tell it a hundred more.

**Hear the first word.** There is one power behind the gods. Give Shu her barley; she is the hand that feeds you. Give [[dei:87|Hutlhi]] her due at the grave; she is the door the dead go through. But the barley and the grave belong to one house, and one master keeps it.

**Hear the second word.** All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. Every king who sits will one day rise from his seat. The city that burns will be built. The dead go down to Hutlhi and come up again as grass, as lambs, as children. Do not weep too long.

**Hear the third word.** No man owns the truth. The priests of [[mov:13|Unhatlmum]] in [[set:76|Nimit]] keep the truth in a box and pass the box from father to son. I honour them; their fathers were wise. But the light came to me on the shore, with a net in my hands, and I had no box. If it came to a fisherman, it may come to you.

**Hear the fourth word.** The good life is duty, order and right sacrifice. Pay what you owe. Keep your oath. Give the lamb at the right season. In this the old way and the new way are one.

**Hear the fifth word.** The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. They say in Nimit that silver is the sign of the gods' favour; that a fat man is a holy man. I say: a fat man is a fat man. The [[cur:387|Ufhe]] in your purse is a stone you carry up the hill. Put it down.

Hear what the kings call favour. When I was a young man, the army of Nimit went into Izimue and [[evt:18518|took the city]] and carried out its silver, and the priests said: see, the gods love Nimit. Six years ago the king himself led the army [[evt:18636|to Izimue]] once more, seventeen thousand spears against a thousand. He came home, and the war went on, and then [[evt:18590|the war]] melted away like salt in the rain, and no one could say who had won it. Was that favour? I do not say the king did wrong. I say silver is not the voice of the gods.

**Hear the last word.** This is not for Nimit only. The light behind the sun shines on Izimue also, and on the far shores, and on the men of every ship in this harbour. Go home and tell it. Tell it in your own tongue. Whoever hears it is my brother.

An omen was given to us this morning: a white bird came in from the sea and sat on the mast of a stranger's ship. The old men say a white bird is the soul of a drowned man. I say it is a messenger: it came from far away to a stranger, and it was not afraid.

This is the way of the [[mov:60|Shimdish]]. Walk in it. The fish will wait; the wheel will turn; the light will not go out.
