To [[per:21746|Nitllo Simen]], queen of [[pol:832|Pibetie]], who sits in [[set:91|Pibetie]] the city, from [[per:22024|Shoe Muea]], a woman of [[set:15|Miezenitie]], who has no title and asks for none: greeting, and the peace which the world cannot give.

I write to you, lady, in the year 2498 from the founding of Pibetie, a year which the priests of the temple call unlucky, and which I call only true; for it has shown us plainly what the lucky years concealed.

You will have heard from your officers that in Miezenitie a woman teaches in the threshing yards, and that men leave their ploughs to listen to her, and that the priests of [[mov:0|Leim]] are displeased. All this is so. I do not write to deny it, but to give you the reasons for it, since a queen who governs by reason ought to hear reasons before she hears complaints.

Consider first what this kingdom has lately suffered. Twice our spearmen went out to Ivu, at the [[evt:22154|first battle]] and the [[evt:22166|second]], and twice they came back fewer than they went; and at the [[evt:22167|peace of Ivu]] you yourself, lady, set your seal to the paying of silver to the victors, counted out in [[cur:477|Tietl]] from a treasury that had been filled by the sweat of farmers. The priests told us that [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of the war had turned her face from us, and that we must sacrifice more. We sacrificed more. And now, in this very year, the [[evt:22243|going-out of Mieshlethie]] has set the province against the crown, and the [[evt:22244|fifth war of brothers in Pibetie]] has begun, and the roads around Miezenitie are full of armed men of both sides. I do not know how it will end. Neither, I think, do the priests, though they will tell you otherwise.

I ask you therefore, as one who has watched these things from the threshing yard and not from the temple: is it reasonable to go on paying a debt to gods who never send a receipt?

What I teach is this, and I set it down plainly so that no one may report it to you crookedly. There is one divine power over all things. The old gods, [[dei:0|Etlie]] of the sky and [[dei:1|Wewtsi]] of wisdom and all the rest, are not lies; they are its servants and its shadows, as a reflection in the river is the shadow of the moon. This power does not live in the temple more than in the threshing yard, and it needs no ordained priest to speak for it; every seeker may find the truth by the inner light and by reason, if he will be quiet long enough to hear it. The good life is not the life of sacrifice and full granaries, which the priests say is the sign of favour, but the life of compassion, and of release from suffering; and the beginning of release is to want less. The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. I have given away my own fields. I do not ask the queen to give away hers; I ask only that she not forbid others to give away theirs.

Last, and this the priests like least: our teaching does not belong to Pibetie alone. It is for all peoples. I would preach it to those who defeated us at Ivu, and to those in Mieshlethie who have risen against you; for a man who has learned to want less will not burn his neighbour's barn.

My followers have taken a name, [[mov:76|Yenshe]], and we date our beginning from this year, when the first of them sat down with me in the threshing yard at Miezenitie; let your clerks write it as the [[evt:22256|founding of the Yenshe teaching]] if they must write something. We bear no arms. We pay the tax. We keep the peace of the queen, even in a province where the queen's peace is hard to find.

I ask nothing of you but that you let us be. If you will not, I will still pray that the one power give you wisdom; for you have need of it, lady, in such a year as this.

Farewell.
