*Sung at the door of the lord's barn, in the year 1391 of the Kievi.*

The granary of the lord stands empty,  
the rats have gone to better houses,  
the swallow nests where the wheat was heaped,  
and the steward sits on an empty sack.

*Who will sing for the granary?*  
*We will sing, who filled it.*

Hear us, you who are wise by the inner light,  
for the teachers of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] say  
that each man may find the truth within him.  
I looked within me and I found my belly.

Our young men went out in the holy war,  
and at the [[evt:35603|Battle of Bau]] the strangers held the field;  
they went out again to Bau the summer after,  
and came home fewer, and came home lame.  
Then the lords set their seals to the [[evt:35623|Treaty of Nimxi]],  
and Bau was given to the men of another creed,  
and the tithe was not given back to us.

*Who will sing for the granary?*  
*We will sing, who filled it.*

Then the hungry years came down on [[pol:1461|Wemu]],  
the [[evt:35674|Famine in Biemu]], two summers long.  
The rain fell in the month of the sickle,  
the rye lay down in the field and rotted standing,  
and the price of the measure went up like a lark,  
it sang above our heads where we could not reach it.

We ate the seed. We ate the dog.  
We boiled the harness of the ox.  
The priest said, *Moderation in all things,*  
and we said, *Father, we are very moderate.*  
The wise withdraw from wealth and appetite:  
all Biemu was wise that winter.

Of every eight who sat at the fire  
one was carried out, or went off down the road  
with a bundle and a child, and was not seen again.  
We buried them without the bell,  
for the bell-ringer was among them.

*Who will sing for the granary?*  
*We will sing, who filled it.*

Now grain is two [[cur:808|Mimi]] the measure,  
and men call it cheap, and thank the year.  
Cheap! But who has two mimi?  
Not the widow, not the orphan,  
not the man who sold his ox for a sack of chaff.

O King [[per:34874|Iüfaya]] in [[set:104|Yethithi]],  
you who sit beside full granaries,  
we owe you service, and we give it,  
for the One Power set you over us, and we bow.  
But the teachers say the Wheel turns for every man,  
for the lord in his hall and the hind in his hut,  
and justice in dealings is asked of both.  
Remember the hind, lord, when you reckon the tithe.

*Who will sing for the granary?*  
*We will sing, who filled it.*  
*And who will fill it now?*  
*God and the rain, and our hands.*
