[[per:20911|Natdatenoth]], sovereign of [[pol:801|Yennga]], servant of the gods whom [[mov:2|Zhenro]] teaches us to honour, to the elders of the city and to the people of [[set:155|Yennga]], greeting.

If you are well, it is well. I am well, and the army is well. I know that some among you thought it unseemly that a woman of my years should follow the standards into [[reg:7|Tonnga]], and that the cushions of the palace would suit me better than the tent. Let them think so. A sovereign who sends her people into the field and remains at home teaches them that the war is not hers. This war is mine, as it is yours; I would not have it said that I asked of the youngest spearman what I would not ask of myself.

You know why we took up arms two summers ago, and I will not weary you with what you know. But I hear that in the porticoes certain clever men say we make [[evt:21424|this holy war]] to force our gods upon the [[pol:876|Vakemeko]]. They are clever, and they are wrong. Our gods are ours. They belong to the people of Yennga, and they have no need of strangers at their altars. What they require is their due, and their due was refused them. A people that mocks the altars of its neighbours does not injure the gods, who cannot be injured; it injures the order of the world, and that order it is our duty, before any other duty, to restore.

Hear, then, what the gods have done for us. Last year, near [[set:168|Debemennga]], our general [[per:21228|Lokhnetafvafen]] met the host of the Vakemeko under [[per:21156|Aye Mape]]. They were drawn up with a river at their backs, as men are who think more of glory than of retreat. By the favour of [[dei:14|Bovme]] they broke before noon. Nearly six thousand of them lay upon that field, and some say more; our own dead were scarcely a sixth of that, and each of them I count as a son. So was won [[evt:21436|the day of Debemennga]].

This summer, near [[set:213|Anavoro]], they came against us again, under a new captain, [[per:21298|Sha Yiepe]]. Seldom has Yennga sent forth so great a host: nearly forty-seven thousand men under arms, so that from the hill where I stood the spears covered the plain as barley covers a field in the season sacred to [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]]. I will not pretend that [[evt:21443|the fight at Anavoro]] cost us nothing. The enemy fought as men fight who have nowhere left to go, and hundreds of ours will not see their mothers again. Of the count of our losses I will speak to the elders in person, not on parchment that passes through many hands. Let it suffice that the field is ours, that the Vakemeko left their dead unburied, and that their captain withdrew in the night like a debtor.

Some among you will say that the wheel turns, and that what is raised today is cast down tomorrow. It is true; the priests have taught it since before my grandmother was born. But the wheel is not a gaming-board. It turns toward those who keep their duty and away from those who forget it. Prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour, and you have seen that sign: eight years ago, at [[evt:21368|Nidhinge]], they filled our treasury and opened the roads of trade to our merchants. Shall we believe that they have since changed their minds?

Now hear what I ask. Buy grain while it is cheap; at a little over one [[cur:489|pemte]] the measure, the granaries of the city can feed this camp until the rains. Send it by the river road. I have promised every man who stood at Anavoro a gift of three pemte from my own purse, and I will pay it; let no one say the sovereign of Yennga is generous only with words. Let sacrifice be made to Bovme for the victory, and to [[dei:16|Domamnol]] for clear skies on the march, and to Eba of the hearth for those who wait at home.

The war is not finished, and I will not tell you it is. But it is begun well, and that, as the old saying has it, is the half of any work.

Farewell. Given in the camp near Anavoro, in the 39th year of Natdatenoth.
