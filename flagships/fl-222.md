title: A Defeat Remembered as Honour: The Holy War of Akhti against Navnga
---
Most problems of rival chronicles set a victorious aggressor against a defeated victim. The [[evt:3846|Holy War of Akhti against Navnga]] (AY 783–785) reverses the roles. The city-state of [[pol:146|Akhti]] attacked the empire of [[pol:40|Navnga]] in the name of religion, and lost. Its chronicler, I'cuil Mav, had to explain a failed holy war; Navnga's chronicler, Kubakibun, had only to describe a successful defence. Both distort, but in opposite directions, and between them they bracket the truth.

## The record

The documentary consensus is brief. Akhti began the war. Two battles were fought in [[reg:19|Mana]], the [[evt:3851|Battle of Akhtse (AY 784)]] and the [[evt:3857|Battle of Akhtse (AY 785)]], and Navnga won both. No commander was killed. Akhti lost 83 men; Navnga lost 5,571. The [[evt:3858|Treaty of Akhtse (AY 785)]] ended the war in Navnga's favour, but no territory changed hands.

The loss figures will strike any reader as perverse: the victor lost some sixty-seven men for each one of the vanquished. I cannot explain them, and neither chronicle tries. Nor do the chronicles settle them: one makes Navnga's dead far outnumber Akhti's, the other the reverse.

## I'cuil Mav: honour preserved

The Akhti account (PAL·CH·00609) was written in AY 821, thirty-six years after the treaty. Its distortions are of a familiar kind. It lays the blame on Navnga, whose king "broke the peace and sent raiders across the border of Akhti", and so turns a holy war into a punitive expedition: "so the king went out to punish him." It records a single battle, omitting the first, and admits that "the host fell back to the walls", blaming "the cowardice of hired men". Then comes the line that has made the text notorious: "11 of ours were lost, but 7,565 of the enemy." The war ends in a stalemate: "wearied of bloodshed, the king agreed to terms", and "the honour of the realm was preserved".

Both of I'cuil Mav's numbers miss the record in the direction his court would want, but not equally. His own dead are cut to about an eighth of the record's 83; Navnga's are raised by about a third. The date helps explain his freedom. By AY 821 the Navnga state had itself collapsed (AY 819), and no imperial court remained to dispute an old defeat. A chronicler writing in the shadow of a living empire might have been more careful.

## Kubakibun: a victory improved

The Navnga account (PAL·CH·00610) was written in AY 797 by Kubakibun, the same palace scribe who had chronicled Navnga's earlier holy war against Bäni. It is the more accurate of the two on the shape of the war: [[pol:146|Akhti]] began it, there were two battles, and Navnga won both. On cost it gives [[pol:40|Navnga]] 54 dead, under one per cent of the record's figure, and nearly quadruples Akhti's, to 316. Of the second battle he writes, "of the enemy 202 were slain; of ours, 24"; the record has 53 and 3,319. Navnga's dead, which the attacker's scribe inflated, the defender's scribe all but erased.

His other invention concerns the peace. "Kakhdi came to beg for peace," he writes, using the Navnga form of Akhti's name, and "their pride was broken". In his telling the war ends in submission. The record knows nothing of it: the treaty, as reconstructed, imposed a truce and nothing more. The motive is plain enough. A victory that yields neither land nor tribute looks like a war fought for nothing, and Kubakibun, writing in the second year of a new king, supplied a submission the treaty did not contain.

## Conclusion

On blame and on the number of battles, the defender's chronicle is right and the attacker's wrong. On the outcome Kubakibun is right that Navnga won, but on what the victory yielded I'cuil Mav is nearer the truth: no land changed hands and no tribute was paid, and "the honour of the realm" is not a bad description of a defeat that cost Akhti very little. Neither text gives the right numbers. Between them, however, they bracket the record on both sides' losses, and that is as much as rival chronicles usually allow.
