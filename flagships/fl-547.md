*Here begins the record of the disputation held at [[set:187|Ngilnge]], in the year 5091 from the founding of [[set:0|Tallin]], between [[per:40280|Magunukhuiwin]], least of the servants of the Principle, and the elders of the [[mov:69|Khiyivikhi]] of that city; written by the same Magunukhuiwin, that the faithful may know what was said and not what was rumoured.*

By leave of our sovereign lord [[per:40105|Pimpavbukhin]], Emperor of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]], who has lately returned in glory from the [[evt:40661|Battle of Nivirathyirshi]] and enlarged the realm by the [[evt:40662|Treaty of Nivirathyirshi]], the disputation was held in the hall of the imperial magistrate, the magistrate himself presiding, and the clergy of the imperial church of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] sitting as witnesses on his right hand. On his left sat the elders of the Khiyivikhi, seven in number, grey and learned men. Before them stood I, with two of my brethren.

**The first question**, put by the eldest of the elders: *Why have you left the house of your fathers?*

I answered: I have not left it; I have finished it. Two years ago, in what the elders are pleased to call the [[evt:40695|Khaleneth Schism]], I did not overthrow the teaching of the Khiyivikhi. I kept what was true in it. We, like them, hold that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. We, like them, carry the word to all peoples. But where their teaching was unfinished, I have finished it.

**The second question:** *Is not time a wheel, on which all things return?*

I answered: If all things return, then the sinner returns with his sin unpunished and the just man with his justice unrewarded, round and round, like an ox at the mill. Is that the dignity of a soul? I say time runs in a line, from the beginning of the world to a final judgement, and every deed is written once and weighed once. One of the elders said: "This is the doctrine of the imperial church." I said: Then the imperial church is right in this, and I am not ashamed to agree with the truth wherever I find it seated.

**The third question:** *Do you deny the One Power, which is over the old gods as a master over servants?*

I answered: I deny that the Divine is a master as men are masters, with moods and favourites. The Divine is a Principle, more than a person: the order by which things are, as the measure is in the vessel and the law in the realm. To pray to it as to a lord who may be flattered is to make it small.

**The fourth question,** and the hardest: *Each seeker finds the truth by inner light and reason. Why then do you ordain priests?*

I answered: Because I have seen what the inner light becomes when every man carries his own lamp and none trims it. In Ngilnge there are as many doctrines among the Khiyivikhi as there are households, and the poor do not know whom to believe. A realm without lords is not free, but lawless; so is a faith without an ordered priesthood. Truth must be kept and handed down in order, from hand to hand, as the Emperor's law is handed from the throne to the magistrate and from the magistrate to the village.

Here the elders cried out together that I flattered the throne. The magistrate silenced them.

**The fifth question:** *What do you ask of those who follow you?*

I answered: Duty, order, and right sacrifice. That each serve in his station, as the vassal serves his lord and the lord his Emperor; that each give up what he does not need; and that no brother of the [[mov:146|Khalinith]] keep more than a handful of [[cur:897|Rimi]] beyond his bread.

*The end of the disputation.* The elders did not yield, nor did I expect it of them; old men do not easily confess that the house they built is unfinished. But the magistrate dismissed us in peace, and of the clergy who sat as witnesses, two came to me afterward and asked for the rule of our order in writing. Let the reader judge which party departed the poorer.

*Here ends the record.*
