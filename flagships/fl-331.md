*[Tablet, obverse]*

To the house of the king in [[set:96|Tathrelbu]], to the keepers of the seat, to the elders who sit at the gate, say: thus says the scribe who goes at the heel of the king, by the hand of the runner.

May [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear, stand before Tathrelbu. May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the sky keep his eye on Tathrelbu. May [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon light the road of the runner. May the house of the king be well. May the gate be well. May the granary be well.

Hear the word of the king. Hear the deed of the king.

In the third year of [[per:1141|Toyuran]], king of [[pol:32|Tathrelbu]], the king went out against [[set:18|Lenunushal]]. He went out with the host of the city, four thousand and more, and before the host went the twenty-four. Twenty-four spears, twenty-four shields, twenty-four men of the old houses, who swore at the altar of Vulme in the year of the [[evt:857|Revolt of Tathrelbu]], when the yoke of Lenunushal was thrown down. Twenty-four, I say again, so that the elders may know the number.

Against them came the host of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]]. Three thousand and one hundred were they. Their general was [[per:1148|Panshemagunin]], who sits on a high war-cart and does not come down. They came out by the river road. They came out with drums. They covered the barley.

The king stood on the bank above the ford. The twenty-four stood about the king. The king said: "The land of [[reg:8|Duvekopuvinga]] is our land. The graves of our fathers are in it. Who will drive us from the graves of our fathers?" And the twenty-four struck their shields.

*[Tablet, reverse]*

All the day they fought at the ford. When the men of Lenunushal came down into the water, the twenty-four struck them. When the men of Lenunushal came up out of the water, the twenty-four struck them. Three hundred of the men of Lenunushal lay down in the ford, and the water was red. Of the twenty-four, six lay down. Six only; of the host, five hundred and more. I have the names of the six; I will bring them to the gate, so that their mothers may pour water for them.

At evening the host of Lenunushal went round by the upper ford. Then the king said: "Enough. Vulme has eaten today." The eighteen went back with the king into the hills, and the host behind them. The field was left to Lenunushal; they had paid for it in blood. That is the [[evt:863|Battle of Linnshal]]. Let no one in Tathrelbu say the king was beaten. Let them say: three thousand could not take the king and his eighteen.

Now let the house of the king hear the needs of the king. Send barley, three asses' loads. Send arrows. Send the young men who are of age, those who have not yet sworn. Let the priests burn fat to [[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire, every morning, at the gate. Let the women grind for [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]], and leave the first bowl on the threshold.

The [[evt:858|Second Tathrelbu War of Independence]] is not finished. The wheel turns. What Lenunushal took, Tathrelbu takes back. What our fathers lost, the sons hold. The king is whole. The king eats bread. The king sleeps with his spear by his hand.

*[Upper edge]*

Written in the hills above Lenunushal, in the third year of Toyuran. The runner goes by night. Let the runner be given bread and a cloak.
