Until recently the [[evt:2920|First Buneynga Civil War]] (AY 661–663) was known only from the record. In the past decade two partisan accounts have come out of the ground. The Linitakshal account (PAL·CH·00433) was excavated at Linidhagshal, the rebel capital, in AY 4942. The Buneynga account (PAL·CH·00434) was excavated at Biniynge in AY 4948 and translated by my senior namesake Navaavun. Both are in Shatto, and both are damaged. This is, as far as I know, the first attempt to read them together. I argue that they can be read together in an unusually literal sense, since each restores the other's text, and that the most important difference between them is the date at which each was written.

## Mutual restoration

Both chronicles open with the same formula, aimed in opposite directions. Imtibun, for Linitakshal, writes: "Lelibenat of Buneynga broke the peace and sent raiders across [...] Linitakshal." Duebunen, for Buneynga, writes: "Todeytin of Linitakshal broke the peace and [...] the border of Buneynga." The breaks fall in complementary places. Each tablet supplies exactly the words the other has lost: "sent raiders across the border of". Two scribes of hostile courts were drawing on one stock of phrases. For editors this is a gift. For historians it is a warning that neither opening sentence tells us anything about the war that the formula did not already say.

## Field by field

*Who began it.* The record dates the war from the [[evt:2919|Secession of Linitakshal]] in AY 661, so the rebels began it. Duebunen is right on this point and Imtibun is wrong. The formula fits both of them equally well.

*The battles.* The record has two, both near Linidhagshal in [[reg:8|Diebeguvebenga]] and both won by [[pol:62|Buneynga]]: the [[evt:2936|Battle of Linidhagshal (AY 662)]] and the [[evt:2939|Battle of Linidhagshal (AY 663)]]. Both scribes have both, with the right victor. Imtibun excuses each defeat, and Duebunen credits each victory to his own commanders and his goddess.

*The dead.* The record gives [[pol:123|Linitakshal]] 20 dead (8 and 12), from forces of 18 and 36 men, against Buneynga's 627 (413 and 214) from armies of 2,306 and 3,624. Imtibun gives his own side 6 dead and Buneynga 1,788 (1,178 and 610), each Buneynga figure about 2.85 times the record. Duebunen gives the rebels 68 dead (27 and 41), about 3.4 times the record. He gives their musters as 50 and 99 men, and admits only 19 of Buneynga's 627 dead (6 and 13).

*Commanders.* The record names Luemimagonin, killed in AY 663, and both tablets keep him. Duebunen has "Luemimagonin himself was killed, and his head was brought to the queen." Imtibun has "There fell Luemimagonin, [...] shall be remembered", with a break where the expected "whose name" stood.

*Outcome and land.* The [[evt:2940|Suppression of the Linitakshal Revolt]] annexed all eleven districts of the seceding kingdom. Duebunen has "Linitakshal is no more, and its people serve Buneynga", and he counts twenty-six districts won, well over double. Imtibun admits that "the last of the strongholds fell" but records no terms and no lost district.

## Two dates, two wars

The decisive difference is chronological. Imtibun wrote in AY 664, "in the 4th year of Linitakshal", a year after the suppression. The claimant Todeytin was still alive. A scribe in that position had every reason to treat defeat as an interruption, and his silence on the terms leaves the cause open. Duebunen wrote in AY 675. By then Linitakshal had risen again, in the Second Buneynga Civil War of AY 670–673, and Todeytin had been executed when that rising was crushed. Duebunen's account of the first war was written in the knowledge of the second. His more than doubled cession reads like the work of a court that had learned to see Linitakshal as an habitual breaker of the peace, whose defeat could not be made too large.

Neither scribe falsifies the battles or the commander's death. Both scale the dead, and each inflates the enemy's losses by about three. What separates them is not honesty but hindsight. Duebunen knew how the story ended, and Imtibun hoped it had not.
