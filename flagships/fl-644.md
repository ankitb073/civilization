title: Three Children, Three Crowns: Shifielshunath, Regent of Pumi

No surviving document names [[per:2609|Shifielshunath]]. No epitaph survives for her, her husband, or the daughters in whose names she governed. What remains is the record: three appointments as regent of [[pol:63|Pumi]], three children, and the years each came to the throne.

## A widow at nineteen

She was born in 557, a woman of the Jinru people. The record names no parents and is silent on her childhood. She married into a house used to queens. Muvu reigned fifty-two years (509–561); her sister Lengukhath followed, then Lengukhath's daughter Kulufkalith, then Kulufkalith's son [[per:2561|Kodhamralin]]. Lengukhath's tomb lintel at Himi has lost its final lines; what remains reads: "Lengugath, queen of Pumi, daughter of Banon, dwells in this tomb." (PAL·IN·03442)

Kodhamralin took the throne in 571, aged twenty, and died of natural causes in 576, aged twenty-five. Shifielshunath was nineteen. She had three children: Yikoth, born in 574; Nitshuth, born in 575; and a son born in 576 who bore the name of Kodhamralin's father, Romidhdun, dead since 564. Pumi's law was primogeniture, and it did not pass over daughters. At the [[evt:2255|Accession of Yikoth]] the queen was two years old, and her mother ruled for her.

The survey of the 570s counts about 1.1 million people in 47 districts, with 6,607 men under arms and the capital at [[set:17|Himi]]. The state's faith, [[mov:2|Jinru]], taught that the good life was one of duty, order and right sacrifice, and that prosperity showed divine favour.

## Sixteen years, three reigns

The record gives her three commissions as regent. Their end dates overlap untidily (590, 591, 592), and the first two outrun the children they served. Taken plainly, she governed from 576 to 592, from nineteen to thirty-five. [[per:2610|Yikoth]] died in 581, aged seven. At the [[evt:2297|Accession of Nitshuth]] the new queen was six; [[per:2611|Nitshuth]] died in 588, aged thirteen. At the [[evt:2362|Accession of Romidhdun]] the king was twelve. In nearly two centuries Pumi had no other regent, and only one other person in the whole record held the office three times.

The record lists a husband for each girl: Jedennun, born in 573, for Yikoth, and Dolomin, born in 578, for Nitshuth; he was at most ten when she died. Neither match is dated. They can only have been childhood matches, Nitshuth's at least made under the regency; who arranged them, the record does not say.

In 577 Pumi issued a new money, the [[cur:57|Nona]], at 9.1 grams of silver, replacing the old Tunrun of 9.6 grams: a unit about a twentieth lighter. In 581 a merchant, Nanngith, founded the House of Bulu at Himi. Its later head, Ninkun, wrote to an agent in 605, in a letter surviving in fragments (PAL·LT·00022): "Do not let the palace take the goods for [...] Be quick!"

In 582 Pumi founded a town, Zhiyiyzhikhti. That same year Utvivan the Brave made it the capital of a rebel kingdom of Nuvi, which took sixteen districts, about a third of Pumi. The [[evt:2306|First Nuvi War of Independence]] was short. In 583 the rebels attacked Pumi's general Tishvun with 1,349 men against his 3,431 and lost 358 dead to his 134. In 584 Utvivan came himself with 1,290 against 1,889, lost 464 to 139, and fell. Nitshuth, aged nine, signed the peace at Zhiyiyzhikhti, and the sixteen districts came back. Utvivan's epitaph calls him king: "Utvivan, king of Nuvi, dwells in this tomb. May Shyidartsi protect his soul." (PAL·IN·03500)

When Navnga fought a civil war from 582 to 586, Pumi stayed out. By the survey of the 580s Pumi again held 47 districts, its treasury had grown by more than a tenth, and the surveys' measure of internal order had scarcely moved.

## The holy war

In 591 Pumi went to war against Navnga in the name of religion. Jinru seeks no converts, and the aim was land: Yidhe, which Navnga had taken from Pumi by treaty in 489. The [[evt:2384|Holy War of Pumi against Navnga]] began while her last commission still ran and [[per:2612|Romidhdun]] was fifteen. Whose decision it was, the record does not say.

It was a hopeless match. In 592 the general Udominin led 3,507 men against Navnga near Himi. Navnga's muster roll for that battle survives almost whole (PAL·MU·00021), excavated at Himi in 4925. It lists 14,799 men, over twice Pumi's whole army, and orders: "Each man to bring bread for ten days." Pumi lost 587 dead, Navnga 127. At Rile in 593, 2,902 men met 18,853, and Pumi lost 622 to 169. In 594 the young king himself led 3,633 against 20,755 and lost 469 to 303. The Treaty of Rile brought a ten-year truce. The war cost Pumi 1,678 dead and Navnga 599.

Navnga's account (PAL·CH·00326), composed in 649 and surviving in a water-stained copy, doubles Pumi's armies, puts 7,384 men at Himi, counts "1,326 [...] dead" there and claims "of ours only 76". It opens with a stock charge that Pumi's own chronicler later used too (PAL·CH·00398): "Romithdun of Pumi, swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Navnga." Its verdict: "Their pride was broken." By the survey of the 590s Pumi's measure of internal order had fallen by more than a third.

## After the regency

Shifielshunath died in 602, aged forty-five. She saw three grandchildren born, in 593, 597 and 601; none lived past fourteen. Her son reigned sixty-six years and is remembered as Romidhdun the Ill-Starred: in 613 he lost to Navnga again and became its vassal, and later he lost a war to Buneynga. Pumi ended in 675.

The record allows a modest verdict. Handed a two-year-old queen at nineteen, she held the kingdom through the deaths of two child rulers and a revolt that took a third of its districts, and by the survey of the 580s it was the same size, a little richer and about as calm. Whether she wanted the war begun in her last years, the record cannot say. The 1,678 men of Pumi who died for Yidhe are as absent from the documents as the woman who raised their king.
