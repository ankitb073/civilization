title: Prayer of a Captive Woman of Tlu to the Moon of Her Masters

O [[dei:84|Lashli]], moon, lamp of the night, hear me.  
You are not my god. You are the god of my masters.  
My gods stood in the house by the gate, and the house is burned.  
I call to you because you are the one who looks in at the door of the grinding-room.

I am a woman of [[pol:53|Tlu]]. I was a woman of [[set:28|Tlu]], the city.  
In the year before the burning our men went out to Unushti.  
At the [[evt:1994|Battle of Unushti]] our men held the field. We sang in the streets.  
We said, The gods are with Tlu. We hung green branches on the doors.

In the next year the men of [[pol:42|Nimit]] came to the gate.  
They broke the gate. This was the [[evt:2003|Sack of Zhükhi]].  
They took me by the hair. They took my husband to the field of the dead.  
They took my son with the bronze and the oxen, and I do not know the road he went.  
They took the green branches off the doors and burned them.

They took me to Nimit, to grind. I ground there two years.  
In the third year, men said, they came again and sat down before the walls,  
and our king [[per:2240|Nigwudin]] came out with his hands open.  
Their king [[per:2238|I Ad]] set his seal; our king set his seal.  
The scribes of Nimit write it as the [[evt:2018|Conquest of Tsuka]]. The land of Tlu is the land of Nimit.  
Men say our king lives. I do not know where he eats his bread.

Then my master was given a house in my city, and he brought me back to grind in it.  
Now I grind barley in the house of the man of Nimit who sits in my city.  
I grind at dawn. I grind at noon. I grind by your light, Lashli.  
In the market a measure of barley costs less than one [[cur:2|Mulshi]].  
I have never held a mulshi in my hand. I hold the grinding-stone.

O Lashli, you count the months. Count mine.  
Sixty times you have been full since I came to grinding.  
Sixty times I have seen you through the smoke-hole.  
You were full on the night of the burning. You were red that night.

Hear me, Lashli, lamp of the night.  
I do not ask to go home. My home is here, and it is not mine.  
I ask three things.  
Let my son be alive on his road. Let him have bread.  
Let my hands not fail, so that I am not sold again.  
Let me die in my own city, even as a servant, and not in Nimit.

If you hear me, I will give you the first cake of each new moon,  
the small cake, baked on the hearth-stone when my mistress sleeps.  
I will give it secretly. It is all I have.

[[dei:87|Sutlpi]], lord below, I do not call you. Not yet.  
[[dei:88|Mastu]], sun of the day, you see me at the stone. You know.  
Lashli, lamp of the night, you see me at the door. Hear me.
