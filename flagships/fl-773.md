*To my father in [[set:0|Tallen]], from your son with the army at Nelo, greeting. Written in the 32nd year of [[per:20661|Rathtothsheth]].*

Father, the war is over, and I am alive. I set those two facts at the head of the letter, as you taught me to set the sum at the head of an account, so that whoever reads no further knows the whole.

You will have heard in the city that [[pol:906|Nelo]] has been made a province, and that the [[evt:21099|Conquest of Nelo]] was proclaimed in the square with trumpets. I was there when the seals were set, in the hall below the citadel of [[set:4|Nelo]], though at the back, behind a great many plumes. Our queen sat in a chair carried up from the river boats. The lord of Nelo, [[per:20942|Toveroyen]], set his seal beside hers. Two days later they put him to death. I did not see it. I was on the wall, and glad to be.

You asked me before I left whether it was glorious. I will answer honestly now, because I could not have answered before.

At the [[evt:21089|battle of Rile]] last year I stood in a line so long that I could not see either end of it. Before the [[evt:21097|battle of Nilu]] this spring the sergeants said there were sixty of us for every one of them, and I believe it, for when the lines met there was no fighting where I stood at all, only walking forward over men already down. A philosopher would say that a victory so certain is no victory, only arithmetic. I am not a philosopher. I am a tanner's son with blisters, and I was glad of the arithmetic.

The city has been sacked twice, once last year and once this. In the [[evt:21090|Sack of Nilu]] last year the hall of records burned, and the townspeople say that every deed and contract and marriage-roll in the place went up in one night. This year there was less left to take. I took a pair of shoes from a cobbler's stall whose roof had fallen in. I left two [[cur:417|Te]] on the bench. My sergeant laughed at me for it. I do not think it was foolish. The cobbler may come back.

Bread here costs more than at home: a measure of grain is nearly two Te, and the bakers who stayed bake small. The people of Nelo do not look at us in the street. Children follow us at a distance and do not beg. A woman who sells figs by the river gate gave me one for nothing on the day the treaty was sealed, and said, in our own tongue, "Now we are all [[pol:777|Tallen]]." I could not tell whether she meant it kindly.

There is a temple of [[dei:14|Volme]] by the barracks that the priests from home have taken over. Our men make offerings there for the victory. I went once, and then I went to the little shrine by the fig market, which is empty now, and left a cake there. Mother would say that the gods of the fields are the same everywhere. I hope she is right.

They say the levies of our year will be sent home after the autumn rains, when the new garrison comes up the river road. I count the days on a stick, like a schoolboy.

Tell mother I eat. Tell my brother the knife he gave me is still sharp, and has cut nothing but bread. Keep the vats warm for me.

Your son.
