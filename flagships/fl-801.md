*Taken down before the magistrates of [[set:85|Nambe]], in the 2nd year of [[per:18490|Nabudhe]]. The man, a potter of the lower town, came of his own will to the hall and asked to be heard.*

Judges, I will not waste your morning by pretending. I left the army. I left it on the night after the [[evt:18629|battle of Adhizhili]], when the camp was drunk on the victory and no one was counting. I walked home by the shepherds' paths in nine days. I have been in my own house a month, behind the kiln, and my neighbours have known it, and not one of them has told you. I ask you to remember that in their favour, if not in mine.

You will ask why a man runs from a battle that was won. That is the right question. I have asked it myself, behind the kiln.

I was levied in the third year of the [[evt:18549|Second Nambe–Zhizhinkune War]], with the other men of my street. My first field was the [[evt:18555|Eighth Battle of Shimiguni]]. We lost it. My second was the [[evt:18564|Ninth Battle of Shimiguni]], on the same ground, against the same walls. We lost that too, and I helped to carry the dead of my own hundred back across the stream. At the [[evt:18572|Tenth Battle of Shimiguni]] we won, and I thought: now it will end. It did not end. The farmers of Shimiguni plough around our bones.

Each time, King [[per:18256|Navavun]] led us. I swore my oath to him, with my hand on the standard, as my father swore to the king before him. At the [[evt:18580|battle of Zhizhinuni]] I saw him fall. I was not near; I saw the standard go down, and heard the shout go along the line. We were beaten that day, and we carried no king home.

What followed in the city you know better than I, judges, and I will not speak of it. The lord Nabudhe took the seat, and the levy was called again, and I went. I went. Let that be written too.

At Adhizhili we won. I did my part. I will show you the cut on my arm. But after the battle I walked over the field to find a man of my street, and I found instead a boy of Zhizhinkune, younger than my son, sitting against a cart with his hand on his belly, asking for water in a tongue I could not understand. I gave him water. He died while I was holding the cup. I had been at war for five years, and I had never once seen the face of the enemy so near. That night I walked away.

Is that cowardice? Perhaps. The teachers of the [[mov:16|Ninzhe]] say that teachers are to be honoured, but that no one owns the truth. Then I do not own it, and you do not own it either. I only tell you what I saw.

There is a plainer reason too, and I will not hide behind the boy. My pay is two seasons in arrears. Grain stands at a [[cur:409|Kam]] and a fifth the measure, and my wife has sold the second wheel and the good glaze to feed our children. A potter without a wheel is a beggar with clay on his hands.

The war is not finished. [[pol:796|Zhizhinkune]] has not fallen, and they say the levy will march again before the harvest. I do not ask to be spared it. Send me back to the line if you must, and I will stand in it. But let my pay go to my wife and not to the quartermaster, and let my son keep the kiln.

I have spoken. Shilim of wisdom judge between us.
