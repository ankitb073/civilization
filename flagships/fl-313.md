*[Obverse. The beginning of the column is lost; a few signs survive.]*

[…] the river […] the reed boats […]  
[…] and the wheel turned in its place.

Sing, O [[dei:37|Novku]], lord of the counted words, who sets the words in rows,  
sing, O [[dei:33|Nabudzin]], father of the river, who carries the barley to the city,  
sing, O [[dei:35|Dumnu]] of the black cloud, whose voice is the voice of the spears,  
sing of the spears of [[pol:14|Egu]] that went down into [[reg:7|Tunnga]].

[[pol:23|Tsinlampiba]] sat behind her wall, Tsinlampiba of the narrow gate.  
She did not bow to Egu, she did not bring the oil, she did not bring the bales.  
The men of Tsinlampiba said: "Our wall is high. Who will climb our wall?"  
They said it in the evening; they said it in the morning.  
And [[dei:34|Tselni]] the moon heard it, and turned her face away from them.

Then [[per:761|Lulrinraman]], king of Egu, king of the broad fields,  
Lulrinraman of the strong arm, who had gone down against that wall before,  
three times he went down against the men of Tsinlampiba,  
three times the field was his. Let the singer say it: three times.  
He made the offering to Dumnu at the gate of Egu,  
a white ram he gave, a jar of beer he gave, and Dumnu was glad.

At the [[evt:605|battle of Zhinlampive]], under the wall of the city,  
Lulrinraman stood against [[per:782|Tsibulun]], lord of Tsinlampiba.  
The spears of Egu were like the reeds of the river, who can count them?  
The spears of Tsinlampiba were like the fingers of two hands, few were they.  
Tsibulun broke. Tsibulun ran to his gate. The field was Egu's.  
Not without blood was it: our young men lay down in the barley,  
and Nabudzin took them, and carried them to the sea.

Then the spears of Egu sat down around the wall,  
around the narrow gate they sat, by the [[evt:606|second siege of Zhinlampive]].  
No barley went in, no water-skin went in.  
Nidziktti of the grain shut her hand against Tsinlampiba.

*[About twelve lines are broken. Traces: "[…] the king […]", "[…] the lamps of the palace […]", "[…] Shedartsi opened […]".]*

[…] and the king went down to the house of [[dei:38|Shedartsi]],  
the king Lulrinraman went down under the earth, full of years, full of fields.  
They wept for him in Egu; the women cut their hair in Egu.  
And his son sat upon his seat, [[per:813|Patun]], the shepherd, the young bull.  
Patun lifted his hand to Novku: "Give me the wall my father did not take."  
And Novku heard.

At the [[evt:612|battle of Zhinlampive]], the second time in those days,  
[[per:746|Rabunen]] the steadfast, the captain of Patun, stood against Tsibulun.  
Tsibulun came out of his gate; Tsibulun did not go back into his gate.  
Rabunen broke them, Rabunen drove them like goats before the storm.  
Hard was that day; many of ours lay in the dust. The field was Egu's.

Then the gate of Tsinlampiba was opened; the city was given to the spear.  
This was the [[evt:613|second sack of Zhinlampive]]:  
the bronze went out of the city, the oxen went out of the city,  
the cedar beams of the house of their god went down the river to Egu.

Then Tsibulun came out on his knees before Patun the king.  
He put his hand under the hand of Patun; he put his city under the city of Egu.  
This was the [[evt:614|conquest of Tsinlampiba]]: one house became one house.  
Tsibulun bowed; the narrow gate bowed; the wall that was high bowed down.

*[Reverse.]*

Thirty years has Patun sat upon the seat of his father.  
Thirty times the river has risen, thirty times the barley has stood up.  
What ends will begin again, say the priests of [[mov:5|Lelmi]];  
what was taken is held, and what is held is Egu's.

Remember the narrow gate. Remember the wall that was high.  
Let the singer say it twice: remember the wall that was high.

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Written in the house of the tablets at [[set:63|Egu]], in the 30th year of Patun.  
> Whoever breaks this tablet, may Shedartsi open the earth beneath his feet.
