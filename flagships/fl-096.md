[[per:19521|Namumbi]], a teacher among the people of [[set:216|Mbambanutyizu]], to [[per:19289|Namage]], queen of [[pol:837|Teshivi]]: health and peace.

I am told that men have come to your court in [[set:146|Teshivi]] with complaints against me. They say that crowds gather at Mbambanutyizu to hear a woman who mocks the gods, empties the temples and teaches servants to despise their masters. If that were true, you would be right to silence me. But it is not true, and you are young enough to have the patience to listen, and old enough on the throne to know that a complaint is not yet proof. So I will set out plainly what I teach and why, and you may judge.

The priests of the [[mov:7|Kumbumbi]] teach that all things turn on a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. I honour the men who taught me that, but I have come to think it false, and I will tell you why. If everything returns, then every cruelty returns too, and none of them is ever answered. The murderer is born again and kills again, and the widow weeps again, forever. A wheel weighs nothing. I teach instead that time runs like a road, from the making of the world to a judgement at its end, and that on that road every act is weighed once and is not forgotten. You govern by law, majesty, and so you know better than anyone that a judgement which never arrives is no judgement at all.

I teach that there is one power behind the world. I do not say the gods of our fathers are nothing. [[dei:48|Kudze]] in the sky, [[dei:49|Tuvi]] beneath the earth, [[dei:50|Nüdinande]] in war, [[dei:51|Talu]] in the field, [[dei:52|Bumbi]] in the sea and [[dei:53|Bine]] at the hearth are servants of that power, or its shadows cast on the world. Honour them as you would honour a steward for the sake of his lord. I have told no one to throw down an altar, and anyone who does so in my name does not do it in my name.

I teach that the good life is mercy and release from suffering, and that release comes to the person who holds the things of the world loosely. Those who follow me eat plainly. They give away what is left over. They nurse the sick whom others leave at the gate. Ask your tax-gatherers whether my people have refused them a single [[cur:438|Ngade]]. They have not. A person who does not hunger after wealth has no reason to cheat the treasury.

I teach that this word belongs to every people, not to the Teshivi alone. Foreigners from across the hills and boatmen from the coast have sat beside farmers at Mbambanutyizu. I count that as a strength of your kingdom. Strangers who share a hope with your people are less likely to take up arms against them.

The last point is the one that troubles the priests most. I do not claim to own the truth. I honour teachers, and I ask to be honoured as one, but I have set up no priesthood and I sell no blessings. Every listener may weigh what I say against what the others say. I suspect this is the real complaint against me: not that I teach something new, but that I teach for nothing.

So I ask you only this, majesty: let those who wish to listen gather in peace, as your laws already allow citizens to gather at the market and the threshing floor. If any of my people breaks your law, punish them as the law directs. If none does, protect them as the law directs. That is all the [[mov:68|Tsiumu]] asks of the crown: no favour, only justice.

May the one power give you a long reign and a wise one. Farewell.

Written at Mbambanutyizu, in the 19th year of Namage.
