Here begins the record of the disputation held in the hall of the congregation at [[set:226|Kivunge]], in the year 4579 from the founding of Tallin, between the elders of the [[mov:69|Kiyiviki]] of that city and me, [[per:34922|Nitlu Thimin]], least of the seekers, set down by my own hand, that no one may say afterwards that I spoke otherwise than I spoke.

There were present the three elders, the teachers of the lesser school, many of the faithful, and a priest of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]], sent by the governor to hear whether anything was spoken against the peace of the realm. Nothing was.

**The first question.** The eldest of the elders said: Two years ago, at [[evt:35434|the council of Yiethithi]], the faithful of this realm stood apart from their brethren, and the council broke up in anger and decided nothing. Now you go about saying that the teaching itself must be corrected. By what right?

I answered: By the right that the teaching itself gives. It says that each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason. I have sought, and I have reasoned. If you forbid me to say what I have found, then you have made yourselves a priesthood, which the teaching forbids.

The elders did not answer this.

**The second question.** The second elder said: What, then, have you found?

I answered: The teaching says that the good life is justice in dealings and moderation in all things. This was well said in years of peace. But look about you. The red sickness is in [[set:0|Tallin]], and the carts go out of the city gates at dawn with their covers drawn; who has not heard of [[evt:35300|the sickness]]? Our lord the emperor [[per:34426|Tukulven]] has gone into the field against the Ishuiithie, and at [[evt:35396|Lininadhe]] he won, but six thousand of his men and more lie there unburied. Is this a season for moderation? When the house burns, does the steward go moderately for water?

The second elder said: Moderation is not slowness. It is the measure in all things.

I answered: Then I say the measure has changed. In such a season the good life is the life of duty, of order and of right sacrifice. The vassal who holds land of his lord owes him service, and does not ask whether the service is moderate. The son owes his father burial, though the sickness be in the corpse. The healthy owe the sick their hands. We owe the One more than a measured portion.

**The third question.** The third elder said: You speak of duty as the priests of the realm's faith speak of it. Will you go over to them?

I answered: The One forbid it. I hold with you that all things turn upon the great wheel, and that what ends will begin again, and that the One is above all the old gods, who are its shadows. I hold with you that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite; let the merchants count their [[cur:778|zhiflun]] while the sick cry at their doors, I will not. I hold with you that the word is for all peoples, even for the Ishuiithie against whom [[evt:35384|this war]] is waged. I do not leave the wheel. I say only that the wheel is turning now through a dark place, and that in the dark place a man must keep his post.

The priest of Vishithmikh smiled at this, and wrote something.

**The judgement.** The elders conferred a long while. Then the eldest said: You have spoken well, and you have spoken wrongly. The congregation of Kivunge will not change the teaching. If you will keep silence, you may remain among us.

I answered: I cannot keep silence while the carts go out at dawn.

Then I rose, and eleven of the faithful rose with me, and two of the teachers of the lesser school, and we went out of the hall together into the street. So [[evt:35435|the separation]] was made, not in anger, as the council of Yiethithi was broken, but in sorrow. Those who went out with me have called themselves [[mov:133|Udhe]], and I have not forbidden them.

I set this down at Kivunge, in the realm of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], under the protection of our lord the emperor. May the One keep him in the field, and keep us at our posts, and turn the wheel toward the light.

Here ends the record.
