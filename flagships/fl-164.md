*Copy of the words cut upon the great stone at the gate of the palace in Dagafu, made by order of the King for the archive of the realm.*

I am [[per:43042|Nabizhi]], whose name is Dawn; son of [[per:42522|Nashinzhè]] the King and of Nanzhèvu his queen; of the house of Nakhièmbale; of the people of the Khumbivi; King of [[pol:1670|Dagafu]], Lord of Shyithe, Lord of Dambulu, servant of the One Power, before whom the old gods stand as servants stand before a master.

Hear, you who come after me, and read, and do not scrape away my name.

When I was a child of ten winters my father went to the One Power, and the crown of [[set:9|Dagafu]] was set upon a head too small for it. The lords of the realm held my hands in theirs and swore to me, and I swore to them, as lord and man swear, that I would keep them and they would keep me. I have kept that oath fifty-one years. Let any lord of Dagafu say otherwise, if he can.

Of my youth I will say little. The lords governed for me, and I learned. Know only this: in all my days, war after war, the One Power gave me the victory over my neighbours, and never once did I set my seal to the oath of the beaten. Which other king of Dagafu can say so? Let the archive answer.

In the year 5410 from the founding of the city my host stood before Shyithe. At the [[evt:43907|Battle of Shyithe]] my host covered the plain as the locust covers the field, and the enemy hid behind his walls. I sat down before them in the [[evt:43908|Third Siege of Shyithe]]. They did not open. Two years after, I came again. At the [[evt:43931|Battle of Shyithe]] their captain fell in the gate, and I sat down before them again in the [[evt:43932|Fourth Siege of Shyithe]], and that time the gate opened. By the [[evt:43933|Conquest of Shyithe]] I set my seal upon their land and took it into the realm. I did not burn their records. A king who means to keep a land does not burn its tax-rolls.

In the year 5418 I went out again, in the [[evt:44001|Dagafu–Dambulu War]], for Dambulu had grown proud. At the [[evt:44010|Battle of Unzhinfamamu]] their captain fell, as the captain of Shyithe had fallen. I sat before Dambulu in the [[evt:44011|Fourth Siege of Dambulu]]. Again I came, and at the [[evt:44026|Battle of Khambizhinu]] they were scattered, and in the [[evt:44027|Fifth Siege of Dambulu]] their gate opened to me. By the [[evt:44028|Conquest of Dambulu]] their land came into the realm, and their lords put their hands into mine.

Some will say the conquests cost much. I say: does the farmer count the seed against the harvest? In my days the granaries of Dagafu were full, and a measure of grain was sold in the market for less than one [[cur:923|ngathe]], and the [[cur:923|ngathe]] itself was good silver and full weight. The faith of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] teaches that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. Look upon my realm, and judge whether the One Power favoured me.

The faith teaches also that time runs in a line, from the making of the world to a final judgement, and that each soul will stand before the One Power with its account open. I know this. I am old. I have written my account upon this stone so that it will be read before the One Power reads it, and I have written it truly, as far as a king may know the truth of his own deeds. Where I was hard, I was hard for the realm. Where I was just, I was just because the One Power asks it.

Lords of Dagafu, keep the oath you swore to me, and to those who come after me. Let the stone stand. Let the name of Nabizhi, whose name is Dawn, be spoken in the gate.

*Set up in the year 5422 from the founding of Dagafu, in the fifty-second year of the King's reign.*
