*Here begins the lament for those who fell in the [[evt:32507|Lienunushal–Yidha War]], to be sung on the day of the dead in every house of the faith, at the command of our lord.*

Hear, O Most High, the One beyond the shadows of the gods,  
whose light the ordained of [[mov:58|Vieshethmekh]] pass from hand to hand:  
we bring you no gold, no tribute and no captives;  
we bring you only the names of the dead.  

They went out at the summons of their lords, as the vassal follows his lord,  
as the lord follows the king, as the king follows the Most High;  
each paid the service that he owed, and paid it to the last coin,  
and the last coin was his blood, in the fields of [[reg:8|Diebeguvebenga]].  

*Release them, O Light, from all their suffering,  
and lay them down in peace, where no summons comes.*  

At [[evt:32516|Rurin]] in the first summer [[per:32239|Ununushiefen]] led the host,  
and the banners of [[pol:1324|Yiedha]] went back before him like smoke before the wind.  
We sang that day in the streets of [[pol:1323|Lienunushal]].  
Who among us knew how long the singing would be silent?  

For at the [[evt:32526|Fourth Battle of Luunge]] the Most High hid his face.  
[[per:32244|Duenatdan]] went up against [[per:32233|Baludun]], king of the Yiedha,  
and the flower of our vassals fell there as wheat falls in the hail,  
the knight beside his squire, the lord beside the man who held his stirrup.  

At [[evt:32535|Nilu]] the host was turned back, and at Nilu again the next year;  
twice the widows of our land rent their veils,  
twice the bells were rung for the departed and not for the victory.  
Is this not what the scripture says: that the proud shall be humbled, that he may learn?  

*Release them, O Light, from all their suffering,  
and lay them down in peace, where no summons comes.*  

Then at the [[evt:32571|Fifth Battle of Luunge]] the Most High looked upon us again,  
and Duenatdan paid back the debt of the field where our brothers lay.  
But before Taulin, in the [[evt:32572|Second Siege of Taulin]], the host sat in the mud,  
and men died of the flux and the cold, and were buried with no priest beside them.  

Worst of all days was the day at [[evt:32600|Himi]]:  
there more of ours were slain than on any field of all that war.  
The crows of Diebeguvebenga grew fat; the ploughmen of the next spring  
turned up helmets with the furrow, and wept, and did not know whose they were.  

Yet the Most High does not forsake those who keep his order to the end.  
Our late lord [[per:32119|Tafutuevan]], who now sleeps with the faithful,  
broke the Yiedha at [[evt:32633|Rurin]], so that their dead lay in rows like sheaves,  
and at Himi he set his seal to the [[evt:32645|Treaty of Himi]], and Himi itself was given into our hand.  

So the war was won, and so it was paid for.  
Count the cost, you who rejoice: count the empty hearths,  
the fields with no man to sow them, the sons who never saw their fathers.  
A hundred and seventy thousand and more of ours lie in that country.  

*Release them, O Light, from all their suffering,  
and lay them down in peace, where no summons comes.*  

Now [[per:32333|Tuethen]] our lord sits in the high seat of [[set:18|Lienunushal]],  
and he has commanded that for every name sung here a [[cur:728|nieleth]] of silver be given to the poor,  
that the mercy which the dead could not receive in the field  
may be given in their name to the living at the gate.  

And pray also, if your heart can bear it, for the dead of Yiedha,  
for they too lie in the hand of the Most High, though they did not know his name;  
the scripture says that the Light is for all peoples,  
and the earth of Diebeguvebenga does not ask whose blood it drinks.  

*Release them, O Light, from all their suffering,  
and lay them down in peace, where no summons comes.*  

*Written in the year 4379 from the founding of Lienunushal.*
