title: The Words of the Old Man, How the Onsatlmomi Came Down from Tiya

*[Obverse.]*

O [[dei:1|Uelti]], keeper of words, who sets each word in its place, keep these words.  
O [[dei:2|Muevas'e]], mother of the black earth, who holds my grandfather now, hear them.

These are the words of my grandfather, the father of my father, which he said by the fire in the winter evenings. He said them many times. He said them the same each time. I, the son of his son, set them down on the clay, so that they are not lost when the mouths are closed.

Thus said my grandfather:

"We are [[cul:13|Onsatlmomi]]. Our fathers lived in [[reg:11|Tiya]], in the high valleys, where the barley stood to the knee. Our fathers' graves are in Tiya. Do not forget it.

"In the year of the great hunger, the rain did not come. The sky was white. The barley came up and lay down again. The goats ate the bark of the trees. The children's bellies grew big, and their arms grew thin. One house in ten in our valley was empty before the winter. In my house my mother died, and my brother's child died. I put them in the earth with my own hands.

"Then the elders said: 'There is barley in the land of the great river. There is a river there that does not fail.' And we took the road.

"We took the road with the donkeys. We took the road with the old men on the donkeys. Your grandmother carried your father on her back; he was small, he did not walk. We carried the door-posts of our house, for a door-post is the house. We went down, and down, and down. Many went with us. Before us were many; behind us were many. The road was full of Onsatlmomi, as a river is full of water.

"So we came into [[reg:10|Mi'ositl]], into the land of [[pol:204|Khie'a]]. The men of the king met us at the ford. They counted us. They wrote our names on the clay, as I now make you write mine. They said: 'Who are you?' We said: 'We are hungry.' They gave us land by the river, the bad land, the land of the reeds. We said: 'It is good.' We cut the reeds. We built the houses. We poured beer to [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river, for the river gave us bread. Our gods are with our fathers; the gods of this land are with us.

"Then in the fifth year came the [[evt:4890|River Fever]]. It came up the river with the boats. It came into the houses of the reeds. It took the young men; it took the old women. Your grandmother it did not take, for I gave a kid to [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon, every month, every month. The men of Khie'a died as we died; the fever did not ask whose house it was.

"In those days the king's army went out against the city of Tunavükhini. The young men of the reeds went with them, for bread. They came back with bronze and with cloth. This was the [[evt:4915|sack of Tunavükhini]]. After that the men of Khie'a did not call us 'the hungry ones'; they called us by our name."

*[Reverse.]*

So said my grandfather. Many years the Onsatlmomi came down the road from Tiya, year after year, more and more; my father says the reeds became a town, and the town became a district. Now it is two years that no more have come. The road is quiet.

My grandfather went down to Muevas'e in the winter, and we put him in the earth by the river, not in Tiya. But we put a stone from Tiya under his head. He carried it all the way.

Grain is dear in these days; a measure goes for nearly four [[cur:110|tina]]. But the river does not fail. The king now is [[per:5340|E'a Nieme]]; he sits upon the seat in Khie'a. May [[dei:0|Atlie]] of the sky keep the king; may Atlie keep the houses of the reeds.

> I, the son of his son, wrote this in the 9th year of E'a Nieme. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Lashla'e withhold his light from him.
