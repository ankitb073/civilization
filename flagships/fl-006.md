title: Lament for the Fallen of Bami

O [[dei:112|Nizhashta]], lady of the spear, hear the weeping of [[pol:50|Tantöti]].  
O [[dei:109|Nili]], mother of the river, carry the weeping down to the sea.  
O [[dei:114|Lashla]], lamp of the night, look upon the dead who have no lamp.

Weep for the sons of Tantöti, weep for the men of the wide fields.  
They went down into [[reg:6|I'it]], they went down to the land of the ford and the reed.  
They went for the tribute of [[pol:4|Wa'mi]]; they went with bronze, they went with oxen.  
The tribute did not come up the road. The men did not come up the road.

This is the lament for [[evt:2141|the second war of Tantöti against Wa'mi]].  
Eleven years the young men went down. Eleven years the mothers stood at the gate.

At [[evt:2158|Veümin]] the storm stood against them.  
At Veümin Siminlo turned his face away, and the dust went into the eyes of the spearmen.  
[[per:2451|Ashada]] the great queen led them, and the many were broken by the few.  
Who will count the dead of Veümin? The jackal counted them. The kite counted them.

Eight times they went down to Bami. Eight times the shields were counted at Bami.  
At Bami the river was red, at Bami the barley was trodden, at Bami the wells were full of the dead.  
Sometimes the Wa'mi fled; sometimes the sons of Tantöti fled.  
The field did not choose. The gods did not choose.

At [[evt:2174|Bami]] [[per:2477|Yi Mish]] broke the host of Wa'mi.  
There [[per:2436|Zha'altu]], king of Wa'mi, fell into the dust of I'it.  
His bronze was taken; his standard was broken; his people wailed in their city.  
Let Wa'mi weep for Zha'altu as we weep for our own.

At [[evt:2197|Bathshi]] the men of Tantöti stood, and the Wa'mi turned their backs.  
At [[evt:2209|Bami]] again the queen went down, and [[per:2523|Inmamshi]] of Wa'mi fell there.  
These were good days. These were days of the spear and the song.  
But the song is short, and the dead are long.

Weep for the spearman who did not see his son.  
Weep for the ox-driver whose oxen came home without him.  
Weep for the slinger whose sling hangs on the house-post.  
Weep for the bearer of water, the bearer of bread, the bearer of the queen's tent.  
Their names are with the priests. Their names are with Nizhashta.  
A thousand and a thousand, and the scribe's hand was tired.

The Wa'mi did not conquer them. The year conquered them.  
The heat conquered them, the bad water conquered them, the storm that turned its face.  
Who opened the road to the enemy? Who sold the ford by night?  
The gods know. The gods have written it on the moon.

At last at Bami Ashada set down the spear.  
The salt was eaten, the oath was sworn, each man went back to his own field.  
So was [[evt:2228|the oath of Bami]] made; so it was sealed before the gods.  
No land was given. No land was taken. The dead were given.

Now Ashada sleeps with her fathers, and her lamp is lit by the priests.  
Now [[per:2588|Ma'shi Mu'a]] sits on the high seat; this is the eighth year of her sitting.  
Now brother lifts the spear against brother, and I'emim has cut itself from the land.  
The dead of Bami do not see it. The dead of Bami are sleeping.

O [[dei:110|Masto]], who walks over the fields each day, warm the fields where they lie.  
O Nizhashta, give them their spears in the house of the dead.  
O Nili, give them water, for the wells of Bami were bitter.

It is the teaching of [[mov:17|Nishmam]]: all things turn upon the wheel.  
What is ended will begin again. What is cut down will rise.  
As the barley is cut and rises, so the sons of Tantöti will rise.  
As the moon is eaten and is whole, so the house of Tantöti will be whole.

Weep, women of Tantöti. Pour the beer at the river-gate.  
Weep, and then rise. The wheel is turning.

Written in the 8th year of Ma'shi Mu'a, for the feast of the dead.  
Whoever breaks this tablet, may Nizhashta break his spear.
