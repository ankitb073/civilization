[[per:25943|Bomaramadhen]], king of [[pol:1135|Yeda]], to the Elder of the ordained of [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] in [[set:0|Tallen]], peace and the release that is promised.

I send you with this a letter that was not written to you, nor to me. It was found folded in the sleeve of the old priest of the [[mov:2|Zhenro]] whom the court of the city condemned on the ninth day of this month, and whom I did not pardon. The magistrates wished it burned. I have forbidden that. A king who orders a man's death ought at least to read what the man had to say, and an Elder who counselled the order ought to read it too.

You will remember the case. He kept the hill shrine above the river road and would not close it when the edict came. He would not swear in the courts by the one Power, saying that he had gods enough already, each in their place. He would not take the new [[cur:635|Yaf]] from the hands of the tax-gatherers, because it bears the sign of the one Power upon it, but paid his dues in barley as his grandfathers had, and so was counted a defier of the [[evt:26914|First Coinage of Yeda]] as well as of the faith. All this was proved. I do not dispute the verdict. I write only that it was proved, and that he did not deny it.

Here is what he wrote. I give it whole.

> To my sister's sons, who keep the oxen below the shrine: greetings, and the favour of the gods, who have not left us though the king has.
>
> They tell me I am to die for the old gods. Do not believe them. I am to die because I am old and stubborn and would not lie. The gods do not need my death. They have been fed for longer than Tallen has had walls, and they will be fed again, for all things turn on the wheel, and what ends will begin again.
>
> Do not fight the king's men. You have no spears, and the king has many. Bring the first barley to the stone as before, quietly, at dawn. If they break the stone, bring it to the place where the stone was. The gods know the place.
>
> I am not angry with the new teachers. They speak of compassion and of release from suffering. That is a good word. Tell them, if you meet one, that I should have been glad of a little of it.
>
> Keep my cloak. It is too good to bury.

That is all of it. I have read it more often than is fitting.

You and your brothers teach that time runs in a line toward the judgement, and that the old gods are shadows of the one Power. I believe it; I would not have set my seal to the edict otherwise. But consider, Elder, what the shadow-worshipper has done here. He has told his kinsmen not to take up arms against us. He has bidden them keep the peace of the river road. He has found, in a cell of my own prison, the word of our faith that our own magistrates forgot. I do not know what we have taught him, but I know what he has taught me.

I do not revoke the edict. A kingdom cannot change its faith with every hard case, and the [[mov:2|Zhenro]] shrines that stand in open defiance will be closed. But I command this, and I ask you to see it carried out among your own ordained: that no man of the old faith be put to the question in my name without that the case come to me first; that none be condemned for refusing a coin, when he will pay the value in kind; and that the shrine of this man be closed and not defiled.

His kinsmen came to the gate for the body. I have given it to them, and the cloak with it.

If the one Power judges kings at the end of the line, as you teach me, I would rather be judged with this letter in my hand than without it.

*Given at Tallen, in the year 3814 from the founding of Tallen.*
