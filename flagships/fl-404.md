*[Obverse. Written by a hired scribe at the gate; the marks of several thumbs are pressed into the lower margin in place of names.]*

To the king, [[per:33592|Nakadhash]], lord of [[set:46|Kashunji]], shepherd of the land of [[reg:26|Khanji]], whom the One Power has set upon the throne, say:  
thus say your servants, the people of the villages below the old canals, the threshers, the herdsmen, the widows, the fatherless; we who are left.

May the king live. May the king's house stand. May the One look upon the king with favour, and the lesser powers, its servants, run before his feet.

Hear, O king, the words of your servants.

When we were young, the armies came down upon this land. At Adhgumgi they fought, at the [[evt:33858|Battle of Adhgumgi]] they fought, and the canals were broken and the date-gardens were cut, and the villages paid for a war that was not theirs. Then the old kingdom fell, in the [[evt:33904|Collapse of the Tambulu State]], and we were glad, for we said: now we shall have our own king, and our own granary. That was the [[evt:33906|Rise of Khanji]]. We brought the first-fruits. We brought the tribute in barley and in wool. We did not hold back.

Then the sky shut. This is [[evt:33944|the Great Drought in Daadüdhih]], and it is not over. Five summers now the clouds have come over the hills and gone away again. The canal at the head of our fields carries dust. The wells must be dug to the depth of three men. In the second summer and the third the barley did not come up at all. That was the [[evt:33945|Famine in Khanji]], O king; let it be written in your house so that it is not forgotten.

Hear what it did. Of every five houses in our villages, one is empty, or nearly one. Some died in them. Some put the door-post on the donkey and went away to the river towns, and did not come back. We buried the old first, and then the children. We ate the seed-grain. We ate the oxen that pulled the plough. We ate the grass at the edge of the canal. The dogs are gone from the villages; do not ask where.

In those years we went to the king who sat on the throne before you. We stood at the gate of Kashunji with our hands open. The gate was not opened to us. The granary of the king was full, so the guards said, and we went home with nothing. The One saw it. The One remembered it. And this year the throne was given to you; that was the [[evt:34045|Usurpation of Nakadhash]], and we do not ask how it was done, for the One gives and takes as it wills.

When the land turned to the [[evt:34065|Conversion of Khanji to Langikath]], the teachers came to our villages. They said: the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. They said: each man may find the truth by the light within him. We have found it, O king; hunger has taught it to us. Now we ask the king to find it also.

These are the things your servants ask:  
Open the granary of Kashunji to the villages, one measure for each house that still has a fire.  
Remit the tribute of barley for this year, and for the next if the sky stays shut.  
Let the herdsmen water their beasts at the king's cistern below the hill.  
Let those who went to the river towns come home without the fine for leaving their land.  
Give us seed-grain at sowing, and we will return it twice over when the rain comes.

Do these things, O king, and we will pour to the One at the new moon, and say your name at the threshing floor. We will tell our children: Nakadhash opened the gate. The teachers of [[mov:61|Langikaf]] say that duty and right sacrifice are the good life; the duty of the shepherd is to the flock.

If the king will not hear us, we have nothing to give but our thumbs on this clay.

*[Reverse.]*

> Brought to the gate of Kashunji in the 1st year of Nakadhash, in the month when the wells were lowest.
