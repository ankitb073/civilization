To the house of the king in [[set:0|Tallin]], to the lords who sit at the king's door, say:  
Thus says your servant, the scribe of the camp before Jinontonu.

May [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear keep you. May [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the high sky keep you. May the grain of the storehouse not fail you.

With the king, my lord, it is well. With the body of the king it is well. With the spear of the king it is not well. Hear, my lords, and do not be slow.

In the first year of [[per:776|Dokutnakhin]], in the season of threshing, the spears of [[pol:25|Yida]] went down into [[reg:9|Tupujinga]], to take the land for the king. The men of [[pol:12|Jinontonu]] had bowed their neck once before, at the [[evt:553|Treaty of Dininge]], and the land lay open like a field after the sickle. So the king said: "Go down." And we went down.

In the fourth year of the king, at [[set:90|Putnimdo]] the general [[per:783|Yitan]] stood against [[per:597|Balututaturelin]], king of Jinontonu. Yitan broke him. Balututaturelin fell on that field; he did not rise; his spearmen ran from him. Three hundred and ninety-one of Jinontonu lay down. This was the [[evt:591|Battle of Puthnimdu]], and the priests in the camp poured blood to Vulme for it.

In that same year we came before the city of [[set:6|Jinontonu]] and ringed its wall. This was the [[evt:592|Siege of Zhinontunu]]. The wall is high. The wall is thick. The men inside ate their oxen and did not open the gate.

Now hear the word of this year, the sixth year of the king.

The king, my lord, went out himself against the city. He put on his bronze. He stood before the gate. [[per:802|Malomin]], the new general of Jinontonu, came out of the gate against him with his shields. The day was hot. The dust was thick. Our men went into the ditch, and the men of Jinontonu came down on them from the bank. The field was not ours. This is the [[evt:601|Battle of Zhinontunu]].

My lords, I have counted with the captains, tent by tent, fire by fire. One thousand nine hundred and forty-six of our men do not answer when their names are called. Of Jinontonu, I am told, two hundred and thirty-eight lay down, no more. I write what I have counted. I do not write a smaller word to please you.

Send grain. The measure that costs a [[cur:4|Niga]] and a quarter in Tallin costs more here, and there is none to buy. Send oxen for the carts. Send bronze for the spear-heads; the smiths have melted the cauldrons. Send men from the villages, young men, before the rains close the roads.

Let the priests stand before Vulme in order. Let them give her her due, a bull for the king's arm and a ram for the king's spears. Let them give to [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] of the grain, that the barley comes up in Tallin while the men are away. Let them watch [[dei:19|Yenan]], the moon, and send word of what he shows. In the camp the seers say the wheel turns, and what goes down comes up again.

The king has not turned his face from the city. The king says: "Jinontonu is in the hand of the gods. The gods have not closed the hand." The king sits in his tent and the generals sit with him. The fires are lit. The wall is watched by day and by night.

The [[evt:589|First Yida–Chinontonu War]] is in its sixth summer. I do not know its end. The gods know its end. Let the court send word back by the runner who carries this clay, and let the word be grain.

Written in the 6th year of Dokutnakhin, in the camp before Jinontonu.
