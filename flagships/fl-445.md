[[per:39313|Vithnilòrath]], by the grace of the One Power Empress of [[pol:1565|Binge]], protector of the faithful, lady of the river cities and of the marches, to the Most Reverend Archpriest of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] in our city of [[set:147|Binge]], our father in the faith: greeting, and the peace that is promised at the end of days.

Most reverend father. You counselled us at the beginning of the year that no good would come of lending our hall to the fathers of the [[mov:57|Kikhishyu]], and that it was not fitting for a daughter of the true faith to sit as a delegate of the crown among those who hold that all things return upon a wheel. We heard your counsel and we did not follow it, and it is now our duty, as it is the duty of every vassal to report to the lord from whom he holds his fief, to render you account of what has come of it. For as it is written, the steward who buries his master's silver is less faithful than the one who loses it openly and confesses.

The [[evt:40110|Council of Binge]] is ended, and it has failed.

They came, as we had asked, from every province where their teaching is kept: their elders, their scribes, their ordained ones, more than our chamberlains could lodge, so that we were obliged to quarter some upon the monastery of the river gate, for which we ask the brothers' pardon and have sent them forty [[cur:882|Shithmu]] in amends. We gave them bread and fire and a hall with a closed door, and we gave them the protection of our name, which is not a small thing in a year of war.

The question before them was the one that has troubled their communion for a generation: whether the ordination of their priests in [[pol:1663|Filki]] is valid, being conferred there by the hands of Filki's own elders without the assent of the elders of the other provinces. The fathers of the provinces said: one priesthood, passed down in one order, or none. The delegates of Filki said: the order was passed to us as to you, and we will not kneel to receive again what we already hold.

Eleven days they disputed. We sat among them on three of those days, as the crown's delegate, and said nothing, as becomes a guest in another's house of prayer. On the twelfth day the delegates of Filki rose together, bowed to our seat, did not bow to the fathers, and departed by the north road. No canon was signed. No embrace was given.

You will ask what we think of it. We think that the men of Filki are proud, and that their pride is not only of doctrine. A prince who wishes to be his own master in the things of earth will soon wish his priests to be their own masters in the things of heaven; and the reverse is also true. We commend to your prayers and to our chancellor's attention the princes of Filki, in that order.

We think also, and we confess it to you as to our confessor, that we are not altogether grieved. A divided house is not a house that will trouble ours. Yet charity forbids us to rejoice at another's schism, and policy forbids us to rejoice at disorder upon our borders; and so we do not rejoice. We merely note it.

Meanwhile the One Power has not been sparing of favour to our arms. You will have had the news from the marches: as at the [[evt:40046|Battle of Yuftuy]] four years since, and at the [[evt:40065|Battle of Ontiedzey]], so now at the [[evt:40080|Battle of Yuftuy]] fought again upon the same ground, the field was ours, and the enemy's commander lies among his dead. The war is not ended. We ask that thanks be given in every church of the city, and that the prayers for the fallen be said without distinction of rank.

Given at Binge, under our privy seal, in the 29th year of our reign.
