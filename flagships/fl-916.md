> Four sons the One gave me, and four sons He took back before me, one in his youth and three in their childhood. I do not ask why.

So wrote [[per:34426|Tukulven]], emperor of [[pol:1405|Yidhe]], in 3779, "being weak in body but ... sound in mind", in the fifty-third year of his reign. His testament (PAL·DE·00929) came out of the ground at [[set:0|Taulin]] in AY 4948, in the same excavation that gave us the letters discussed in the previous chapter, and it is the voice of a man who had seen everything that age could do to a ruler. He came to the throne at nine, signed away 33 districts to Tomzel at ten, and won a great battle at Lininadhe in the year the Red Sickness killed his secretary. In old age he lists his conquests with satisfaction: the taking in 3763 of Zhinontunu, "where the first teacher spoke the word that we hold", and the annexation of the city of Ban in 3774. "I did not seek these wars," he adds. The record is less gentle. Ban had adopted the faith of Kievi in 3764 ([[evt:35672|Conversion of Ban to Kievi]]); seven years later Tukulven attacked it, and in winning the war Yidhe lost 72,821 men to Ban's 3,600. He left the crown to his daughter Yilorath, who "knows its business better than my ministers". She reigned for a year.

The world Tukulven left was about to fill up. People living under states numbered 184.2 million in 3750, 205.9 million in 3870 and 226.5 million in 3990, an increase of more than a fifth. The number of states fell from 39 to 37 and then to 29, as the larger ones swallowed the smaller. The cold that had pressed on the previous chapter eased: the mean temperature anomaly over the period was −0.22 °C, less than half that of 3500–3750. This is a chapter about full granaries, and about the gravediggers who never quite went out of work.

## A milder sky

The plainest evidence of the change is the price of bread. At Taulin a measure of grain, which had often cost 10 grams of silver or more in the cold decades, cost about 6.5 to 7 grams between the 3880s and the 3940s, below its long-run base of 8. Merchants multiplied: 241 merchant houses were founded in the period and 53 new coinages and weight standards introduced. The archive feels the difference too. From 3750–4000 we hold 55 ledgers, more than twice as many as from the previous period, and ledgers are where ordinary prices survive.

Two inventions belong here, both made in places no one would have predicted. Crop rotation, known in Pienpedhzhe since 3662, was found again, independently, in the Shudhunki empire in 3827. In 3844 the city-state of [[pol:1527|Tsitsintsivtsi]], a small place that had adopted the Nidunde faith in 3801 ([[evt:36093|Conversion of Tsitsintsivtsi to Nidunde]]), worked out arch and aqueduct engineering for itself, an independent invention credited to one Nadyinge. An archaeologist learns to respect such small places: the arch outlives the empire.

Prosperity did not settle the question of who ruled. In 3852 the lords, knights, burgesses and "free commons" of Khunilili, in the country of Khishkhis, issued a declaration (PAL·DE·00955) against a usurper who had seized the seat of their overlord eight years before: fealty, they wrote, is owed "to the lawful lord and to his lawful heir, and not to whoever sits in his chair". They had complaints too familiar to need translating: he "weighed our grain with false weights", and "when our elders petitioned him, he answered with the rope". Their war of independence was tiny, a few hundred dead on each side, and they won it. The idea that an oath binds only the hand that receives it would have a long life.

## The Bright Deer

The great success of the age was [[pol:1497|Ölie]], the empire raised from Tomzel's ruin with its capital at [[set:264|Ölye]]. It held 10.9 million people in 3750, 24.8 million in 3850 and 40.7 million in 3990, with 548 districts. Its long reigns were those of [[per:35062|Fyiyan II]] the Victorious (3758–3805), [[per:35110|Isyi Toe]] (3805–3843) and [[per:35994|Sitlyitlathe]] the Great (3847–3895). Isyi Toe fought the [[evt:36226|Ölie–Ane War]] (3822–3832) against the kingdom of [[pol:1498|Ane]] for land in [[reg:13|Iiyyi]]. Ölie lost 155,563 men to Ane's 52,638 and won: thirteen districts, a town, 51,922 kilograms of silver, and Ane as a vassal.

In 3875 a poet of the household spoke a "Panegyric on the Bright Deer" (PAL·PO·00040) before Sitlyitlathe's throne. Readers of the previous chapter will remember "Bright Smoke", the satire nailed to a door in the same city in 3712. Set side by side, the two poems are a lesson in how the past reaches us. The satire survives as a copy made before it was torn down; the panegyric survives as an original in the university library at Ölye. Praise is kept. Mockery survives by accident. Of the emperor's wars the panegyrist says: "Let no one ask the cost of that war. A king counts the end of a road, not its stones." Of his table: "Who has seen the emperor drunk? No one." Of his treasury: its coins "go to the granaries and the roads, and the poor of the capital eat in the lean months from his barns." The barns were needed. Grain at Ölye rose from 6.6 grams of silver in the 3850s to 14.8 in the 3890s, the last decade of his reign.

## The fall of the first Yidhe (AY 3780–3883)

Yidhe was larger still. Under [[per:35369|Tumaanin]] the Great (3804–3829) and [[per:35795|Yuthzhifpunin]] the Wise (3829–3867) it reached 505 districts, and by 3870 it held 17 per cent of all the land in the world. In 3880 it numbered 37.2 million people. Three years later it was gone.

Yuthzhifpunin's own "Testimony" (PAL·SP·00012), written in 3866 to be read in the churches, shows us the turn. In 3862, he says, "the province of Lininadhe, which had eaten of my bread, lifted its heel against me", and there began "the war of brothers". Three times the rebels came; three times they were beaten; the rebel chief bent the knee, and "I did not hang him. Let it be written that I did not hang him." The record confirms the victories and adds what the old king left out. The rebels came with 37, 34 and 37 men. The royal hosts that met them numbered 200,482, 303,134 and 153,165, and lost 58,865 men between them. No handful of rebels killed those soldiers. In the same year, 3862, famine struck Nivi and killed 196,711 people, one in seven, and armies of two and three hundred thousand marching through a hungry country lose their men to hunger and fever, not to the enemy. Nor does the Testimony mention the other event of 3862: the migration of the Jego into Yidhe, 1,247,354 people moving across 66 districts over the next eleven years. Yuthzhifpunin calls himself one of "the people of Jego". Of the Jego on the roads he says nothing.

The record lists exactly three causes for the [[evt:36859|Collapse of the Yidhe State]] in 3883: the secession, the famine and the migration, all of 3862. By 3880 its measure of stability had fallen to nothing, as Dagafu's and Tomzel's had before their falls. The empire split six ways. The heir that held Taulin kept the name, a new [[pol:1562|Yidhe]] that would last until 4387; another heir was the [[pol:1565|Binge]] empire, which would last until 4245. The two would spend much of the next chapter at war. A third, the kingdom of Linath, had a short and bitter career: Miohitl made holy war on it, and after the Treaty of Lininadhe in 3898 its queen gave young members of her court as hostages. The eldest wrote home from the old tower at Tlue (PAL·LT·01794): "We were afraid on the road, and the little one cried ... The bread is whiter than ours and the wine is thinner."

Yidhe's was the greatest fall of the age, not the only one. [[pol:1373|Ishuiithie]], beaten by Tukulven in 3748 and split by the [[evt:35474|Secession of Tsiinakhithi]] in 3752, fell in 3760 under its last queen [[per:34436|Amanilyian]]; its heirs included a revived [[pol:1513|Tomözel]] and the merchant republic of [[pol:1514|Ameyithi]]. The [[evt:36331|Collapse of the Shudhunki State]] in 3835 followed a lost war with [[pol:1467|Ngzhinakhzi]]; the king [[per:35662|Nayif]] had set up a diorite stele claiming 26,100 enemy dead at Aguga in 3818 (PAL·IN·02755), where the record counts 12,608. Out of Shudhunki came the [[pol:1540|Fièvi]] empire, which by the next chapter would be one of the great powers.

Below the giants, the middle powers kept long reigns. The kingdom of [[pol:1464|Dagafu]], heir of the old empire, was ruled for sixty-one years by [[per:34593|Nakumbèn III]] the Old (3727–3788) and later by the queen [[per:35942|Navavun]] (3841–3876); it grew to 22.8 million people by 3990. Imshi's queen [[per:35018|Imamnim]] the Victorious reigned from 3755 to 3800. Lienunushal was held for more than a century by [[per:35129|Mienuth]] (3769–3808), [[per:35440|Duepavukhen I]] (3808–3845) and the queen [[per:35981|Nakdamagunath]] the Old (3847–3887), the last two ruling the kingdom of Rieney as well. Long reigns were not peaceful ones. The record counts 22 civil wars and 59 usurpations in these 250 years, among them the [[evt:36241|Secession of Yyitl]] in 3825, when Oi Ash claimed the throne of Yisamashtsi. That empire was itself new: the old Yisamashtsi had adopted [[mov:96|Setlietlpovi]] in 3761 ([[evt:35671|Conversion of Yisamashtsi to Setlietlpovi]]) and broken apart in 3774, leaving an heir of the same name at [[set:31|Bakh]].

## The counting of the dead

The wars of a fuller world were fought on a larger scale and recorded with no more honesty. Victory steles were still cut and set up, and still lied by multiples. Yuipabi of [[pol:1523|Yisamashtsi]] claimed 70,800 enemy dead at Eol in 3882 (PAL·IN·02806); the record gives 16,063. His successor Tsimabi claimed 68,300 at the same place four years later (PAL·IN·02809), against 13,469. [[per:36957|Omyi]] the Usurper, after beating Imshi at Yyixi in 3949, had a basalt stele cut claiming 579,700 dead (PAL·IN·02865). The record gives 79,377. I have handled such stones in the field. They are heavy, beautiful and almost always wrong by a factor of four to seven. Chronicles were little better: the Khibna account of the Second Ishuiithie Civil War, written in 3755 (PAL·CH·06265), reports a retreat that "cost us 7 men, and them 20,842".

The kingdom of [[pol:1398|Imshi]] shows what victory cost. Under [[per:36620|Shiilu II]] (3902–3951) it fought the [[evt:37476|Imshi–Kunnga War]] (3928–3938), lost 220,226 men to Kunnga's 54,928, and won 42 districts and three towns. Then it lost the [[evt:37706|Imshi–Yisamashtsi War]] and paid 226,189 kilograms of silver at the Treaty of Nisshudi in 3951. It grew all the same, to 26.8 million people in 3980.

The greatest war of the period was the [[evt:37802|14th Tuvizhinge Trade War]] (3954–3968), fought by the new Yidhe against the kingdom of [[pol:1430|Lienunushal]] over the trade of [[reg:9|Tuvizhinge]]. Yidhe lost 292,032 men, Lienunushal 148,989. By the Treaty of Zhinontunu (PAL·TR·02418) Yidhe paid 14,852,063 zhiflun, which at 12.6 grams a coin comes to 187,136 kilograms of silver, and opened its markets to Lienunushal's merchants free of tolls. Yidhe's official account, written in 3973 (PAL·CH·06731), tells a different war. At Rin in 3961, it says, "131,947 of them lay dead on the field, and of ours only 4,099"; the record gives 45,021 and 8,472. It passes over the defeat at Zhinontunu in 3959, where Yidhe lost 49,755 men. And it ends: "The honour of the realm was preserved."

Ölie paid in men too. In the [[evt:38125|Binge–Ölie War]] (3988–3996) it lost 232,391 men to Binge's 112,285; its queen Ie Toe the Brave was killed at Zhükhi in 3989, and the throne passed to a girl of four and then to a usurper, Ipil Nyime. His stele for Kadhdanul in 3996 (PAL·IN·02900) claims 169,600 enemy dead. The record gives 27,328. Ölie won the war and 111,780 kilograms of silver, but in 3990 its stability stood near nothing.

## Faiths of plenty

By 3870 Veshethmekh, the faith of the One, held 21 per cent of the land, ahead of Uthungala at 16 per cent, Mie'to'ieshio at 13 and [[mov:69|Kievi]] at 9. A new religion appeared in 3776, the [[evt:35815|Founding of the Ngzhimbu Faith]] by [[per:35319|Nazhige]] at Khambive, in Kambive, one of the kingdoms born of Dagafu's collapse. [[mov:134|Ngzhimbu]] never became a great faith, but it never died either.

The quarrels within the established faiths were quarrels about wealth. In 3787 [[per:35408|Loraamadhin]] broke with the elders of Kievi in the city of Binge in [[evt:35887|the Tuthtu Schism]], founding the [[mov:135|Tuthtu]] movement. His own record of the debate (PAL·CH·08446) has him refuse to teach as certain that time turns on a wheel: "When the house is burning, the wise man does not dispute whether the fire began in the kitchen or the stable. He carries out the children." In 3916 the priest Iminimin led the Shamu schism from Veshethmekh at Shiüzhi in Imshi, and told the elders that riches are a sign of the One's favour: "The granaries of Imshi are full" (PAL·CH·08447). In 3985 Nikhzhin led the Tutpothtu schism from Kievi, again at Binge, and his "Defence" (PAL·SC·00131) accuses the elders of building "a new house with a stone floor" with silver that flowed in after a sacked city's fall. One preacher said wealth was a blessing, another that it was a snare; none could ignore it.

Plague, though rarer, still came. [[evt:36187|The Bloody Ague]] broke out at Ishwyithie in 3821 and killed about 1.2 million people, one in six where it reached. In 3927 [[evt:37372|the Harbour Death]] broke out in the harbour quarter of Chizhnambi and killed about the same number. Its gravedigger kept a book (PAL·CH·08489): "I have dug forty graves this month where I dig four." When the yard was full the council gave him the brothers' orchard outside the wall, and afterwards the brothers set up "a post of oak with no names on it, for there were too many names." The last leaf records the levy taken twice from the parish for a new war: "The levy is not given back."

## The hungry close

Even the mild centuries had dry spells, such as [[evt:36982|the great drought in Daadüdhih]] from 3890 to 3906, but the period ended worse than any of them. In 3996 [[evt:38135|a great drought]] began in [[reg:1|Yizamashti]] over 333 districts, and that year famine struck Iith, the heartland of Imshi, and killed 2,128,280 people, one in seven. In 3997 Mount Khakhngan erupted. Grain at Imshi cost 28.9 grams of silver a measure in the 3990s and 29.7 at Chizhnambi, more than three times the base and the dearest bread either city saw in the chapter. At Taulin it cost 6.3.

Beneath all this, languages kept dividing. Khunimbe split from Khundangji, itself only three centuries old, about 3899; Shienshe and Maf followed about 3999. In Tasemem, the s that had become h about 3554 now vanished altogether, when the language lost its h about 3940. We have 894 documents from the period, 420 of them inscriptions. The stones speak loudest, and, as we have seen, they lie by multiples.

By 4000 Ölie was the largest state in the world and inwardly spent. Yidhe and Binge, heirs of the same fall, faced each other across Diebeguvebenga. Imshi was hungry. The next chapter, an age of usurpers and hunger, begins with them.
