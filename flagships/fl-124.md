**Third day out.** The steamer keeps a good eleven knots. Some four hundred of us in the steerage, mostly families from the mill quarters of [[set:116|Imshi]], and a dozen clerks of the Colonial Board in the saloon above. The proclamation of Her Majesty [[per:46184|Sha Faya]] is pasted by the companion-way, and the children have it by heart: *that the Kingdom of [[pol:1953|Imshi]], having shown its strength in arms, shall now show it in the plough.* My neighbour in the next berth was at the [[evt:47289|Battle of Imshi]] as a boy, and at the [[evt:47290|Eleventh Siege of Shiüzhi]] after it, and says that if the nation could swallow Iith it can digest a coast with no master on it. He drinks too much, but he is not wrong about the coast. The land of [[reg:0|Nukmüzhi]] has had no lord these nine years.

**Ninth day out.** Heat. The sea itself seems to sweat. The ship's engineer, who reads the scientific papers, says the summers have grown hotter year on year for eighty years and more, ever since the mills first darkened the sky, and that the professors have a name for it, the [[cli:251|Industrial Warming]]. I said it was a strange thing to be proud of. He said he was not proud of it, only that it was measured.

**Day of landing.** The coast is brown where the charts say green. We came ashore by lighter and stood bareheaded while the Board's officer read the instrument of the [[evt:47500|Founding of the Colony of Sasi]], and the flag went up on a pole cut from the only tall tree in sight. The chaplain of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] spoke on the wheel that turns, and on moderation, and on our duty to carry the truth to all peoples. Then the surveyors laid out the streets with string, and that was the [[evt:47501|Founding of Sasi]]: string, a flag, and four hundred people sweating in their best clothes.

**Twentieth day at Sasi.** No rain. The wells the Board promised are dry or brackish. The people of the country come down to the lines in the evening, thin as rakes, and stand without asking. Our own sacks are counted out by the clerks to the ounce.

**Thirty-first day.** The country people say the rains have come short for several years, and this year not at all. Their fields are dust. It is [[evt:47454|the famine in Nukmüzhi]], and we have come into the middle of it with our string and our flag. Today the chaplain gave half his ration at the line, and said the wise withdraw from appetite. The Board's officer wrote him up for it.

**Fortieth day.** The returning steamer carries our message, to be wired to Imshi from the first cable station she touches: SITUATION GRAVE STOP NATIVE POPULATION STARVING STOP COLONY RATIONS FOURTEEN DAYS STOP REQUEST GRAIN SHIPS. At home, the newspapers say, grain is under an [[cur:1097|if]] the measure. Here there is no price, because there is no grain.

**Fifty-eighth day.** Reply from the Board, brought by a coasting schooner: grain ships to be sent "as tonnage permits." Tonnage, it seems, is wanted for the garrison at the conquered towns of [[evt:47291|Iith]]. My neighbour has stopped talking about digestion.

**Sixty-fourth day.** Buried two of ours today, an old man and a baby, and could not count the others' dead. The chaplain says the wheel turns; what ends will begin again. I asked him whether he meant the rain. He said he meant everything.

**26 Shi 2521.** A smudge of smoke on the horizon at noon. The whole colony on the beach. The clerks, who will not be hurried in anything, have already got out their ledgers. If it is grain, I will write it here tomorrow. If it is not, I will write that too. The engineer says the [[evt:46452|Onset of the Industrial Warming]] was before any of us was born, and that we are only its latest passengers. I do not know about that. I know that the smoke is getting nearer.
