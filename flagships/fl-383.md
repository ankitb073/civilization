*[Tablet 1. The king's letter.]*

Thus says [[per:20110|Khushni]], king of [[pol:867|Mane]], lord of [[set:13|Tsivintuha]], in the 40th year of his reign, to the household of the old priest of the [[mov:2|Tlindu]] in the hill-village above the river:

The tablet your father wrote in the house of the guard I send to you. My scribe copied it; a king must know what is said in his prisons. The words are your father's own; I have not changed one of them. Read them, and then read what I say after.

*[Tablet 2. The enclosed letter, in a rougher hand.]*

> To my wife, to my sons, to my daughter who is with child: may the gods of our fathers, each in their place, keep you.
>
> I am well in my body. They give me bread and water. The guard is a young man from the plain; he does not strike me.
>
> The servants of the king came to the shrine on the day of the new moon. They said: "The king has given the word. The rite of the Tlindu is ended in Mane. Bow to the one power of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]], and go home." I said: "I have poured for the gods of this valley for forty years, and my father before me, and his father before him. Shall I tell the gods: I am tired of you?" They took the bowls. They broke the altar-stone. They brought me here.
>
> Do not try to buy me out. Do not sell the field. Do not go before the king. Keep the field, and keep the bowls if any were saved, and pour at the new moon in the house where no one sees.
>
> They say tomorrow I will be taken out. I am not afraid. What ends will begin again. The wheel turns, and I will turn with it. Kiss the child for me when it comes. If it is a son, do not give him my name; it will be a hard name to carry. If it is a daughter, tell her that her grandfather did not bow.

*[Tablet 3. The king's letter, continued.]*

Now hear the king.

You will say that the king is cruel. Hear first how things stood. In the [[evt:20674|Yide–Mane War]] the men of Yide came down upon us with a great host. At the [[evt:20681|Battle of Shiyithtinnge]] our host was broken and its commander slain. At the [[evt:20687|Battle of Yuftuy]] in the next year we were broken again, and the omens were black.

In this year I went out myself to Yuftuy. Before the battle I bowed to the one power alone, and not to the shadows. Their host was twice ours. Yet at the [[evt:20700|Battle of Yuftuy]] the men of Yide fell like barley before the sickle, two thousand and more of them, and our losses were not a quarter of theirs. The priests said to me: "See, the light has given the victory. But the land is divided; half of it still pours to the shadows. A house divided cannot stand." Then I gave the word, and the [[evt:20727|Persecution of the Jinru in Mane]] began in the land of Mana.

Your father was a stubborn man. My officers told me so; his own letter tells me so. I sent to him three times. Three times I said: bow, and go home. Three times he would not bow. A king who gives a word and then takes it back for one old man is not a king. I could not take it back.

Yet the one power teaches compassion and release from suffering, and I have not forgotten it. Your father was not beaten. He was given bread. He was given his own clothes to die in. He was buried, not thrown out; my men marked the place, and the messenger will show it to you.

With this tablet I send thirty [[cur:454|Ihal]] of silver, for the widow and for the child that is coming. It is not a price; nothing is the price of a man.

Keep your field. No man of mine will take it. But do not pour at the new moon, not even in the house where no one sees. The servants of [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] see more than your father thought.

The wheel turns. May it turn gently for your house.
