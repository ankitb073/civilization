**Book the First, in which I say what I was**

To you who turn the wheel and are not turned by it, I confess; and to you, reader, whoever you are, of whatever people, I confess also, since the One has no favourites among nations and I should be ashamed to have any.

I was born in [[pol:953|Vami]], in the reign of a king whose name I need not write, of a family that kept the old altars with exactness and some pride. As a boy I knew the hours of every god of the [[mov:1|Yacuam]]: when to pour to Nili at the river, when to feed [[dei:7|Yüp]] her portion of the fire, when to lift my hands to [[dei:8|Nixil]] of the sky. I did these things well. I was praised for doing them well. I thought that being praised was the same as being good, which is the first error of the young and the last error of the old.

**Book the Second, in which I say what I saw**

In the first year of our present king [[per:23717|Mimaiw]] the province of Nistloti broke from the realm, and a claimant set himself up there, and men who had eaten bread at one table took up spears against one another. The clerks call this the [[evt:24236|Secession of Nistloti]], as if a province could be cut away like a cloak's hem without blood.

I was at Lithi. I will not pretend I was a hero there; I carried water. At the [[evt:24274|Battle of Lithi]], the second fought upon that ground, as at the [[evt:24259|Battle of Lithi]] the year before, the field was ours, and the priests poured thanks to [[dei:11|Nizhaxta]], lady of the spear, and I poured with them. That night I went down among the wounded of both sides with my water-skin. A man of Nistloti, dying, asked me which god I had prayed to before the battle. I told him. He laughed, though it hurt him, and said: "So did I."

I could not sleep after that. If both of us had prayed to the same lady of the spear, and she had given the field to one, then either she was unjust, or she was not the one who gave it. I could not believe her unjust; I had loved her too long. So I began, very slowly and with great reluctance, to believe the second thing.

**Book the Third, in which I say what I found**

I will not weary you with the years. I will say only that I learned to sit still; that in sitting still I found, not a new god, but a light that had been in me all along and that no priest had lit; and that by this light the old gods appeared, not as liars, but as servants and shadows of a single Power, as a lamp's flame throws many shadows on the wall but is itself one.

I found also that this Power asks no blood. It asks compassion. It asks that we release one another from suffering, beginning with the suffering we cause. It turns the wheel, and all that ends begins again; but a wheel may turn toward mercy as easily as toward slaughter.

Last year, at [[set:195|Tizha]], seven people sat with me under an olive tree and asked me to teach them. I said I had nothing to teach that they could not find in themselves. They said: then teach us how to look. That evening is what my friends now call the [[evt:24321|Founding of the Ninxisatl Faith]]. I did not found it. I only stopped standing in its way.

**Book the Fourth, which is a prayer**

This year the king's arms have finished their work in Nistloti; the [[evt:24311|Suppression of the Nistloti Revolt]] is proclaimed in every market. I do not rejoice and I do not mourn. I pray for the king, and for the claimant, and for the water-carriers of both sides.

To every people, the [[mov:85|Ninxisatl]] say: look within. The light that showed me the dying man's face will show you yours.

*Written at Tizha, in the year 204 of Vami.*
