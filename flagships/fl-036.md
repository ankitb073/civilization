**A HYMN OF THANKSGIVING**
*for the Victory granted to the Arms of [[pol:1655|Ölie]] at Unushti in the Year 3825 of the Myitiyishiu; to be sung in the Temples of the Realm upon its Anniversary. With a Preface to the Reader.*

**To the Reader.**

It will be asked, and I have heard it asked in the copying-rooms of [[set:264|Ölie]], why a hymn of victory should be copied out in the second year after the peace sworn at [[set:194|Tlalaazhash]], while the indemnity owed to [[pol:1521|Ayud]] is still being counted out in good [[cur:917|nyinan]], and the merchants of Ayud walk our markets by right of treaty. The objection is a fair one. I shall answer it in three points, and the reader may then sing or not, as his conscience directs.

*First*, that the victory was real. In the year 3825, at the [[evt:41707|Battle of Unushti]], the army under [[per:41321|Ie Ivie]], numbering by the muster-rolls somewhat above eighty thousand, met [[per:41299|Mashi Ash]] with a host not much smaller, and left of the enemy near twenty-eight thousand upon the field, our own dead being less than a fifth of theirs. These figures are not a poet's; they are the clerks'. No later misfortune unwrites them.

*Second*, that the victory had a cause, and the cause is instructive. Let the reader attend to the chronology, for in it lies the whole argument. In the year 3812 the realm, at the royal command, put aside the worship of its fathers, the [[evt:41622|conversion of Ölie to Ndingilathul]]. In the year 3824 His Majesty [[per:41061|Isyi Athe]], with a wisdom that does him the greater honour for being a correction of himself, restored the old worship of the [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]]. In the year 3825 came Unushti. I do not say the gods are merchants who pay for sacrifices by the weight. I say only that the order of these years is a fact, and that a learned man may draw from it what a pious man already knows.

*Third*, that the later fortunes of the [[evt:41693|Ölie–Ayshi War]] do not abolish the earlier. That the war for [[reg:10|Myizhitl]] afterwards turned against us I do not deny: at the [[evt:41730|Battle of Zhaalakhazhash]] under [[per:41197|Sitlyitlfoyatl]], at Unushti a second time, at Khash, and at [[evt:41758|Zhaalakhazhash]] once more, until the [[evt:41759|Treaty of Zhaalakhazhash]] bound us to twenty years of peace. Some have said that we went to war in appetite, because Ayud lay weakened by the [[evt:41625|famine in Ayshi]] and seemed an easy harvest. If so, the faith we had restored rebuked us as it rebukes every excess: it asks moderation in all things, in victory and in grief alike. A people that forgets its victories in its defeats is twice defeated. Therefore let Unushti be sung, and let it be sung soberly.

**THE HYMN**

Praise to the many gods who share the world,  
each in his place, each given his due;  
praise to the order kept by the ordained,  
the old truth handed down, and handed true.  

Twelve years we wandered from our fathers' road,  
and the altars of the realm stood strange and cold;  
the king turned back, and the fires were lit again,  
and the gods remembered Ölie as of old.  

At Unushti the morning rose on steel,  
and Ie Ivie set the ranks as a mason lays a wall;  
the host of Ayud came down like rain on the plain,  
and like the rain it fell, and ran away, and that was all.  

Not by our arm, though our arm was strong;  
not by our numbers, though our numbers stood;  
but by the order of the gods restored  
was the field of Unushti watered with their blood.  

And if the years that followed were not kind,  
let no man curse the gods nor curse the king;  
the wise withdraw from appetite and pride,  
and of a just day, justly, they may sing.  

Praise to the many gods who share the world;  
keep the realm in measure, keep the king;  
and when the twenty years of peace are done,  
find us still faithful, and still remembering.  

*Copied at Ölie, the 7th day of Nizö, in the year 3831 of the Myitiyishiu.*
