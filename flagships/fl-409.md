*[Obverse. The tablet is ruled into strophes; a refrain sign is written in the margin beside each.]*

Weep, O [[dei:73|Tidu]], lord of the storm, let your rain be tears.  
Weep, O [[dei:77|Pugazhu]], lord of the sun, let your face be hidden.  
Weep, O [[dei:75|Bumbi]], lady of the sea, let your waves beat their breasts.  
Weep for the king. Weep for [[per:3|Nangatsu]], the first who sat upon the seat.

*Ai, the king. Ai, the shepherd of [[pol:3|Tagath]].*

He was the strong bull of the city.  
He was the high wall of the city.  
He was the deep well of the city, and the city drank.  
When he went out, the men went out behind him.  
When he sat down, the elders sat down before him.  
When he spoke, the elders said: "It is so."

*Ai, the king. Ai, the shepherd of Tagath.*

He set the stones of the seat in [[set:9|Tagath]].  
He set the stones of the house of [[dei:76|Tsilmu]], lord of wisdom.  
He set the boundary stones in the barley,  
and gave each field its place, and each man his due.  
In his days the barley of [[dei:74|Binge]] stood thick to the knee.  
In his days the boats came in full from the sea.  
Sixteen years he sat upon the seat. Sixteen harvests he gathered.

*Ai, the king. Ai, the shepherd of Tagath.*

Then the king lay down. The king did not rise.  
His hand was cold on the staff; his eyes were shut to the sun.  
The women cut their hair. The men tore their cloaks.  
They carried him to the house of the dead, to the house where no lamp burns.  
They laid the bronze beside him, the cup beside him, the bread beside him.  
They shut the door. The door does not open.

*Ai, the king. Ai, the shepherd of Tagath.*

And his son was a child. His son was ten years old.  
Who will hold the staff? Who will sit upon the seat?  
Then [[per:23|Natange]] stood up, the wise woman, the steadfast one.  
She held the staff for the child; she kept the seat for the child.  
Six years she held it; six harvests she gathered.  
Then she lay down also, and went to the house where no lamp burns.  
Weep for Natange, who held the staff. Weep for her a little, with the king.

*Ai, the keeper of the staff. Ai, the shepherd of Tagath.*

*[Reverse.]*

Now the child is a man. Now the son is the king.  
[[per:24|Nangatsu]] sits upon the seat of his father, Nangatsu the son,  
nineteen years he has sat upon it, nineteen harvests he has gathered.  
He has the face of his father. He has the voice of his father.  
Every year he goes down to the house of the dead,  
every year he pours the beer at the shut door,  
every year the singers sing this song for the father.

All things turn on the great wheel, say the priests of [[mov:11|Kyif]];  
what ends will begin again.  
The father ended; in the son he begins again.  
The father lay down; in the son he stands up.  
Let the singer say it twice: in the son he stands up.

Weep, O Tidu. Weep, O Pugazhu. Weep, O Bumbi.  
And then, O gods, be glad, for the seat is not empty.

*Ai, the king. Ai, the shepherd of Tagath.*

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Sung at the shut door in the 19th year of Nangatsu, king of Tagath. Whoever breaks this tablet, may Tidu break his roof and Bumbi drown his boat.
