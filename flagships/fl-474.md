**THE PRESIDENT SPEAKS TO THE PEOPLE**

*FULL TEXT OF THE ADDRESS DELIVERED FROM THE STEPS OF THE OLD PALACE YESTERDAY AFTERNOON, AS TAKEN DOWN BY OUR SHORTHAND REPORTER. 14 CHIDU 155 OF THE DUTHLADUL.*

*The President, received with mingled cheers and hissing, spoke without notes to a crowd that filled the square and the adjoining streets.*

---

Citizens of [[pol:1915|Kankush]]!

A little more than a year ago you stood in this square, many of you on this same pavement, and you said to the man who lived behind me in that palace: enough. You did not ask. You told him. And [[per:45471|Nanagu]], who called himself your master, came down those steps a private man. That was the [[evt:46344|Kankush Revolution]]. That was your work, not mine. I was only the man you sent up the steps afterwards to sweep the floor. (*Cheers.*)

I have come today to tell you what I found on that floor, and what I have done with it, and I will not lie to you. A republic that lies to its citizens is only a monarchy with more paperwork. (*Laughter. A voice: "Tell us about the treaty!"*) I am coming to the treaty, friend. Hear me first.

The republic did not make this war. When Khishkhis marched on us in the [[evt:46339|Third Khishkhis–Kankush War]], it was that man who answered from that balcony, before a single one of you had a vote; it was his ministers who had sold the muskets and pocketed the price of the boots. And so, in the year of the revolution, twenty-three thousand of our sons went out to meet three hundred thousand at the [[evt:46350|Battle of Jyijinjivji]], and seven thousand did not come home. (*Cries of "Shame!"*) Shame, yes. Shame on the throne that left them so poorly armed.

The republic inherited that war as a man inherits a debt. And I will tell you plainly, because you have read it already in the papers: the republic did no better. At the [[evt:46360|Battle of Ndulamlol]] this spring our army was beaten again, and more than five thousand more were lost. I signed the orders for that battle. I carry it. (*Silence.*)

Then they came to the hills above this city. You heard the guns; some of you lost your roofs. For the fifth time in its history this city was besieged. The [[evt:46361|Fifth Siege of Khankhush]] was not a story in a paper. It was the bread queue in the Chandlers' Street. It was grain at a price no labourer can pay.

So I went out to them, under a white flag, and I signed the [[evt:46362|Treaty of Khankhush]]. (*Uproar. Some minutes passed before the President could continue.*)

Let me tell you what is in it, every line, because you will hear worse from the reaction. We give up the frontier districts where the two battles were fought. We pay an indemnity, in [[cur:1082|Yivi]]. We accept that in matters of war and foreign policy we shall walk behind Khishkhis, as a vassal walks behind a lord. And there is to be twenty years' peace.

Twenty years' peace! (*A voice: "Peace on our knees!"*) On our knees, friend, but alive. On our feet, in that cemetery? I chose knees. I would choose them again.

And who will pay the indemnity? Not you. I give you my word in this square. The crown lands are being sold. The jewels in that palace are being counted. The great houses that grew fat on the old king's contracts will pay their share, and then a second share. The teachers of [[mov:162|Duthladul]] have taught us for generations that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. Very well: the republic will help the rich to be wise. (*Prolonged cheering.*)

Our faith teaches also that no one owns the truth. Not the priest. Not the king. Not the president. If you think I have done wrong, you have the vote now; use it.

The wheel turns, citizens. What ends will begin again. The throne has ended. The war has ended. Let the republic begin; let it begin with duty, with order, and with bread.

Long live [[set:97|Kankush]]! Long live the republic!

(*The President withdrew into the palace amid cheers. Mounted police cleared the lower end of the square without serious incident.*)

— **[[per:45620|Nalish]]**, President
