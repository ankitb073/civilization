*[Obverse.]*

In the 14th year of [[per:12456|Nanjijim]], king in [[set:49|Adhgumgi]], lord of [[pol:549|Khanji]], I set down the words of my grandmother. She sat by the door in the sun. She said them. I pressed them into the clay. Let [[dei:28|Khudhaze]] of the high sky be witness. Let [[dei:31|Khumbe]] of the night be witness. I have added nothing. I have taken nothing away.

Thus said my grandmother:

"In the days of my strength, when my children were at my knee, there was a great house over all the land. Then the great house fell. This was the [[evt:12226|Collapse of the Yif State]]. Every lord went to his own hill. Our lord went to his hill, and the hill was Khanji. This was the [[evt:12230|Rise of Khanji]]. We said: good. A near lord is better than a far lord.

"Then the river was sick. The water was sweet, and men drank it, and men died. A man was well in the morning; in the evening he was cold. This was the [[evt:12263|River Death]]. Seven summers and more it stayed. My husband's brother died of it. My eldest son died of it. I do not say more of that.

"When the River Death went away, we said: now the gods are satisfied. We gave the bull to [[dei:30|Pugazhu]] of the sun. We gave the fish to [[dei:29|Bumbe]] of the sea. We said: now there will be quiet.

"Then the hungry people came.

"They came over the hills from [[reg:25|Nginakhzi]]. The barley had not come up in their land. This was the [[evt:12417|Famine in Ng'jinaguh]]. Their old men died on the road. Their children had faces like old men. They carried pots with nothing in them. They carried their gods wrapped in cloth.

"These were the [[cul:50|Khukhudhul]]. Their speech was not our speech. Their bread was not our bread. But their hunger was our hunger. I knew it. I had known it in the years of the River Death.

"The first ones came to our village in the spring. My husband said: shut the gate. I said: the gate is not shut against Pugazhu's light; it is not shut against the hungry. I gave a woman of them barley. I gave her child milk. She wept on my hands. I did not know her words, but I knew her weeping.

"They came that year, and the next year, and the year after. Eight summers they came. They came like the water comes in the season of flood: a little, and a little, and then the fields are covered. The scribes of the king say a hundred thousand came into [[reg:26|Khanji]]. I did not count them. Who counts the drops of the flood?

"There was quarrelling. A man of ours and a man of theirs fought over a field by the river. A man of ours was killed. A man of theirs was killed. The elders sat at the gate. The king was a child; the great men of Adhgumgi spoke for him. They said: let the hungry people take the waste land, the land of the stones, and plough it. Let them pay the tax in [[cur:290|Kavu]] like us. Let them pour out to our gods at the festival. So it was done.

"Now the land of the stones is green. Now their sons and our daughters go down to the river together. Now I have a granddaughter-in-law of the Khukhudhul. She grinds my barley. She grinds it well."

Thus said my grandmother.

*[Reverse.]*

So I, her grandson, set it down. This is the [[evt:12418|Migration of the Khukhudhul into Khanji]], as my grandmother saw it with her eyes. The priests of [[mov:4|Khupande]] say: all things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again. The great house fell; Khanji rose. The river was sick; the river is well. The hungry came; the hungry have eaten.

Khudhaze, keep the rain over Khanji. [[dei:32|Nizhinande]], lady of the spear, keep the spear asleep. Pugazhu, ripen the barley of the land of the stones. Let no one break this tablet.
