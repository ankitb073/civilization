*A New Ballad of the Long Road, made by one of the [[cul:21|Khueniwmo]] now living in [[reg:15|Ban]], upon the [[evt:42169|Migration of the Kunelmu into Baan]]; to be sung to the tune of "The Ferryman's Daughter." Copied at [[set:100|Chivil]], 11 Lashli 2021 of the Cyivi. Price one-quarter [[cur:846|Il]].*

**1.**  
Come all you good people of Ban, and attend,  
You that pass us at market and do not say *friend*;  
You have heard us called beggars, and worse, at the inn:  
Now hear how we came here, and where we have been.  

**2.**  
In [[reg:7|Duina]] my father had four fields of rye,  
And a roof of good thatch, and a well never dry;  
But the rain went away and the frost came in spring,  
And the year of the [[evt:42168|Famine in Tunnge]] took everything.  

**3.**  
We ate the seed-corn, and we ate the plough-ox,  
We ate nettle and dock, and the moss from the rocks;  
My mother lay down in the cold by the door,  
And my father said, *Children, we stay here no more.*  

**4.**  
We were not the first, and we were not the last:  
On the road out of Duina were thousands that passed,  
With barrows and bundles, the old on our backs,  
Eight hundred thousand of us, and more, by some tracts.  

**5.**  
The tollmen at bridges took half of our bread,  
The farmers set dogs on us, so it was said;  
We slept under hedges, we slept in the rain,  
And we buried my brother at the edge of the plain.  

**6.**  
Seven years on the road (I was twelve when we set),  
Seven years, and some never came to its end;  
Some turned aside, and some drowned at the fords,  
And some sold their daughters to soldiers and lords.  

**7.**  
At last in the province they gave us a waste,  
A marsh no man wanted, and bade us make haste;  
We dug it and drained it with spade and with hand,  
And now it bears barley. Now ask whose is the land!  

**8.**  
For the tax-man comes quarter-days, wanting it in coin,  
And grain is near three Il the measure to buy in,  
And the priest of the [[mov:69|Cyivi]] says, *All things are one wheel;*  
*What goes down will come up.* Well, we've gone down, I feel.  

**9.**  
They say the good King [[per:41701|Omafmamshil]] will hear  
The petitions of strangers one day in the year;  
I have written mine fairly, and signed it, and sealed:  
*Let the Khueniwmo keep the marsh they made a field.*  

**10.**  
I am promised to a girl of the country of Ban,  
Her father calls me *the foreigner* still, when he can;  
But my small sister speaks your tongue just like you,  
And she knows not one word of Duina's songs. It is true.  

**11.**  
So when you pass us at market, and hear our hard speech,  
Remember that hunger is a hard thing to teach;  
We came not for your silver, we came not for war,  
We came as all creatures come: for bread, and a door.  

*Finis.*
