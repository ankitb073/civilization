**[[per:22838|Ihi]], king of [[pol:987|Mane]], to the first among the priests of the [[mov:57|I'ishiu]] in [[set:13|Tsivintuha]], greeting and peace. In the year 502 of the I'ishiu.**

You asked me, reverend father, to send you the letter which the old man of the [[mov:3|Dziyithtsi]] wrote to me in the days before his sentence was carried out, that it might be kept among the records of this year. I send it, copied in my own secretary's hand from the leaf on which he wrote it. I have changed nothing, not even where he speaks hardly of me. A king who edits the last words of a dead man is a thief twice over.

Here is what he wrote.

> *To Ihi, who sits upon the throne of Mane, from one of the servants of the many gods, now in the king's prison.*
>
> *I am told that I may live if I will say before the priests that there is one power only, and that the gods of my fathers are its shadows. I thank the king for the offer. It is kindly meant, as a physician's knife is kindly meant. But I cannot take it.*
>
> *Your priests and mine agree that all things turn upon a great wheel, and that what ends will begin again. We part only here: you say the power is one and the gods are its shadows, and I say the gods are many, each with a place and a due. The king's priests teach that the soul may be released from suffering. Very well: let them release me. I do not fear the ending, for we are both taught that it is not an ending.*
>
> *I ask only two things. Let my wife keep the house; it was bought with my own [[cur:540|Makh]], honestly earned at the loom. And let my grandsons be taught their letters, whichever gods they come to serve. A man who reads may find his own way, whatever the king decrees.*
>
> *I hold no hatred against the king. He does what his priests tell him is right. I do what my fathers told me is right. One of us is mistaken. The wheel will show which.*

So he wrote, reverend father, and so he died, without cursing and without recanting.

Let me now say plainly what I think, since you have always asked me to speak to you as to a confessor.

I do not doubt the truth of our teaching. There is one divine power; the old gods are its servants or its shadows; and the life that leads to release from suffering is the life of compassion. Nor do I doubt that the kingdom is safer when its people pray with one voice. A divided altar makes a divided city, and a divided city is a gate left open.

Yet I confess that this letter has troubled my sleep. We preach compassion; and we have made, in this year's [[evt:23208|Persecution of the Retti in Mane]], a great many widows. We teach that the message is for all peoples, and that men must be won to it; and we have sought to win this man with a rope. I ask myself whether a truth that must be enforced by the magistrate is not, in some measure, doubting itself.

I do not say the decree was wrong. The council advised it, the priests approved it, and I put my seal to it with open eyes. I will not now pretend my hand was guided by another. But I have given orders that his wife keep the house, as he asked, and that his grandsons be sent to the temple school at the crown's expense. If they come to the true teaching, let it be because they read and understood, and not because they remember the gallows.

Pray for me, reverend father, as I pray for the kingdom. And pray, if our teaching permits it, for the old man also; for whatever he believed, he believed it bravely, and bravery is not so common in this world that a king can afford to despise it.

Given at Tsivintuha, under the small seal.
