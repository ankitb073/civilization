title: Memoir of a Spearman of Tonnga on the War with Pümi

I, a spearman of [[pol:403|Tonnga]], a servant of the king, a man of the river country, speak these words in the 140th year of Tonnga. My hair is white. My knees are weak. Let the scribe press the clay. Let [[dei:136|Duvemino]] the storm-lord hear, let Tuvel the moon hear, let Vinba the earth hear, that I speak what my eyes saw.

In the days of my father the men of [[pol:310|Pümi]] shamed Tonnga in the [[evt:10260|sixth war for the trade of Tunnge]]. My father came home without his shield. He said: the son will take back what the father lost.

In the 114th year of Tonnga the king [[per:10372|Numaenen]], great king, shepherd of the river people, said: the whole land of [[reg:7|Tonnga]] is ours, and Pümi sits in what is ours. So began the [[evt:10409|war of Tonnga and Pome]]. I took up the spear. I was young. I was glad.

In the 115th year we went down to the [[evt:10446|field of Anavun]]. [[per:10667|Lokhtalen]], general of Pümi, came out against us. His men were many, many as reeds in the marsh. Three men of Pümi stood for every man of Tonnga. We fell like barley under the sickle. The king drew back. I carried my brother's son on my shoulders to the camp.

In the 116th year [[per:10565|Neyavun]], the king's general, the lion of the ford, led us by night to [[evt:10458|Diviminnge]]. Lokhtalen came out again. But the storm-lord was with us. Of us there fell not a thousand. Of them there fell thousands upon thousands, five thousand and more; the ditches were full of them; the dogs were fat. That was a good day. That was a day for Tonnga.

In the 118th year Pümi beat us by Rubon. I will not speak of it.

In the 119th year the king Numaenen went out himself to [[evt:10492|Nuwkennga]]. The men of Pümi ran. They threw down their spears; they threw down their shields; four thousand and more of them lay on the field, and of us not four hundred. Then we sat down before [[set:17|Pümi]], the city of their kings, at the [[evt:10493|siege of Himi]]. We cut their palm trees. We burned their threshing floors. But the walls were high, and Naebithen the river fed them, and we did not go in.

Then the gods turned their faces. Hear it, sons of Tonnga. In the 121st year we were broken at Tsinlampiva. In the 122nd year we were broken at Duveminnga. In the 123rd year we were broken at Noltsennga. In the 124th year we were broken at Tsinlampiva again. Why were we broken? Not by the spears of Pümi. Duvemino sent rain upon rain; the barley rotted in the ear. And behind us brother rose against brother, house against house, in the [[evt:10527|strife of the brothers of Tonnga]]. A man cannot fight with his face to the enemy and his back to his kin.

In the 124th year the peace was sworn at Tsinlampiva, the [[evt:10549|peace of Zhinlampive]], before [[per:10521|Ralokhnon]], king of Pümi. Tonnga gave silver to Pümi, [[cur:213|Tsetku]] upon Tsetku, and swore to lay down the spear for ten years. In that year the king Numaenen went down to [[dei:137|Shuyedartsi]], lord of the house below. Neyavun went down after him; he fell by Noltsennga in the strife of brothers, with his face to the foe.

For ten years the brothers strove. The old house fell. In the 133rd year Tonnga rose again, and the king sits in [[set:60|Virnatho]], and the land is quiet.

Hear me, young men. Of Pümi there fell half again as many as of Tonnga. Count the dead and say who was strong. Pour beer to Vinba. Pour water to Naebithen. Give bread to Nithikhti the grain-mother. Keep the storm-lord sweet. Do not quarrel, brother with brother. Then Tonnga will not be broken.

So speaks a spearman of Tonnga. The scribe has written it.
