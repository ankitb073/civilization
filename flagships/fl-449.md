*Printed by authority in the official gazette and supplied by wire to the principal newspapers of the kingdom.*

**TO THE DELEGATES OF THE ASSEMBLIES OF MUBIL, LATELY THE GUESTS OF THIS CITY**

Gentlemen and ladies of the delegation,

I write to you as one who sat among you, and not only as the sovereign in whose capital you were received. When the elders of the faith resolved that a general council should meet in [[set:6|Zhinontunu]], I asked to attend its sessions in the character of a delegate of the faithful of this kingdom, and my ministers, after some debate, consented. I sat on the benches of the lower hall with the delegates of the provincial assemblies. I heard every speech. I therefore write with some knowledge of what passed, and with a full share of responsibility for how it ended.

It ended badly. There is no use in the official phrases. The [[evt:46450|Council of Zhinontunu]] was summoned to keep one body of believers in one fellowship, and it has closed with your benches empty and your delegation on the night train to the frontier. The newspapers of both our countries have already printed their accounts. Some of ours have printed things I am ashamed to have read over my breakfast. I ask you not to take them for the voice of this kingdom.

Permit me, nevertheless, to say plainly what I believe the council did, since I believe it has been misreported to you as well as to us.

The assembly adopted five articles. The first declares that the teaching of the [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] is one, and that those who divide it are cut off. The second, which you found most bitter, provides that the elders of each city shall be appointed by the elders of the chief city and by no other. The third fixes the books to be read in the assemblies as those read in the time of the founder. The fourth requires a full year of instruction before any person is received. The fifth, which was carried without a single voice against it, provides that the poor shall be fed at every assembly.

I do not pretend that I voted against any of them. I did not. Our faith holds that truth is kept by an ordained priesthood and passed down in order, and I could not see how order is kept if every city appoints its own. But I heard your objection, and I understood it better than the reporters allowed. You come from a republic. [[pol:1674|Mubil]] has no king and wishes no chief city to stand over its assemblies as a king would stand over a province. You feared that the second article would make your elders the clerks of [[pol:1786|Zhinontunu]]. That fear was not foolish, and it was not answered as it deserved to be.

Nor was it answered by your departure. When your senior delegate rose and led you out of the hall, the vote on the fifth article was still to be taken. You left before the assembly bound itself to feed the hungry. I do not think that is how you wished to be remembered.

I make, therefore, three proposals, which I have laid before my ministers and which I now make public so that no one may say they were offered in a corner.

First, that the Crown will bear the entire cost of a second sitting, including your passage by rail in both directions and your lodging in the capital; the Treasury estimates the sum at some thousands of [[cur:1057|lodhu]], and I count it cheap.

Second, that the manner in which the second article is to be applied in your country be referred to a commission of six, three of your choosing and three of ours.

Third, that until the commission reports, no elder of Mubil shall be declared cut off by anyone in this kingdom, from the pulpit or in the press.

The faith teaches that time runs in a line toward a final judgement, and that the good life is one of compassion and release from suffering. We shall not be judged for the wording of an article. We may well be judged for the quarrel.

I remain, with respect and in hope of your reply,

[[per:45677|Takhpaadhiyath]], Queen

*Zhinontunu, 23 Pivu 2551 of the Vishithmikh*
