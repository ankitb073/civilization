Among the documents of [[per:33088|Nifpulin]]'s house, the one I would save from a fire is not a monument. It is a clay muster roll drawn up in AY 3536, in his mother's sixth year, for an army "under the command of Natlomin", the general who would win the first battle of Nifpulin's own reign. It lists eight towns, the arm each sent and the number of men: shield-bearers from Tallin and Shivange, horsemen from Nilu, Putnimdu and Virathkanu, spearmen from Binge and Rurin. Its total is 28,686. That figure agrees exactly with the record of the battle that followed. Its last line is a clerk's note to himself: "The horses to be counted again at the river." No such roll survives from Nifpulin's own wars. His victory stelae, by contrast, inflate the enemy dead by anything from two to seven times. Between the roll and the stelae lies the reign of the man later called the Great. It was a long reign, prosperous and orderly, and it rested on numbers like those on the roll.

## Grandson of the founder

Nifpulin was born in 3560 into the Jego house that had made the [[pol:1405|Yidhe]] empire. His grandfather Nuftafbafin had founded both house and state in 3526 and died five years later. The throne then passed to Nifpulin's mother, [[per:32745|Shathnadhuth]], a girl of twelve, who reigned for thirty-three years. His father appears in the record without a name. Shathnadhuth died in 3564, aged forty-five, and the [[evt:33482|Accession of Nifpulin]] made her four-year-old son emperor of some nine million people, ruled from Taulin, with [[per:33089|Nushen]] as regent.

The regency opened the first war of the reign. The [[evt:33484|Second Yidhe–Punikyithdiy War]] ended in 3568 with the [[evt:33521|Conquest of Punikyithdiy]], signed at Ontiedzey after a siege. Twenty-six districts were annexed, at the cost of fewer than a thousand Yidhe dead. The defeated king, [[per:32817|Uruthun]], was executed that year. Nifpulin was eight.

## Khash

His own first war came at seventeen. The [[evt:33602|Yidhe–Khash War]] of 3577–3580 was fought against the kingdom of Khash and its young queen, [[per:33195|Nitllo Maf]]. Nifpulin commanded in its last two battles. At the [[evt:33625|Battle of Khungumnu (AY 3579)]] he beat an army less than half the size of his own. At the [[evt:33643|Battle of Navnge (AY 3580)]] the armies were nearly matched, some 34,000 against 30,000, and he broke the enemy. Khash lost nearly fifteen thousand men and Yidhe about 2,500. Of all his own battles it was the most evenly matched, and the victory that owed most to the field and least to the muster.

It was also the most exaggerated, at least in sheer numbers. Two stelae stand at Navnge. One credits him with 15,700 dead in a battle of 3578 that a general fought. The other claims 35,800 for his own victory of 3580. The Yidhe account of the war, written the following year, raises the second figure to 58,185, more than the 47,378 men it says the enemy brought, and puts Yidhe's losses at 518. The record gives about 2,500. The [[evt:33645|Treaty of Khash (AY 3580)]] took fifteen districts and the town of Khungumnu. His son and heir, [[per:33623|Tunufmalin]], was born the same year.

## Lienevkan

For fourteen years Yidhe fought no battle that the record notes. A holy war against Ini'i, declared in 3586, dissolved within a year. In 3594 Nifpulin's younger son died at five. That year the [[evt:33754|Yidhe–Bidadothip War]] began, against a kingdom ruled by [[per:33317|Thathiga]], a queen of twenty who would reign for nearly half a century and be remembered as the Old. No muster roll survives from this war, but the record counts the army: some 56,000 men went to the [[evt:33785|Battle of Lienevkan (AY 3596)]] against fewer than four thousand. They won, and lost 141 of their number to the enemy's 732. A second battle there the next year went the same way. The [[evt:33816|Treaty of Lienevkan (AY 3599)]] took eleven districts. The war had cost Yidhe 933 dead against some 2,400 of the enemy. A stele for the last battle at Lienevkan, in 3599, fought by one of his generals, claims 3,900 enemy dead. The record gives about 530.

## Last wars, and the end

The pattern broke in the next war. In the [[evt:33896|First Yidhe–Vieneynga War]], fought against the young [[pol:1429|Vieneynga]] empire, Nifpulin led more than 48,000 men in 3609 against some 32,600 under [[per:33479|Vavmanen]] at the [[evt:33933|Battle of Tinlamvukhnir (AY 3609)]]. He won, but the enemy lost about six thousand men and Yidhe more than seven thousand. The Yidhe account of the war swells the enemy to 77,023 and says Yidhe lost 4,152. The record gives 7,239. That gap, of some three thousand men, is the measure of what a court chronicle could leave out. The stele for the battle, bolder than the chronicle's 20,158, claims 41,900 dead. The [[evt:33963|Treaty of Tinlamvukhnir (AY 3610)]], granted "by Nifpulin, emperor of Yidhe" to [[per:33424|Natuluekhnen]], later called the Unfortunate, took eleven districts and two towns. Like many treaties of its age, it asked to be "read aloud in the markets of both lands". His youngest daughter died in 3608, aged nine.

The [[evt:34033|Yidhe–Zhienru War]] of 3617–3621 was his last. It ended with the [[evt:34093|Conquest of Zhienru]] after a siege of Vimginge and the annexation of thirty districts. Zhienru's queen, [[per:33415|Dashath]], who had ruled since she was twenty-one, was executed at forty-two. Nifpulin's reign thus began and ended with the execution of a defeated ruler. The first came when he was still a child. Of the second the record gives no detail.

Nifpulin died a natural death in 3623, aged sixty-three. In that year began a plague the chronicles call the Black Fever, which would kill nearly three million people across the region, and whether it touched the old emperor the record does not say. The [[evt:34101|Accession of Tunufmalin]] passed the throne quietly to his son of forty-three, who reigned until 3647. His widow, born in the same year as he, outlived him by nine years. The house went on to rule Yidhe until its fall in 3883.

## What "Great" measured

The record supports the epithet in its own terms. Under Nifpulin the empire grew from some 110 districts to 211, and from nine to nearly thirteen million people. By the record's own measure its order stayed high throughout, and the succession passed without a crisis. The cost is also on record. His five wars killed about 23,000 soldiers of Yidhe and 66,000 of its enemies, and two defeated rulers were put to death. The clerk who counted the horses at the river kept the truest kind of record such a reign could leave. It was a reign run by counting, and by spending what was counted.
