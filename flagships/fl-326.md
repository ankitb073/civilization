*[Obverse. The beginning is lost.]*

[…] and I was young, and my beard had not come. My father gave me a spear of bronze. My mother gave me bread and onions in a cloth. I went down with the spears of [[pol:26|Duvekopuvinga]], to the road of the traders, to the low land of [[reg:8|Duvekopuvinga]], which the men of [[pol:7|Buneynga]] said was theirs.

Hear why we went down. The men of Buneynga sat on the road. They took the toll of the donkeys. They took the toll of the boats. They took a tenth of the copper and a tenth of the wool. The merchants of [[set:17|Pumi]] cried out at the gate. So the spears went down to [[set:62|Bimunga]], where the roads meet. I will tell what I saw. Let the young men hear it.

At [[evt:789|Bimnge]] the first time, [[per:951|Jilshinlamath]] the lady of the spear led us, and against her stood [[per:1035|Tuyettungatu]] of Buneynga, a woman hard as a whetstone. Their spears were more than ours. The day was long. The field was Buneynga's. I carried my uncle's body out of the barley.

At [[evt:797|Bimnge]] the second time, Jilshinlamath led us again, and against her stood [[per:1039|Natuvanan]], the grey one. Our spears were more than theirs. We broke them. We drove them into the river. The field was ours. But Jilshinlamath lay down on that field and did not rise. She won the field and lay down on it. The women of Pumi cut their hair for her.

Then [[per:1082|Kulvalomin]] took the spear. Kulvalomin the stubborn, Kulvalomin who does not sleep. Nine fields there were in that war, and on six of them Kulvalomin stood.

At Bimnge the third time the field was Buneynga's, and our dead were more than theirs. At Bimnge the fourth time the field was Buneynga's; that was a bitter day, the bitterest, and I do not speak of it.

At [[evt:812|Bimnge]] the fifth time, [[per:1079|Tepimtuth]] the wise led us against Natuvanan. Natuvanan's men lay down like cut reeds, three of theirs for each of ours and more. The field was ours.

At Bimnge the sixth time the field was Buneynga's. Tuyettungatu again. Always Tuyettungatu.

At [[evt:819|Shiniy]], by the houses of Sheney, Kulvalomin stood against Tuyettungatu, and [[dei:14|Vulme]] went before us. More than eight hundred of Buneynga lay down in the mud. Of ours, few. I took a bronze helm from a dead man there; I have it still.

At [[evt:822|Bimnge]] the eighth time, Kulvalomin stood against [[per:1090|Pabukutibun]]. Their spears were more than ours; it did not help them. The field was ours.

At Bimnge the ninth time, the field was Buneynga's.

*[Reverse.]*

Ten summers we went down. Nine times we stood at the meeting of the roads. Five times the field was theirs; four times the field was ours. And our dead and their dead, if you lay them side by side, are near the same in number, though theirs are somewhat the more. [[dei:15|Vinba]] of the black earth drank from both cups alike.

Then Tepimtuth went down to Bimunga, and Tuyettungatu came out to her. The two women sat down under one awning. They swore by the gods of the sky and the gods of the earth. That is the oath of [[evt:831|Bimnge]]. No one took the low land. No one gave up the low land. The spears were laid down, and I came home, and my beard had come.

I say this: we were not beaten. Let no singer of Buneynga say that we were beaten. The wheel turned, and turned back, as the priests say it turns.

Now the spears go out again. Last year Kulvalomin stood at Bimnge once more, and the field was ours. This year, in the first year of [[per:1097|Panshelon]] our king, the men of Yida come against us; another war is at the door. My eldest son takes up the spear. I give him my helm from Shiniy. I give him bread and onions in a cloth.

May Vulme go before him. May Vinba not drink him. May the wheel bring him home.
