**The year of the late rains. Day 20.** I began these leaves because my sister said I should set things down instead of saying them to her at midnight. So: the rains are late. Not very late. Old Nadu, who reads the sky from the ridge, says the clouds stand "low and unwilling." That is what he always says.

**Day 49.** Still nothing. The terraces of [[reg:29|Mbambanluli]] have gone from green to the colour of a lion's back. I teach the older children their letters on the threshing floor. Today one asked whether the brown is bad. I said it depends. It does not depend.

**Day 70.** We sowed anyway, like everyone. If you wait for certainty you sow nothing. Seed barley cost a third more than last year at the lowland market: a measure of seed for a measure and a third of wool. The trader said it was the roads, not the sky. I do not think he believes that either.

**Day 91.** The first rain. The space of a meal, then sun. The children ran out into it. By evening the ground was hard again.

**Day 119.** The spring below the shrine runs at half. The elders have set a rota at the well: our end of the village draws water four days in seven. People have cleared out old cisterns. My neighbour found one under his storeroom floor that nobody remembered.

**Day 140.** Travellers from the south say the whole basin is brown, worse than anyone's grandmother can remember. A drover at the well called it [[evt:41825|the Great Drought in Yimla]], carefully, as though trying the words on. By morning everyone was saying it. It is strange how a name makes a thing more real. Before, it was a bad year. Now it has a name, and names do not leave quickly.

**Day 161.** Harvest, such as it is. Our terrace gave perhaps one sack in five of a good year. My sister's husband did not bother to cut his. He says the reaping would eat more bread than it brings.

**Day 182.** Barley costs twice what it did at the turn of the year. The goats are being sold. The market is full of goats and nobody is buying, so a goat brings half what it brought and barley costs twice. You can do that sum in your head.

**Day 217.** Three children have stopped coming to the threshing floor. One family has gone down to the lowland towns to look for work. I do not know about the other two. The elders have opened the common granary and give a bowl of porridge at midday to the children who come to their letters. The next day more came. I am not proud of how glad that made me.

**Day 238.** The elders met under the great tree and named it what it is: [[evt:41829|the famine]]. They counted the households that have nothing left. They did not count the dead. I do not think anyone knows the number. I know of two, both old, both up in the hills.

**Day 252.** Grain came up from the lowlands on mules, bought with the shrine's silver and the elders' own. Barley, oil, a little salt. The line at the granary door was as long as the afternoon, and quiet. Nobody shouted. That is the thing I want to remember about this year if I remember nothing else: nobody shouted.

**Day 273.** Old Nadu went up the ridge to read the sky for next year. He came down and said nothing. I did not ask.

**Day 287.** My sister asked what I mean to do with these leaves. I said I do not know. Perhaps someone reads them in a year when it rains, and thinks: so that is how it was in [[reg:29|Mbambanluli]] in the year of [[evt:41829|the famine]]. I hope it rains. That is all. I hope it rains.
