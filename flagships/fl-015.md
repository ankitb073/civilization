title: Hymn for the Victory at Unzhinlathu

**I.**

Not to the old gods first, who are but servants at the gate,  
but to the One, whose light the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] keeps,  
we lift this song; for nothing stands except by that light,  
and nothing falls except when its measure is fulfilled.  
Sing, citizens of [[set:5|Nilu]], but sing with tempered voices:  
the just man boasts not of himself, but of the order that upholds him.  

**II.**

Long was the debt, and our fathers' fathers carried it,  
since in the [[evt:15560|Third Luthe–Nilu War]] the men of Luthe came  
and took from us the fields that were not theirs to take.  
Three hundred years the wrong lay written in the book of heaven,  
and heaven, which forgets nothing and hurries nothing, waited for its hour.  

**III.**

Then [[per:18227|Navave]] was given the crown before he could speak,  
and in his name the counsellors of [[pol:686|Nilu]] weighed the cause  
and found it just, and sent the army into [[reg:27|Luthe]].  
Say not that a child made war. The law made war through him,  
and the law is older than any king, and wiser than any counsellor.  
So began the [[evt:18351|Nilu–Luthe War]], not from greed but from the scales.  

**IV.**

Hear also the hard years; a hymn that hides its sorrows is a lie.  
At Khanindu, at [[evt:18407|Yave]], at [[evt:18412|Kavu]] by the water,  
[[per:18114|Nashinjè]] our general was thrust back from the field,  
and [[per:18282|Naviyu]] of Luthe stood upon the hills and praised himself.  
Why does the One permit the just to stumble?  
That they may learn the measure; that pride be burned out of them  
as the refiner burns the dross from silver, and the silver is not lessened.  

**V.**

Then Nashinjè came again to Khanindu, chastened and patient,  
and at [[evt:18437|Khanindu]] the field that had been their boast became their grave.  
[[per:18336|Nangaju]] of Luthe fled before him, and fled again at Khadambe.  
And at [[evt:18465|Unzhinlathu]] Naviyu, who had laughed at Kavu,  
laughed no longer, but went down from the hills with few behind him.  

**VI.**

Once more the scale dipped against us at Khadambe,  
where [[per:18375|Navthu]] of Luthe held the ground and our men gave way.  
For the One will not let a people grow drunk upon its fortune;  
a single bitter cup was mixed for us, and we drank it, and were sober.  

**VII.**

Then [[per:18389|Naniji]] took the command, a man of few words and exact orders,  
and twice before the walls of Unzhinlathu he met the queen of Luthe,  
[[per:18330|Nabathu]], who came out in arms to guard that city.  
Twice she went back from the field; and in this year, at the [[evt:18505|second meeting]],  
her men were scattered, and she asked for terms.  

**VIII.**

So in Unzhinlathu the queen set her name beside the name of our king,  
and the [[evt:18506|Treaty of Unzhinlathu]] gave into the hands of Nilu the fields of Luthe,  
which our fathers lost and our sons have won again.  
Of theirs, five-and-seventy thousand lie in the earth of that country;  
of ours, fewer, and every one of them is named before the altar.  
Weep for them, and do not weep too long: they paid the debt in full.  

**IX.**

Now, citizens, let no one of Nilu grow insolent in victory.  
The One who gives can also take, and takes most swiftly from the proud.  
Deal justly with the new fields; let the [[cur:359|fiyi]] weigh true in their markets;  
let the judge of Luthe and the judge of Nilu read from the same law.  
Prosperity is the sign of favour: keep the favour by keeping the measure.  
This is the victory that does not rot: a just peace, honestly kept.  

*Sung at Nilu, in the year 2180 of the Uthungala.*
