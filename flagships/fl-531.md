*In the Name of the One Power, who sets the road of time from the first day to the Judgement, and weighs all dealings in the balance.*

[[per:39465|Nakhizhi]], by the grace of the One King of [[pol:1672|Fuvu]], second of that name, to the lords spiritual and temporal of our realm, to the elders of the faith of [[mov:24|Uthungala]], to the barons who hold of us by service of horse, and to the masters of the guilds of our city of [[set:8|Khambive]]: greeting, and the peace which, by the favour of Heaven and the valour of our vassals, we have won.

You asked of us, at the late assembly, a full report of the negotiations at Bige, since it was not our custom, nor that of our royal fathers, to go in our own person to treat with an enemy. We send it now, in the year 5029 from the founding of Khambive, that it may be read in council and laid in the treasury of charters.

**Of the causes.** Let none say that Fuvu went to war for greed. For years the lords of [[pol:1675|Bige]] closed their roads and their river-crossings to our merchants, levied tolls no just man would levy, and seized the goods of our subjects as though the Lord had given them the river for their private purse. Is it not written that the false balance is an abomination? We bore it long. At last we bore it no longer, and so began the [[evt:40088|Eighth Fuvu Trade War]].

**Of the war.** We need not rehearse at length what all of you witnessed. At the [[evt:40108|Battle of Khambe]] we ourselves led the host, and the enemy broke before our banner and fled leaving the field heaped with his dead. At the [[evt:40148|Battle of Bige]], under our captains, we drove him again within his walls, and the city fell to our arms; and if the records of that city were burned in the confusion, let the blame fall on those who chose to defend a lost wall rather than open a gate. At the second [[evt:40161|Battle of Bige]], their lord [[per:39625|Navivi]] came out in person with all the strength left to him, and the slaughter of his people was so great that the heralds gave up the counting of it. More than ten thousand of the men of Bige fell in that war, by the reckoning of our clerks, and we think the true number far greater; of ours, God took fewer than two in three of theirs.

**Of the negotiations.** When Navivi sent to sue for peace, our counsellors would have had us send an envoy. We judged otherwise. A lord who has bled his vassals for a cause should look his enemy in the face when the cause is settled. We rode therefore to [[set:271|Bige]], with forty knights and the elders of the faith, and lodged in the city by right of conquest.

Navivi received us in the ruined hall. He was grave and courteous, and spoke more humbly than his fathers had taught him. He asked first that the truce be long; we granted fifteen years. He asked next that his city be spared further burden; we answered that it had been spared enough, having been twice entered by our arms. He then offered the river-tolls abated by half. We answered: not by half, but entire. Our merchants shall pass his roads and markets freely. At this he was silent a long while, and then consented.

Lastly we required the cession of the lands and the town that had been the seat of his raiders, which lie along the road our merchants take. He wept, it is reported, though not before us. These terms, being three, were written in the [[evt:40163|Treaty of Bige]], and sealed by us and by Navivi, before the elders of both peoples, upon the Book.

**Of the fruits.** The roads are open. Grain stands in Khambive at less than one and a half [[cur:925|Guvu]] the measure. Prosperity, as our teachers say, is a sign of divine favour, and we have it.

Given under our seal at Khambive. Let it be read in every hall that owes us service.
