**To the Governor of the District, at his hall in the town.**

**Petition of the undersigned households of the upper villages, concerning the want of food.**

Lord Governor,

We write concerning what the drovers on the road have begun to call the [[evt:38136|Famine in Nuekhmokh]], as it falls on our village. We write to you yourself because your steward has not answered our three messages, and the clerk at the granary tells us that our village is "not yet on the roll". We do not know what roll. We know that we are hungry.

**1. The harvest.** The spring sowing came up thin because the rains were late, and the second sowing was burned off by the hot wind in its sixth week. Of the forty-one households who sign this, not one has brought in more than a third of an ordinary crop. Most have brought in nothing worth threshing. The beans are finished. The common store-house of the village has had no meal in its bins for nineteen days.

**2. Prices.** What can be bought is bought from the dealers who come up the road with their own mules. A sack of meal that cost a day and a half of wages at the New Year now costs five days of wages, where there are wages. Oil is sold by the cup. The dealers say the price is set in the town; in the town they say it is set by the dealers. We ask your office to find out which is true.

**3. The children.** The noon porridge at the school was stopped at the end of last term for want of grain. The midwife has weighed the small children twice this month on the miller's steelyard. Two infants have died here since the sowing, and she has told the mothers it was fever. The mothers say it was hunger, and we believe the mothers.

**4. Those who have left.** Eleven young men have gone to look for work in the town and on the estates. None has sent money yet. Three families have shut their houses and gone to kin elsewhere in [[reg:0|Nukhumukh]], although the drovers say the whole country is short, and we do not know where they think they are going.

**5. Rumours.** People are saying that grain from the district granary has been loaded onto carts at night and sent where the price is higher. We do not say this is true. We say people are saying it, and that it would help if your herald said in the market that it is false.

**We therefore ask:**

(a) that this village be written on the roll for relief bread at once, and that the bread be brought here and not only to the town, since the toll at the bridge is now worth more than a ration;

(b) that the district granary be opened to our common store at the old price;

(c) that collection of the land levy and of the mill dues be put off until the next harvest;

(d) that the water-carts come up twice a week, as they did in the last dry year;

(e) that seed be given out before the rains, so that no family is driven to eat its seed.

We are not asking for alms. Most of us paid the levy last year in full. We ask that the [[evt:38136|Famine in Nuekhmokh]] be treated by your office as what it is in our village, and not only as what it is in the tallies of your clerks. Hunger in [[reg:0|Nukhumukh]] does not wait for the roll.

This petition was read aloud on the threshing floor to all households before it was signed. Those who cannot write have made their mark, and the scribe who wrote these words for us has witnessed each mark.

*Signed and marked by forty-one heads of household.*
