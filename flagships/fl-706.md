**To the Governor of the District, at the District Offices.**

**Petition of the undersigned households of the upper villages, concerning the shortage of food.**

Respected Governor,

We write concerning what the radio has begun to call the [[evt:38136|Famine in Nuekhmokh]], as it falls on our village. We write to you directly because the agricultural officer has not answered our three letters, and the relief office tells us that our village is "not yet on the list". We do not know what list. We know that we are hungry.

**1. The harvest.** The spring planting came up thin because the rains were late, and the second planting was burned off by the hot wind in its sixth week. Of the forty-one households who sign this, not one has brought in more than a third of an ordinary crop. Most have brought in nothing worth threshing. The beans are finished. The cooperative store has had no meal on its shelves for nineteen days.

**2. Prices.** What can be bought is bought from the traders who come up the road in their own lorries. A sack of meal that cost a day and a half of wages at the New Year now costs five days of wages, where there are wages. Cooking oil is sold by the cup. The traders say the price is set in town; in town they say it is set by the traders. We ask your office to find out which is true.

**3. The children.** The school canteen closed at the end of last term for want of supplies. The nurse at the clinic has weighed the small children twice this month and written the weights in her book. Two infants of this village have died since the planting, and she has told the mothers it was fever. The mothers say it was hunger, and we believe the mothers.

**4. Those who have left.** Eleven young men have gone to look for work in the town and on the estates. None has sent money yet. Three families have shut their houses and gone to relatives elsewhere in [[reg:0|Nukhumukh]], although the radio says the whole province is short, and we do not know where they think they are going.

**5. Rumours.** People are saying that grain from the district store has been loaded onto trains at night and sent where the price is higher. We do not say this is true. We say people are saying it, and that it would help if your office said in public that it is false.

**We therefore ask:**

(a) that this village be entered on the register for relief rations at once, and that rations be delivered here and not only in town, since the bus fare is now worth more than a ration;

(b) that the district grain reserve be opened to the cooperative store at the controlled price;

(c) that collection of the land levy and of the electricity arrears be suspended until the next harvest;

(d) that the water tanker come twice a week, as it did in the last dry year;

(e) that seed be issued before the rains, so that no family is driven to eat its seed.

We are not asking for charity. Most of us paid the levy last year in full. We ask that the [[evt:38136|Famine in Nuekhmokh]] be treated by your office as what it is in our village, and not only as what it is in the newspapers. Hunger in [[reg:0|Nukhumukh]] does not wait for the list.

This petition was read aloud in the meeting hall to all households before signing. Those who cannot write have made their mark, and the secretary of the village committee has witnessed each mark.

*Signed by forty-one heads of household.*
