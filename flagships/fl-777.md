*The lament of the villages below [[set:183|Khadhanga]], in the year 4094 from the founding of Lienonoshal.*

Hear, Duemamnol of the sky, who sees the high and the low,  
hear the villages of Khadhanga, that were a city's and are a lord's.  
Seven and twenty harvests we have reaped for strangers;  
hear us, for the stewards do not.  

Our fathers were the men of [[pol:1210|Khadhanga]], free men of a free city,  
who owed their tithe to their own temple and their service to their own walls.  
Then the hosts of [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]] came down like the locust on the vine,  
seven years in the field, and the field was ours no more.  

At the [[evt:29282|battle of Khaadhange]] our fathers stood before the gate,  
four thousand against a host no man could count, and they did not run.  
Then our lord [[per:28514|Benerelen]] went out to sign before the emperor,  
before [[per:28212|Vievmagonen]] in his tent of purple;  
he set his seal to the [[evt:29284|Conquest of Kadanga]],  
and they put him to death, though he had signed. This our fathers saw.  

Since that year we are vassals of vassals.  
The knight holds of the count, the count holds of the emperor,  
and we hold of no one; we are held.  
At the feast of [[dei:17|Niedhekhte]], at the harvest,  
the steward comes with his rolls, and his clerk, and his scales,  
and takes the tenth for the temple, and the third for the lord,  
and the levy-grain for the emperor's wars.  

Grain at the market is cheap, two-thirds of a [[cur:625|Mava]] the measure;  
cheap for the steward, who sells it; cheap for the man in the city.  
For us who grow it, it is cheap as our sweat is cheap.  

Then the emperor's son, [[per:28987|Natdatathrelen]], whom they call the Conqueror,  
went up against Yida with a host greater than his father's.  
They took our sons for the march to Yaaril;  
two from the mill, three from the upper village, the widow's only boy.  
We heard of the [[evt:29570|Second Sack of Yaaril]], and the bells were rung for it,  
and we rang them, and we did not know which of our sons were living.  
We heard of the [[evt:29588|Conquest of Yida]], and the bells were rung again.  
Of eleven who went from our parish, six have come home.  

Is this the order of the world, as the priests teach,  
that the good life is duty, and the duty is to the lord?  
Then we have lived a very good life, and are very poor.  
Prosperity, they say, is the sign of the gods' favour;  
then the gods have favoured the steward, and looked away from us.  

Yet we keep the feasts. We bring the first sheaf to the altar.  
We light the fire to Eba at midwinter and give the cake to the moon.  
We do not curse the emperor; it is not permitted, and it is not wise.  
We only ask, with our faces to the ground, that the gods remember  

that Khadhanga was a city, and its villages were its own;  
that our fathers stood at the gate and did not run;  
that we have reaped seven and twenty harvests for strangers,  
and would like, before we die, to reap one for ourselves.
