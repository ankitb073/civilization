**Speech of the President of the Republic, Citizen [[per:45268|Mashe Mish]], to the People Assembled in the Great Square of [[set:114|Thimisitsiy]]**

*Taken down by our reporter and printed in full. 18 Lia 111 of the Ishyidikh.*

Citizens! (*Great cheering, which lasted some minutes.*)

A year ago you stood in this square without bread, without a voice, and without a law that any man in a palace was bound to respect. Today you stand in it as the masters of [[pol:1922|Yiutl]]. (*Cheers.*) I did not give you that. Nobody gave it to you. You took it, and you took it with less blood than any of the old rulers ever spent on a border fortress.

Let us remember how we came to this. Two years ago the young king, [[per:45253|Mashe Shyia]], third of that name, declared that he alone was the law. He put an end to the old councils. He put an end to the old charters. He said the crown answered to no one under heaven. (*Cries of "Shame!"*) You know what followed. Taxes rose at the city gates. Printers were fined for printing the truth. Grain went to the palace granaries while the bakers of the lower town shut their doors.

And then you rose. (*Tremendous cheering.*) The [[evt:45977|Yiutl Revolution]] was not the work of a faction or a plot. It was made by the porters at the river stairs, the women of the market, the journeymen of the print shops, the clerks of the joint-stock houses and the stokers at the new engine works. It was made by people who had decided that a nation is not the property of a family.

People ask me: where is the king? I tell them, as I tell you: he lives. (*Murmurs.*) He lives, and he will go on living, because this Republic does not need his blood to prove its right. Kings needed blood. The Republic needs only your consent. (*Prolonged applause.*)

Citizens, I will not flatter you with promises I cannot keep. I will give you figures instead. Grain stands today at about one and two-thirds [[cur:1075|Lial]] the measure. That is too dear, and it is still rising, and I will not pretend to you that a change of government can command the harvest. The public granaries are open under the eyes of elected inspectors. Every contract of the state is printed and posted for any citizen to read. The presses are free. (*Cheers.*)

I will say something now that some of you will not want to hear. Ten years ago the old crown sent our sons to take Faamni. You remember the [[evt:45880|Siege of Faamni]] and the [[evt:45886|Battle of Thushashfi]]. You remember the [[evt:45888|Conquest of Faamni]], which was celebrated in this square with fireworks. Ten thousand of our men did not come home from that war. Ten thousand, citizens, for a piece of land the king's ministers wanted on their maps. (*A voice: "Never again!"*) The Republic will keep Faamni, and its people will become citizens as you are. But the Republic will never again send its sons out to die so that a palace can boast of it.

The priests of the [[mov:161|Ishyidikh]] teach that there is one Power over all things, and that the good life is mercy and release from suffering. They teach that all things turn on the great wheel, and that what ends begins again. Then let the wheel that brought down the crown turn now toward mercy. Let the Republic be the first state of this land that measures its greatness by how few of its children go hungry. (*Great cheering.*)

So I ask you three things. Keep the peace in the streets, because disorder is the last hope of the old order. Pay your taxes honestly, now that you can see where every coin goes. And on election day, come out and vote, every one of you, so that no man in this city can ever again say that he alone is the law.

Long live the Republic! Long live the people of Yiutl!

(*The President left the platform amid scenes of the greatest enthusiasm. The crowd remained in the square singing until late in the evening.*)
