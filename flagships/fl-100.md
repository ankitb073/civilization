[[per:21786|O'e Shimen]], a servant of the one power, to those at [[set:36|Esuezholnogath]] who have begun to walk the way with me, and to any in [[pol:922|Ana]] who will read this with a quiet mind: greeting, and light.

You have asked me, now that I am in [[set:7|Ana]] and you are without me, what you should do, and whether what we began together this year, which some already call the [[evt:21978|founding of the Povi faith]], is a new thing or an old one. It is both, as a lamp is both: the oil is old and the flame is new. I will answer you in order, as reason asks.

First, why I began. Eight years ago, when I was a young man, I stood among those who carried the dead back from Fyinan. You remember that year. At the [[evt:21733|Battle of Fyinan]] our general fell, and more than ten thousand of our men with him, and at the [[evt:21734|Treaty of Fyinan]] we opened our markets to those who had beaten us, because we could not do otherwise. Before that there had been [[evt:21696|Foyitlithyi]], which we won, and [[evt:21716|Shithiy]], which we lost; and the priests had sacrificed before each, with the same words and the same oxen. I asked myself then: if the gods are many, and each has a place and a due, and each was paid the due, why did the dead lie as they lay? I did not find the answer in the temple. I found it, after long thought, in myself.

Second, what I teach. There is one divine power. The gods our fathers named are not nothing; they are its servants, or its shadows cast upon the world, as a lamp casts the shadow of a hand upon a wall. Honour them as servants, but do not mistake the hand for the shadow. And all things turn upon a great wheel: what ends will begin again. The dead of Fyinan are not lost; the wheel will bring them round. This is why I do not grieve as others grieve.

Third, how we come to know it. No priest ordained me, and I will ordain none of you. Each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason. When you meet, therefore, let no one sit higher than another. Let each say what he has understood, and let the others test it as a money-changer tests a coin, by weight and by ring.

Fourth, how we must live. Do not think that because we have no ordained priests we have no order. The good life is still the life of duty, order and right sacrifice. Keep your oaths. Pay your debts. Offer what is due, but offer it to the one power through its servants, and know to whom you offer. And withdraw from wealth and appetite. In this the teachers of [[mov:25|Mie'to'ieshio]] are right, and I do not quarrel with them. A man who counts his [[cur:498|shi'ma]] at night does not see the light that is in him. Grain is dear enough this year that no one among you should hoard it.

Fifth, to whom we speak. Our message is for all peoples, not for Ana alone. Speak it to the porter and the magistrate, to the merchant from abroad and to the very men who beat us at Fyinan, if they will sit with you. A truth that belongs to one people is a family custom, not a truth.

Sixth, concerning the king. Pray for [[per:21366|Yuesh'al]], who has sat upon the seat thirty years and is old. Obey him in all that is his. This year he has sent the young men out again, and the [[evt:21844|Seventh Ana–I'a'ie'i War]] has begun. I do not tell you to refuse the levy; duty is duty. I tell you to go without hatred and without appetite for plunder, and to bring back from that land, if you bring back anything, only the friendship of those you meet.

I will come to you when the roads are dry. Until then keep the lamps lit, and keep the way of the [[mov:73|Povi]] as I have given it to you, neither adding to it from fear nor taking from it from ease.

Written in Ana, in the year 2859 from the founding of the city.
