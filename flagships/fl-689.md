Of [[per:54798|Shush'esim'im]] the record keeps two dates, one office and two tablets. He was born in AY 883, a man of the Unsatlmum, and his name means *swift eagle*. From 903, when he was twenty, until his natural death in 948, at sixty-five, he was a priest of [[pol:167|Tivintusa]]. The record names no parent, wife, child or grave, and is silent on his childhood, looks and house. Both tablets came out of Nambili's trenches along the old city wall of [[set:13|Tivintukhe]] in AY 4909; the preliminary report (PAL·EX·00003) does not say where in the city either lay.

## Born into the drought

The record dates the Great Drought of Mikhukhizhi from AY 868, fifteen years before his birth, counts Tivintukhe within it, and ends it in 964, sixteen years after his death. His whole life fell inside it. At the end of the 880s the empire held 3,086,561 people in 169 districts; when the state ended in 933, the last survey counted 1,798,202 in 163.

His first year as a priest was the worst. The Famine of 903 in Mana killed 512,767 people, about a quarter of the province. An anonymous diarist of the capital (PAL·DI·00007) saw grain at "near four" law'e and wrote, "The priests read the liver"; the king's edict (PAL·DE·00053) capped the price at 2.6. Whether the young Shush'esim'im stood among those priests the record does not say. The [[evt:4826|Tivintusa–Tonnga War]] opened with a victory at Viririnu that year and ended in defeat in 910. From 907 his own people were leaving Tsiey for Mikhukhizhi in the lands of Khie'a; the record counts 224,964 on that road by 932. Four kings named Pa'ish reigned in his lifetime. The last, [[per:5257|Pa'ish IV]], came to the throne in 914 at fifteen, with a regent holding the seal.

## Three lines of clay

His first tablet (PAL·RE·00018), of the 12th year of Pa'ish, AY 925, was "kept at the temple of Sad", the sky god. Apart from its heading and signature it reads, in full:

> The herds were driven to the hills for water. Grain stood at 1.74 Law'e the measure. The canals stood dry and cracked.

The last sentence is a formula: in 868 Ma'she Nieme, a priest at Khie'a, closed his own record of dry years (PAL·RE·00015) with the same words. The middle sentence is not. The record's price of grain at Tivintukhe in the 920s is exactly 1.74 [[cur:101|Law'e]] the measure, dearer than the 1.27 of his birth decade but less than half the famine price. The tablet's title dates the dry years from 924, when the record begins a new drought in Baan; for Tivintukhe it was the latest turn of the old one. Two years later the king built a temple in the capital for Niw'i, goddess of rivers (PAL·IN·03626).

He wrote in a beaten capital. In the [[evt:4933|Second Yida–Tivintusa War]] the enemy had won at Tivintukhe itself in 919 and 920, and the Treaty of Tivintukhe gave Yida 37 districts and a tribute for twenty-two years. The same excavation yielded two steles of the Yida king Rülinpen I claiming 4,400 and 8,300 enemy dead at Tivintukhe (PAL·IN·00329, PAL·IN·00330); the record gives Tivintusa's losses as 1,651 and 1,397. Against that noise, a priest who set down the price of grain correctly deserves a hearing.

## The omens of the kings of former days

His second tablet (PAL·OM·00065), of 932, is the 27th of the liver-omen series "If the Sheep", "Written according to its original and checked by Shush'esim'im, the diviner", for Pa'ish, "great king of Tivintusa". Each of its 27 omens pairs a mark on a sheep's liver with an event of the past, the oldest from 825 and the latest from 920. Fourteen foretell ill.

This was a working reference: a king whose diviner found two Fingers on the liver needed to know what had followed before, and a flattering precedent was useless. So the tablet is franker than any stele. Of the famine in his first year as a priest it says "one in five of the people died", if anything too few. Of the reigning king's grandfather, Pa'ish I, it says "a servant will seize the throne of his lord". Pa'ish IV himself appears three times, always in an ill omen: as the child on his father's throne "while Nän held the seal", as the king whose army "Yid scattered near Yuftuy" in 917, and through his general's rout before the capital in 920. His victories in the [[evt:5006|Holy War of Bäni against Tivintusa]] are absent, though he had raised steles for Khuele and Hiviyu claiming 1,100 and 3,700 enemy dead (PAL·IN·00333, PAL·IN·00336), where the record counts 136 and 1,704. Perhaps the series admitted only settled history: the tablet Ya'tle copied for the king of Tiy in 922 (PAL·OM·00050) also stops short of its own day, by ten years to his twelve. It opens with the same words, "By the word of Sad, lord of the oracle," and scores two of the same battles, Nimid in 889 and Mienioshtse in 894, with the signs reversed.

## After the empire

The year after he checked that tablet, Tivintusa collapsed and split into three successor states. Tivintukhe was left without a state, and by 934 it was Yida's. The record nevertheless keeps Shush'esim'im as a priest of Tivintusa until 948, fifteen years after there was any Tivintusa to serve. Whether he stayed at the temple of Sad under Yida, followed a successor king, or simply grew old, it does not say; it holds no later tablet in his hand. Pa'ish IV died in 945, three years before him.

The [[mov:13|Onsatlmom]] faith he served taught that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. He lived sixty-five years with little of it, and wrote down what he saw: the herds going up to the hills, the true price of grain, the omens of a king's defeats presented to the king himself. It is a thin record, but not a dishonest one.
