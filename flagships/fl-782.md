title: A Farmer's Prayer for the Return of the Sun, from Yizamashti
---
Sun, eye of the gods, hear a farmer of Yizamashti, who has no temple but his threshing floor and no priest but himself.

Six winters ago, in a country we have never seen, the mountain broke open. We did not hear it. Men say it was the [[evt:21504|Eruption of Mount Punimongu]], and that its smoke went up into the house of the sky. We knew nothing of mountains. We knew only that in the summer after, you rose pale, like a coin of tin, and set red as a wound, and that the wheat stood in the field and did not fill.

Four summers you looked on us through a veil. The learned call those years the [[cli:128|Volcanic Winter of Mount Punimongu]]; we called them the grey years. There was frost in the month of the harvest. The vines blackened on the stake. We reaped straw. The price of a measure of grain rose past any honest reckoning, and the men who had grain grew fat, and the men who had none grew wise.

For the teachers of the [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]] tell us that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, and practise moderation in all things. In the grey years all Yizamashti was wise. We withdrew from appetite because appetite had withdrawn from us. We were moderate in bread because there was no bread. I do not mock the teachers. But wisdom forced on a man is a hard school, and its pupils do not all live to finish.

Then, two summers ago, you came back. I remember the morning. You came over the hill as you used to, gold and hot, and the wheat on my slope stood up and turned its face to you. My wife wept on the threshing floor. We reaped a true harvest. Now a measure of grain costs a little under one [[cur:480|Eshueya]], and a man can pay it and still keep salt in the house.

So I thank you, sun. I have hung a wreath of the first ears on my door-post for you, and I give you the first loaf, burned on the stone at dawn.

But hear the rest of my prayer, for it is not finished.

In the year you returned, our queen [[per:21220|Ola]] made our enemies pay at the [[evt:21639|Treaty of Kheye]], and we said, The gods have turned their faces back to [[pol:890|Tomözel]]. But the war with the Yennga did not end with the grey years. Last year the queen's host was beaten at the [[evt:21646|Battle of Nuvun]], and the queen's own city was taken, the [[evt:21647|Sack of Ishwyithie]], and the smoke went up from it as the smoke went up from the mountain. This year my wife's brother fell in the field of Viririnu, and the queen has set her seal to the [[evt:21654|Treaty of Ishwyithie]], and land that our fathers ploughed is given away.

The philosophers say that you do not care for men; that you rise over the just and the unjust alike, and over the victor and the vanquished. Perhaps. But a farmer must speak to someone at dawn, and you are the one who is there.

So return, sun. You have returned to the sky; return now to the city. Return to the hearths of the queen and of the poor. Return to the widow of my wife's brother, who sits in the dark at midday. Shine on the stones of the new boundary, so that the men on both sides can read them and be just. Let the grey go out of men's hearts as it went out of the fields.

Rise tomorrow, and the day after. That is all a farmer asks, and it is everything.
