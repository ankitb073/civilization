title: Lament for Tallen, Whose Daughters Went Out
---
*[Column I]*

Weep, O [[set:0|Tallen]], city of the great gate.  
Weep, O Tallen, mother of cities, whose daughters have left her house.  
Once all roads came to Tallen. Now the roads go away from Tallen.  
Weep, O Tallen. Weep, O house of the seat.

[[dei:16|Domamnol]], father of the high sky, you saw it.  
[[dei:15|Venba]], mother of the earth, you felt it, as a mother feels the child go out.  
[[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, you saw it by night, and you hid your face.  
[[dei:14|Volme]], lady of the spear, where was your spear?  
[[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire, the hearth of the empire went cold.

In the old days, in the days of the fathers of our fathers,  
[[pol:306|Yeda]] was one. Yeda was one house, one seat, one staff.  
The barley of the river-lands came to Tallen in boats.  
The silver of the hill-lands came to Tallen in sacks, weighed in the [[cur:164|Nevba]].  
The priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] stood in one order, and the gods were fed in one place.  
Three hundred years the house stood. Three hundred years the staff was one.

*[Column II]*

Then came the year of the breaking. The year of the breaking came.  
The governors stood up in their cities. Each said: "I am a king."  
The seat in Tallen said: "Sit down." They did not sit down.  
The seat in Tallen called the host. The host did not come.  
That was the [[evt:10011|Collapse of the Yeda State]]. One house became six houses.

One daughter went out, and she is [[pol:440|Navnga]].  
One daughter went out, and she is [[pol:441|Nodhenga]].  
One daughter went out, and she is [[pol:442|Jenontono]].  
One daughter went out, and she is [[pol:443|Vovo]].  
One daughter went out, and she is [[pol:444|Denenga]].  
Five daughters went out, and each shut her door against her mother.  
Each has her own seat. Each has her own staff. Each sends no barley.

*[Column III]*

And the mother? The mother stayed in her house.  
[[pol:439|Yeda]] remains, small Yeda, in Tallen by the great gate.  
The name is not lost. The seat is not lost. The gods of Tallen are not lost.  
That was the [[evt:10012|Rise of Yeda]], the new Yeda out of the old.  
But the great hall is too wide now for those who sit in it.  
The storehouses are too high now for the barley that comes.  
The great gate is too broad now for the boats that come to it.  
The old men stand on the wall and look to the river, and say: "Once."

Weep, O Tallen. Twenty-four years you have wept.  
Twenty-four harvests have come to you, and each was smaller.  
The young men do not remember the one house. They remember only the six.  
The singer remembers. The singer weeps for them.

*[Column IV]*

All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again.  
So the priests of Jenro say. So the singer says after them.  
Let the wheel turn, O Domamnol. Let it turn back to Tallen.  
Let the daughters remember their mother's house.  
Let the boats come back up the river. Let the sacks come back from the hills.  
Let the gate of Tallen be too narrow again for those who come in.

Until then, weep, O Tallen. Weep, O house of the seat.  
In the 326th year of Yeda the singer wrote this, and laid down the reed.
