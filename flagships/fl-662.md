Almost every document that bears on [[per:25907|Nofath]] came out of the ground in one season, Nambili's at Linnshal in AY 4953: a song, a coin, a king list, an epitaph and two omen texts on potsherds. Not a word survives in her own voice. The omen texts, copied in 3021 and 3106, give her the same five words, "while Nofath held the seal", and file her among the bad signs. The record keeps only an outline, but read with care it shows more competence than the diviners allowed.

## A daughter of Bemonga

She was born in AY 2902, while [[set:62|Bimnge]] still belonged to the Peme Empire; the record does not say where. She was nine when that empire broke apart in 2911 and her father, [[per:25762|Dolon]], became the first ruler of the city-state of [[pol:1142|Bemonga]]. Its only survey, taken as it ended, counted 460,585 people in twelve districts and an army of 2,823 men. Its people were Jego, its gods those of [[mov:2|Jinru]], its money the silver [[cur:554|molshe]] of 7.60 grams. In 2917 Dolon laid a foundation tablet whose surviving lines say he "built this temple for Volme, his lady", the goddess of war (PAL·IN·05441).

In 2920, at eighteen, she married [[per:25902|Talediemenen II]], who was seventeen, heir of [[pol:1139|Lienonoshal]] and son of its king, Talediemenen the Cruel. The [[evt:26229|Marriage of Talediemenen II and Nofath]] joined the two houses. Within a year it had failed as policy. Dolon died in 2921, aged 55; her brother [[per:25906|Okotnakhen]], eighteen years her elder, took his seat; and that same year Lienonoshal went to war on Bemonga to seize its land.

## A song from the other side

The one document addressed to her is a wedding song her husband wrote in 2922, at nineteen (PAL·PO·00087). It remembers her arrival "in a litter hung with the dyed cloth of your country", admits "The granaries go to the army", and tells her "that a marriage is not a treaty." It says she "wept for it in the night where you thought I did not hear." We have only his word for her tears. And it never names her brother: "another sits in his seat", the prince writes, as if Bemonga's new ruler were no kin of hers.

The war was no contest. In every battle Lienonoshal fielded ten to fifteen men for each of Bemonga's. At Budh in 2926 her husband himself led 29,860 men against her brother's 2,059, losing 91 to Bemonga's 305. At Linidhagshal in 2927 Okotnakhen lost 1,330 men to 235, and the [[evt:26311|Zheyeyzhekhte–Bemonga War]] opened a second front. In 2928 Bimnge fell after a battle and a siege, and Talediemenen II and Okotnakhen signed the annexation of all twelve districts in Bimnge itself. Over the [[evt:26245|Lienonoshal–Bemonga War]] Bemonga lost 2,471 men and Lienonoshal 551. A Lienonoshal chronicle of 2938 boasts "520 of them lay dead on the field, and of ours only 38" (PAL·CH·04797); the record never has Lienonoshal losing fewer than 74 in a battle of this war. Where Nofath stood, the record does not say.

## Twelve years with the seal

Her children came late: [[per:26184|Nuerneneshieth]] in 2939 and [[per:26185|Shiennatdath]] in 2941, when she was 37 and 39. The record does not say why. Okotnakhen died in 2943, aged 59, without office since the conquest. In 2945 Talediemenen II died at 42, and the [[evt:26513|accession of Nuerneneshieth]], a child of six, made her mother regent. In 2948 the girl died at nine, and the [[evt:26525|accession of Shiennatdath]], aged seven, made Nofath regent again. The record runs the first regency to 2955, seven years after its child died; read plainly, the seal stayed in her hand from 2945 to 2957, twelve years. In four centuries of Lienonoshal the record names only one other regent.

The record keeps no act in her name, but it measures the kingdom. Its 89 districts stood unchanged at the end of the 2940s and of the 2950s. Its treasury grew from 326,870 kilograms of silver at the end of the 2930s to 419,216 a decade later. Between 2945 and 2956 the record names no war, battle or treaty. Stability fell to 0.39 by the end of the 2940s, the lowest of any Lienonoshal survey before the 3230s, yet no revolt followed.

In 2956, still within the regency, Lienonoshal struck its first coinage, the Mava, at 9.6 grams of silver against the 5.4 of the Tonyo it replaced; the first issue shows the "head of Shiennatdath, diademed" (PAL·NU·00134). The same year the kingdom went to war with Tobezhenga. At Dininge in 2957 its general Pavekuevvan, outnumbered 31,288 to 28,577, lost 620 men to the enemy's 7,257. Zhinontunu was sacked that year; Nambili's excavation there found the "burnt destruction layer, ash and fallen brick" (PAL·EX·00006). Whose war it was, the record does not say: the coin bears a fifteen-year-old's face, but her mother held the seal.

## Among the ill omens

The regency ended in 2957, and Nofath died in 2959, aged 57, of natural causes. Her daughter reigned 56 years and is remembered as the Great. Memory was less kind to the mother. A pupil's king list of 2999 runs from Talediemenen to Shiennatdath, "may she live for ever", with no line for the regent (PAL·SX·00470). Shiennatdath's own epitaph names only her father, "daughter of Talediemenen", before a fracture takes the rest (PAL·IN·05426). The diviners kept Nofath only inside a bad sign: "If the oil sinks and does not rise: a child will sit upon the throne" (PAL·OM·00267); "a young prince will rule, and another will hold his seal" (PAL·OM·00268).

The oil that sank and did not rise belonged to Nuerneneshieth, the child who died at nine, not to her mother. Against the record, her twelve years look less like an omen than a long act of holding. A bride from a conquered city, widowed at 43, kept her husband's kingdom whole and its treasury growing until a daughter could take it. The record holds no epitaph for her. A husband's song and a diviner's formula keep her name.
