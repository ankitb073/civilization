Thus says [[per:20090|Dodemenen]], the great king, king of [[pol:801|Yennga]], beloved of [[dei:14|Bovme]], shepherd of many peoples, to the young man of the house of [[per:20477|Tevodeven]], who dwells in my house in [[set:155|Yennga]] as the surety of the tablet: hear, and be wise.

The servants tell me that you weep at night. I do not blame you. A colt taken from the mare weeps, and then it grows, and then it carries a king. But hear why you are here, and then weep less.

Your fathers were a stubborn people. Many neighbours have taught them the weight of the spear, and still they have not learned it. Tribute was due to Yennga, and they held it back. My messengers went to them, and they sent my messengers home with their beards cut. So [[evt:20591|this war]] came upon [[pol:889|Yerenonieren]], and it was their doing, not mine.

In the third year of the war my army came before your city. Your men came out against us, and [[evt:20621|before the walls of Yerenonieren]] my soldiers struck them down. Many fell; those who lived ran back behind the gate like goats into a fold.

In the next year your people gathered again at Zhiyiyzhikhti. They hid among the rocks and the thorn. My soldiers went up into the rocks and pulled them out one by one, and [[evt:20628|that day at Zhiyiyzhikhti]] their dead lay in the clefts so thick that the jackals did not need to dig. Then my army returned to your city, and [[evt:20629|the city was sacked]], the third time it has been sacked, and its gold went into my treasury.

In this year, your king Tevodeven himself came out. The night before the battle [[dei:19|Yenan]] the moon rose red. My priests said: it is the blood of Yerenonieren. His priests said: it is the blood of Yennga. My priests read truly. [[evt:20657|At Zhiyiyzhikhti a second time]] your king's men were broken in the morning, and he fled before noon. Then [[evt:20658|the city was sacked a fourth time]], and I did not stop my soldiers.

Of my own men, some fell in these years. [[dei:18|Eba]] received them in the fire, and their mothers have their names. Of your people, who can count? The hills of Baan are full of them.

Then Tevodeven came to me in the broken gate of [[set:242|Yerenonieren]]. He put dust on his head. He set his seal beside my seal on [[evt:20659|the tablet of Yerenonieren]]. For ten years no spear will be lifted between us. And you were given to me, that the tablet should not be forgotten.

Now hear what I require of you. You will learn the speech of Yennga. You will eat at the table of my sons and you will ride with them. I do not ask you to bow to the gods of [[mov:2|Zhenro]]; they belong to my people, and they have no need of strangers. But when the priests make sacrifice to [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] at the harvest, you will stand silent, and you will not turn your back.

When the gifts of your king come to Yennga in their season, weighed out in good [[cur:412|tafvaf]] and not in clipped silver, I will send word to your mother that you are well, and that you are fed, and that you have grown. When they do not come, I will send her no word. Let her ask your king why.

Be wise, young man. The great wheel turns. What is low is raised, and what is raised is brought low. Your people are low now. If you learn well in my house, perhaps one day you will raise them, as a loyal friend of Yennga.

Written in the 36th year of Dodemenen, in the house of the king, at Yennga.
