*[Obverse.]*

To my father, at the farm below the hill of the two oaks, a day's walk from [[set:130|Fiwi]]: your son greets you, from the garrison at [[set:114|Tamesitsiyi]].

May [[dei:3|Nielei]] of the river water your fields. May Etlie of the sky send the rain at the right time and not at the wrong time. May my mother's knees be better. I am well. My feet are hard now. The sergeant says I am a soldier. I do not feel like one.

Father, this is the first year of the new king, [[per:17553|Boziniliean]]. We heard it here by the runner at the turn of the year. We were made to stand in rows in the yard and swear the oath to him, with our hands on the standard. The priest of [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] cut the ram's throat and read the liver. He said the liver was good. Then he said it was good again, more loudly, and the captain looked at him. I do not know what that means. The men say it means the liver had a spot on it.

There was another omen. On the night of the oath a star fell over the old palace of the kings of this country. The men of the town came out on their roofs to see it and talked low among themselves. The next day the captain doubled the watch on the gate.

*[Reverse.]*

You asked me in your last message whether this is the place where grandfather fought. It is. The old men of our garrison told me. In the days of King [[per:17019|Ishminpish]] the army of [[pol:690|Yiotl]] came down this valley. There was a battle by the river below the town, where now we water the horses. Then another battle, and then their lord, [[per:17139|Shoe Tluee]], came out and gave up his country, and it was the [[evt:17195|Conquest of Tamesitsiyi]]. Forty years and more ago. Grandfather was here. Perhaps he stood where I stand on the wall. I looked for his mark on the stones. I did not find it.

The people here are of this country still. They speak among themselves in their own way, with words we do not use, though they speak plainly to us. They pay the tribute at harvest, in barley and in silver, and we stand by the measuring-place with our spears so that the measure is honest. Barley is two [[cur:375|Liwi]] and a third the measure in the market. My pay is less than it should be because the paymaster takes for the bread and for the boots.

There is a girl who sells onions by the well. Her grandfather fought against my grandfather. She told me this, and laughed, and gave me an onion. I do not know if that was a kindness or a joke. I have kept the onion.

Father, I ask you: is the barley in? Did the brindled cow calve? Did my brother marry the daughter of the smith or not? Write by the trader who carries the salt, or by the next levy.

They say the new king will call up more men, and that the garrison here will stay. Others say he will send us home, to show his mercy at the beginning of his reign. The captain says nothing. Pray to Lashlae that the second saying is true.

Your son, of the garrison of Tamesitsiyi, on the wall.
