title: The Long Apprenticeship: Yegan I of Yarel

Most rulers called "the Great" earned the name early and spent the rest of their lives living up to it. [[per:27386|Yegan I]] of [[pol:1137|Yarel]] did the opposite. He began his reign with ruinous defeats and spent the next half-century climbing out of them. He reigned for fifty-six years, from AY 3065 to 3121. At its end he left a larger, richer and steadier empire than the one he had nearly lost in his twenties. The record allows us to follow that slow recovery closely. What it reveals is less a great man than a persistent one.

## The usurper's boy

Yegan was born in AY 3048, a Jego. His father, [[per:27375|Pashson]], is remembered as "the Usurper": in 3063, in a year the record marks as a crisis, he seized the throne from Queen Shetkoyeveyath II, who had lately won a hard war in person. Pashson held it two years and died in 3065, aged thirty-six. The [[evt:27742|Accession of Yegan I]] gave the throne to a youth of seventeen, with no regent and no inherited legitimacy except his father's success. The empire he received had some 174 districts and eleven and a half million people. The record's measure of its internal order had been falling for some twenty-five years.

## The war with Peme

At twenty-one Yegan chose war. The [[evt:27772|First Yarel–Peme War]] (3069–3076) was an attack on the kingdom of [[pol:1141|Peme]] and its seasoned king, [[per:27346|Nalban II]]. It went badly from the start. His generals lost at Binge in 3071, with 28,043 dead, and again the next year. In 3074 the king took command himself, and the muster roll drawn up for that campaign survives (PAL·MU·00669). It lists eight towns and their contingents of cavalry and infantry, 107,497 men in all, and closes with a line that says much about the army's morale: "Deserters to be branded and returned to their towns."

At the [[evt:27841|Battle of Zhiyiyzhikhti (AY 3074)]] that army was beaten by 39,277 men under [[per:27437|Afen]]. Yarel lost 26,806 men and Peme 1,976. Two years later, at the [[evt:27867|Battle of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 3076)]], Yegan lost again, to [[per:27498|Balnofmalen]]: 24,413 dead against 2,550. Nalban raised three steles for the war, claiming 84,300, 128,800 and finally 180,300 enemy dead. The true figures were grim enough. By the [[evt:27868|Treaty of Piyikhzhidhe (AY 3076)]] Yarel paid an indemnity as the vanquished party. The war had cost it 101,215 men. The king was twenty-eight.

## The slow recovery

He did not command again for eighteen years. The Yarel–Mane War (3083–3085) was entrusted to a general, who won twice and besieged Tivintukhe. The [[evt:27947|Treaty of Tivintukhe (AY 3085)]], whose parchment text survives (PAL·TR·01840), opens, after an invocation: "Peace is granted by Yegan, emperor of Yarel, to Ashne of Manke, on these conditions." [[pol:1098|Mane]] and its king [[per:27440|Ashni III]] gave up nine districts and two towns, with a river as the new frontier. A boundary stone in Nivi marked the gain.

The next war was aimed at the city-state of [[pol:1198|Punikyithdiy]], a fragment of a fallen kingdom. The [[evt:28027|Yarel–Punikyithdiy War]] (3091–3096) opened with another defeat under a general. Then Yegan, now forty-six, took the field at the [[evt:28051|Battle of Akhtse (AY 3094)]]. He defeated the city's ruler, [[per:27443|Kaflukhnen]], with 162,182 men against 11,594. Generals then took and sacked [[set:54|Ontiedzey]] twice, and in 3095 its archives burned. Yegan raised limestone steles for both of those victories, though he had commanded at neither. The [[evt:28081|Conquest of Punikyithdiy]] in 3096 annexed seventeen districts.

## Revolt and amnesty

Six years later the conquered city rose. The [[evt:28179|Punikyithdiy War of Independence]] began in 3102 under [[per:27792|Keremathin]], and the causes the record gives are striking. They are not the recent conquest but the old humiliations: the defeat at Binge in 3071, the defeat at Piyikhzhidhe in 3076, and the treaty that followed. A quarter of a century later, Yarel's enemies still remembered the young king's failures.

The rebels could not fight. At the [[evt:28228|Battle of Ontiedzey (AY 3105)]] Yegan brought 173,778 men against thirty under [[per:27872|Vepansin]]. He won, and lost 18,900 men, most of them, we may guess, to the siege camp rather than the enemy. His basalt stele claims "He killed 100 soldiers" (PAL·IN·02102); the record says thirteen. The [[evt:28229|Suppression of the Punikyithdiy Revolt]] that followed is more interesting than the battle. It restored thirty-five districts to Yarel and granted an amnesty. Keremathin lived for another thirty years. Other defeated rulers in these Lives did not survive their submission. Keremathin, pardoned by a king of fifty-seven, did.

## Old age and inheritance

His last war, the Yarel–Tobezhenga War (3113–3117), was fought entirely by his general. It ended with the sack of [[set:6|Zhinontunu]] and the [[evt:28415|Conquest of Tobezhenga]], sealed with [[per:27770|Balsheykolvan]], which added fourteen districts. The Tobezhenga historian, writing twenty-four years later (PAL·CH·05174), accused "Yegan of Yarel, swollen with pride", and recorded that "the gates were opened to the enemy". The king, by then nearly seventy, set up yet another stele in his own name.

His private losses in these years were heavy. His wife died in 3099 and his elder daughter in 3117. He died in office in AY 3121, at seventy-three. His funerary stele (PAL·IN·05421) reads: "Yegan, king of Yarel, son of Pashson, dwells in this tomb." Fifty-six years into his reign, the son still named the usurper as his title to the crown. The Accession of Komagonath passed the throne to his younger daughter, [[per:28078|Komagonath]], who reigned forty-three years and won her own epithet, "the Conqueror". Their house ruled Yarel until the empire's collapse in 3198.

The record measures what Yegan's long reign achieved. Between 3070 and 3120 Yarel grew from 176 districts to 230 and from eleven and a half million people to some fifteen million. Its order, so shaky in his youth, grew steadier in every decade of his later reign. "The Great" may overstate it. Yegan lost both battles he fought as a young man, and in old age he took credit for victories that were not his. The steadier achievement belongs to the middle-aged king who stopped commanding and started choosing his generals well, and to the old man who granted a rebel an amnesty. Yegan I learned slowly, but he learned.
