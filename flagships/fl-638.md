The best likeness of [[per:37966|Dipimtin]] is a silver coin. It is the first issue of the [[cur:897|Rimi]], struck around AY 4070 and known from a hoard. The obverse shows his head, veiled, facing left; the reverse a bull walking. The metal is about nine parts in ten fine, a little over nine grams. When it was struck he was sixty-five and had reigned thirty-three years, and he would reign eleven more. Posterity called him "the Old". Of all the epithets in this series it claims the least, and it may be the most honest.

## Heir by seniority

He was born in 4005, a man of the Jego, and his name means "storm eagle". His mother, [[per:37581|Kudheth]], came to the throne of [[pol:1562|Yidhe]] in 4021 in succession to her own father. His father never ruled and died in 4030. An elder brother died the following year. Under Yidhe's law of seniority, the [[evt:38587|Accession of Dipimtin]] in 4037 made him emperor at thirty-two. The empire then held some twenty-four million people and was still recovering from a famine that had killed more than a million and a half in the early 4030s. He married a woman six years younger. Their only child, a daughter, was born in 4042.

## The war of fourteen summers

A year later he went to war. The record traces the [[evt:38627|Second Yidhe–Lienunushal War]] (4043–4056) to a war fought nearly ninety years before. It was waged against [[pol:1430|Lienunushal]], a kingdom four and a half centuries old. An old lancer, writing his memoir thirty years later, says the men who rode in it called it "the war of the fourteen summers, because I was nineteen when the banners went out ... and past thirty when they came home".

Dipimtin commanded five of its battles. The first two were disasters. At the [[evt:38644|Battle of Zhinontunu (AY 4045)]] he led 75,130 men against 44,708 under [[per:38105|Namelen]] and lost 15,434 to the enemy's 1,841. A stele raised by Lienunushal's queen claims 98,800 Yidhe dead. Its carvers used the old royal formula, changing the title and the pronouns for a queen. At the [[evt:38645|Battle of Pikhtunge (AY 4046)]] he met that queen, [[per:37847|Biethnemagunath]], in person: 76,921 against 58,685, with 22,514 Yidhe dead against 7,265. In two summers he had lost about 38,000 men. "Of those two years," the lancer writes, "I remember mostly the rain, the burying, and the silence round the fires." He calls the queen "a hard enemy", and adds: "It is no shame to an eagle to be called fierce."

Then came a clear victory. At the [[evt:38654|Battle of Zhinontunu (AY 4048)]] the emperor beat [[per:38116|Zhieyshakuevan]] with only a modest edge in numbers, losing 1,658 men to 7,236. In 4049 his daughter died, aged seven. His general Tutafbafin carried the war through the next years. Two of Dipimtin's monuments claim the general's victories for him: 115,700 dead at Nin in 4052 and 27,500 at Zhinontunu in 4053, against record figures of 16,585 and 12,914.

He returned to the field at the end. The muster roll for the [[evt:38741|Battle of Zhinontunu (AY 4055)]] lists 60,799 men from eight towns, ending: "Deserters to be branded and returned to their towns." The queen beat him again; Yidhe lost 4,761 men to 5,055. A year later, at the [[evt:38743|Battle of Uthtafpu (AY 4056)]], he brought 83,052 against 23,820 under [[per:38164|Shienlun]] and broke them decisively. The losses were not close: 668 against 7,749. The [[evt:38745|Treaty of Linnshal (AY 4056)]] was sworn in the enemy capital, [[set:18|Linnshal]]. It gave Yidhe thirty-nine districts and five towns, an indemnity and trading rights. Over the whole war Yidhe lost 76,888 men and Lienunushal 79,960. Lienunushal fell into civil war in 4062 and broke apart in 4070, and the record counts Uthtafpu and the treaty among the causes. The lancer was given "a field by the river" for his service and ploughed it for the rest of his life.

## An old king's wars

The rest of the reign was fought by others in his name. The [[evt:38831|Yidhe–Yaaril War]] (4065–4068) is a translator's lesson in formula. Yidhe's chronicler says [[per:38208|Tudhe II]] of [[pol:1406|Yaaril]], "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Yidhe". Yaaril's chronicler, writing half a century later, says Dipimtin, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Yaaril". The same sentence served both sides; the record names Yidhe as the attacker. The [[evt:38854|Treaty of Yaaril (AY 4068)]], sworn at Yaaril, took five districts and a town and cost 718 Yidhe lives. Yaaril, a kingdom since 3526, collapsed in 4082. His younger brother had died in 4065. In 4068 two neighbouring states allied against him.

In 4070 came the [[evt:38870|First Coinage of Yidhe]], in which the Rimi replaced the old [[cur:778|Zhiflun]]. It is the most lasting act of his old age, and the only one that put his face before ordinary people.

The [[evt:38934|20th Diebeguvebenga Trade War]] (4075–4077) was fought against [[pol:1644|Kuevezhenga]], one of the states that had risen from Lienunushal's ruins. Yidhe's chronicle of the war, written decades later, gives the enemy 12,810 men in one battle and 25,837 in the other, more than twice the record's numbers. The war cost Yidhe 1,864 men; Kuevezhenga lost 3,884. The [[evt:38949|Treaty of Linnshal (AY 4077)]] gave Yidhe six districts, a vassal and its trade. It was sworn by an emperor of seventy-two and by [[per:38383|Bueven II]], a king of four. History, with its usual irony, gave the child-king the same epithet as the old man.

## The end of the line

Dipimtin died in 4081, aged seventy-six, of natural causes. He left no living child, and the house that had ruled since 3891 ended with him. The record marks a crisis, and the [[evt:38985|Usurpation of Bilzhiyshen]] put a man of thirty-nine on the throne. Within four years [[per:38440|Bilzhiyshen]] was at war on two fronts. Yidhe itself endured until 4387.

Over his forty-four years the empire grew from about 220 districts to about 280, and from twenty-four million people to nearly thirty-two. Its internal order was slightly firmer at the end than at the start. His wars cost Yidhe some 79,000 soldiers. He commanded five battles and won two. The coin flatters him less than the stelae do. The truest epitaph is the lancer's request to his sons: "When you pass the stones at Zhinontunu, say a prayer for those under them, theirs and ours."
