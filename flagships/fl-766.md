title: Letter of an Old Levy-Man of Man'e to His Wife, from the Border before Puntyiddiy

To my wife, mother of my sons, say: thus says your husband, the spearman. May [[dei:24|Navbudin]] the river keep you. May Binbe the earth keep the barley in the ground. I am well. My knees are not well. I am well.

The king's men called the levy, and they called me, though my beard is grey, because I know the road. I know the road. Five and twenty years ago I walked it behind the old king, [[per:8136|Sha Ash]], father of our lord. I was young then. You were young. You stood at the door with the first child on your hip and you did not weep until I was past the well.

Hear what I remember. We went down to Yuftuy. At the [[evt:8095|Battle of Yuftuy]] the old king stood in the front of the spears, and we broke them. Then we came to [[set:54|Puntyiddiy]], the city with the high gate. Their king was a boy, [[per:8415|Cuubithun]], with no beard on his face. He came out and set his seal, and the land of Puntyiddiy became the land of [[pol:312|Man'e]]. The scribes call it the [[evt:8097|Conquest of Punikyithdiy]]; the scribes have their own names for everything. Then the boy was put to death. I saw it from far off. I did not like it. Do not tell the priest I said so.

We came home with a cloak each and a bronze pin each. You still have my pin. Keep it.

Then the men from the north came in the days of our lord [[per:8554|Sha Ash]], son of the old king. Eight years ago our levy beat them under these very walls, at the [[evt:8251|Battle of Ontiedzey]]; my brother's son was there, he told it at the feast. But at Akhtse the field was lost, and the [[evt:8272|Treaty of Akhtse]] gave Puntyiddiy away to them, the high gate and the fields and all. All that walking, all those dead, for twenty years. Twenty years, and then a seal on a tablet.

Now we stand at the border and look at the gate. We are set to watch; we do not fight. Men say the northerners who took it from us have quarrelled among themselves, and that a king of its own sits again in Puntyiddiy, as before. In the evening the old men of the town sing on the wall, and we hear them across the fields. It is the same song they sang when we came the first time.

Barley is dear here. A measure costs one [[cur:184|Ush'e]] and a little more, and the levy is paid in barley, not in silver. Send me nothing. Keep it for the boys.

Say to my eldest: mend the roof of the byre before the rains. Say to my youngest: he must not come to the levy. If the king's men come for him, let him be at his uncle's in the hills. I have walked this road for two kings. That is enough walking for one house.

May Navbudin keep you. May the storm pass over the roof. When the barley is cut, I will be home.
