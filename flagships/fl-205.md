title: A Total That Agrees by Accident: The Khupande Rising of AY 523–528 Reconsidered
---
The [[evt:1877|Khupande War of Independence]] (AY 523–528) contains a trap. The Khupande chronicler Nakhiji puts Nilu's dead at 2,861, and the record gives 2,857. The near-match tempts one to trust the rebel tradition. I want to show that it is an accident.

## The tablets

The Nilu account (PAL·CH·00240, Injèlu, excavated at Nilu in AY 4901) is Naneje's. It is dated "in the 556th year of Nelo", AY 555. That was twenty-seven years after the war and twenty-three years after the Nilu state had collapsed. He wrote for the palace of Nelo, which counted its years from Nilu's foundation and so claimed its past. He was a young man during the war. The Khupande account (PAL·CH·00239, Khukhande, excavated at Dunjinil in AY 4915) is Nakhiji's, dated "in the 62nd year of Khupande", AY 584, in the reckoning of a kingdom that had lasted five years. Nakhiji was born in AY 542, fourteen years after its end.

## Field by field

*Who began it.* The war grew from the [[evt:1876|Revolt of Khupande]] in AY 523. Naneje agrees: "there came word that Navande of Khopande had gathered an army against Nelo." Nakhiji blames [[pol:5|Nilu]]: "Nambave of Nilu, swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Khupande. The gods commanded that the wrong be avenged."

*The battles.* The record has four engagements, all won by Nilu: the [[evt:1885|Battle of Avash (AY 524)]], the [[evt:1892|Battle of Avash (AY 525)]], the [[evt:1903|Battle of Khanindu (AY 526)]] and the [[evt:1920|Battle of Avash (AY 528)]], the last two in [[reg:27|Luthe]]. Naneje records all four. Nakhiji records the first two and gives both the same excuse: "because the river rose and cut the army in two". An excuse repeated word for word is a formula, not a report. After AY 525 he is silent until the fall of the royal city.

*The dead.* The record gives [[pol:76|Khupande]] 20 dead and Nilu 2,857. Naneje gives the rebels 65: 16, 10, 16 and 23, against the record's 5, 3, 5 and 7, a steady inflation of a little over three times. His rebel musters (49, 43, 43 and 64, against 23, 20, 20 and 30) are roughly doubled. For his own side he admits 755 dead, about a quarter of the true cost. Nakhiji gives his own side 2 dead, one in each battle he records. For Nilu he gives 2,408 and 453, which is where the trap lies. The record's figures for those two battles are 1,275 and 240, together 1,515. Nakhiji has multiplied each by about 1.9, and the product happens to land within four of the record's total for all four battles. A total that agrees with the record is not thereby a true one.

*Commanders.* No commander fell in battle. Translators should note that the record has a Nambave commanding on each side at Avash in AY 524, and a Navande on the Nilu side in AY 525 as well as the rebel king of that name. Naneje's "Nambave came against us ... Nambave waited until their water ran out" is therefore not garbled. Neither tablet mentions that the rebel king Navande was executed at the suppression.

*Outcome and land.* Nilu won. The [[evt:1921|Suppression of the Khupande Revolt]] annexed all sixteen of the rebel kingdom's districts. Its terms included an amnesty, which evidently did not cover the king. Naneje is blunt: "the royal city of Khopande fell to our army. Khopande is no more, and its people serve Nelo." But he counts thirty-seven districts won, more than twice what Khupande ever held. Nakhiji records no surrender and no cession. His war ends without dishonour, and the fall of the capital gets a single elegiac line: "the royal city was taken. Who will now remember the kings of old?"

## What each scribe needed

Naneje's court had inherited Nilu's victories and wanted them large and cheap. The rebels are multiplied, the conquered land is doubled, and three-quarters of Nilu's dead are left out. Nakhiji's needs were different. Writing half a century later, for the memory of a kingdom that had lasted five years, he could not claim victories that every listener knew had not happened. He could shorten the war, blame the river, and end with a question rather than a treaty. That question may be the most honest line in either tablet.
