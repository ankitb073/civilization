**To His Majesty the King, and to the Ministers of His Council.**

**Petition of the residents of the [[reg:29|Mbambanluli]] district, concerning the famine of the years 4937 to 4940 and the food emergency which continues. Submitted in the year 4941.**

1. We, the undersigned, are farmers, herders, schoolteachers, clerks of the former district office, and the families of those who have died. We write because there is no one else to write to. Since the dissolution of the old kingdom twelve years ago, our district has had no governor, no magistrate, no customs post and no relief office. The officials left; the telegraph line was cut for its copper; the district store was emptied and not refilled. We have been, in plain terms, nobody's people.

2. We ask Your Majesty to read what follows as a statement of fact, and not as a complaint against the Crown, to which our grandparents were loyal for nearly a hundred years.

3. **The cause.** For as long as anyone now living can remember, the rains in our district have come later and left earlier. The old men call it the heat of the factories; the schoolteachers call it by its proper name, [[cli:251|the Industrial Warming]], and tell us that it has been measured since the [[evt:46452|onset of the Industrial Warming]] more than a century ago. Whatever its name, its effect on us is simple. The wells drop each summer. The millet burns before it heads. The springs that our fathers dug have gone salt.

4. **The famine.** In 4937 the rains failed outright, and they failed again in each of the three years that followed. We record the [[evt:47756|famine of 4937 in Mbambanluli]] as follows, from our own count, house by house: of every hundred persons living in the district before the failure, forty-six are now dead or gone. In numbers, about one hundred thousand people. We cannot say how many of these died and how many walked away, because those who walked away did not write back. We fear that many of them also died on the road.

5. **The present condition.** The harvest of this year is better than the last four, but it is not enough. There is no seed stock. The herds are gone. Children under five are visibly underweight in every village we have surveyed. Grain reaching us by private carriers is sold at prices which no family here can pay.

6. **What we ask.** We do not ask for charity. We ask for administration. Specifically:

   a. that emergency grain be shipped to the district at the Crown's expense, and distributed under the supervision of a named official answerable to Your Majesty;

   b. that seed for the next planting be issued on credit, repayable from the harvest;

   c. that engineers be sent to deepen the wells and survey the old reservoirs;

   d. that the district be placed formally under the protection of the Crown, so that there is once again a door at which our people may knock.

7. We are aware that Your Majesty has many districts and many claims upon the treasury. We would only observe that a district without government is not a saving to any treasury. It is a loss which has not yet been counted.

8. We have kept the old prayers in the temples, as our parents taught us. We ask our gods for rain and Your Majesty for order. We do not think it unreasonable that one of the two should answer.

9. The [[evt:47756|famine]] is over in the reports of the merchants. It is not over in [[reg:29|Mbambanluli]].

*Signed on behalf of the residents of the district, with the marks of those who cannot write.*
