To the queen of [[pol:68|Eshue'ethie]], my mother, the lady of my father's house, say:  
Thus says [[per:2127|E'a Sim'a]], your daughter, who is in [[set:108|Ashash]].

May [[dei:0|Atlie]] of the high sky keep you. May [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river keep you. May [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] the moon keep you by night, and keep my father, and keep the house of my father.

With me it is well. With my body it is well. With my heart it is half well. Hear me, my mother, and do not be angry that I write so much.

On the day of the full moon they led me to the house of the king of [[pol:67|La'im]]. They put the red veil on my head. They put the bronze bracelets on my arms, the ones you gave me, and I did not let their women take them off. The priests of La'im poured oil for Atlie, and oil for [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth, and they broke the cake over our heads. The people of Ashash shouted at the gate. They threw barley. This is the [[evt:1802|Marriage of Esiesh Ash II and E'a Sim'a]]. It is done. Our houses are joined.

Hear now about my husband.

His name is [[per:2125|Esiesh Ash]], like his father. He is a boy, my mother. He has fifteen summers. I have eighteen. He is shorter than I am by a hand. At the feast he did not know where to put his cup. But he is not a bad boy. He looked at me under the veil and he said: "Are you afraid?" I said: "No." He said: "I am." I laughed, and then he laughed. So it is not all bitter.

Hear now about his father.

The king, [[per:2060|Esiesh Ash]], is old. His beard is white. He sat at the head of the feast and did not laugh. His eyes go from one man to the next, all the time, like a dog that guards the sheep. You know how he came to the seat, my mother; the whole country knows it. The men of Ashash do not speak of the [[evt:1727|Usurpation of Esiesh Ash I]] where the king can hear. But the women of the house speak of it at the loom, softly. Ten years he has sat in the seat, and still he watches the door.

The king's wife is kind to me. She gave me a cloak of fine wool and showed me the storehouse. The storehouse is full, my mother, fuller than ours. Barley is cheap in the market; a measure goes for less than a [[cur:41|Yashta]]. The gods have not turned their face from this house, whatever men say of it.

Now hear what I ask, and do not refuse me.

Send me my old nurse. The women here are not my women. Send me the little cup with the moon on it, from your chest. Send me word of my father, whether he hunts, whether he sits in the gate, whether he asks for me.

And tell my father this: I have not forgotten whose daughter I am. I am a wife of La'im. I am a daughter of Eshue'ethie. I am both, and I will be both. If a day comes when the two houses quarrel, I will stand in the doorway between them and say: "Enough." Tell him I said it.

My husband is calling. The boy wants me to see his new horses. I will go and see his horses.

May [[dei:1|Uelti]] of wisdom put a good word in my mouth in this strange house. May the wheel bring me home to you once more, if only for a feast.

Written in the 11th year of Esiesh Ash, king of La'im, in the house of the king.
