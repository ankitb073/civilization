title: Lament for the Thousand of Bimnge

*[Obverse.]*

Weep, O [[set:17|Pumi]], city of the king, weep and cut your hair.  
Weep, O mothers of Pumi, weep at the doorposts.  
Weep, O wives, pour out the water, pour out the oil,  
for the sons of [[pol:26|Duvekopuvinga]] are lying in the earth of the north.

Twenty years and more have turned on the wheel,  
the barley has been cut twenty times and more,  
and still the women of Pumi go out at the new moon,  
still they pour the water, still they call the names.

O [[dei:14|Vulme]], lady of the spear, you took them.  
O [[dei:15|Vinba]], broad earth, you drank them.  
O [[dei:19|Yenan]], moon of the night, you saw them lie down.  
Give them back! You do not give them back.

In the days of the [[evt:874|Duvekopuvinga–Buneynga War]] they went out,  
the young men went out with the bronze on their shoulders,  
the young men went out singing on the road to the north,  
and [[per:1082|Kulvalomin]] went before them, the general of the king,  
and the drums went before them, and the dust went up behind them.

The men of [[pol:33|Buneynga]] were few behind their walls.  
Few were they, and yet our sons fell.  
Not by the spear alone they fell: the marsh fever took them,  
the bad water took them, the cold of the hill nights took them,  
the arrow from the reeds took them, the stone from the wall took them.

**First weeping: for Bimnge.**  
At the [[evt:881|Battle of Bimnge]], by [[set:62|Bimunga]] in the marsh,  
there fell nine hundreds, there fell ninety and more, of the sons of Pumi.  
Nine hundreds lay down in one day. Who will plough their fields?  
Nine hundreds lay down in one day. Who will pour for their fathers?  
The field was ours. The field was ours. But the sons were not ours any more.

**Second weeping: for Biniynge.**  
At the [[evt:887|Battle of Biniynge]] they fell, two hundreds and more.  
The field was ours. The sons were not ours.

**Third weeping: for Biniynge again.**  
At the [[evt:896|Battle of Biniynge]] in the year after they fell again,  
nearly six hundreds of them fell before the walls.  
The field was ours. The sons were not ours.

*[Reverse.]*

Then came the [[evt:897|First Siege of Biniynge]],  
and the men of Buneynga bowed down at last,  
and [[per:1097|Panshelon]], our young king, in the second year of his reign,  
swore the [[evt:898|Treaty of Biniynge]] before the gods.  
The land in the north came to Duvekopuvinga.  
Silver came, the [[cur:16|Bima]] was weighed out in the house of the king,  
the traders of Buneynga came down to our markets.  
But the sons did not come home.

The scribes of the king counted them, the scribes of the king wrote them down:  
one thousand, and eight hundreds, and nineteen.  
One thousand, and eight hundreds, and nineteen.  
Each one had a mother. Each one had a name.  
The scribes wrote the number. The mothers keep the names.

Kulvalomin also has gone down to the house below the earth,  
he who led them out, he went down after them in his old age,  
may he find them there, may he lead them still.

And now in our days the young men went out again,  
to the [[evt:952|Second Battle of Tathriubu]] and to Shiniy they went,  
and Panshelon brought them home with spoil.  
Vulme was kind. The wheel has turned. What ends will begin again.  
But let the young men not forget the thousand of Bimnge.

O Vulme, take the blood we have given, and ask no more.  
O Vinba, keep them gently in the dark earth.  
O [[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire, warm them where they lie.  
O [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]], let barley grow on the place where they fell.

Let the priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] pour for them at every new moon.  
Let the women of Pumi sing this lament at every new moon.  
Weep, O Pumi. Weep, and then rise up, and cut the barley.

*[Colophon.]* Set in clay in the 22nd year of Panshelon, king of Duvekopuvinga.
