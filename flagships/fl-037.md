### Canto the Fourth

Not to the shadow-gods of the old shrines,  
whom the One has made His heralds and His hinds,  
but to the One Himself, the Lord of Lines,  
who draws all time toward Judgement as one winds  
a thread upon a spindle, I commit  
this song of [[pol:1785|Tallin]] and the arms of it.

And you, great [[per:42887|Pavbukhshifin]], whose arms  
were lately crowned upon the field of [[evt:43419|Turamtu]],  
suffer an older war to sound its alarms  
once more in verse, and hear what your fathers knew:  
how [[pol:1788|Tunnge]], swollen with its ancient pride,  
held [[reg:8|Diviguvivinge]] that was ours, and lied.

Then rose the [[evt:42969|war of Tallin against Tunnge]],  
and [[per:42198|Shinlilalin]] led the eagles out;  
at [[evt:42991|Yinnge]] first the iron tongue was sung,  
and [[per:42320|Nathbibaaluthin]] turned about,  
and left the meadow to the sons of light,  
though dearly bought, and bitter was the night.

O muse of grief (if grief may have a muse  
within the faith that teaches us release),  
now tell of [[set:75|Shiniy]], where we came to lose  
the flower of the empire and its peace.  
The fog came down from the fens like a shroud;  
the drums were muffled and the dead were loud.

There [[per:42339|Nathdun]] lay in hollows like a thief  
and struck the column where the column bent.  
I will not count the slain; to count is grief;  
the reckoners of the treasury were spent  
before the tale was done. And in the van  
our Shinlilalin fell, the better man.

He did not flee. They found him with his sword  
still in his hand, his face toward the foe,  
and on his breast the token of the Lord.  
So at the [[evt:42999|first field of Shiniy]], so,  
the empire learned that pride may have its fall,  
and that the One is master over all.

* * *

But Heaven does not leave its servants long  
in mourning. Up from the ranks there came  
[[per:42550|Tilmumbiyin]], young, and of a temper strong  
as tempered steel; at Yinnge, in the same  
red meadow where we bled, he set his line  
and broke Nathbibaaluthin, by sign

of the One's favour. Then the empress came,  
[[per:42203|Shithkhukhuth]], the mother of the realm,  
herself in harness, and at [[evt:43020|Ture]] her name  
was written on the field; she took the helm  
and [[per:42533|Pimdun]], king of Tunnge, gave her place,  
and could not meet the lightning of her face.

And to Shiniy came Tilmumbiyin again,  
and there was Nathdun waiting, as before;  
but now the fog was ours, and ours the plain,  
and Tunnge paid, at the [[evt:43049|second field]], the score  
it owed us for our dead; the fens ran red;  
the shade of Shinlilalin raised its head.

At [[evt:43056|Thinirnge]] the last host of Tunnge broke.  
Then by the sea, at Togannge, the walls  
were girded for the eighth time, and the smoke  
of our encampments hung above its halls,  
until King Pimdun, weary of the cost,  
came out to treat for all he had not lost.

So at the [[evt:43058|peace of Togannge]] it was sealed:  
the empress and the king set down their names,  
and Tunnge gave up Diviguvivinge, field by field,  
to Tallin, as the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]] proclaims  
that what is rightly ordered shall endure  
until the Judgement; and the peace was sure.

* * *

Now let the printer's devil set his type,  
and let these leaves go out on the twenty-first  
of Pivu, in the year two thousand two hundred and fifty-four  
of the Vishithmikh, while yet the fruit is ripe  
upon the boughs, and let our sons rehearse  
the deeds of Tallin in this humble verse.
