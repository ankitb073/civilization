*Begun in the year 1993 of the Khikhishyu, by the steward of a manor in the country about [[set:54|Pinidhiyidhdiy]], that his lord and those after him may know what was done and what was suffered.*

**At the sowing.** This is the fourth year of the drought, and another spring without rain. Since [[evt:39608|the drought began]] the old men have said each year that the next would mend it, and each year they have been wrong. The river is so low that a child can walk across where the ferry used to run. We sowed what seed we had kept back, and some we borrowed of the lord's granary against the harvest. I wrote the debts in this book. May the One forgive me if I never strike them out.

**In the second month.** No green in the furrows. The priest of the [[mov:57|Khikhishyu]] read to us on the holy day from the scripture of the patient servant, who lost his fields and his flocks and did not curse the One; and he said that suffering is a wheel, and what turns down will turn up again. The people listened. Then they went home to their pots, and the pots were empty.

**At midsummer.** The price of grain in the market of the city is now eight [[cur:880|rithtu]] and more for a single measure. Those who have silver eat. Those who have none sell what they have, a cow, a cloak, a daughter's dowry chest, and then they have nothing to sell. I have seen a mother trade her wedding ring for half a loaf.

**In the same week.** The lord came out from the city and sat in the hall and heard the tenants. He remitted a third part of the dues of grain, as a good lord should when the land fails; for the tenant owes the lord his service, but the lord owes the tenant his protection, and protection is bread when there is no war. The tenants kissed his hand. One old man said: "Lord, it is not enough." The lord said: "I know it."

**After midsummer.** A great many of the Jego have taken to the roads with their carts and their children, going out of [[reg:19|Mankhi]] toward the country they call Mikhukhizhi, where it is said there is still water. The priest blessed them at the crossroads. They say this is the [[evt:39612|Migration of the Jego into Mikhukhizhi]], as though it were a feast; but I saw the faces, and it was not a feast. Some of our own young men have gone with them. Who will plough if the rain comes back?

**At the harvest.** There is no harvest. I write the word, and there is nothing under it. We cut what stood, and it was straw. The tithe-barn is empty. In our village, of the hundred and forty souls I counted at the new year, nineteen are buried since the sowing, most of them old or very young. The priest says the dead are released from their suffering, and I believe him, for he does not lie; but I think the living are not released.

**At the same season.** Word comes from the city that our king, [[per:39172|Tafdiminin]], has ordered prayers in every house of the faith, and that the royal granaries of [[pol:1567|Pinidhiyidhdiy]] are to be opened to the poor of the capital. May the One reward him. I remember five years ago, when the Tzey rose and were put down in the [[evt:39685|Suppression of the Tzey Revolt]], and Tivintukhe after them in the [[evt:39687|Suppression of the Tivintukhe Revolt]]; men said then that the realm had never been stronger. A strong realm cannot command the clouds.

**Before the winter.** They call it the [[evt:39611|famine in Mankhi]] now, as if it had a name and a border. It has neither. The [[cli:213|Great Drought]] does not stop at the edge of the manor. I have given out the last of the lord's seed-corn to the widows, by his leave, and I have written it here so that no one says the steward stole it.

The scripture says that the merciful shall be shown mercy. I set it down here, at the end of this year's leaves, in the hope that the One reads stewards' books.
