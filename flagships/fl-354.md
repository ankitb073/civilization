*[Obverse. Unbaked clay, poorly smoothed; the hand is hurried. The text begins at the top edge.]*

[[dei:16|Dumamnul]], father, see what I write. [[dei:15|Vinba]], mother, hold what I write. [[dei:19|Yenan]], who counts the nights, count these nights with me.

I am a scribe of the house of tablets in [[set:17|Pumi]]. I was a scribe of the house of tablets. There is no house of tablets now. I write on new clay, because the old clay is gone. Hear what I saw.

In the second year of our king [[per:1097|Panshelon]], we were the strong ones. Our spears sat around the walls of Biniynge; that was the [[evt:897|First Siege of Biniynge]], and the gate of Biniynge opened to us. At the [[evt:898|Treaty of Biniynge]] they gave us fields, they gave us silver. We sang in the streets. We said: [[dei:14|Vulme]] loves [[pol:26|Duvekopuvinga]].

Then the men of [[pol:25|Yida]] came against us. They came down for our land, to take it. That was the [[evt:884|First Yida–Duvekopuvinga War]]. At the [[evt:911|Battle of Virathkhanu]] our young men lay down by the thousand. I did not see it; the runners told it. The runners wept as they told it.

Then the men of Yida came to our walls and sat down in a ring around Pumi. That was the [[evt:912|Siege of Himi]], so the men of Yida call it, for that is their name for our city. The ring did not break us. The ring went away. We said: it is over.

It was not over. In the year that is past they came again, many, many, as the ants of the threshing floor are many. Our king went out himself. Panshelon went out with the bronze on his breast, against eleven thousand spears. That was the [[evt:918|Battle of Rile]]. He did not run. I say it: he did not run. But the men of Yida were too many, and the field was theirs.

Then they came into Pumi.

I saw it from the roof of the house of tablets. They came in at the river gate. They came in at the gate of the barley-merchants. I saw them in the street of the potters. I saw them in the street of the weavers. They took the cloth from the looms. They took the [[cur:16|Bima]] from the money-boxes of the merchants; they weighed it on our own scales and laughed. They took the goats. They took the oil. They took the young men with cords on their hands.

Then they came to the house of tablets. I was on the roof. I saw a man with a torch. I saw another man with a torch. The reed mats caught. The roof-beams caught. The baskets of tablets caught. The shelves fell. The tablets of the grain-count fell, the tablets of the fields fell, the tablets of the kings' years fell, the tablets of the oaths of the fathers fell. They cracked in the fire. They broke on the floor. I came down by the back wall. My hands were burned. I did not save one tablet. Not one.

[[dei:18|Eva]] of the fire, why did you eat the words of our fathers? Were they your bread? Eva, give them back.

That was the [[evt:919|Sack of Himi]], as the men of Yida call it. We call it the burning.

*[Reverse.]*

After the burning our king sat with the queen of Yida in the ruins of Pumi, and they swore. That was the [[evt:920|Treaty of Himi]]. Virathkhanu is theirs now, and fields of ours are theirs now. Silver goes to Yida each year, for many years. The merchants of Yida walk in our market. For ten years the spears will sleep.

The priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] say: the wheel turns; what ends will begin again. They say it in the burned courtyard. Let them say it.

I write the count of the grain again on new clay. I write the names of the fields again, as far as I remember them. I write this also, so that it is remembered: the house of tablets burned, and a scribe saw it.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in Pumi, in the 7th year of Panshelon, on new clay. Pour water for the tablets.
