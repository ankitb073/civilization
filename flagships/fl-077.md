To the elders of [[set:10|Navnga]], to the priests of [[dei:25|Novegu]] the wise, to the keepers of the storehouse, say:

Thus says [[per:6308|Nathmanen]], king of [[pol:165|Yida]], your lord. It is the 3rd year of Nathmanen. So I count it. Let no one count it otherwise.

With me it is not well. I sit in the house of strangers. I eat bread that is not my bread. I drink water that is not the water of [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] our river. My feet are cut. My asses are dead on the road. Of the men who went out with me from the gate, few are with me now. The rest have gone back. Let them go back. Let them eat their shame.

Hear me, elders. Do not say: Nathmanen broke Yida. Do not say: Nathmanen took the seat and the seat broke under him. Hear how it was, and let the gods judge.

Before my day, in the days of my fathers, the land was cut, and cut, and cut again. At [[evt:5729|Virathkhanu]] the spearmen of Yida fell. At [[evt:5750|Shivange]] they fell. At [[evt:5797|Piyikhzhidhe]] they fell. At the [[evt:5798|peace of Piyikhzhidhe]] fields were given away, and towns were given away, and silver was sent out year upon year. Then in the days of the king before me, at the [[evt:6018|peace of Binge]], the land was cut again, and more towns were given, and the elders of Yida put dust on their heads. I did not give those towns. I did not set my hand to that clay.

Then the old king went down to his fathers, and the seat stood empty, and the great houses quarrelled over it like dogs over a bone. I sat in the seat. Was it not better that one man sit in it than that the dogs tear it apart? So came the [[evt:6030|day of my taking the seat]]. Let my enemies call it what they call it. I call it the keeping of Yida.

But the gods had turned their faces. [[dei:21|Duminu]] the storm-lord held back the rain. The barley was thin. The poor man paid in [[cur:89|Nulith]] of silver what he had paid before in copper. Then Namlo lifted its hand, at the [[evt:6039|revolt of Namlo]]. Men who ate my bread rose against me. They sent to the great houses in secret. They sent silver. So came the [[evt:6040|war of Namlo]], and I went out against it, and behind me the great houses opened their gates to my enemies.

So came the [[evt:6052|breaking of Yida]]. The one land became two. [[pol:262|Mankha]] took one half, at the [[evt:6053|rising of Mankha]], and set its own lord on its own seat. The other half calls itself [[pol:263|Yida]] still, and sits in another city, and does not call me lord. Two seats; and neither is mine.

I do not weep for myself. I weep for the house of Novegu, where the lamp is not fed. I weep for the gate of Navnga, where no king sits. I weep for the wheel of [[mov:3|Ryethki]], which turns, which turns, and has turned me under.

But the wheel turns. What ends will begin again. Hear me, elders. Keep the storehouse sealed. Keep the lamp of Novegu fed. Keep my name in your mouths. Send me grain, if you have grain. Send me a scribe, for this scribe is a stranger and writes slowly. Send me word.

Let Novegu give you wisdom. Let Shineyka of the sea keep the boats in the harbour. Let the gods who turned their faces turn them again.

Send word quickly. I am waiting.
