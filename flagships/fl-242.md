title: Nine Years at Zhinontunu: The Historical Tonofmalen

At its height the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] faith counted some 396 million adherents. It is natural to imagine its founder on the same scale, a prophet whose words reached the nations in his lifetime. The record allows something smaller and more interesting: a provincial teacher who taught for nine years, left a handful of sayings that cannot all be his, and whose movement took centuries to become what his followers said it had always been.

## What can be established

[[per:18406|Tonofmalen]] was born in AY 2210, a man of the Jego people and a subject of the Kingdom of Tobejenga, whose seat was at Zhinontunu. The record places the [[evt:18520|Founding of the Veshethmekh Faith]] at Zhinontunu in 2256, when he was about forty-six, in a kingdom whose state cult was the Jinru. He died in 2265, of natural causes, at fifty-five. No battle, office, marriage or persecution is attached to his name, and that silence is itself evidence: the courts of his day had no reason to record him.

## The sayings and their problem

The earliest witness is the Sayings of Tonofmalen (PAL·SC·00030), a clay tablet compiled by Natramadhen around AY 2290, a generation after his death. It holds six sayings. Two are distinctive: "The world has a beginning and it will have an end; live as one who will be asked for an account", and "Do not mock the old, for you are walking toward them". The other four are not. "Obey those who hold the teaching, as they obeyed those before them", "Whoever feeds the hungry stranger has fed the god" and "This teaching is not for one people but for every nation; carry it to the ends of the roads" all appear, nearly word for word, in the Sayings of Ihishe (PAL·SC·00029). That collection was set down in AY 2197, nearly sixty years before Tonofmalen founded anything. These lines belonged to a common stock of religious formulae. They tell us what his community taught, not what he said. The linear world with its final account, which the stock does not supply, is the safest place to look for the teacher himself.

## How slowly the faith grew

The legend makes the founding a beginning of conquest. The record makes it the beginning of a long wait. For a century the faith held only a few districts around its birthplace, and it spread widely only from the twenty-ninth century. The earliest royal edict in its favour that the archive preserves is the Edict against the Jinru, issued in Yeda in AY 3008: "The Veshethmekh alone shall be honoured." The first formal establishment came in AY 3095. In that year Konefkoth of Tobezhenga, ruling from Zhinontunu itself, decreed that "the Veshethmekh is the faith of Tobezhenga". Eight hundred and thirty-nine years separate the founding from that edict.

## The founder as an authority

As the faith grew, its founder became a court of appeal. The first sect, the [[mov:108|Totheyi]] of AY 3114, kept four of the six sayings in Tonofmalen's collection but opened its own with the turning wheel, abandoning the one teaching I have called most likely his. Later movements followed: the [[mov:116|I'ashakh]] in 3303 (not the older faith of that name), the [[mov:129|Myituwize]] in 3581 and the [[mov:137|Shamu]] in 3916. The Acts of the Council of Pikhtunge (PAL·SC·00109), from AY 4105, decreed that "the books read in the assemblies are those read in the time of the founder, and no others". The delegates of Elekha left before the vote, and within two years the [[mov:141|Pyizhenatlshi]] had broken away. Councils summoned to keep the faith one were followed by the [[mov:142|Ishlish]] in 4176, by the [[mov:165|Shilesh]] in 4704 and by the [[mov:168|Lave]] in 4808, while the [[mov:143|Mituth]] (4186) and the reformers of the [[mov:156|Tuedinatlshi]] (4534) went their own way. Each claimed the founder; none could produce more of him than six sayings on a tablet compiled after his death.

The historical [[per:18406|Tonofmalen]], then, is a Jego teacher of Zhinontunu who taught for nine years and perhaps believed that the world would end in a reckoning. The rest is the work of his followers. This is not a lesser story than the legend. It is the more remarkable one: the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] was made by a community that kept faith with a modest beginning for eight centuries.
