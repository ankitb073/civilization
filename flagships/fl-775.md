title: Lament of the Village of the Three Wells below Movosh

Hear, you who pass on the road to [[set:14|Movosh]], the complaint of a village that has no voice in any council, set down by one who learned his letters from the old schoolmaster, before the schoolmaster was told to teach in the speech of [[pol:922|Ane]].

**I.**

We were a village of [[pol:1065|Mii]]. Our grandfathers paid their tithe of barley to the queen's steward at the threshing floor, and drank with him afterward; and when the tithe was heavy we said so to his face, and he said so to the queen. That is what a country is, I think: a place where a complaint has somewhere to go.

Then came the long war, eleven summers of it. In its ninth summer the soldiers fought over our barley fields, in the [[evt:25345|Battle of Muvush]], and the barley was trodden into the earth, and the men of Mii fell back; three summers later they were broken for good at the [[evt:25400|Battle of Biyitl]]. In the year 3141 from the founding of Ane, our queen [[per:24851|A'man'ive]], whom the old women still call the Generous, sat down with [[per:24979|Yalni Lieze]] at Movosh and put her seal to the [[evt:25402|Conquest of Mii]]. We were given away, the wells and the fields and the graves, like a dowry.

**II.**

Twenty years have gone since then, and both the sealers are dead. The queen died within three years, of grief, the women say; the conqueror died eleven years ago, and they did not tell us how. Now [[per:25127|Shimeninieme]] is lord over us, whom they call the Fair. I have never seen him. I have seen his steward.

The steward does not drink with anyone. He comes with a scribe and two soldiers, and he measures in the measure of Ane, which is larger than ours, so that the same tithe is more barley. He reckons the tax in [[cur:498|Shi'me]], and barley stands at a Shi'me and two-thirds the measure in the market at Movosh, and the tax must be paid in coin by the autumn whether the barley sells or not. When we complain, he writes the complaint down, and it goes to Ane, and from Ane nothing returns.

**III.**

They took our sons for the wars at Ivu. Three summers running the recruiter came, and twice the young men came back, and the third time fewer came back. The heralds cried a great victory and the [[evt:25562|Treaty of Ivu]], and we were told to rejoice, and we hung branches on the door of the shrine, as we were told. The mother of the boy who did not come back hung a branch too. That is the virtue they teach us now: to rejoice on command.

**IV.**

The priests of Ane say the wise man withdraws from appetite and wealth, and asks only for justice and moderation. I have no quarrel with that. We have been made very wise in this village. We have withdrawn from wealth entirely. What I ask is the justice part, and the moderation of the measure.

**V.**

I do not hate the men of Ane. The soldier who sleeps in the house of my brother mends the roof without being asked, and he too has a mother somewhere. But a village that is ruled from far away becomes a thing that is counted, and not a thing that is heard. We are a line in a ledger in Ane. Let this, then, be our line in another kind of book: the village of the Three Wells, below Movosh, which was of Mii, and remembers.
