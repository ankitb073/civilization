**CITY-STATE OF NYINSHITLAZ**

**REPORT OF THE FIRST MAGISTRATE TO THE COUNCIL OF THE CITY ON THE NEGOTIATIONS AT FYINAN**

*Laid before the Council and ordered to be printed. [[set:123|Nyinshitlaz]], 2 Toe 2605 of the Iishyu.*

Members of the Council,

On my return from Fyinan I have the honour to lay before you an account of the negotiations which I conducted there in person, and which ended in the [[evt:46101|Treaty of Fyinan]], now deposited in the archive of the City under seal.

**1. The situation on my arrival.** When I left [[pol:1925|Nyinshitlaz]] by the night train the Council will recall that our forces had already obtained every object that the war could reasonably obtain. At the [[evt:46065|Battle of Pivithie]], in the second summer of the war, and again at the [[evt:46088|Second Battle of Fyitlivul]] the year before last, the army of Fyinan was driven from positions its own newspapers had called impregnable. At the [[evt:46099|Battle of Fyinan]] it was beaten beneath its own walls, and the [[evt:46100|Second Siege of Fyinan]] closed the last road by which the city could be fed. I do not trouble the Council with the enemy's losses in these engagements; Fyinan has never published them, and I doubt it dares to. Our own officers, who walked those fields, will tell any member who asks what they saw there.

**2. The opening of talks.** I was received at Fyinan on the second day after the guns ceased. I record that I went unarmed and with a staff of six, through streets in which our shells had fallen, and that I was not insulted. I record also that the first proposal of the Fyinan delegation was that each party should withdraw to the frontier of four years ago, as though the war had been a misunderstanding between neighbours. I reminded them that it was not Nyinshitlaz that had closed the river tolls, armed the border posts and told its people that our city would not fight. That proposal was withdrawn within the hour.

**3. The meeting with the ruler of Fyinan.** On the third day I met [[per:45306|Poafoyatl]] alone, with one interpreter each. He is a courteous man and an obstinate one, and I believe he understood his position better than his ministers did. He asked for the restoration of nothing. He asked only that the terms be such as his people could bear without shame. I told him that the government of [[pol:1942|Fyinan]] had made shame inevitable when it chose war, but that Nyinshitlaz did not require more than was just.

**4. The terms.** The Council has the full text before it. In brief:

(a) Fyinan cedes to Nyinshitlaz the two border districts contested since the beginning of the [[evt:46050|Nyinshitlaz–Fyinan War]], with their roads, wells and telegraph lines.

(b) A truce of twenty years is sworn between the two cities, before the priests of both.

(c) No indemnity is demanded. I considered one. I judged that a beaten neighbour who owes us money is a neighbour who will look for a reason not to pay it, and that the districts are worth more to us than any sum in [[cur:1071|Khuiu]] that Fyinan could raise.

**5. The signing.** The treaty was signed at [[set:57|Fyinan]] by Poafoyatl and by myself, [[per:45122|Oitl Foyatl]], on behalf of this City. Our priests of the [[mov:57|Iishyu]] offered the prayer for the release of the suffering of the dead of both sides. I joined in it, and I invite the Council to do the same at its next sitting.

**6. Recommendation.** That the Council ratify the treaty without amendment; that the casualty returns of our own forces be published in full, as the citizens are entitled to see them; and that a vote of thanks be recorded to the army, which made this peace possible, and to the people of the City, who paid for it.

I have the honour to be the Council's servant,

**OITL FOYATL**, First Magistrate
