From the camp at [[set:132|Kanindu]], to the Council of the Empire and to the magistrates of [[set:9|Dagafu]], the secretary of the king sends greeting. If you and the city are well, it is well; the king and the army are well.

I write by the king's command, on the evening of the battle, in the year 3280 of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]], so that the city may hear the truth from us before it hears rumour from the roads.

**The battle.** This morning the king, [[per:30492|Nabingu]], led the army of [[pol:1279|Dagafu]] in his own person against the host of [[pol:1276|Luthe]], which [[per:30750|Naguvu]] commanded. The enemy had drawn up on the rising ground before Kanindu, with the stream on their left and the vineyards behind them. Our host was the greater, and we do not pretend otherwise; but the greater host is not the surer one when the lesser stands on its own soil and has beaten us before. From the second hour to the ninth the lines pressed against one another, and neither yielded. At the ninth hour the king brought up the reserve on the right, and the enemy's left gave way, and then the whole of their line; and Naguvu withdrew toward the hills in good order, which I set down because it is true, and because a victory over brave men is the more honourable for it.

The field is ours. The cost I will not hide from the Council. We have lost, as near as the officers can count, some fifteen thousand men, and the enemy scarcely fewer. Let no one in the city call this a cheap day.

**The place.** The Council will not need me to remind it what field this is. It was here, two and twenty years ago, in [[evt:30810|the former war]], that the king's father fell at the head of his army, and here that [[evt:30838|the day of Kanindu]] was lost; and in the year following came the defeat [[evt:30857|at Gyi]], and [[evt:30858|the peace of Gyi]], to which the king, then a child, set his seal because the Council advised it. We kept that peace. For fifteen years not a spear of Dagafu crossed into [[reg:27|Luthe]], and for five years more we sought redress by embassy before we sought it by arms. Let the world say what it pleases of Dagafu; it cannot say that we broke faith.

Today the king stood where his father fell. When the field was won he did not cheer, nor let the trumpets sound. He went bareheaded to the place where the old standard was taken, and stood there for the space of a prayer, and then gave orders that the enemy dead be buried with our own.

**A reflection, which the king permits.** The teachers of the Uthungala say that time runs in one line from the making of the world to its judgement, and that no day returns. Then this day is not a repayment of that one; the dead of the former war are not raised by the dead of this. What is asked of us is not vengeance but justice, and moderation in the use of fortune. A city that grows drunk on one victory is already halfway to its next defeat.

**The requests of the army.** [[evt:31128|This war]] is not finished, and I would deceive the Council if I wrote that it was. [[evt:31145|Today's battle]] has opened the road; it has not ended the journey. Luthe has other armies and other generals, and Naguvu lives.

The king therefore asks:

- that the pay of the legions, now two months owed, be sent in [[cur:713|Shiègi]] of full weight, and not in the clipped coin of the frontier markets;
- that the grain of the river districts be carted to the depots without delay, for the price at the camp has already risen;
- that the new levies be trained before they are sent, and not after;
- and that the city hold no triumph, and raise no arch, until the war is ended and its end is known.

The king commends himself to the Council and to the people of Dagafu, and asks the prayers of all who keep the inner light.

Farewell.
