title: Lament for Khie'a of the High Gate

O city, your lament is bitter. O [[set:83|Khie'a]], your lament is bitter.

Khie'a of the high gate, Khie'a of the broad street,  
Khie'a whose granaries were full, whose quays were full,  
the house that was the head of the lands, the head is cut from the body.

[[dei:0|Atlie]] of the sky looked down, and he turned his face away.  
[[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river looked up, and he went down into his bed.  
[[dei:1|Uelti]] of wisdom closed the house of tablets; the scribes went out weeping.  
O city, your lament is bitter.

Once the governors came up the river road with tribute.  
They came with oxen, they came with barley, they came with silver,  
tina upon [[cur:110|tina]], weighed at the gate of the god.  
Once the far towns sent their sons to the host of [[pol:204|Khie'a]].  
Now the road is empty. Now the tribute does not come.  
O city, your lament is bitter.

Who brought this upon you? Hear, and I will tell it.  
In the days of the fathers [[evt:6197|Esiesh Ada took the seat]] that was not his.  
The order of the house was broken; the seal was stolen from its box.  
The governors saw it. The governors remembered it.

Then the strangers came down. The Ryethti came down from the far country,  
they came with their herds, they came with their tents,  
they came in their thousands into Mikhukhizhi,  
[[evt:6232|the people of Ryethti came]] and did not go back.  
Their fires were on our hills. Their sheep ate our barley.  
O city, your lament is bitter.

Then the king sent the host against Bäni, to take its land.  
[[evt:6326|The war against Bäni]] went out with drums, with spears, with banners.  
And while the host was far, the house fell down behind it.

In the 179th year of Khie'a, in that year it was done.  
The house of Khie'a was split like a log beneath the axe.  
This was [[evt:6358|the breaking of the house of Khie'a]].  
One half stayed with the city; one half went away.  
[[pol:275|Mi'ositl]] took its own seal, Mi'ositl took its own king,  
Mi'ositl shut its gate against the city of the high gate.  
O city, your lament is bitter.

[[pol:274|Khie'a]] remains, but it is a small Khie'a.  
The palace stands, but its halls are half empty.  
The king sits, but his feet do not reach the far rivers.  
The war against Bäni was not finished; it was let fall like a spear from a dead hand.  
The host came home, and there was no empire to come home to.

Weep, women of the broad street. Weep, men of the quays.  
Weep, old men who remember the great house.  
The child does not know what was lost; the child plays in the dust of the gate.

[[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of war, why did you not stand at our right hand?  
[[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth, why did you let the strangers eat our fields?  
[[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon, you saw it each night, and you said nothing.  
O city, your lament is bitter.

Yet hear, O city: the teaching of [[mov:0|La'im]] is the wheel.  
All things turn on the great wheel; what ends will begin again.  
The barley is cut, and the barley returns.  
The moon is eaten, and the moon returns.  
So the house of Khie'a will return to its high gate.  
So the tribute will come again up the river road.

Eighteen years I have sung this lament, and the city is not yet whole.  
Let the singer who comes after me sing it until it is whole.

Written in the 197th year of Khie'a. O city, your lament is bitter.
