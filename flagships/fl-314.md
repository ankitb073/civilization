*[Obverse. Sealed with the king's seal on the upper edge.]*

To the steward of my house in [[set:10|Navnga]], to the keeper of my storerooms, say:

Thus says [[per:738|Nathbikafbafen]], the king, the strong one, the king of [[pol:8|Navnga]], your lord.

With me it is well. With my house it is well. With my spears and my donkeys and my camp it is well. May it be well with you. May [[dei:20|Nothol]] keep the fire in my hearth. May [[dei:24|Navbuthen]] keep the water in my canals. May the gods of the [[mov:3|Ryethki]] keep my city, each in his place, each in her place.

Hear now the word of the camp, and do not let the women weep in the streets, for the word is not as the liars say it.

The men of [[pol:27|Vuvo]] are oath-breakers. Did they not swear to me at the [[evt:563|Treaty of Vuvu]]? Did they not lay their hands on the bread and the salt before me, in the first year of my kingship, when I broke them at the [[evt:562|Battle of Vuvu]], I myself with my own spear? They swore. They forgot. Their silver came slow, their tongues came quick. Therefore I went down into [[reg:16|Yida]] against them a second time, as my fathers went down, as I went down before. This is the [[evt:624|Second Navnga–Vuvo War]], and the fault of it is on the head of Vuvo.

Hear now what was done at Vuvo. My general [[per:824|Lukhnelun]], the man I raised from the dust, went out with four thousand spears against the walls of [[set:56|Vuvo]]. [[per:825|Madun]], the general of Vuvo, did not come out to fight as a man fights. He hid behind the ditch. He hid behind the reeds. He set his men in the mud where the donkeys sink. Then [[dei:21|Duminu]] of the storm turned his face from us that day, for the rain came down, and the ground went soft under our feet.

So it was at the [[evt:626|Battle of Vuvu]], the second one. The field was left to the Vuvo. I do not hide it. But hear me: of the Vuvo, the dead were more than the reeds of the river. Their dead lay in the ditch, their dead lay in the reeds, their dead lay in the mud they made for us. Who can count them? Not I. Not the scribe. Of ours, some hundreds lay down; they were brave, and their names are with the priests.

Lukhnelun has drawn back to the river. He is not broken. I am with him. The camp is strong.

Now, steward, hear my command, and do it quickly.

Send me barley, two hundred loads, on donkeys, by the river road. Send me bronze for spearheads. Send me the young men of the third quarter, who have not yet gone out. Pay to each man who goes out his [[cur:2|Mulshi]] of silver from my storeroom, the full weight, and do not let the weighers cheat them, for a man cheated does not fight.

Let the priests pour oil to Navbuthen at the river gate every morning. Let them burn a ram to Nothol at the new moon. Let them go up to [[dei:25|Novegu]] and ask for wisdom for the king, that the king may see the way through the reeds.

And hear this also, and keep it close. The men of the other border have risen; this is the [[evt:625|Tivintusa–Navnga War]], and they have come against our land while my spears are in Yida. Shut the gates at dusk. Set men on the walls. Do not let the merchants of the east road come in without a word from the steward. Write to me what you hear, every word, quickly.

The gods of Navnga are with the king. The oath-breakers will be broken. As it was before, so it will be again; the wheel turns.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in the camp in Yida, in the 25th year of Nathbikafbafen, in the month of the rains.  
> Let the runner not sleep until this tablet is in the steward's hand.
