*[Obverse.]*

To [[per:48|Izho'tla]], my son, the strong one, the seed of my house, say: thus says [[per:4|Shoshsizhash]], king of [[pol:4|Wa'mi]], your father.

With me it is not well. The fever of the river-month has come into my bones and will not go out. The priests have given the black kid; [[dei:6|Nili]] of the river took the kid but did not take the fever. I am old. Thirty years I have sat on the seat of Wa'mi, since the first year, since there was a seat. I have seen thirty floods. I will not see another.

Hear me now, for this is not my word only.

Six years ago your mother lay sick, as I lie sick now. You were in the hills with the spears; you did not come in time. Before she went down, she called me to her bed, and she said: "When I am in the earth, and when you are going down to the earth after me, and when my son sits on the seat, tell him these words. Tell him that they are his mother's words." I swore it to her by [[dei:8|Nish'il]] of the sky. I have kept the words in my heart six years. Now I give them to the clay, so that they do not go down with me.

These are the words of your mother, the queen. Hear them as if she spoke.

"My son, you are a man and not a child. You have a beard and a spear and a house of your own. But a man on the seat is a child again, for the seat is new to him. Do not be ashamed to ask.

"Do not let the men of the palace quarrel at your door. Hear one, then hear the other, then decide. Do not decide before you have heard both.

"Give the gods their due. Give the fire to [[dei:7|Yub]] in the morning, give the fat to her in the evening; the hearth of the palace must not go cold. When the storm comes, give the bull to [[dei:9|Shimintlo]], and do not wait until the roof is off.

"Love your wife. Do not take the bread from the mouths of the poor in a year of hunger. Remember that prosperity is a sign that the gods are pleased; when the granary is empty, ask first what you have neglected, and only then ask what the people have neglected.

"Do not go out to war for pride. Go out when [[dei:11|Nizhashta]] calls, and not before. A king who goes out for pride comes home on a litter."

These are her words. I have added nothing. I have taken nothing away.

*[Reverse.]*

Now my words, and they are few.

Keep the city. Keep the wall of [[set:26|Wa'mi]]. Keep the priests of [[mov:1|Ya'cuam]] in their order, each in his place, each in his turn, as the gods are each in their place. Keep the scribes, for they keep the words; without the scribes, your mother's words would have gone into the earth with me.

The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. I end; you begin. When the wheel brings you to your own sick-bed, call your son, and give him these words also, and your own.

May [[dei:10|Masto]] of the sun look on you every day. May Nish'il keep the sky over the city. May the earth be soft for me, and may I find your mother where she is waiting.

I have pressed my seal. It is enough.

*[Left edge, in another hand.]*

> The seal of the old king, broken before the new king in the 1st year of Izho'tla.
