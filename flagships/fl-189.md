**ON THE RELIABILITY OF THE CHRONICLES**  
*An essay by the Chairman, [[per:47103|Nazhigi]], printed for the schools and reading-rooms of [[pol:1638|Khishkhih]]. [[set:85|Nambi]], 2 Bini 2526 of the [[mov:70|Vimikhulu]].*

Comrades, students, teachers:

Our faith teaches that teachers are to be honoured but that no one owns the truth. It is a hard saying for a historian, because every chronicle ever written was written by someone who hoped to own it. I set down here, for the use of the schools, what the archive teaches us about that hope, and how a modern state guards against it.

**I. The arithmetic of vanity.** Take three wars of the age of clay, each known to us from rival accounts, and set the accounts beside what the archive, by patient comparison of tablets, tallies and boundary stones, has established.

In the [[evt:548|first war of Navnga against Vuvo]], some forty-seven centuries ago, the archive finds that Navnga began the war, fought two battles and won both, lost 693 men to Vuvo's 2,967, and took 8 districts. The Navnga chronicler reports that Vuvo began it, that Navnga lost only 298 men while killing 5,583, and that 14 districts were won. The Vuvo chronicler reports that no battle was fought at all, and that the affair ended in an honourable peace.

In the [[evt:1110|war of Talenonoy against Shue'ethie]] the archive counts six battles, three won by each side; 3,589 dead for Talenonoy and 5,811 for Shue'ethie; the commander Luesh'anieme killed; 6 districts to the victor, Talenonoy. The Talenonoy account counts four battles, of which it won three; it gives its own dead as 858 and the enemy's as 13,309; it claims 14 districts. The Shue'ethie account admits six battles but gives Talenonoy 6,430 dead to its own 1,968, and calls the result a stalemate over a few villages.

In the [[evt:1416|second war of Virnado against Talenonoy]], the archive finds three battles, all won by Virnado; 465 dead for Virnado against 2,460 for Talenonoy; 19 districts taken. The Virnado account blames Talenonoy for the war and claims 45 districts. The Talenonoy account admits two battles and calls the whole thing a stalemate.

Observe the pattern, for it is the same in every case. The victor blames the vanquished for beginning the war. The victor halves his own dead and multiplies the enemy's. The victor doubles his conquests. The vanquished, for his part, does not deny the battles so much as he denies their meaning: there was no war, or there was a stalemate, or there was an honourable peace. Every scribe in the age of clay wrote for a king, and no king ever paid for bad news.

**II. The arithmetic of ruin.** Nor were the old chroniclers more honest about the fall of their own states. The [[evt:1589|collapse of Yida]], the [[evt:2822|collapse of Talenonoy]], the [[evt:4077|collapse of Tallin]], the [[evt:5441|collapse of Mii]]: the archive records more than a dozen such breakings between forty-five and thirty-eight centuries ago, Tallin breaking twice, Yida twice, Nelo twice. The court poets of those states wrote laments for their cities and blamed the gods, or traitors at the gate, or the weather. Not one that we possess blames the court.

**III. The modern remedy.** How, then, does a state of the present age avoid the vanity of the past? By statistics; by the wire; by publication. When the armies of the aggressor crossed our frontier, the bulletins of this government reported every engagement within the day. At the [[evt:48356|battle of Dambüzhi]] and again at the [[evt:48362|battle of Dadunde]], the enemy was defeated, and the people of Khishkhih read of it over their breakfast. By the [[evt:48363|treaty of Dadunde]], which I had the honour to sign, the aggressor became a vassal of Khishkhih and swore twenty years of peace. These facts are not my boast. They are in the gazette, and any citizen may check them.

So too with [[evt:48330|the Red Ague]], which still afflicts our cities. The government has not hidden it. The sanitary returns are printed weekly, district by district, and the losses, which are grievous, are mourned openly.

**IV. Conclusion.** The wheel turns, comrades. What ended in the age of clay begins again in every age, and the temptation of the scribe is eternal. I do not say that the Party is free of it. I say only that we have built the machinery that makes its temptations visible: the census, the ledger, the open archive. Honour the teachers; read the chronicles; and count for yourselves.
