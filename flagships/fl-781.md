*In the 13th year of [[per:13865|Ma'she Foyatl]], king of [[pol:410|Pibetie]].*

**The day the ewes went up to the high pasture.** Grey sky. Grey sky in the morning, grey sky at noon. [[dei:0|Etlie]] of the sky has a cloth over his face. Five years he has worn it. Five years the sun is a white coin in the cloud, and a man can look at it and not blink.

**The third day.** Snow on the high pasture in the month of lambing. Snow in the month of lambing, as in the first grey year. I brought the ewes down again. Two lambs dead in the night. I skinned them. I put the skins on the orphan lambs and gave them to the mothers. One mother took. One mother did not.

**The fifth day.** I think of the first year. In the first year the grey came down on the grass. It was not snow, it was soft like ash from the hearth, and the sheep would not eat it, and they coughed. A salt-trader came through in that year. He said: a mountain far away has burst open, a mountain called Tsimi, and the ash of it is in the sky. This is the [[evt:13850|Eruption of Mount Tsimi]], the men of the town call it now. I did not believe the salt-trader. A mountain far away, and the ash here? Now I believe him.

**The eighth day.** The grass on the low ground is short. The grass is short, and yellow at the root. The ewes are thin. The wool is thin. The wool-buyer will give little.

**The tenth day.** The king's men came up the valley. They took twelve wethers for the army at [[set:91|Pibetie]]. They gave a tally-stick for them. They said: the army has beaten the rebels at the [[evt:13979|battle of Pivithie]], the third time, and the [[evt:13980|Suppression of the Myinve Revolt]] is finished, and the rebels have put their seal on the tablet. They said: be glad. I am glad. I would be gladder with twelve wethers.

**The thirteenth day.** I went down to the village for barley. A measure of barley is two [[cur:224|Iwso]] and a half, and more. Before the grey years it was two Iwso and a little. I gave a fleece and a cheese and the last of my copper. My wife made bread with chaff in it. The children ate it and asked for more.

**The fifteenth day.** The moon came up red over the ridge. [[dei:4|Lashla'e]] of the moon, why are you red? Lashla'e, are you angry, are you sick? The old women say: the moon has been red since the grey came. The old women say: the moon has blood in his eye.

**The eighteenth day.** A good thing. The sun came out at noon, a little, for the time it takes to milk a ewe. The lambs ran in it. I sat on the rock and held my face up. My wife came out of the hut and held her face up. We did not speak.

**The twentieth day.** I made the offering to [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] of the earth. A cheese and a little milk, at the stone by the spring. Muevas'e, mother, make the grass. Make the grass long. The sheep are your children. The sheep are my children.

**The twenty-second day.** The headman says the priests in the town have read the signs, and the [[cli:82|Volcanic Winter of Mount Tsimi]] will pass. They say this every year. I do not know. I count the ewes in the evening. Forty-one. In the first grey year, sixty-three.

**The twenty-fifth day.** Grey sky. A little warmer at noon. The ewes are up on the middle pasture. I go up after them.
