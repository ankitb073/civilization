title: Naming an Age from Inside: The First Years of the Age of Networks

Ages are usually named by those who come after them. This one we are naming while we live in it. The convention now current among colleagues dates the Age of Networks from AY 4947 and the [[evt:47877|Invention of Electronic computing in Baani]], which the record credits to [[per:46776|Ivbin]], then a man not yet thirty. I do not quarrel with the date. I do quarrel with the confidence. On the evidence of the age's first eight years, the name describes a hope rather than a record. What the record of AY 4947–4955 shows, above all, is war: at least seven wars, fought with armies as large as any the record knows, and so entangled with one another that they form, if anything, the true network of the age.

## Seven wars

The [[evt:47926|Yisamashtsi–Bakh War]] ended in 4947 with victory for [[pol:1952|Yisamashtsi]], which at Tunavükhini put more than one and a half million men against some forty thousand. In the [[evt:47942|Khishkhis–Kabaf War]] (4946–4949), our own [[pol:1638|Khishkhis]] fought the city-state of [[pol:1990|Kabaf]] and annexed it. At the [[evt:47960|Battle of Athnambi (AY 4948)]] our armies numbered more than twenty-four million against fewer than one million. I add, because a scholar should, that the record's count of our own dead in that war runs to some five million, about six times Kabaf's. That figure does not appear in our victory celebrations. It is in the record.

The city-state of [[pol:2002|Navnge]] was fought over in four of the five years from 4948. In the [[evt:47951|Eleventh Yidhe Trade War]] (4947–4949), [[pol:1674|Mbilu]] defeated it and sacked its city; in the [[evt:48002|Second Shivange–Navnge War]] (4950–4952), [[pol:1863|Shivange]] finished what Mbilu had begun and annexed what remained. [[pol:1961|Divigvivinge]] fared little better. In the [[evt:47969|Virathkhanu War of Independence]] (4949–4951), [[pol:2007|Virathkhanu]] broke away from it, winning at the [[evt:48005|Battle of Himi (AY 4950)]], where a Divigvivinge commander fell, and again at the [[evt:48007|Battle of Himi (AY 4951)]]. In the same years Yidhe attacked Divigvivinge in the [[evt:48003|War of the Divigvivinge Succession]] (4950–4954), fought a second battle at Himi in 4951, and extracted an indemnity in 4954. Divigvivinge thus fought two wars at once, on the same ground, against a rebel and a claimant.

The seventh war is still undecided as I write. In the [[evt:47943|First Zhigu–Biniynge War]], begun in 4946, fortune has swung from side to side: [[pol:1963|Biniynge]] won at the [[evt:47961|Battle of Taalinnuy (AY 4948)]], [[pol:1991|Zhigu]] at Nunnga in 4949, Biniynge at the [[evt:48004|Third Battle of Tathtidh]] in 4950, and Zhigu again at Taalinnuy in 4952. I make no prediction.

## What the record can and cannot tell us

Set against these wars, the invention that gives the age its name has, so far, left no mark on the record of events. No battle, treaty or collapse of the past eight years can be connected to it. That is not a reason to doubt its importance; the printing press of AY 4593 left little immediate mark either, and only later readers saw what it had begun. But it is a reason to hold the name loosely. The engines of the previous age supplied the means for armies of tens of millions, and [[cli:251|The Industrial Warming]], under way since AY 4818 and already tied by the record to famine after famine, has not paused for the change of name. An age called after a machine may yet be remembered for its wars, or for its weather.

Contemporary history is the hardest kind. We know the battles and not the treaties still to come; we can count the dead but not yet weigh what they died for. Earlier ages were named with hindsight, and hindsight chooses what to remember: the people who lived through the Age of Sail and Print would, I think, have named it for its plague. Our successors may judge us more kindly, or less. What I can say with confidence is narrower. In its first eight years, the Age of Networks has been an age of interlocking wars on the largest scale the record knows, and our own state has been among its principal belligerents.
