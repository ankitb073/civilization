Most of the rulers in this series acquired a name from posterity: the Great, the Conqueror, the Brave, the Last. [[per:38819|Maanishifin]], who reigned over the [[pol:1562|Yidhe]] empire for fifty-three years, from AY 4121 to 4174, acquired none. His personal name means "thunder glory", and his three victory stelae speak in the usual formulas of a great king. The record that lets us check them tells a harder story. In his reign the armies of Yidhe lost more than 150,000 men. Most of that was in a war he inherited and lost, and some in a war against his own brother. Then famine killed a million and a half of his subjects within a few years. Perhaps posterity found no word for such a reign. This chapter tries to find a fair one.

## A war inherited

He was born in 4105 into a young and violent house. His grandfather Bilzhiyshen had seized the throne in 4081 and is remembered as the Usurper. His mother, [[per:38489|Bilkulufath]], called the Brave, came to the throne at twenty and ruled for thirty years. His father, who has no name in the record, died in 4118. A sister born in the same year as Maanishifin, perhaps a twin, died at three. A younger brother, Lithzhin, survived. The family belonged to the Jego people and ruled a great empire of some thirty-four million people, governed from Taulin.

In 4119 Bilkulufath began the [[evt:39320|Second Yidhe–Binge War]], a war of conquest against the [[pol:1565|Binge]] empire. In 4120 she led more than 130,000 men to a victory and was killed on the field. The [[evt:39365|Accession of Maanishifin]] in 4121 gave her war to a son of sixteen. No regent is recorded.

## Eight years of defeat

The war went badly from the moment he inherited it. A royal army was beaten in 4121. In 4123 the young king took command himself at the [[evt:39379|Battle of Nudhuruigirimu (AY 4123)]], against the Binge general [[per:38764|Bumetaalin]]. He had 82,000 men against 64,000. He lost, and nearly fourteen thousand of his soldiers died. The record lists eight battles in the war after his mother's death and gives Yidhe two victories. Neither was under his command. In the worst years, 4124, 4129 and 4130, Yidhe lost some twenty-five thousand men in each of three battles.

His rock relief at Nudhuruigirimu, cut in 4130, is the most audacious document of the reign. It celebrates a victory of 4128 on that same field, won by one of his generals, and claims 124,900 enemy dead. The record gives about 20,500. Within a year of its carving the war was lost. The [[evt:39450|Treaty of Zhidhzhinge (AY 4131)]] obliged Yidhe, the attacker, to pay Binge an indemnity of 345,612. It was signed for Binge by [[per:38727|Lòpavbukhin]], who had been a child-king at three and was now eighteen. Over twelve years the war had killed some 125,000 soldiers of Yidhe against 77,000 of Binge. By the record's own measure, order within the empire fell that decade to almost nothing.

## The brother's revolt

In 4141 his brother [[per:38821|Lithzhin]] took a hundred and twenty-five districts out of the empire as the [[pol:1676|Biniynge]] empire, and the [[evt:39528|Second Yidhe Civil War]] began. It was a war between near equals. Lithzhin's forces in its four battles numbered between 77,000 and 119,000 men. The royal armies sent against them numbered from 65,000 to more than 140,000. Every battle was a royal victory, and in every one the rebels lost more men than the king. At the [[evt:39567|Battle of Budh (AY 4146)]], where Maanishifin commanded in person, the rebels brought some 92,000 men against his 75,000 and lost about 3,800 to his 1,700. Over six years the war cost Yidhe some sixteen thousand soldiers and the rebels fifty-six thousand.

The Biniynge account of the war, written in 4155 by the scribe Taaruyin, admits only about half the record's count of its own dead, and raises the royal losses by more than half. Even so, the rebel chronicler strays far less from the record than the king's own stelae for the war. It ends: "the gates were opened to the enemy. The gods had turned their faces from us." The [[evt:39578|Suppression of the Biniynge Revolt]] was signed at Thinirnge in 4147 by the two brothers, and it granted an amnesty. Lithzhin lived another fourteen years. Maanishifin's two stelae for the war claim 30,300 and 65,500 enemy dead, where the record gives about 3,800 and 15,500. Whatever else he was, he did not kill his brother.

## Famine and the last wars

His queen died in 4151. In 4153 Yidhe went to war again, in the [[evt:39683|First Yidhe–Piekhkunga War]]. In 4155, the year of its decisive battle, famine struck the empire's home region of Yidhe and killed some 1.5 million people, nearly one in ten of those who lived there. That same year the king led nearly 180,000 men, the largest army of his life, against fewer than 16,000 under [[per:39170|Yelefpen]] at the [[evt:39695|Battle of Tiyinal (AY 4155)]]. He won, at a cost of some 1,200 dead. How such a host was fed in a famine year the record does not say. We may suspect the villages it marched through could have told us. The [[evt:39699|Treaty of Pikhtunge (AY 4156)]], sworn with the Piekhkunga queen [[per:38854|Kuemduth]], gave Yidhe thirteen districts, and Pikhtunge was sacked. A later Yidhe chronicle claims that Piekhkunga "broke the peace" and that the stolen land was returned. The Piekhkunga chronicler claims that Yidhe lost more than four thousand at Tiyinal. Both are wrong, in opposite directions.

In 4159 Kawnunuy, under its queen [[per:39024|Shiwukyenuth]], invaded. The [[evt:39717|First Kawnunuy–Yidhe War]] ended in 4161 with the [[evt:39768|Treaty of Zhinlampive (AY 4161)]], under which Kawnunuy paid Yidhe an indemnity of 72,447. That was about a fifth of what Maanishifin had paid Binge thirty years before. It was the only war of his reign that ended with money flowing toward him, and it was a war he had not begun. Lithzhin died the same year.

## The empty house

His only children were twin daughters born in 4123, the year of his first defeat. One died in 4168 and the other in 4172. Maanishifin died a natural death in 4174, aged sixty-nine, with no child or brother living. The [[evt:39864|Usurpation of Tutaalin I]] followed immediately. The record marks it as a crisis, and its measure of order in the empire stood at zero. [[per:39384|Tutaalin I]], a man of forty-seven with no recorded parentage, ended the house of Bilzhiyshen after ninety-three years. Yidhe itself survived it by more than two centuries and fell only in 4387.

What should we call him? He was not a conqueror, though he took a province. He was not merely unlucky, since he chose to go on fighting. He was not cruel, since the brother who rebelled against him was spared. Perhaps the plainest word is the truest. He was a king who went on spending lives, his soldiers' as freely as his enemies', and the record that counts them is the only monument that tells the truth about him.
