[[per:32920|Yielenuyen]] headed the [[hou:1355|House of Nieyney]] for five years. The house lasted two hundred and eighty-four. Of the 2,505 merchant houses, banks and companies the record knows, none lasted longer; the next, the House of Dundivu, managed 267. Of the founder the record keeps a few lines: a Jinru man of the [[pol:1328|Pienpedhzhe]] Empire, born in AY 3529, dead of natural causes in 3560 at thirty-one, father of one recorded son, and author of one act, the [[evt:33324|Founding of the House of Nieyney]] in 3555. No letter, seal or ledger of his survives; on his parents, marriage and reasons the record is silent. What can be recovered is the world that shaped him, and what grew from the one thing he did.

## A city with a long memory

[[set:6|Zhinontunu]], the house's seat, was a port nearly four thousand years old. An unnamed poet of AY 314, mourning its fourth sack, begged: "Who will sing a fifth? Let there be no fifth." (PAL·PO·00068). By Yielenuyen's birth there had been twelve. The latest came in 3471, when the army of Pienpedhzhe itself sacked the city, then held by the kings of Tuevezhenga. Its king Khuetnakhunuefen afterwards "built this wall in Zhienuntunu" (PAL·IN·05734), but by 3490 his kingdom was gone and the city was Pienpedhzhe's. The record's century census counts 180,074 people there in AY 3400 and 15,492 in AY 3500. It does not divide that loss between sword, flight and hunger; the next count, in 3600, finds 79,518. Yielenuyen grew up somewhere between those figures, in a city its own empire had sacked within living memory.

## A war over faith

He was born under a queen, Piemtaleth the Pious, and was three when Lienunushal opened a holy war on Pienpedhzhe that lasted until he was eighteen. In 3539, when he was ten, Piemtaleth died, her son [[per:32814|Natdapuefnen]] succeeded, and the realm took the [[mov:58|Veshethmekh]] faith of one divine power and a final judgement. The war went on regardless. Lienunushal's lament for its dead, written in 3549 (PAL·PO·00012), explains why: the people of Pienpedhzhe "took the Name into their mouths and not into their hearts, and they would not give up the land".

Pienpedhzhe's court lied differently. Viebanaven's chronicle of the war, written at [[set:164|Pinpidhzhi]] in 3556 and now water-stained (PAL·CH·05938), admits one defeat, blamed on a rising river, and passes over the four other battles Lienunushal won, two of them under Natdapuefnen's own command. Of his victory at Nuwkennga in 3541 it says: "The scribes counted 11,765 enemy dead; we lost 660." His basalt stele from that field, which I translated after its excavation in 4944, raises the enemy dead to 27,300 (PAL·IN·02501). The record gives 4,967 losses for Lienunushal and 1,611 for Pienpedhzhe. Two years after the conversion, the stele is still raised "for Buevme, his lady", the old war goddess of the Jinru.

A neighbour's accounts show what it cost traders. The day-book of the House of Athuwbu, kept at Nuwkennga in 3533 and now missing several leaves (PAL·LE·01888), notes in its margin: "The caravan guards ask double because of the Holy War of Lienunushal against Pienpedhzhe." The Treaty of Munu ended the war in 3547 with a fifteen-year truce and nothing more; the lament's line that Lienunushal gave "no city in that peace" is, for once, accurate.

## The founding and the heir

In 3555, at twenty-six, Yielenuyen founded his house. The record does not say where his capital came from. He dealt in timber, grain, copper and spices. In the city's prices for the decade of the founding, a beam of timber fetched just over a fifth of a [[cur:747|Rielu]] (11.2 grams of silver); a measure of grain or a mina of copper about 0.6; a mina of spices about 4. The route table of AY 3500 joins the city to five towns, which the record also names as the house's partners: inland Puthnimdu, the busiest road, and the ports of Uthtafpu, Ngiunge, Pikhtunge and Khaadhange. Puthnimdu lay in the Yidhe Empire, Pikhtunge and Khaadhange in the Kingdom of Yaaril.

The survey of the 3550s found the empire at 156 districts and 12,252,641 people, its fullest in his lifetime. He died in 3560. Of the five houses founded anywhere in the 3550s, two ended that year, one of them founded the same year as his.

[[per:33046|Upiemten]] took the house at twenty-two. The record calls him Yielenuyen's son yet dates his birth to 3538, when Yielenuyen was nine; either a date is wrong or the word covers an adopted heir. The Treaty of Anavun of 3566 (PAL·TR·02165) ceded forty-seven districts to Ayshi, imposed 11,022,768 Rielu in reparations, and by its third article provided that "the merchants of Ayshi shall trade in the markets of Byimbidizhi free of all tolls" (Byimbidizhi is Pienpedhzhe). By the end of the 3560s the realm held 109 districts and 9,948,445 people. Upiemten kept the house for fifty-three years.

## Two hundred and eighty-four years

The house had eleven heads, all men; seven of the ten after the founder are recorded as sons of the head before. It came through the Black Fever of 3623, the collapse of Pienpedhzhe in 3704, after which Zhinontunu became the capital of the Kingdom of Zhienunkunu, and the sack of 3763. Its one surviving document is the testament of its fourth head, Zhieyununen, a papyrus of 3650 from the cathedral archive at Zhinontunu (PAL·WL·00645). He styles himself "head of the house of Nieyney at Zhienunkunu" and bequeaths grain and copper from the warehouse, two of the founder's four goods ninety-five years on, with 14.1 Rielu to the faith "that the priests may pray for me". To his son he leaves "the house, its books, its credit and its debts".

Of those books not a leaf survives. The house was dissolved in 3839, at the death of Vievuvueman, its eleventh head. The rulers of Pienpedhzhe left steles that multiplied the dead. Yielenuyen left a house that outlived their empire by 135 years. Whether he meant it to, the record cannot say.
