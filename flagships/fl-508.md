*[Obverse. A prayer for recitation by a priest, with the people's response marked "People".]*

O [[dei:16|Domamnol]], father of the high sky, hear us!  
O [[dei:15|Wenba]], mother of the earth, who receives the dead, hear us!  
O [[dei:17|Nedhekhte]] of the grain, O [[dei:18|Ewa]] of the fire, O [[dei:19|Yenan]] of the moon, hear us!  
O [[dei:14|Wolme]], lady of the spear, who has given us the field, hear us!

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

The [[evt:14468|Black Ague]] came out of [[set:112|Rela]]. It came out of Rela in the first year, black in the armpit, black in the groin.  
It came down the roads of the traders. It walked with the donkeys. It slept in the boats.  
It went where the salt went. It went where the copper went.  
We did not see it come. Who sees a thing that walks with the donkeys?

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

Hear the names of the cities, O gods. Count them, Yenan, you who count.  
It came to [[set:0|Tallen]], the house of the king. It came to [[set:17|Peme]] of the high walls.  
It came to [[set:10|Navnga]] and to [[set:68|Poyonga]]; it came to [[set:6|Jenontono]] on the river.  
It came to [[set:66|Shevanga]], to [[set:62|Bemonga]], to [[set:50|Tora]], to Eneke,  
to [[set:162|Khongomno]], where our spears stood, where the field was ours, and the sickness came into the camp.  
Forty cities and more beside, and the villages without number.  
In every city the doors are marked with ash. In every city the dogs are fat.

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

We have counted the dead, O gods, and we cannot count them.  
The fathers have buried the sons; the sons have no one to bury the fathers.  
The ox stands in the furrow with no one to drive it.  
The grain stands in the field with no one to cut it; Nedhekhte, your grain is falling on the ground.  
The price of bread has gone up in the market; a measure is dear at a [[cur:241|Noype]] and two fifths.  
The priests who poured for you are dead. The new priests do not know the words.

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

If we have sinned, tell us the sin.  
If the king has sinned, tell the king the sin; [[per:14344|Opemten]] the great king will make it good.  
If the sacrifice was poor, tell us; we will make it rich.  
If a god was forgotten in the feast, tell us the name; we will feed that god first.  
We have done as the priests of [[mov:2|Jenro]] taught us: each god in their place, each with their due.  
What did we leave undone?

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

Now we give. To Domamnol, a white bull. To Wenba, a black ewe, buried alive in the field.  
To Ewa, the clothes of the dead, burned at the gate. To Yenan, a lamp at every door through the dark of the moon.  
To Nedhekhte, the first sheaf, though it is a small sheaf. To Wolme, the spear of a dead captain.  
Take them, and take the sickness with them.

All things turn on the wheel. What begins will end. The Ague began; let it end.  
Let the wheel turn, O gods; let it turn past this year and not come round to it again.

*People:* Turn it back, turn it back; let the Ague go home.

*[Lower edge.]*

> To be said at the gate of Tallen at dawn and at sunset, every day until the gods hear, in the 14th year of Opemten.
