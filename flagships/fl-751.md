To my dear cousin, son of my mother's sister, in the hill country, greeting in the name of the One who releases from suffering, and peace to your house and your vines.

I write to you in the year 1095 of the [[mov:67|Tankhidil]], from the village of the ferry, a short walk below the walls of [[set:172|Toniziwikhizim]], where we have lived these six years like sparrows in a barn, under a roof that belongs to another man.

You will have heard, even in the hills, of the [[evt:32103|Sack of Tungaruikhrimu]], and how the men of [[pol:1177|Itsitsi]] came over the walls after the great defeat in the field. I will not write all of it again; my wife says that I tell it too often, and that a wound which is always opened does not close. I will say only that my father's shop of candles in the street of the chandlers was burned, and that we went out through the river gate with the children and one mule and the clothes we stood in.

But here is the thing I did not tell you before, because I hoped it would mend. When the men of Itsitsi set fire to the city, they set fire also to the hall of records by the great sanctuary, where the rolls of every house and garden and shop in the city were kept since the old kings. All the rolls burned. My grandfather's deed to our house burned with them, and the seal of the old lord upon it.

Now that the city is rebuilt in part, a man has come forward who says the plot in the street of the chandlers is his, and he has built a stable on it. He has two witnesses, both his cousins. I have no roll, for the roll is ash. The magistrate is not unkind. He says: bring me a paper. I say: the paper is in the same place as my father. He shrugs. It is the will of the queen's law, he says, and the queen's law needs paper.

So I ask you, cousin, a hard thing. Do you remember the day of your sister's wedding, when my father stood witness and named our house in the street of the chandlers as his dwelling? You were there. The priest wrote it in his book in your parish. If that book still stands, I beg you, have the priest copy the line and set his seal on it, and send it down with the wool-carriers. It may be nothing. It may be the house.

Of the rest, there is little good. Our lady queen [[per:31624|Wyishemovi]] made a peace with Itsitsi after the sack, and paid them, and the price of that peace is still in our bread; grain is two [[cur:714|Ödon]] the measure and the bakers make the loaves small. Then came the [[evt:32113|Tsey–Toniziwikhizim War]], and the levies, and my eldest was taken for the spear. Yet there is one good word: this year at the [[evt:32151|Battle of Nivanga]] the queen herself stood with the host, and the men of Tsey were driven back, and my son came home with all his fingers. We lit a candle for it, a poor one, of my own making.

The brothers say that all life is suffering, and that compassion is the road out of it. I try to believe it. I try to show compassion even to the man with the stable. It is easier on feast days than on market days.

Pray for us, cousin. My wife greets your wife. The children are well, though thin. If the priest's book has burned too, write to me anyway, so that I know there is someone in the world who remembers where our house stood.

Your cousin, the chandler without a shop.
