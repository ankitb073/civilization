title: What the Scribe of the Granary Saw in the Year Ana Was Broken

This is the tablet of what I saw with my eyes, I, a scribe of the house of the granary in [[set:7|Ana]], written in the 287th year of Ana. [[dei:0|Atlia]] of the sky, be witness. Uelti of wisdom, be witness. Muevas'a of the earth, be witness. [[dei:3|Niele'i]] of the river, be witness. [[dei:4|Lashla'a]] of the moon, be witness. [[dei:5|Niezhashta]] of war, be witness. I set down nothing that I did not see. I set down nothing that I did not hear.

**Of the old wound.** In the days of my father's father, [[evt:7574|Tiy rose against Ana]]. In the days of my father's father, [[evt:7590|Unhatlmum rose against Ana]]. The rebels were few, and the king of that day struck them down. But the young men of Ana died in the hills, thousands upon thousands, for every rebel a hundred of ours. The wound closed above. Beneath, it did not close.

**Of the years before.** In the years before the fall, grain was dear. A measure of barley cost nearly two [[cur:131|i'ash]] in the market of Ana. The poor sold their doors for bread. The priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] said: prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour. Where is the favour? No one answered them.

**Of the year of the fall.** In the 278th year of Ana, in the month of the barley harvest, Niele'i did not rise. The priests went down to the river with a white bull. The bull would not go into the water. For three nights Lashla'a the moon was hidden behind cloud. An owl sat on the roof of the house of Atlia and was not driven away. I saw the owl. I saw the bull.

Then the runners came.

On the first day a runner came from [[pol:350|Tlayiyan]]. He did not bow. He said: thus says the lord of Tlayiyan: I am king; I send no grain to Ana.

On the second day a runner came from [[pol:351|Mii]]. He did not bow. He said: thus says the lord of Mii: I am king; I send no silver to Ana.

On the third day a runner came from [[pol:352|Lishkho]], from the city where the rebels of Tiy once sat. He did not bow. He said: thus says the lord of Lishkho: I am king; I send no men to Ana.

On the fourth day a runner came from [[pol:353|To'i'io'a]]. On the fifth day a runner came from [[pol:354|Foyatlitie]]. They did not bow. They said what the others had said.

I stood in the court of the palace. I saw the king sit in the gate. He heard the runners. He did not speak. On the fifth day he rose, and he tore his garment from the neck to the belt, and he went into his house. That was [[evt:8021|the breaking of Ana]]. Six lords, where there had been one. The empire of [[pol:229|Ana]] was poured out on the ground like water from a broken jar, and it cannot be gathered up again.

**Of what came after.** The old king laid down the seat in that same year. Another sat in his place, and in the next year another. The old king lived five years more in his house, and he did not come out, and then he went down to his fathers. I poured water for him at the new moon.

[[evt:8022|Ana stands]]. The city stands. The gods sit in their houses. The walls are whole. But the roads that ran out from Ana to the five lands are empty of tribute. The donkeys that came heavy go back light. The scribes of the granary are fewer. I am one of the few.

Hear, you who come after. The lords of the five lands ate the bread of [[pol:349|Ana]]. Their fathers bowed in the gate of Ana. Their fathers poured to the gods of Ana. Let them remember it. If they remember, may the gods keep them. If they forget, may Niezhashta remember for them.

The great wheel turns. What ends will begin again. So the priests say, and I have written it, and I am a scribe and not a priest. This is what I saw. The tablet is finished.
