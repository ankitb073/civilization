title: Victorious by Inheritance: Fyiyan II of Ölie

[[per:35062|Fyiyan II]] is remembered as "the Victorious", and the name is true in the narrowest sense: every war fought in his name ended in a treaty in his favour. But the first of those treaties was sworn when he was three, and most of the battles that won the others were fought by other men. His forty-seven years on the throne of [[pol:1497|Ölie]], from AY 3758 to 3805, raise a question that court biography is built to avoid. What does a king contribute to a reign that his mother, his generals and his soldiers largely made?

## A throne won by a death

He was born in AY 3756, a Lizo of the house founded by Fyiyan I "the Silent". In the year of his birth his father, [[per:34894|Oi Yuive]], led Ölie into the [[evt:35518|20th Mikhukhizhi Trade War]] against the young kingdom of [[pol:1507|Tlue]], a war for markets rather than land. In 3757, at Kivunge, the king commanded 49,685 men against a Tlue force that the record puts at about fourteen thousand. Ölie won, and its king was among the dead. Nothing in the surviving sources explains how a king falls in a battle his army won so clearly. Ambush, accident and a stray arrow are all possible, and none is recorded.

The [[evt:35538|Accession of Fyiyan II]] in 3758 therefore placed a child of two on the throne. His mother, [[per:35061|Ie Mish]], governed for fourteen years as regent. Under her the war was won by a veteran general. The [[evt:35552|Treaty of Tivnu (AY 3759)]], sworn in the infant king's name with [[per:35048|Tha]] of Tlue at [[set:248|Tivnu]], gave Ölie fourteen districts, an indemnity, rights of trade and Tlue's submission as a vassal. The record's count of that war's dead runs heavily in Ölie's favour: 2,188 for Ölie and 6,860 for Tlue. In 3760, still under the regency, Ölie proclaimed itself an empire. By the end of that decade the record's measure of order in the realm stood lower than at any later point of the reign. Whatever the regent's gifts, she held together a state that had just lost its king in a strange victory.

## The long war with Tomözel

The [[evt:35630|First Ölie–Tomözel War]] began in 3765, when the king was nine, and lasted ten years. Its target was the land of the [[pol:1513|Tomözel]] empire, whose own throne was seized in 3770 by [[per:35219|Tsimalyian]] "the Usurper". For most of the war Ölie's armies were led by the same general who had won the trade war. They won almost every battle, but lost one at Lyismiyi in 3771 to the usurper himself. In 3772, the year the regency ended, another general won at Lishe. Two years later the king set up a limestone stele there in his own name, the Victory stele of Fyiyan II: Battle of Lishe (PAL·IN·02719). It claims 11,900 enemy dead where the record gives 2,369. A young king's first public act was to take credit for a battle he did not fight.

He did fight the last one. At the [[evt:35753|Battle of Foyitlithyi (AY 3775)]] Fyiyan, aged nineteen, led 42,156 men against 10,707 under [[per:35225|Isyi Sima]], and won, losing 825 men to the enemy's 3,021. The bronze text of the [[evt:35754|Treaty of Foyitlithyi (AY 3775)]] survives (PAL·TR·02295). "Peace is granted by Fyiyan, emperor of Ölie," it reads after the opening invocation. Tomözel ceded "20 districts in all, the river Itlte to be the frontier", and paid a vast indemnity "as satisfaction for the costs of the war". The costs had indeed been heavy. Over ten years the record counts 17,831 dead for Ölie and 14,110 for Tomözel. Twenty districts were bought at nearly nine hundred Ölie lives apiece.

## The king in the field

The middle years of the reign belong more clearly to Fyiyan himself. In the [[evt:35861|Ölie–Tivinno War]] (3784–3787) he attacked the city-state of [[pol:1515|Tivinno]] and commanded twice before Kheye. At the [[evt:35869|Battle of Kheye (AY 3785)]] his 47,152 men beat the 2,633 of the city's ruler, [[per:35106|Ia Ash]], at a cost of 83 dead. At the [[evt:35870|Battle of Kheye (AY 3786)]] his 42,505 beat 2,681 under Oi Nyime and lost 138. A general took Tivnu itself in 3787 and sacked it. The [[evt:35878|Conquest of Tivinno]] annexed eleven districts.

Here we have the rare luck of hearing the other side. The Tivinno account of the Ölie–Tivinno War (PAL·CH·06414), set down by a historian of the fallen city four years later, opens: "Fyiyan of Ölikha broke the peace and sent raiders across the border of Tivinno." Its figures are bent like the king's. It claims 238 Ölie dead at Kheye in 3785, where the record has 83, and only 266 of its own, where the record has 871; Fyiyan's diorite stele for 3786 claims 4,000 enemy dead where the record has 516. Each side lied in its own direction. But the Tivinno historian ends with a line no royal stele could have written: "Let this record keep what the sword has taken."

The [[evt:35939|Ölie–Mane War]] (3793–3800) followed the same course. Fyiyan commanded at the [[evt:35973|Battle of Omatse (AY 3797)]], 48,581 men against 5,793 under [[per:35469|Ye Ta]], and won at a cost of 462 men to the enemy's 1,338. His generals fought the other three battles. The [[evt:36016|Treaty of Dainagafu (AY 3800)]], sworn with [[per:35270|Maeshe Maf]] of Mane, brought seven districts, an indemnity and another vassal.

## Father, death and inheritance

Of the private man the record keeps a little. His son [[per:35110|Isyi Toe]] was born in 3781 and his daughter [[per:35111|Foyatlathe]] in 3785. A younger daughter, born in 3800, died at thirteen, after her father. In 3798 Fyiyan wrote to the ruler of Yiotl, whom he calls "our cousin", to arrange Foyatlathe's marriage to a young man of that royal house. The Letter of Fyiyan II to Isyi Yuive I (PAL·LT·01471) is brisk: "…let the bride-price be sent with my envoy. I send you gold, a chariot and two horses." The marriage took place the next year, when she was fourteen. The letter is the only words we have that may be the king's own rather than his scribes'. They are the words of a man arranging an alliance.

Fyiyan II died of natural causes in AY 3805, aged forty-nine, and the [[evt:36057|Accession of Isyi Toe]] was uneventful. The account of his reign is easily drawn up. Ölie grew from about 250 districts and eleven million people at his accession to more than 310 districts and sixteen million at his death. Its treasury more than doubled, and its order steadied. Two neighbours became vassals, a third gave up twenty districts and a fourth disappeared. Against this stand more than 22,000 soldiers of Ölie dead in battle, most of them in the long war with Tomözel.

The house he carried lasted until 3993, and the empire until 4114, when it broke into successor kingdoms. "The Victorious" is the court's word, and it is not false. It might more accurately belong to Ie Mish and to the generals whose names the steles leave out, and above all to the men of Ölie who did the dying.
