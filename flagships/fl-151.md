title: Tidings of the House of Tablets

Hear, people of [[pol:28|Tilcui]]! Hear, men of the harbour, men of the fields, men of the potters' street! Hear the tidings of the house of tablets!

Thus says [[per:2192|Piscualosh'a]], the scribe, servant of [[per:2080|Pizha]] the king, whom [[dei:110|Masto]] of the sun has lifted up:

In the first year of Pizha the king, I went before the king. I said: "My lord, your storehouses are counted by the memory of old men. The old men forget. The old men die. The grain goes out at the back door, and no one knows where it goes." The king said: "What will you do?" I said: "I will make a house of tablets." The king said: "Do it." I did it.

Hear what I did.

I set the scribes in rows, ten and ten, in the house beside the palace. Each scribe has his stool. Each scribe has his reed. Each scribe has his clay.

To the scribe of the grain I gave the storehouses. Each measure that goes in, he writes. Each measure that goes out, he writes. He writes the name of the man who brings it. He writes the name of the man who takes it.

To the scribe of the silver I gave the [[cur:20|sazhon]]. Each sazhon that comes in from the market, he weighs and writes. Each sazhon that goes out to the soldiers, he weighs and writes.

To the scribe of the boats I gave the harbour of [[set:131|Tilcui]]. Each boat that comes in from the sea, he writes: its master, its cargo, its due. [[dei:113|Udash'a]] of the sea sees the boats; now the scribe sees them too.

To the scribe of the men I gave the levy. Each house, how many sons; each son, how many years. When the king calls the spears, the tablet answers.

Each tablet has its seal. Each seal has its owner. No tablet without a seal; no seal without a man who answers for it. One tablet goes to the palace. One tablet goes to the house of the gods. If the two do not agree, the scribe is beaten.

Hear what came of it.

In this year the grain was counted. Not one measure went out at the back door. A measure of barley in the market costs less than one sazhon, and the storehouses of the king are full to the roof. Is this not the favour of the gods? The priests of [[mov:17|Nishmam]] say: prosperity is the sign of the gods' favour. I say: the gods favour the house that counts.

The old men said: "Piscualosh'a plays with mud." Let the old men come to the storehouse and count. Let them count the grain I have saved them. Then let them say "mud."

Hear, you who would be scribes. Come to the house of tablets. Bring your own reed. Learn the signs. A scribe eats bread every day, in the drought year and in the flood year. [[dei:109|Nili]] of the river rises and falls; the tablet does not rise and fall.

May [[dei:114|Lashla]] of the moon, who counts the months, bless the counters. May [[dei:111|Siminlo]] of the storm spare the house of tablets from fire. May Masto light the road of Pizha the king. May the tablet outlast the man who wrote it.

The wheel turns. What ends will begin again. But what is written stays written.

This tablet, the tidings of the [[evt:1846|Invention of Scribal bureaucracy in Tilcui]], is set up at the gate of the market, in the 2nd year of Pizha, by the hand of Piscualosh'a, so that every man who passes may have it read to him.
