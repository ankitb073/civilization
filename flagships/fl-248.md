title: Prosperity in a Sacked City: The Historical Uzholpaya

The [[mov:82|Is'ava]] school teaches, among other things, that prosperity is a sign of divine favour. Its founder, [[per:23046|Uzholpaya]], established it at Pivithie in AY 2668, a year after that city was sacked for the fourth time. That conjunction is the most interesting fact we have about him, and almost the only one. This essay sets out what the record establishes, reconstructs his city from its archaeology, and warns against the most tempting legend about him.

## The man

Uzholpaya was born in AY 2634, a man of the Nishmam people and a subject of the Kingdom of Pibetie, whose seat was Pivithie. The [[evt:23207|Founding of the Is'ava School]] took place there in 2668, when he was about thirty-four. He died of natural causes in 2684, at about fifty, after some sixteen years of teaching. No text by him, no collection of his teachings and no document naming him has been catalogued. Everything else must come from his city.

## The city

The material record of Pivithie in his lifetime is a record of defeat and repair. When he was a boy, Pibetie lost a holy war to Ana and ceded territory by the Treaty of Nyinsitlasa in 2646. The city was besieged in 2655 and again in 2660, and in 2661 the old kingdom of Pibetie collapsed and a new one of the same name rose in its place. In 2665 Ipil Lieze I built a wall, as the Wall inscription of Ipil Lieze I at Pivithie (PAL·IN·05201) records. It did not hold. Pivithie was besieged in 2666 and sacked in 2667, and the victor commemorated the day in the Victory stele of Weshsanieme II: Battle of Pivithie (AY 2667) (PAL·IN·01703), set up for the Yiotl Empire. By treaties of 2667, 2670 and 2671 the kingdom ceded land to three different enemies.

The year of the founding, 2668, was also the year an eleven-year-old, Ipil Lieze II, came to the throne under a regent. Four years later Pibetie struck its first coinage, and in 2689 the king recorded the building of a gate at Pivithie (PAL·IN·05202). Uzholpaya taught through the worst of these years and died before the end. The kingdom fell to Ana in 2699, fifteen years after his death.

## The teaching in its setting

The school's doctrine, as the record summarizes it, says little of the world's beginning or end. Many gods share the world. Each seeker may find the truth by inner light and reason. The good life is one of duty, order and right sacrifice, and prosperity is a sign of divine favour. The school belongs to one people and seeks no converts.

Read in a sacked city, the doctrine of prosperity can be taken two ways. It may be a promise, that duty and right sacrifice would restore what had been lost, a reading the coinage and the new gate might seem to bear out. Or it may be a reproach, that the city had lost favour and must regain it. The record does not let us choose, and I do not think we should pretend otherwise. The archaeology does establish that the school was founded in a community rebuilding under pressure. A philosophy of one people, seeking no converts, fits a city whose neighbours were taking it apart.

## The legend of a school of founders

Pivithie produced a second founder within a generation. Ue'manim, born in 2659, founded the I'ashakh faith there in 2694. It is tempting to make him Uzholpaya's pupil and Pivithie a school of founders. The two doctrines do overlap: both say little of the world's beginning or end, and both commend duty, order and right sacrifice. But they differ on the gods, on the source of truth and, decisively, on converts, which Ue'manim's faith seeks and Uzholpaya's school does not. Shared tenets in one city prove a shared culture, not discipleship. No source connects the two men.

The historical [[per:23046|Uzholpaya]], then, is a Nishmam teacher of Pivithie who founded a school in the year after the sack and taught for sixteen years while his kingdom shrank around him. The [[mov:82|Is'ava]] outlived both the kingdom and its conquerors. That durability, not any recoverable word of its founder, is the best evidence of what he gave his city.
