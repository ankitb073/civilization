*Fair copy of the inscription to be cut upon the western gate of the palace of [[set:147|Binge]], as approved by His Majesty on the 13th of Naalikh, in the year 2256 of the Vishithmikh.*

I, [[per:42565|Nithkuvbin]], second of that name, by the grace of the One sovereign of [[pol:1710|Binge]], called by my people the Conqueror, son of [[per:42530|Kuwvenathdan]], of the line of the first Nithkuvbin, of the people of Imu, set down these words in the fifty-third year of my reign, that those who come after may know what was done and by whose hand, and may give thanks to the One who did it through me.

I was five years old when my father was taken from us and the crown was set upon my head. Men who ought to have known better said then that a child's reign would be the ruin of the realm. Let them read this stone. For it is the custom of the weak to count a king's years, and the custom of the wise to count his provinces.

In my youth, while I was yet under the guidance of my council, the borders of Binge were widened more than once, and the men of the realm learned that the house of Nithkuvbin keeps what it takes. Of those years I say little, for the credit belongs in part to others; and a king who claims every deed claims none.

In the year 2236 of the Vishithmikh the court of Iith, misled by evil counsel, sent an army against my realm, thinking an old enemy grown soft. It was answered at [[evt:43388|Tanshedi]]. It came again, and was answered at [[evt:43392|Khasnin]]. It came a third time, and at [[evt:43403|Shiüzhi]] it was answered for the last time. I did not seek that war; I finished it. By [[evt:43404|the peace of Shiüzhi]], in the year 2240, Iith paid to my treasury the cost of its folly in good silver, bound itself to my house as a dependent, and swore to keep the peace for twenty years. Let it remember its oath.

It is falsely said by some of the pamphleteers of the ports that our own losses in that war were heavy. I answer that every soldier of Binge who fell defending his hearth is recorded in the books of the churches and remembered at the altar, and that a nation which counts its dead before it counts its honour does not deserve to keep either.

In the year 2247 I took up [[evt:43482|the war against Khyie]], whose princes had long troubled the trade of Yizamashti and threatened my subjects there. Three times my armies met theirs: at [[evt:43492|Lithi]], at [[evt:43510|Yyixi]], and at [[evt:43519|Tiaiosh]]. Three times they prevailed. I name no general here, for the generals were my servants and the victory was the One's; but let it be known that I chose them, and that I chose well. By [[evt:43520|the peace of Tiaiosh]] in the year 2251 the princes of Khyie gave up to my crown a broad country with its towns, and those towns now pay their dues in good [[cur:956|tuthshi]] to the treasury of Binge.

I have been, all my life, a servant of the faith of [[mov:58|Vishithmikh]]. I have built churches and endowed them. I have fed the poor at the palace gate on every feast. For the faith teaches that time runs in a line toward the Judgement, and that every king will stand there with the herdsman and give an account of his mercy. I have tried to make that account a good one. If I have been hard to my enemies, I have been gentle to my subjects, and the One will weigh the two.

I commend the realm, when the One shall call me, to my son [[per:42725|Tathtuven]], whom I have taught to rule as I have ruled: with a firm hand, an open purse for the churches, and a closed ear to flatterers.

Let this stone stand as long as [[pol:1710|Binge]] stands. Let him who defaces it answer for it at the Judgement.
