*[Obverse]*

By [[dei:141|Nabüdin]] of the river, lady of the boats. By [[dei:139|Nidiktti]], lady of the grain. By [[dei:136|Duemino]] of the storm, who sinks the boat of the liar. By [[dei:137|Shyedartsi]] of the house below, who keeps the oath-breaker. By the life of [[per:294|Duebunen]], lady of [[pol:7|Buneynga]]. The oath is sworn.

This is the tablet of the bag of the house of [[hou:1|Lümi]].

[[per:395|Dyeyshatun]], son of no great house, a man of the harbour of [[set:11|Buneynga]], who set up the house of Lümi in the year that is past, opens the bag. Into the bag go these:

From Dyeyshatun: thirty [[cur:7|Tyettku]] of silver, by the weight of the lady's stone; and two boats with their oars; and the storehouse by the harbour gate, with its door and its lock.

From the shipmaster of the lower harbour: ten Tyettku of silver; and one boat.

From the herdsman of the upper pastures: ten Tyettku of silver; and ten horses, mares and geldings, sound in wind and hoof.

From the widow of the weaver: ten Tyettku of silver; and forty bolts of woollen cloth, of her own looms.

From the copper-smith of the street of fires: ten Tyettku of silver.

Seventy Tyettku in all are in the bag. The bag is sealed with five seals. The bag is set in the storehouse by the harbour gate.

*[Reverse]*

Now hear the words of the bag.

Dyeyshatun holds the bag. Dyeyshatun buys and sells. He buys copper. He buys horses. He buys the spices that come up the river. He buys grain in the year of plenty, and sells grain in the year of want. He sells the cloth of the widow in far markets. Whatever he buys and sells, he buys and sells for the bag, and not for himself alone.

Six years the bag stays shut. No partner takes out his silver before six years. Whoever takes out his silver before six years, he takes out his silver only, without increase, and his name is struck from the tablet.

At the end of six years the bag is opened before the five. The silver is weighed. Each partner takes back his silver first, as he put it in. Then the increase is divided. Of the increase, two parts go to the partners, each by his share. One part goes to Dyeyshatun, for his labour and his boats and his roads.

If a boat sinks, by the will of Duemino, the loss is the bag's, and no man is blamed. If a boat sinks, and Dyeyshatun was drunk, or sailed in the month of storms against the word of the shipmaster, the loss is Dyeyshatun's.

If Dyeyshatun dies, the bag is opened before the five, and the house of Lümi passes to his heir, if his heir swears this oath.

Whoever lies about the bag, whoever hides silver from the bag, whoever sells the goods of the bag for his own purse: may Duemino sink his boat; may Nidiktti dry his field; may Shyedartsi open the ground under his feet. The priests of [[mov:21|Kunelmu]] have heard this oath and keep a copy in the house of the gods.

*[Left edge]*

Before witnesses: the priest of the river shrine; the overseer of the harbour; the scribe who wrote this tablet. Sealed with five seals. In the 36th year of Duebunen.
