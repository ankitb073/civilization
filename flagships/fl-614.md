The tomb lintel of [[per:7462|Mi'shöyueva]], from the royal tombs of Ana, survives complete: "Mi'shöyueva, king of Ana, son of Esiesh Niema, dwells in this tomb. May Atlia protect his soul." It was cut in AY 1205. Six years earlier, in the forty-fourth year of his reign, a palace scribe named Nistlo'shim'a compiled the Annals of Ana. Their heading promises the deeds of the years "to the 44th year of Mi'shöyueva". The entries stop in 1154, the year he was born. Whether the last tablets were lost or never written, the court left no chronicle of the reign of the king whose name means "spear fire" and whom tradition calls the Victorious. His life has to be put together from treaties, the accounts of his enemies, one letter, a merchant's day-book and his tomb.

## A king at two

He was born in 1154 into the Lizo house of I'pil Luesh'a, which had ruled [[pol:229|Ana]] since 1038. The empire itself was a successor state. It had risen in 973 from the collapse of Mii and still ruled from Ane, the old Mii capital. His grandfather had been called the Conqueror. His father, [[per:6944|Esiesh Niema]], came to the throne in the year of his son's birth and died in 1156, aged thirty-two. The [[evt:7122|Accession of Mi'shöyueva]] made a two-year-old king of some six and a half million people, with his mother, [[per:7460|Tietlo]], as regent. He had one sibling, an elder sister.

## Treaties in a child's name

The wars of the regency were fought by generals and sealed in the boy's name. The [[evt:7180|Third Ana–Nimit War]] of 1162–1165 was an attack on the old kingdom of [[pol:212|Nimit]] and its king [[per:7339|Han'e]], later called the Last. Nimid was besieged two years running. The [[evt:7207|Treaty of Nimid (AY 1165)]] survives on a fragmentary silver tablet: "Thus says Mi'shöyueva, great king, king of Ana, beloved of Atlia." The great king was eleven. The treaty took twelve districts and, in a clause found in many treaties of this age, let Ana's merchants "pass through the land of Nimidi unhindered". The Nimit chronicler wrote only that "the border was settled".

Meanwhile [[pol:274|Khyeka]] had invaded. In the [[evt:7194|Khyeka–Ana War]] of 1164–1168 its young king [[per:7521|Nguyumbin]] led his own army twice against much larger Ana forces and lost each time. The [[evt:7236|Treaty of Lininadhe (AY 1168)]] gave neither side anything but ten years of truce. Its tablet, in the same scribe's hand as the treaty of Nimid, nonetheless declares that the enemy "sent tribute to my camp". The tribute is the court's invention, and a modest one.

Tietlo died in 1169, when her son was fifteen. A merchant's day-book of the same year, kept at Fienanna by the house of I'e'a'im, is dated "in the 14th year of Mi'shöyueva" and opens with a balance of 7,460 in the royal coin. It is a reminder of what most of the realm cared about while treaties were sealed in a boy's name.

## The king in the field

He married young. Twin sons were born in 1171, and one died at two. Two daughters followed. His queen died in 1179 at twenty-six, and the record gives him no second wife.

Two years later he took the field himself for the first time. The [[evt:7373|First Ana–Man'e War]] of 1181–1184 was a war of conquest against the [[pol:312|Man'e]] empire, and he commanded all three of its battles. At the [[evt:7386|Battle of Nimid (AY 1182)]] he faced the Man'e king [[per:7544|Sha Himin]], two years his junior, who was killed on the field. Victories followed at Ometh in 1183 and at the [[evt:7403|Battle of Dainagafu (AY 1184)]]. The [[evt:7404|Treaty of Dainagafu (AY 1184)]] took eleven districts and twenty years of tribute, made Man'e a vassal and opened its trade. It was sealed on the Man'e side by Sha Himin's son, [[per:7749|I Himin]], aged four. The king who had signed treaties as a child now received one from a child. I Himin died at nine.

The [[evt:7452|Ana–Unhatlmum War]] of 1190–1193 was smaller and grimmer. [[pol:321|Unhatlmum]] was a kingdom carved out of Nimit in 1178 by the rebel [[per:7695|Yucu'igw'e]], called the Fair. At the [[evt:7472|Battle of Iazhash (AY 1192)]] Mi'shöyueva led twenty-three thousand men against ninety-three, and the next year twenty-two thousand against 124. The enemy lost forty-four men in the whole war, and Ana nearly four thousand. Iazhash was sacked and then besieged. The Unhatlmum account, written three years later, gives its own losses honestly as five and blames a river that "rose and cut the army in two". It ends: "the gates were opened to the enemy. The gods had turned their faces from us." The [[evt:7484|Conquest of Unhatlmum]] in 1193 added six districts. Yucu'igw'e lived on for six more years.

## Last years

The reign ended uneasily. In 1202 Tiy rose in revolt, and in 1204 Unhatlmum rose again. A later Ana account describes the Tiy rebel leader falling on Iazhash "with 84 men, burning the villages as he came". The king's only surviving letter dates from that year. It is addressed to Yidad, king of Onsatlmomi, in the usual formula of royal brotherhood, and it ends with a threat: "You have not sent the gifts that were promised. Send them now, or know that our friendship is ended. If you will not answer, let the sword answer."

He died a natural death in 1205, aged fifty-one. The [[evt:7603|Accession of Eshuesh'ash]] made his surviving son king at thirty-four, and [[per:7971|Eshuesh'ash]] put down both revolts within two years. His own epitaph, in the same tombs, calls him "son of Mi'shöyueva", but its last lines are worn away. After him a child reigned under a regent and is remembered as the Last. In 1250 Ana broke into six states, one of which kept its name and its capital at Ane.

## A verdict from the tombs

The record bears out the epithet. Every battle it lists for his reign was won, whether he or his generals commanded. By the standards of his age the wars were small: some sixteen thousand Ana dead in nearly fifty years, against about five and a half thousand of the enemy. The empire grew from about 329 districts to 346. As an archaeologist I am drawn to what survives intact, and here that is the epitaph. It asks nothing about victories, only that a god protect his soul.
