The best single document for the reign of [[per:34731|Navavun]] is not a chronicle or a stele. It is a papyrus muster roll drawn up for a battle in AY 3792. Eight towns sent men, from 13,893 down to 3,266, and the total comes to 45,589. Among them were 5,254 men-at-arms from Fili, a city his father had conquered when he was a child. The roll ends with a single order: "Deserters to be branded and returned to their towns." Whatever else the epithet "the Victorious" means, it rested on lists like this one. This chapter weighs the epithet against what the record shows of the man.

## A usurper's son, a regent mother

He was born in 3740, a Khukunji, and his name means "flood". His father, [[per:34633|Nalivimve]], had seized the throne of [[pol:1457|Shudhunki]] in 3724 and founded the house that bears his name. His mother, [[per:34729|Namvave]], was forty-two at his birth; an elder sister had been born the year before.

In 3745 his father opened the [[evt:35386|Shudhunki–Fili War]] against [[pol:1504|Fili]], a city-state on the empire's edge. The record lists the empire's steady growth as its cause. In 3746 Nalivimve led 68,788 men against a defending force of twenty-one and lost 11,512 of them. In 3747 he died, and the [[evt:35400|Accession of Navavun]] made a boy of seven emperor, with his mother as regent. The [[evt:35420|Conquest of Fili]] was sealed in the child's name the following year with Fili's queen, [[per:34831|Natange]], at Khambizhinu, and seventeen districts were annexed. Over the whole war Shudhunki lost 19,007 men; Fili lost twenty-two.

Fili's own account, set down by a palace scribe fifty-three years later, turns this into a different story. Nalivimve, "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore", attacked; Fili's army lost three men in one battle and two in another while killing thousands; and then, without explanation, "the royal city was taken. The gods had turned their faces from us." The scribe's casualty figures are wrong, but the grief is real. Natange died in 3749. Namvave had died in 3748, and the record names no one to replace her. At eight Navavun had lost both parents.

## The wars that came to him

Most of his wars were not of his choosing. The empire's growth drew enemies, and in 3752 the Ngzhinakhzi Empire under [[per:34723|Nambave I]] began the [[evt:35480|Ngzhinakhzi–Shudhunki War]]. Shudhunki's generals lost in 3754 and won in 3756 and 3757. At eighteen Navavun took command himself at the [[evt:35540|Battle of Shimbayi (AY 3758)]]. With 58,280 men he beat 24,723 under the empress in person, losing 3,216 men to her 7,128. A defeat under another commander followed in 3759. Then, at the [[evt:35584|Battle of Shimbayi (AY 3760)]], he met 56,362 men with 73,538. The enemy general, Nabuve, was killed, and his army lost 22,334 men to Shudhunki's 10,146. The [[evt:35585|Treaty of Shimbayi (AY 3760)]] survives complete on bronze, and its brevity is eloquent. It has one article: a fifteen-year truce, and "raiders of either side shall be handed over for judgement." No land changed hands. Shudhunki had lost 44,042 men to stay as it was.

The pattern repeated with [[pol:1450|Puvu]]. In the [[evt:35640|First Puvu–Shudhunki War]] (3767–3771) Puvu attacked, and another Shudhunki commander broke its army in 3768. A diorite stele nevertheless credits Navavun with "90,800 soldiers" killed that year; the record gives 32,355, and he did not command. He did command at the [[evt:35714|Battle of Khambe (AY 3771)]], beating 37,245 under [[per:35169|Naange]] with 49,790 and losing 4,232 men to Puvu's 12,089. By the [[evt:35715|Treaty of Khambe (AY 3771)]] Puvu's king, [[per:35022|Navande]], paid a heavy indemnity and accepted a truce. The text orders: "Let it be read aloud in the markets of both lands." Puvu's own historian, writing in 3780, says the king made peace "out of mercy for the common folk", that "the honour of the realm was preserved", and passes over both battles. A lost Shudhunki account claims thousands of enemy dead for a few hundred of its own. Neither side's scribes were content with the truth, which was victory at a real cost.

He married a woman two years younger than himself. Their only child, [[per:35486|Nagyime]], was born in 3762. His elder sister died in 3771.

## The war he chose

The one war he began was about trade. The [[evt:35872|Eighth Ng'jinaguh Trade War]] (3787–3792) was fought against [[pol:1469|Unsinpamam]]. At the [[evt:35881|Battle of Khambizhinu (AY 3788)]] he beat 14,301 men with 50,844, losing 4,240 to the enemy's 2,170. At a second battle there in 3790 he faced the king, [[per:35244|Nakagum II]], with 68,928 against 15,458, and lost 12,268 men to inflict 3,640. Shudhunki's chronicle of the war, finished in 3798, reverses the roles: Nakagum, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Shudhunki". It also enlarges the enemy armies and shrinks Shudhunki's dead. At the [[evt:35926|Battle of Unzhinfamamu (AY 3792)]], the battle of the muster roll, his 45,589 at last killed more than they lost: 4,184 to 2,792. The capital was besieged. The [[evt:35928|Treaty of Unzhinfamamu (AY 3792)]] took fourteen districts with Khambizhinu, made the kingdom a vassal that must "send its levies when summoned", and gave the war's real purpose in a single article: "The merchants of Shudhunki shall trade in the markets of Unzhinfamamu free of all tolls."

## Khambe, and after

In the same year the triumph turned. Puvu had come back in the [[evt:35908|Second Puvu–Shudhunki War]], which the record treats as a direct sequel to the first. At the [[evt:35936|Battle of Khambe (AY 3792)]], against his old adversary Naange, his 47,380 men met 48,484, and Shudhunki lost 18,936 men to Puvu's 6,190. It was the only battle he commanded and lost. His wife had died the year before. He was fifty-two.

He died in 3796, at fifty-six, of natural causes, with the Puvu war still unresolved. The [[evt:35956|Accession of Nagyime]] put his daughter, thirty-four, on a throne whose law favoured sons. In her first year she commanded at Khambe herself and won, and in 3799 she swore the truce that ended the war.

By the count of battles the epithet is earned: of the seven he commanded he won six. By other measures the reign was a long defence that happened to end with more land than it began, about 211 districts in 3790 against 163 when he was born. The cost was paid in levies like those on the muster roll. Puvu fell apart in 3825 and Unsinpamam in 3856. Shudhunki broke into six successor states in 3835, and the record lists his defeat at Khambe among the causes. The flood receded, as floods do.
