The best single document for the reign of [[per:34731|Navavun]] is not a chronicle or a stele, and it does not date from his reign. It is a papyrus muster roll drawn up for a battle in AY 3809, thirteen years after his death, and its towns are the ones his wars held or won. Eight towns sent men, from 6,576 down to 2,782, and the total comes to 32,777. Among them were 4,730 levies from Fili, a city conquered in his name when he was a child. The roll ends with a single order: "The captains of hundreds answer for the absent." Whatever else the epithet "the Victorious" means, it rested on lists like this one. This chapter weighs the epithet against what the record shows of the man.

## A usurper's son, a regent mother

He was born in 3740, a Khukunji, and his name means "flood". His father, [[per:34633|Nalivimve]], had seized the throne of [[pol:1457|Shudhunki]] in 3724 and founded the house that bears his name. His mother, [[per:34729|Namvave]], was forty-two at his birth; an elder sister had been born the year before.

In 3745 his father opened the [[evt:35386|Shudhunki–Fili War]] against [[pol:1504|Fili]], a city-state on the empire's edge. The record lists the empire's steady growth as its cause. In 3746 Nalivimve led 30,675 men against a defending force of 2,101 and lost 192 of them. In 3747 he died, and the [[evt:35400|Accession of Navavun]] made a boy of seven emperor, with his mother as regent. The [[evt:35420|Conquest of Fili]] was sealed in the child's name the following year with Fili's queen, [[per:34831|Natange]], at Khambizhinu, and seventeen districts were annexed. Over the whole war Shudhunki lost 323 men; Fili lost 1,548.

Fili's own account, set down by a palace scribe fifty-three years later, turns this into a different story. Nalivimve, "forgetting the oaths his fathers swore", attacked; Fili's army lost 147 men in one battle and 56 in another while killing more than twice as many; and then, without explanation, "the royal city was taken. The gods had turned their faces from us." The scribe's casualty figures are wrong, but the grief is real. Natange died in 3749. Namvave had died in 3748, and the record names no one to replace her. At eight Navavun had lost both parents.

## The wars that came to him

Most of his wars were not of his choosing. The empire's growth drew enemies, and in 3752 the Ngzhinakhzi Empire under [[per:34723|Nambave I]] began the [[evt:35480|Ngzhinakhzi–Shudhunki War]]. Shudhunki's generals lost in 3754 and won in 3756 and 3757. At eighteen Navavun took command himself at the [[evt:35540|Battle of Shimbayi (AY 3758)]]. With 29,935 men he beat 14,199 under the empress in person, losing 1,336 men to her 3,630. A defeat under another commander followed in 3759. Then, at the [[evt:35584|Battle of Shimbayi (AY 3760)]], he met 25,134 men with 32,793. The enemy general, Nabuve, was killed, and his army lost 9,069 men to Shudhunki's 3,844. The [[evt:35585|Treaty of Shimbayi (AY 3760)]] survives complete on bronze, and its brevity is eloquent. It has one article: a fifteen-year truce, and "raiders of either side shall be handed over for judgement." No land changed hands. Shudhunki had lost 14,806 men to stay as it was.

The pattern repeated with [[pol:1450|Puvu]]. In the [[evt:35640|First Puvu–Shudhunki War]] (3767–3771) Puvu attacked, and another Shudhunki commander broke its army in 3768. A diorite stele nevertheless credits Navavun with "44,600 soldiers" killed that year; the record gives 15,908, and he did not command. He did command at the [[evt:35714|Battle of Khambe (AY 3771)]], beating 16,609 under [[per:35169|Naange]] with 22,203 and losing 903 men to Puvu's 2,834. By the [[evt:35715|Treaty of Khambe (AY 3771)]] Puvu's king, [[per:35022|Navande]], paid a heavy indemnity and accepted a truce. The text orders: "Let it be read aloud in the markets of both lands." Puvu's own historian, writing in 3780, says the king made peace "out of mercy for the common folk", that "the honour of the realm was preserved", and passes over both battles. A lost Shudhunki account claims more than six thousand enemy dead in that battle for fewer than three hundred of its own. Neither side's scribes were content with the truth, which was victory at a cost of some nine hundred dead.

He married a woman two years younger than himself. Their only child, [[per:35486|Nagyime]], was born in 3762. His elder sister died in 3771.

## The war he chose

The one war he began was about trade. The [[evt:35872|Eighth Ng'jinaguh Trade War]] (3787–3792) was fought against [[pol:1469|Unsinpamam]]. At the [[evt:35881|Battle of Khambizhinu (AY 3788)]] he beat 6,377 men with 22,673, losing 400 to the enemy's 504. At a second battle there in 3790 he faced the king, [[per:35244|Nakagum II]], with 30,737 against 6,893, and lost 285 men to inflict 1,171. Shudhunki's chronicle of the war, finished in 3798, reverses the roles: Nakagum, "swollen with pride, coveted the lands of Shudhunki". It also enlarges the enemy armies and shrinks Shudhunki's dead. At the [[evt:35926|Battle of Unzhinfamamu (AY 3792)]] his 22,487 killed more than twice what they lost: 1,347 to 567. The capital was besieged. The [[evt:35928|Treaty of Unzhinfamamu (AY 3792)]] took fourteen districts with Khambizhinu, made the kingdom a vassal that must "send its levies when summoned", and gave the war's real purpose in a single article: "The merchants of Shudhunki shall trade in the markets of Unjinpama free of all tolls."

## Khambe, and after

In the same year the triumph turned. Puvu had come back in the [[evt:35908|Second Puvu–Shudhunki War]], which the record treats as a direct sequel to the first. At the [[evt:35936|Battle of Khambe (AY 3792)]], against his old adversary Naange, his 23,687 men met 21,620, and Shudhunki lost 6,531 men to Puvu's 1,743. It was the only battle he commanded and lost. His wife had died the year before. He was fifty-two.

He died in 3796, at fifty-six, of natural causes, with the Puvu war still unresolved. The [[evt:35956|Accession of Nagyime]] put his daughter, thirty-four, on a throne whose law favoured sons. In her first year she commanded at Khambe herself and won, and in 3799 she swore the truce that ended the war.

By the count of battles the epithet is earned: of the seven he commanded he won six. By other measures the reign was a long defence that happened to end with more land than it began, about 205 districts at his death against 146 at his accession. The cost was paid in levies like those on the muster roll. Puvu fell apart in 3825 and Unsinpamam in 3856. Shudhunki broke into six successor states in 3835, and the record lists his defeat at Khambe among the causes. The flood receded, as floods do.
