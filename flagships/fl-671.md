[[per:38282|Baalnith]]'s name means *wise jar*, and what he left behind is mostly things that hold: a silver coin from a hoard, two stones that keep a boast, and a tomb. He was born in AY 4018 and died of natural causes in 4073, at fifty-five. The record gives him a people, the Nishmam, and one office: ruler of the city-state of [[pol:1633|Weümin]] from 4063 until his death. It gives him no parents, no wife, no children and no trade. He stands in this book among the founders, and he was one, though not of a faith. The faith of his city, [[mov:69|Kievi]], was already more than sixteen centuries old when he came to power. What Baalnith founded was a constitution, and it lasted twelve years.

## A splinter of Imshi

When he was born, the old port of [[set:20|Veümin]], founded 774 years before AY 0, belonged to Imshi, which had held it since 3762. He was thirty-two in 4050 when Imshi collapsed into six successor states. One was Weümin, with its capital at Veümin and rulers chosen by election. Another was Iemim, whose story runs beside it.

Weümin's first ruler, [[per:38156|Baalmaf]], four years younger than Baalnith, left a bronze plaque on a granary (PAL·IN·06224) and died in 4057, at thirty-five. That year the record notes a crisis and a usurpation: [[per:38214|Yi Ath]], thirty-two, took the city. In 4060 he dedicated a temple to the sun goddess Mastu (PAL·IN·06226). Both men call themselves "king of Weümin" on their bronze. The survey of the 4050s shows a city of 1,668,573 people in sixteen districts, with 13,349 men under arms, a treasury of 3,337 kilograms of silver and no coinage of its own.

## The year of the merchants

In 4061 Iemim deposed its first ruler and became a merchant republic. Two years later Weümin followed. The record's entry, the [[evt:38815|Merchant republic Reform in Weümin]], names Baalnith, then forty-five, as its leader and Yi Ath, thirty-eight, as the man deposed. The record attaches no battle and no killing to it. Yi Ath lived nineteen more years and outlived both Baalnith and the republic. The record's decade surveys from the 3930s to the 4050s list no merchant republic anywhere in the world. By the end of the 4060s they list two, and both were splinters of Imshi.

Who these merchants were, the record does not say; it names no merchant house at Veümin in his lifetime. But the figures moved. By the end of the 4060s the city's urban population had grown from 93,926 to 161,573, and its treasury from 3,337 to 46,181 kilograms of silver, almost fourteen times as much. A measure of grain at Veümin cost about 14 grams of silver in the price series for the 4050s and about 8.4 in the 4060s. It is tempting to credit the wise jar, but Iemim shows the same leap in the same decade: its urban population rose from 51,219 to 124,202 and its treasury from 3,571 to 58,181 kilograms. Whatever happened came with the form of government, or the times, as much as with the man.

In 4066 Weümin struck its first coin, the Nashmi, of about 5.2 grams of silver. The specimen from the hoard (PAL·NU·00359) shows a diademed head of Baalnith, with a bull walking on the reverse. Its legend, partly off the flan, reads BĀALNIṮ KĀA. The same second word follows a ruler's name on a coin struck at the city about 3717 (PAL·NU·00292: MANIN KĀA), so I take it for a title. Nabuli, who translated Baalnith's stones, renders his title there as king, and I have no better word. The head of a merchant republic wore a diadem on its silver. The record adds a muddle of its own: its entry for this first coinage names Yi Ath, deposed three years before, as the ruler. The coin names Baalnith.

There was a second tension. Kievi asks for justice in dealings and moderation in all things, and it teaches that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite. A man named for wisdom put his city in the hands of its traders. The record does not say whether he felt the contradiction.

## Seven battles, two of them his

In 4066 the kingdom of [[pol:1521|Ayshi]] attacked to seize territory, opening the [[evt:38834|Ayshi–Weümin War]]. The record gives as its cause Yi Ath's usurpation of 4057, the disorder Baalnith had ended, not his own reform. By the end of the 4060s Ayshi counted 66 districts, 4,651,584 people and 33,292 men under arms; Weümin had 16 districts, 1,685,706 people and 11,086 men. Both peoples were Nishmam and both states were Kievi, and their kings carved their victory stones in the same script for the same river goddess, Nili.

Baalnith commanded in person twice. At the [[evt:38847|Tenth Battle of Niazhi]] in 4067, on his own territory, he faced King [[per:38261|Eshiemimish]] of Ayshi with 3,769 men against 19,560, more than five to one. He lost 900 men; the enemy lost 399. Eshiemimish's limestone stele (PAL·IN·02951), its final lines lost, says: "He killed 4,700 soldiers." That is more men than Baalnith brought to the field.

The next victory was not his. In 4069 the general Yei Lisil, with 7,574 men, beat Eshiemimish's 14,405 outside Veümin; Ayshi lost 3,697 and Weümin 1,894. Baalnith's diorite stele of 4070 (PAL·IN·02952) gives the credit where kings gave it: "Baalnith, king of Weümin, defeated Ayshi in Weümin. He killed 11,500 soldiers." That is about three times the record's figure, and four-fifths of the whole Ayshi army that day.

His own victory came in 4070, at the [[evt:38897|Battle of Bami (AY 4070)]], near a town Ayshi had held since 4059. With 5,942 men he met the general [[per:38219|Shamash]] at the head of 21,949, nearly four to one, and won. Ayshi lost 1,794 men and Weümin 928. Fewer dead, but a larger share of a smaller army, and the record scores the decision as narrow. His stele, found at Bami in 4898, claims 5,200 dead and ends: "May Nili destroy the name of the enemy." The record does not say how a Weümin stone came to stand in a town Ayshi held.

After that the war turned. At Bami in 4071 another Yi Ath, born in 4035 (a namesake, not the deposed ruler), lost 2,716 men to Ayshi's 498. Yei Lisil won again at Niazhi in 4072, barely. In 4073 the younger Yi Ath was beaten outside Veümin, and Ayshi laid siege to the city.

## A tomb and a treaty

Baalnith died that year, aged fifty-five. The record does not say whether he died before the enemy reached the walls or after. His ten years were the longest rule of the city's four rulers. His epitaph from the royal tombs (PAL·IN·06227) survives complete and says only: "Baalnith, king of Weümin, dwells in this tomb." Apart from the name, it repeats word for word the formula on Baalmaf's sarcophagus lid of 4057 (PAL·IN·06225). The republic buried its founder as a king.

The city then elected [[per:38381|Yi Mua]], a woman of sixty-one, born six years before him. In 4075 Shamash beat the younger Yi Ath at Niazhi, and Ayshi's new king, Uzhulnith, claimed the victory on his own stele (PAL·IN·02957), as Baalnith had claimed Yei Lisil's. Veümin was besieged a fourth time, and at Veümin itself Yi Mua and Uzhulnith signed the treaty that gave all sixteen districts to Ayshi. The war had cost Ayshi 8,060 dead and Weümin 9,351. In the price series for the 4070s, grain, salt, iron and timber all cost more at Veümin than they had in the 4060s.

Nambili, digging along the line of the old wall in 4889, found two destruction layers, from the sacks of 3317 and 3320. Nothing in the ground marks 4075. Weümin ended in ink, not ash.

So what did Baalnith found? A government that lasted twelve years and a coin that lasted nine. The habit of claiming other men's victories he shared with his enemy's king. His two stones claim 16,700 dead; the record gives Ayshi's losses in those two battles as 5,491, and he commanded only one of them. The surveys say his city grew richer under the merchants, but Iemim grew richer without him. What remains is smaller and more human. With a third of his rival's men, he held a city of sixteen districts through seven years of war against a kingdom of sixty-six, and he died before it fell.
