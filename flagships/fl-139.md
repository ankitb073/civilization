*Speech of Citizen [[per:45451|Nabizhi]], delivered from the steps of the old customs house at [[set:9|Dagafu]] on 25 Nigu 4698 of the Uthungala, taken down in shorthand and printed by order of the provisional council.*

Citizens! [*Cheers.*] Citizens — and I say the word slowly, because a week ago it was a word for which a man could be taken up by the police, and this morning it is the only title in [[pol:1892|Dagafu]] that matters. [*Great cheering.*]

I will not keep you long. The wagons are standing in the yards, the mills want their hands, and the bakers want their flour. But some of you have walked in from the river districts since dawn, and you have a right to hear from my own mouth what has been done and why.

What has been done is this. The council of the districts has declared that the crown of Dagafu is ended, and that from this day the people govern themselves through men of their own choosing. [*Cheers.*] The man who was king, [[per:45286|Nakhizhi]], is no longer king. He is a citizen like you and me, and I say to you plainly: he is safe, and he will stay safe. The republic does not need his blood. It needs his signature on nothing ever again. [*Laughter and cheers.*]

Why has it been done? I will give you figures, because figures do not make speeches.

Eight years ago the king sent our army across the frontier against Mbilu. At [[evt:46122|Unzhinfamamu]] he won his battle, and the bells were rung, and the newspapers printed his portrait. The war office printed something else, in small type, on the back page: nineteen hundred of our men dead or missing, against twenty-one hundred of the enemy. Near enough one of ours for every one of theirs! [*Cries of "Shame!"*] The next year, at [[evt:46126|Daadadhji]], another victory, and another nine hundred of ours. And then [[evt:46127|Mbilu was sacked]] — for the fourth time in its history, the official gazette told us proudly, as if that were a thing to be proud of. And then the king signed [[evt:46128|the treaty of Mbilu]], and we got six frontier districts and twenty years of truce.

More than three thousand sons of Dagafu, citizens, for six frontier districts. [*Silence.*]

And what did the men who came home find? They found bread at two [[cur:1043|duthvu]] and three-quarters the measure. I have the prices here from the market board, and they have not come down since the treaty. They found the mills working them fourteen hours, and the rents of the river districts raised to pay for the war loans. They found that the king who had sent them to burn a republic would not hear a word of a republic at home. Well! [*Laughter.*] They have come home and made one. [*Prolonged cheering.*]

Now hear me on one thing more. The priests of [[mov:24|Uthungala]] tell us that prosperity is the sign of divine favour. I do not quarrel with the priests. But if that is so, then for years the favour has been sitting in the palace and not in your kitchens, and the One who will judge us all at the end of time has seen it. Our faith also teaches that each of us may find the truth by inner light and reason. If each of you can find the truth about heaven by your own light, you can surely find the truth about the price of bread, and vote on it. [*Cheers.*]

So: [[evt:46155|the revolution]] is made. It was made in one night, and at a smaller price than the king paid in one morning at Unzhinfamamu. Let it stay so. No shops broken. No scores settled in back streets. Justice in dealings, moderation in all things: that is the creed, and we shall be judged by it.

Elections to the assembly will be held as soon as the lists can be printed and the post carries them to every district. Until then, go back to your work, and keep the wagons moving. The republic begins tomorrow morning at the factory gate. [*Long and repeated cheering. The crowd sang until dusk.*]
