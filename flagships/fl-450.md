*[Autograph letter on folded paper, sealed with the small privy signet. Endorsed on the outer leaf in a secretary's hand: "From His Majesty, in the field. Received and read in Council."]*

**From the lines before Khavu, in the province of Luthe, 23 Yuive 4055 of the Myitiyishiu.**

To the Lords of my Council at [[set:264|Ölie]], greeting.

My Lords,

You will by now have had the bare dispatch of the [[evt:44274|Battle of Kavu]], which my secretary sent off by the fast post on the evening of the action. I write now in my own hand, at more leisure, because I know how such news is received in the capital: first with bells, then with arithmetic, and lastly with complaint. I would have you hear the arithmetic from me before you hear it from the taverns.

First, the cause, since there are those among you who still mutter that this war was undertaken from appetite. It was not. The [[evt:44270|Tomzel Claim against Luthe]] was laid before the Council in due form, argued by the learned, and found good in law. The rulers of [[pol:1826|Luthe]] were offered terms and answered them with insolence. A king who suffers a just claim to be laughed at teaches every neighbour that his claims are worth nothing. The doctors of [[mov:25|Myitiyishiu]] tell us that the gods ask justice in dealings; I hold that justice is not served by letting the unjust keep what they have refused to account for. That is the whole of my argument, and I will not make it again.

Now the action. The enemy, under one [[per:43718|Naguvu]], a commander of more stubbornness than science, drew up before [[set:25|Khavu]] behind a line of ditches and felled timber. Our army, under [[per:43559|Tlamfin Lyizi]], whose years have taught him patience and whose patience has not cost him nerve, came on in three bodies at first light. The enemy's fire was heavy until noon, and I will not pretend otherwise. By mid-afternoon his centre gave way; by evening he had abandoned the field and shut himself up in the town, and the field was ours entire.

Their losses I cannot give you exactly, for the Luthe carry off what dead they can, and lie about the rest. But I rode over the ground the next morning, and I tell you plainly that the ditches were full. Our surgeons, who have seen many fields, say they have not seen one so thickly strewn. Whatever figure their envoys put about hereafter, you may safely believe it to be too low.

Our own loss was not light. I will not insult the dead by calling it light. A great army that attacks entrenchments pays the price of entrenchments, and we have paid it. I have ordered that the widows of every man fallen be paid a full year's wage in good [[cur:995|Sozhne]], from my own privy purse if the Treasury is slow. See that it is not slow.

We are now engaged in the [[evt:44275|Siege of Kavu]]. The walls are old, the garrison is thin, and the townspeople, I am told, are already eating their horses. I do not boast of the end before it comes; the gods dislike that, and so do I. But I expect to write to you again from inside the gate.

You will have heard also of the [[evt:44273|Battle of Tliyiya]], where the same Tlamfin Lyizi beat another enemy of ours this very season. Let those who say Tomzel cannot fight two quarrels at once consider that we have now beaten both in the field. I ask the Council to vote him the thanks of the realm, and a sword, and to vote them promptly.

The [[evt:44271|Tomzel Colonial War in Luthe]] is not finished, my Lords. Do not let the capital behave as though it were. Send the remounts you promised in Yuive's first week, and the powder you promised before that. A king may stand in the mud with his soldiers; he cannot shoot with promises.

I am in good health. The camp is wet. The men are cheerful. The kingdom of [[pol:1795|Tomzel]] has seldom been better served.

Your affectionate sovereign,

**[[per:43710|Pitelivie]], King**
