**ADDRESS OF THE CHAIRMAN TO THE CITIZENS ASSEMBLED IN THE GREAT SQUARE OF [[set:247|NUIDADUTH]], ON THE SECOND ANNIVERSARY OF THE REVOLUTION**

*Verbatim text, as taken down by the stenographers of the Office of Information. Printed 14 Ta'a 2472 of the Kievi.*

---

Citizens of [[pol:1935|Nuidaduth]]!

Two years ago I stood on this balcony, and the square below me was half empty, and the half that was full was afraid. Today I cannot see the end of you. *(Prolonged applause.)*

I will not make you a long speech. The old Republic made long speeches. Its deputies spoke for days, and while they spoke, the country bled. Let me remind you how it bled, because some of you were children then, and a people that forgets its wounds will be wounded again.

Twelve years ago our army went out to [[evt:46802|Yiviynge]] and was beaten. The next year it went out to [[evt:46812|Viririnu]] and was beaten. Two years after that it went out to Nuvun, and was beaten, and the next year it went out to [[evt:46841|Nuvun]] again, and was beaten again. Four battles, one after another, and four defeats. *(Cries of "Shame!")* And then the gentlemen of the Assembly put on their good coats and went to Nuvun and signed [[evt:46842|the treaty]] that our enemies dictated to them, and came home, and made speeches about it.

I was not yet President then. I was a citizen like you, reading the newspapers, and I asked myself the question that you asked yourselves: how can a people so numerous, so hard-working, so brave in its sons, lose four times? *(A voice: "Traitors!")* No, friend, not traitors. Something worse. Division. Twenty parties, each with its own newspaper, each with its own general, each with its own price for its vote. A house with twenty doors cannot be defended.

When you made me President, I tried to govern that house. For three years I tried. I tell you honestly: it could not be done. So two years ago, on this day, we closed nineteen of the doors. *(Applause.)* I know there are those who say we closed them too hard. I say: look at the square. Look at one another. Is this a people that has been silenced, or a people that has at last been allowed to speak with one voice?

Now let me speak of bread, because I will not insult you by speaking only of glory.

A measure of grain in this city costs eight [[cur:1084|Mieniu]]. I know it. Every household in this square knows it. It is too much. It was more than this in the years of the defeats, when the Republic was printing excuses instead of bread, but it is still too much. I promise you nothing I cannot do. I promise you that the price of grain will be published every week in every newspaper, so that no speculator can hide behind a rumour. I promise you that the granaries of the State will be opened in a hard winter, and not merely discussed. *(Applause.)*

The teachers of [[mov:69|Kievi]] tell us that the wise withdraw from wealth and appetite, and that justice in dealings and moderation in all things are the measure of a good life. I am not a priest. But I say: a State, too, can be wise. A State, too, can refuse to be eaten by appetite, by the appetite of factions, of profiteers, of men who would sell a battle to buy an election. That is what our [[evt:46950|revolution]] has been: not the end of the Republic's ideals, but the end of its appetites.

They tell us also that the wheel turns, and that what ends will begin again. Our enemies should remember that. Nuvun was an ending. It will not be the last word. *(Prolonged applause. Cries of "Nuvun! Nuvun!")*

I do not say that we will go to war. I say that we will never again be a people that can be dictated to. Each of you will judge by his own inner light whether that is a promise worth keeping. I believe you have already judged.

Long live the People's State! Long live Nuidaduth!

*(The Chairman, [[per:46001|Baalimam]], left the balcony amid sustained applause. The crowd dispersed in good order.)*
