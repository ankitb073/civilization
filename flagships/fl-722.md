*Register of those laid in the eastern field outside the walls of [[set:9|Tagath]], kept by the digger of that field, in the thirty-third year of [[per:17119|Namumbiv]].*

In the whole of the spring, eleven, as is usual: old men, two women in childbed, a drowned boatman, two of the poor from the temple porch. All paid for, except the poor.

**First day of the burning.** A dyer of the lower town, about forty years. Fever, the skin red as if scalded, dead in three days. His brother paid one [[cur:312|Tügin]] and a half.

**Second day.** The same dyer's brother. Not paid.

**Fourth day.** A woman of the street of the cisterns, with her two children, in one grave at her husband's asking. He paid with a copper pot, which I have sold.

**Seventh day.** A soldier, home since last summer from the [[evt:17418|Battle of Hil]] with a wound in the thigh that had healed. It was not the wound.

**Ninth day.** Six. Their names were not given me. Laid in the long trench by the fig tree.

*Note.* The teachers of the [[mov:11|Kyif]] say that no man owns the truth, and I have found it so. The physicians say this sickness comes in on the sea-breath and is the anger of [[dei:75|Bumbi]]. The priests of [[dei:73|Tithu]] say it rides the storm. The women at the well say the soldiers brought it home, and the soldiers say the women. I say only that it began in the lower town, among the dyers' vats, and that it walks uphill a street at a time, like a tax-gatherer.

**Twelfth day.** Nine. Among them the baker at the corner of the well-street, whose bread I have eaten twenty years. Nobody bakes there now. Grain in the market is near nine tügin the measure, and they say it will be ten. I do not know who will buy it at ten. The dead do not eat.

**Fifteenth day.** Fourteen. I have hired two boys at half a tügin a day each. The magistrates send lime in carts and order us to use it. The priests forbid the lime and order us to say the words over each one. We do both, and say the words quickly.

*Note.* It is said in the market that the sickness has gone out to [[set:234|Tangath]] and along the roads to the other towns. It is said that the queen keeps to her palace and burns cedar at the doors. It is said that she has gone to the army, because of the war with the rebels, the [[evt:17426|Fifth Luthe Civil War]]. I write down what is said. I believe none of it until it is brought to my field.

**Eighteenth day.** Twenty-two. I have stopped writing the trades. A man with a shovel has no time for trades.

**Twentieth day.** Nineteen. Both boys came this morning. One went home at noon with a fever, and I paid him for the whole day.

*Note.* When I was young I heard the orators in the square ask what makes a man a citizen of this city: birth, or property, or service at arms. I know the answer now. It is a place in my field. Every street sends its share, the upper town and the lower, and in the trench all are equal citizens at last. I do not say this to be clever. My back aches, and I would like someone to know.

I do not know how this ends. I will keep this register as long as my hand keeps. If another hand continues it, let him know that the trench by the fig tree is full, and that the new one begins at the third olive.
