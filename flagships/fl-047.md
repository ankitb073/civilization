title: Hymn of the Peace of Nimid

*Printed for the day of thanksgiving, 19 Livi 4863 of the Uthungala, to be sung in every assembly hall, factory yard and schoolroom of the Kingdom, at the sounding of the noon whistle.*

**I.**

Sound, whistles of the foundries! Ring, wires, from coast to coast!  
The news runs down the iron road to every town of [[pol:1674|Mbilu]]:  
at [[evt:47904|Nimid]] the foe is broken, the guns are cooling in the rain,  
and the sons of the Light are coming home along the rails.  

*Glory to the One who weighs all deeds with even hand!  
Glory to the measure kept, and to the people of the land!*  

**II.**

Nine years ago the call went out from the squares of [[set:136|Mbilu]],  
from the pulpits of the [[mov:24|Uthungala]] and the presses of the capital:  
to carry the lamp of reason into [[reg:11|Shyiyu]],  
where the shadows of the old gods still sat upon their thrones.  
Not for coal, not for grain, not for the counting-house we marched,  
but that the inner light might shine where it had never shone.  

**III.**

We do not hide the hard years; a people of truth does not lie.  
At [[evt:47812|Mienioshtse]] the line bent back in the mud and the wire,  
at [[evt:47828|Omatse]] the smoke lay on the fields for seven days,  
and at Mienioshtse again the trains ran home with the wounded.  
Yet old [[per:46468|Nashinzhè]], grey in the service of the Kingdom,  
would not yield the colours, and at Iwbetli he paid the debt in full.  

**IV.**

Sing of [[per:46739|Nambave]], who at [[evt:47820|Lishkhu]], in the year after Mienioshtse,  
met [[per:46735|Ie Mèfu]] and his iron host, and drove them from the field;  
sing of [[per:46790|Nakhathash]], who won again at [[evt:47898|Iwbetli]] the year after Nashinzhè,  
and did not give one yard of that hard-won ground away.  
Three times in three years the banners of [[pol:1970|Muiushu]] went down:  
let the schoolchildren learn the names, and say them standing.  

*Glory to the One who weighs all deeds with even hand!  
Glory to the measure kept, and to the people of the land!*  

**V.**

Then at Nimid, in this year of grace, Nambave came again,  
and Ie Mèfu, who had boasted in the foreign papers, met him there.  
From the first gun at dawn until the lamps were lit in Nimid  
the shells went over, and in the evening the field was ours.  
And the foe, who had thrown half a million of his own into the furnace,  
sent word along the wires that he would talk of peace.  

**VI.**

So the Chairman [[per:46767|Nanuve]] went down to Nimid in her plain grey coat,  
and set her name upon the [[evt:47905|Treaty of Nimid]].  
She asked for nothing that was not ours before the war;  
she took no field, no town, no tribute; she wrote down twenty years of peace.  
Let the foreign papers call it what they please:  
moderation in the hour of strength is the sign the One approves.  

**VII.**

And now, you who sing, be silent for one verse.  
Our dead are not counted in this hymn; the bells will count them.  
They lie at Mienioshtse and at Omatse, by the railway and the river,  
and the [[evt:47798|Holy War of Mbilu against Muvush]] is written on their stones.  
Let the factory stop for them; let the furnace bank its fire;  
let every mother of the Kingdom have bread and coal this winter.  

**VIII.**

Then sing again, and loudly, for the Light is not ashamed:  
prosperity follows justice as the rail follows the plain.  
The engines will run again with timber, not with guns,  
and the [[pol:1674|Mbilu]] of our children will remember Nimid.  

*Glory to the One who weighs all deeds with even hand!  
Glory to the measure kept, and to the people of the land!*
