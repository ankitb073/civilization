To [[per:6838|Lumonan]] of [[pol:262|Mankha]] say: thus says [[per:7121|Nuvbolen]], the great king, the king of [[pol:275|Mikhuthitsu]], beloved of [[dei:0|Katsuye]] of the sky, chosen of [[dei:5|Nyeshashuda]] of the spear.

With me it is well. With my house it is well. With my chariots, with my horses, with my granaries, with my land, and with the land that you have given me, it is very well. With you, let it be as the gods will.

The pledges have come. The pledges you sent to me after the oath have come to [[set:23|Unushuki]]. The son of your body has come. The sons of your elders have come. I have counted them with my own eyes. They have entered my house. They have eaten bread at my table. They have drunk from my cup. They sleep in the house by the water of [[dei:3|Nyeleki]], and the river sings to them at night. Do not weep for them. Do not tear your garment. Do not say: "My son is in the hand of the dog of Mikhuthitsu." The dog of Mikhuthitsu feeds your son.

Now hear me, Lumonan. Hear the words, and let your tablet-reader read them twice.

In the first year of Nuvbolen, when Katsuye set me upon the seat, you said: "The new king is a reed. The wind will break him." You said it. Your messengers said it in the gate of my city. I heard it. So began [[evt:6806|this war of Mikhuthitsu and Mankha]], and the fault of it is yours.

In the second year of Nuvbolen your host came out at [[evt:6834|Lunge]]. My host met it. My host broke it. Your dead lay in the field like cut barley. More than a thousand of yours fell there, and the birds were fat.

In the eighth year of Nuvbolen you came again. You came by [[evt:6882|Unushti]], close by my own city. You came with more men than I had; you thought the reed would break. Did it break? More than two thousand of yours fell before my spearmen. Of mine, not a thousand. Who did this? Nyeshashuda did this. I did this.

Then I went down to [[set:13|Kivinkuthakhu]]. I sat before its wall. I sat in the [[evt:6883|siege of Tivintukhe]] until your elders came out to me with dust upon their heads. I did not burn the city. I did not burn its storehouse of tablets. Remember that I did not.

There we swore the oath, the [[evt:6884|Treaty of Tivintukhe]], before the gods of the sky and the gods of the earth, before [[dei:2|Muevathke]] who hears what is sworn upon her. You gave me the fields. You weighed out silver for me. For ten years no spear of yours shall cross into my land, and no spear of mine into yours. Your pledges are the knot of that oath.

Now this is my word concerning the pledges. As long as the oath is kept, they will eat at my table. When the ten years are full, I will send them back to you with garments, with oil, with a chariot for the son of your body. But if your spearmen cross, if your silver comes short, then I do not answer for them. The god of the spear does not answer for them.

And the silver: let it be weighed by the weight of the [[cur:110|dina]] of Mikhuthitsu, and not by the light stones of your merchants. My weighers know the true weight. Do not send me light silver.

I have set the son of your body to learn the tablets with the sons of my house, the reckoning of grain and the names of the gods of [[mov:0|Lakimu]]. When he returns to you he will write better than your scribes.

Let [[dei:4|Lashulake]] of the moon light your road. Let Katsuye keep the rain over your fields, for your fields now feed my granaries too.

Written by the tablet-writer of the great king, in the eighth year of Nuvbolen.
