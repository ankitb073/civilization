*Tablet set up at the market gate of [[set:113|Tlalesyi]], to be read aloud by the heralds on three market days.*

**News for the people of Tlalesyi!** Hear it, you who sell fish, you who sell cloth, you who carry water. A new thing has come into the city. In the 14th year of [[per:14436|Esuefoyatl]], lord of [[pol:619|Tlalesyi]], the black metal was drawn out of the red stone, here, within these walls. I who write this am [[per:14485|Yiediada]] the smith, who made it. Ask anyone in the street of the smiths. They will tell you the same, or they will lie.

**How it was done.** You know the red stone that lies in the earth, which the potters grind for paint. For thirty years I have melted copper and tin; for thirty years I looked at that stone and thought: there is a metal in it, as there is copper in the green stone. Every smith laughed. The fire of a bronze furnace does not open the red stone; it only blackens it.

So I built a furnace taller than a man, of clay and straw, with a belly like a pot, and I set six boys at the bellows, three and three, and they blew from dawn until the sun was low, and I fed the furnace charcoal and stone, charcoal and stone. When the clay was opened at night there was a lump in the bottom like a loaf of black bread, full of holes, ugly, smoking. I struck it with the hammer. It did not break. I struck it again. It grew smaller and harder. That was [[evt:14567|the first iron of Tlalesyi]].

**What it is good for.** It is grey when it is worked and black when it is old. It takes an edge. A sickle of it cuts the barley as well as a sickle of bronze. It does not need tin, which comes from far away and costs much; it needs only the red stone, charcoal, and strong boys. Do the arithmetic, merchants: when barley costs near two [[cur:322|Yeme]] the measure, and tin costs what it costs, which of you will not want a plough that needs no tin?

**What the priests say.** I brought the first blade to the house of [[dei:89|Muivasi]], mother earth, because the stone came out of her body, and I gave her a goat. The priests of [[mov:14|Wimi]] said: this metal is a gift of the gods, and the gods must have their share. I gave it. [[dei:93|Nyishashta]], lady of war, will be glad of it, the priests say. I hope she is not too glad.

Some came also from among the followers of [[evt:14565|the new teaching]], those who say a man may find the truth by his own light and reason. They stood by the furnace all night and asked me how many measures of charcoal, how many of stone, how long at the bellows. They said: this is the work of reason. The priests said: this is the work of the gods. I say: it is the work of my arms and my six boys, and the gods may divide the honour as they please.

**To the lord of the city.** Let Esuefoyatl, our lord, hear this: the smiths of Tlalesyi now hold a thing that no smith of Tlalesyi ever held. I was born in the year this city first stood free under its own lords, and I have served it all my life. Let the lord give the street of the smiths the right to the red stone, and let no stranger carry it away. Let [[dei:90|Namas]] the sky send wind for the bellows, and let [[dei:91|Widasha]] the sea carry our blades to the markets of other cities, but let the art stay here.

**Where to see it.** The furnace stands behind my house, at the end of the street of the smiths, by the well. Come on the next day of rest. Bring no children near the fire.

Thus writes Yiediada the smith. The heralds will read it three times.
