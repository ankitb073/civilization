*[Obverse.]*

Weep, [[dei:3|Niyli]], lord of the river. Weep into your water.  
Weep, [[dei:4|Lashla]], lord of the moon. Weep with your white face.  
Weep, Miwvahi, mother of the earth, for the earth of our city is ash.  
We who are left sing it. We who are left, sing.  

Four summers we were free.  
In the year of the [[evt:18421|Revolt of Unhatlmum]] we threw down the foreign governor's seat.  
We broke his tally-sticks. We burned his tax-roll in the square.  
We said: the barley of [[set:249|Lashimti]] is for Lashimti.  
We said: the sons of [[pol:818|Unhatlmum]] will not carry spears for strangers.  
We danced in the square. We were fools. We were glad.  

Then the enemies came, one, two, three, like dogs to a dead ox.  
From one side, the men of Tivinnu. From one side, the men of Tainakhath.  
And from the river road, the men of [[pol:780|Ini]], more than all.  

*[Reverse.]*

Thirty and four men held the gate. Thirty and four, with the bronze spear.  
The men of Ini were as many as the locusts. They could not be counted.  
Eight of ours lay down in the gate. The rest were driven back.  
O [[dei:5|Niyshashte]], lady of war, where were you? Why did you sleep?  
We gave you a bull at the new year. We gave you the best bull.  

So came the [[evt:18482|Second Sack of Lishimti]].  
The men of Ini went through the streets. They went through the houses.  
They took the barley from the pits. They took the oil from the jars.  
They took the bronze from the doorposts, even the nails.  
The fire went from roof to roof, for the roofs were reed, for it was the dry month.  

The weaver's house burned. The baker's house burned.  
My house burned. My mother's loom burned, and the warp on it.  
My little brother hid in the well-house. They did not find him.  
The old man next door would not leave his doorway. They found him.  

We who are left came out of the reeds of the river.  
We came out on the third day and looked for our houses.  
We found the doorposts. We found the hearthstones, cold.  
We found the dog of the baker, sitting where the bakehouse was, waiting.  

Now we sleep under the city wall, under hides, under mats.  
There is no barley. There is no seed for the planting.  
A woman gave her copper bracelet for two loaves. Two loaves!  
A man gave his good knife for a jar of beans with weevils in them.  

The king is [[per:18343|Nitllu Tli]]. They say the king is gathering the spears.  
They say he will come. They say he will drive out Ini.  
They say. We do not know. We are not told.  
The war is not ended. The roads are not safe.  
The men go to the levy, the women go to the river for water and look behind them.  

Weep, Niyli. Weep, Lashla. We weep too.  
We who are left sing it. We will sing it until there are none of us left to sing,  
or until the walls of Lashimti stand again. One or the other.
