To my mother say: thus says your son. I am alive. [[dei:88|Mastu]] the sun has kept me. Nil'i the river gave me water on the road. I am alive.

Say to my mother: the war is ended. Our lord [[per:1023|Nitllu Mish]] and his brother the king, [[per:1022|Nitllu Ash]], have set their seals at [[set:76|Nimiti]]. The king's scribes write it as the [[evt:933|Suppression of the Miniushti Revolt]]. We say the brothers have made peace. Let the scribes write what they write. I am coming home.

Hear what was done, so that you may tell it to the wives.

Last year, in the harvest month, we went down to Nimiti. We were five and twenty men. Five and twenty, mother, and our lord limping in front on his bad leg, with his spear for a staff. At the first [[evt:928|Battle of Nimid]] their captain, [[per:1157|Atlla]], stood against us with men like the reeds of the river, like the reeds, more than a man could count. We went at them. We killed and we killed. Nine of us did not come back up the hill. The field was theirs.

This year we went down again. We were six and thirty, for some of the boys of the upper villages came to us. This time the king himself came out against his brother. At the second [[evt:932|Battle of Nimid]] we killed many, many, more than we were, many times over, and still the field was theirs, because there was always another rank behind the rank. Fourteen of us went down to [[dei:87|Sutlpi]].

Nine and fourteen. Three and twenty of us in the two years, mother. Their mothers will plough the fields of the dead men. Tell the wife of the potter. Tell the mother of the twins; both are with Sutlpi. I closed the eyes of the elder. I could not find the younger.

The men of [[pol:13|Tivintusa]] lost more than we did. Hundreds, men say. Their women weep too. Their women are our cousins. It is a brothers' quarrel, mother, and the brothers are alive, and men say they ate together after the seals were set, and the boys of the upper villages are in the ground.

I do not know what the quarrel was. Some say it was the land of their father, which was wrongly divided. Some say an insult at a feast. Our lord does not speak to us of it. He gave each of us a copper ring when we marched away, and he wept at the graves, I saw it. He is not a bad lord. He is a lame man who wanted to be a king for a little while.

So ends what the scribes call the [[evt:924|Tivintusa Civil War]], and all the country of [[reg:11|Tiye]] is one again under one king.

On the road, bread is dear. I gave my copper ring to a woman for a loaf and a cup of beer. I am not sorry.

Mother, grind barley for me. Keep the goat. Tell my sister to stop crying. I will come home by the river road before the new moon, and I will sleep for three days, and then I will mend the roof, and I will never go down to Nimiti again.

May Lashli'i the moon light the road for me. Your son.
