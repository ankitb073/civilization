*[Obverse. A petition tablet, its upper edge pierced for a cord; it was perhaps hung at a temple door. Several hands have added marks of assent at the foot.]*

To [[dei:0|Atlie]], father of the high sky, who sees the fields from above.  
To [[dei:3|Niele'i]], lord of the river, who fills the canals.  
To [[dei:2|Muevas'e]], mother of the black earth, who feeds the barley.  
To the lord who sits in [[set:83|Khie'a]], who keeps the gate and the scales:  
the old houses of Khie'a, the sons of the canal-diggers, say this.  

Hear us, Atlie. Hear us, Niele'i. Hear us, lord of the gate.

In the days of our fathers the land of [[reg:10|Mi'ositl]] was ours. Our fathers dug the canals. Our fathers set the date palms in rows. Our fathers poured beer to Niele'i at the river-mouth every spring, and the river rose, and the barley rose. The land was ours. The land was ours.

Then [[dei:2|Muevas'e]] shut her mouth in [[reg:19|Man'a]]. That was the [[evt:6231|Famine in Mana]]. The barley did not come up in Man'a. The goats of Man'a died. The children of Man'a died. We heard it; we pitied them. We are not stones.

Then the [[cul:3|Ziethkhi]] came down out of Man'a into our land. They came down with their goats and their tents and their old women on donkeys. They came down in one year, they came down in the next year, they came down every year. Four and twenty years they have been coming down. That is the [[evt:6232|Migration of the Ryethti into Mikhukhizhi]], as the scribes of the gate write it. We write it: the coming of the locusts.

Hear what the Ziethkhi do, lord of the gate. Hear it, Atlie.

They put their goats in our barley. The barley is eaten to the root.  
They cut our date palms for their fires. The palms of our fathers are cut.  
They draw water from the canal and do not clean the canal.  
They do not pour beer to Niele'i. They do not bow at the house of [[dei:1|Uelti]].  
They speak a tongue like the cawing of crows. Their children throw stones at our children.  
They take the gleanings that belong to our widows.  
They sell their goats cheap, for a handful of [[cur:110|Tina]], and our herdsmen sell nothing.  

When the spears of [[pol:204|Khie'a]] went out to the [[evt:6326|Khie'a–Bäni War]], who stayed behind? The Ziethkhi stayed behind. Our sons went out with the spears. The Ziethkhi sat in our sons' fields.

And then the great house fell. That was the [[evt:6358|Collapse of the Khie'a State]]. The scribes say it fell because of the war; the priests say it fell because of the omens. We say: it fell because the locusts ate it from under. Who held the land when the great house shook? The Ziethkhi held it.

Now there is the [[evt:6359|Rise of Khyeka]] here in our city, and in the other half of the land there is the [[evt:6360|Rise of Mikhuthitsu]]. Two lords where there was one. And between the two lords, in every field, the tents of the Ziethkhi.

*[Reverse.]*

Therefore, lord of the gate, hear our asking.

Let the Ziethkhi keep their goats on the far side of the canal.  
Let them pay for the water as we pay for the water.  
Let them pour beer to Niele'i at the river-mouth, as is his due.  
Let them not cut one palm more.  
Let the land of the old houses be written again on new tablets, field by field, with our fathers' names.  

Do this, and we will pour oil to Atlie for you. We will pour beer to Niele'i for you. The priests of [[mov:0|La'im]] say: each god has his place, and each people its place. Let the Ziethkhi keep their place.

If you will not do it, Lashla'e of the moon sees it, and Nieshashta of the spear sees it.

*[Lower edge.]*

> Written in the 192nd year of Khie'a, in the month of the high river.  
> The marks of the heads of nine houses are set below.
