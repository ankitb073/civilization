*To the Lords of Our Privy Council at [[set:76|Nemeth]], from [[per:41855|Maezhe Lozha]], by the grace of the One Power Queen of [[pol:1755|Tsey]]; given within the walls of [[set:83|Kheye]], 2 Tse 2235 of the E'eshio.*

My Lords,

You will by now have the courier's copy of the [[evt:42333|Treaty of Kheye]], and with it, I do not doubt, a number of anxious questions in the margin. Let me answer the chief of them first. Yes, I am a hostage. No, you are not to be alarmed by it.

The custom of this coast, which neither party could decently set aside, requires that when a peace is sworn inside a besieged town the sovereign who dictates it should lodge within the walls until the first articles are performed, as a pledge that the besiegers will not fall upon the place the moment the gates are opened. [[per:41810|Ae]] asked it of me in a voice that trembled, as if he expected to be refused. I did not refuse. I wished him, and his council, and the whole sullen population of this town, to see the Queen of Tsey walk through their gate unarmed, take up her lodging in their best house, and sleep soundly. A conqueror who fears her hosts teaches them to hope. I intend to teach them the contrary.

I have therefore been a prisoner for six days, in rooms hung with the best tapestries in Kheye, waited on by a chamberlain who bows lower each morning. My guards are their soldiers; their soldiers have not been paid since the [[evt:42332|Ninth Siege of Kheye]] began, and look to my purse rather than to Ae's. I have given them a few [[cur:933|Eme]] each, out of policy rather than charity, and they now salute me in the street.

Let no one at Nemeth pretend this peace was a gift. The [[evt:42239|Eleventh Tsiey Trade War]] was forced on us by the obstinacy of [[pol:1798|Kheye]], which for years would neither open its river to our merchants nor pay the dues that every other port of the coast accepts. Their own chroniclers will call it ambition on our part; let them. We did not choose the quarrel. We chose only to finish it.

And finished it is. You will recall Nimid, when our captains swept their garrison away before ever I came to the throne; the [[evt:42258|Battle of Nimid]] cost them their last foothold near our capital. You will recall the first [[evt:42292|Battle of Omatse]], two years since, where they fled from our lines with scarcely a blow struck. And you will recall, for I commanded there myself, the second [[evt:42331|Battle of Omatse]] this spring, where they put twice our numbers in the field and left near forty thousand of them on it, for some three thousand of ours. I have walked that field. I do not boast of it lightly. But I will not have it said in Nemeth that we were lucky.

As to the terms: Kheye cedes the three towns, the whole of the disputed province and the navigation of the river; it opens its markets to our traders on our conditions; and there is to be a truce of twenty years. I would have had more. Ae would have given less. The articles are what a victor may demand without making the vanquished desperate, and a desperate enemy is the costliest possession a crown can acquire.

The ordained of the [[mov:57|E'eshio]] who came with me preach compassion daily in the square before the citadel, and are heard with more patience than I expected. I have allowed it. A people that has lost a war is the readiest of all to be told that suffering may be released.

I expect to be quit of my captivity within the month, as soon as the garrisons of the ceded towns have marched out. Until then, govern as I have instructed; pay the troops before the clerks; and tell anyone in the capital who calls this a humiliation that I have never been so well entertained in my life.

Your Queen,

MAEZHE LOZHA.
