*[Obverse. Two columns; the refrain is written once and marked by a sign for repetition.]*

Alas for [[set:2|Mieshlothie]], the city of the river-mouth!  
Alas for the gate of the oxen, alas for the quay of the boats!  
Alas for the house of [[dei:0|Atlie]], whose roof was cedar,  
alas for the house of [[dei:1|Uelti]], whose tablets were many!

*Refrain:* Weep, O city; the wheel has turned against you.

In the days of the trade-war, the [[evt:668|First Iiyyi Trade War]],  
the men of [[pol:6|Ana]] came up the river, the men of Ana came up with their boats.  
They wanted the road of the traders. They wanted the salt and the copper.  
They wanted the quay that our fathers built with their hands.

At Sase they stood against us, at the [[evt:696|Battle of Sase]] they stood.  
Our sons went out with the spear. Our sons did not come home.  
[[dei:5|Nieshashta]], lady of the spear, why did you turn your feet away?  
Your feet were red, but not with their blood.

*Weep, O city; the wheel has turned against you.*

Then Ana came to the walls; Ana came over the walls.  
A second time the city fell; a second time the gate was broken.  
This is the [[evt:697|Second Sack of Myishlithie]]; let the mothers name it to their daughters.  
The ox was led away from the stall, the ox did not low again.  
The bride was led away from the house, the bride did not sing again.  
The silver bowl was taken from the table of the god; the god ate from clay.  
In the street of the potters the dogs ate. In the street of the weavers the dogs ate.

O [[dei:3|Niele'i]], lord of the river, you carried their boats to us.  
You were not ashamed. You carried our dead out to the sea.

*Weep, O city; the wheel has turned against you.*

Then the elders sat down with Ana, the elders swore the oath.  
That was the [[evt:698|Treaty of Myishlithie]]; it was bitter in the mouth.  
The fields beyond the marsh were given; a town was given; the far pastures were given.  
The silver goes down the river every year, every year to Ana.  
The boats of Ana tie up at our quay; they pay what they will, and they laugh.

*Weep, O city; the wheel has turned against you.*

And now, alas, the house of the kings is split like a reed.  
That was the [[evt:751|Collapse of the Mieshlothie State]], in the year of the bitter wind.  
The Yi'otl went out from us, they took their own road, they set up their own throne:  
that was the [[evt:753|Rise of Yi'otl]], and the brothers do not eat at one table.  
The city remains, Mieshlothie remains, a small house on the river.  
The great house is gone. The small house remains.

O [[dei:4|Lashla'e]], lord of the moon, you who count the months,  
count for us: twenty-one summers since the gate was broken, and still the widows weep.  
O [[dei:2|Muevas'e]], mother of the black earth,  
you took our sons into your lap; hold them, do not let them go hungry.

*Weep, O city; the wheel has turned against you.*

But hear, O people of Mieshlothie. Hear the word of the priests of [[mov:0|La'im]]:  
"All things turn on the great wheel. What ends will begin again."  
The wheel that turned down will turn up. The quay will be ours again.  
The silver bowls will come back to the table of the god.  
Pour to Atlie of the high sky. Pour to Uelti, who keeps the words.  
Pour to Nieshashta, though her feet were turned away; she may turn them back.

*Weep, O city; and when you have wept, rise.*

*[Lower edge. Colophon.]*

> Written in Mieshlothie, in the 306th year of Mieshlothie, to be sung on the day of the broken gate.
