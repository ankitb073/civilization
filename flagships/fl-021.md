title: The Words of a Captain of Fifty on the War for the Roads of Tomözel
---
These are the words of a captain of fifty in the host of [[pol:649|Eshueethie]], set down in the 17th year of [[per:15657|Ishfama]], the queen, the strong-armed, the keeper of the roads. I am old now. I have read the omens and I have stood in the line. Let the gods of [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]], who weigh the dealings of men, weigh my words and find them just.

Hear how the war began. The merchants of Eshueethie went down into [[reg:18|Tomözel]] with oil and with copper, and the men of [[pol:555|Tleyiyan]] stopped them at the fords. They took a tenth; then they took a fifth; then they took a third. They broke the seals of the jars and spat in the oil. This was not justice in dealings. The elders of Eshueethie cried out at the gate, and so began the [[evt:15750|third war for the trade of Tamthal]], in the year before the queen sat upon the throne.

In the first year of the queen we went up against Foyetlitie. The priests opened a lamb, and the liver was spotted, and the old men said: wait. But the young men did not wait. At the [[evt:15761|battle of Foyitlithyi]] [[per:15037|Esiesh Shimen]], king of Tleyiyan, came out with a host twice as great as ours. We were pressed back. We did not run. We went back step by step, as a bull goes back from fire, and we carried our dead with us. Of them fell more than of us. Let that be remembered.

In the second year the queen herself led us before the city of Tleyiyan. She poured the dawn offering with her own hand. A hawk came down on the left side of the camp and took a dove, and the priests said: so shall the queen take the king. And it was so. At the [[evt:15769|battle of Tliyiya]] the host of Tleyiyan broke like a pot thrown on stone. Fourteen thousand of them lay on the ground, and Esiesh Shimen their king lay among them. Of us fewer than two thousand fell. Then they shut their gates against us and gave us nothing but arrows, and we cut their orchards and went home with the spoil.

In the fourth year our general [[per:15688|Niemamsho]] fought before Tliyiya again, and the day went against us. I do not hide it. But I say this also: their dead were near as many as ours. In the fifth year Niemamsho went down to Tlalamibiyi, and at the [[evt:15787|battle of Zhaalämiviyi]] he struck down [[per:15689|Mikhishöziliyekhan]], the general of Tleyiyan, and nine thousand of his men fell with him. I took a shield that day with a red bull painted on it. I have it still.

In the seventh year the queen met [[per:15038|Yi Mov]], the new king of Tleyiyan, on that same field, and the gods withheld the day. In the eighth year she met him again by Tseyinmiwa, at the [[evt:15811|battle of Tiyinmiw]], and he fled. More than four thousand of his men fell, and of ours fewer than one thousand. Then we sat down a second time before Tleyiyan, and Yi Mov came out to the queen with dust on his head.

So the [[evt:15813|peace of Tliyiya]] was sworn in his own city. He gave up to the queen the land of Tomözel. He opened the fords to our merchants and swore that no one would break their seals. He swore tribute each year, [[cur:343|Moa]] upon Moa, for forty years, and ten years without the spear. The tribute came in that first year, and it comes still, carried up the road on asses, and the treasurers of [[set:3|Eshueethie]] weigh it at the gate.

I took no more than my share of the spoil. The wise withdraw from appetite; a captain who grows fat on plunder is weighed light by the gods.

Now in these days the men of Kaddanul are come against us, in the [[evt:15839|war of Kaddanul against Eshueethie]]. The queen has met them at [[evt:15861|Khash]] and beaten them, and in this very year she has beaten them again at [[evt:15897|Kadhdanul]]. I am too old to stand in the line. My sons stand there. May the gods who weigh all dealings find our cause just, and give the day to the queen, and keep the roads open for the merchants of Eshueethie, as the queen kept them open in the war of my youth.
