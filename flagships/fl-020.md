[…]  
and the host of [[pol:612|Pibetie]] went down to Fyinan like the flood of spring,  
and [[per:16430|Bozinsimen]] went before them, Bozinsimen the lion-hearted,  
whose spear-head was iron of the black hills, whose shield was bronze over hide.  
For the king had said: "[[reg:13|Ieyei]] is the pasture of my fathers. Bring it home."  
So began the [[evt:16567|Pibetie–Ana War]], and the river was its witness.  

At the ford came out [[per:16356|Khikhipil Foyatl]], the grey wolf of [[pol:693|Ana]].  
Bozinsimen met him in the water; the water rose to the horses' bellies.  
At the [[evt:16577|Battle of Fyinan]] the wolf gave back, and the ford was ours.  
In the next year came Tlamfen Yueva with a little host;  
Bozinsimen swept him from the bank as the thresher sweeps the chaff.  

But in the third year the gods turned their faces.  
The priests had read the liver, and the liver was dark;  
the king's men did not listen. Who listens in a year of victories?  
At the [[evt:16588|Battle of Fyinan]] [[per:16521|Tlamfen Yueva]] held the far bank,  
and ten thousand of Pibetie went down into the water and did not come up.  
[[dei:3|Nielei]] of the river took them. Nielei had not been given the due.  
In that year the grey wolf died in his bed at home, and Ana mourned him.  
[…]  
[…] and the son of the wolf came out, young, with his father's name,  
[[per:16497|Khikhipil Foyatl]] the younger, whose beard had not yet thickened.  
At the [[evt:16611|Battle of Fyinan]] Bozinsimen taught him the ford.  
The young wolf ran, and the ford was ours again.  

Hear now the omen. Hear the night before the last day.  
[[dei:4|Lashlae]] of the moon was hidden behind the hills;  
an owl sat upon the standard of Pibetie and would not be driven off.  
The seers said: "Lion, do not cross tomorrow."  
Bozinsimen said: "Shall the lion fear an owl?"  
He poured wine for [[dei:5|Nieshashta]] of war, all the jar, and broke the jar.  
At dawn he crossed. At the [[evt:16627|Battle of Fyinan]] Tlamfen Yueva met him,  
and the spear of Ana found the lion's throat above the shield.  
So fell Bozinsimen in the middle of the river. The river carried his blood.  
Ana held the bank that day. Ana sang that night. Let Ana sing.  

In the house at Pibetie his daughter cut her hair,  
a girl of thirteen summers, [[per:16568|Boinieme]], and she did not weep before the women.  
She said: "The river owes me a father."  
[…]  
Then [[per:16580|Famsha]] went down to the ford, Famsha the patient,  
and the young wolf came out once more against Pibetie.  
At the [[evt:16641|Battle of Fyinan]] the young wolf fell in the water,  
and the son went down to the dark where his father had gone before.  
Then Ana bowed its neck. At Fyinan the elders of Ana swore the oath.  
Ieyei came home to Pibetie, and the pastures of the fathers;  
Ana weighed out silver, heavy silver, to the house of Pibetie;  
for ten years no spear of Ana would cross the ford.  

O Boinieme, daughter of the lion, now in the twenty-eighth year of your seat!  
You have met the rebels at [[evt:16774|Sase]], and they were as dust before you;  
you have bound the [[evt:16794|Mieshlethie Revolt]] as a herdsman binds a goat.  
The river paid what it owed. Etlie of the sky keeps your house.  
Hear, then, the singer, and fill the singer's cup,  
and I will sing how the host came home […]
