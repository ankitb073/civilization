To my husband, who keeps the spears at the ford of the river, say:  
Thus says [[per:1072|Jilialmagunath]], queen of [[pol:10|Lenunushal]], your wife.

May [[dei:14|Vulme]] of the spear stand at your right hand. May [[dei:19|Yenan]] the moon light your road. May you read this clay with your own eyes, and not hear it from a stranger.

I am alive. Hear that first. I am alive, and my body is whole, though my heart is not.

You know that the men of [[pol:14|Egu]] came again this year, in the seventh year of my seat. They were fewer than at Shiniy. They were fewer than we were. Hear that, and weep with me. Five summers ago, at the [[evt:806|Battle of Shiniy]], our spears broke them and sent them home weeping, when I was a girl and my mother held the seat for me. I thought they would not forget it. They did not forget it. They came back to pay it.

I went out against them before the city. I did not send another in my place. I put on the bronze of my father. The priests poured blood to Vulme at dawn, and the smoke went up straight, and they said it was a good sign. It was not a good sign. That was the [[evt:823|Battle of Linnshal]]. Our spearmen stood until the sun was high, and then they could not stand. I came back through the gate with the dust in my mouth, and the gate was shut behind me, and it did not hold.

Hear now what was done in [[set:18|Lenunushal]], the city of our fathers.

They came in over the low wall by the potters' quarter. They went into the house of [[dei:18|Eva]] and put out the god's fire, which had not gone out since the days of the first kings. They broke the jars in the storehouse of [[dei:17|Nidhikhti]] and let the barley run into the street. What they could not carry they burned. They took the bronze from the doors. They took the young men with ropes and the young women with ropes. My women hid me in the cellar of the oil-press, under the empty jars, and I lay there and heard my city weep. This is the [[evt:824|Second Sack of Linnshal]]. The old songs say the first, in the days of our fathers' fathers' fathers, was terrible. I say this one was worse.

When they had eaten their fill, they went out again. They did not stay. They did not take the throne; they took only what they could carry. The city is ours still. It is black. But it is ours.

Now hear what I command, and do it quickly.

Do not come back to the city. Hold the ford. If the ford falls, they come again to the city, and there is nothing left to give them. Send to the villages for barley, for here a measure costs more than two [[cur:9|Nivba]] and a third, and there is little to buy at any price. Send to the herdsmen for sheep. Send the young men who are left, every one.

Know also that the gods have not forgotten us altogether. In this same year the men of Tathrelbu, who rose against my seat, came to me with their hands open and put their hands to the clay. That is the [[evt:827|Suppression of the Tathrelbu Revolt]]. So the wheel turns. What goes down comes up again; the priests of [[mov:2|Jinru]] have always said it, and I hold to it now with both hands.

The [[evt:794|Egu–Lenunushal War]] is not ended. I do not know its end. [[dei:16|Dumamnul]] of the high sky knows its end, and he has not told me. But I will not bow my neck to Egu while one spear stands at the ford.

Write back by the boy who carries this. Tell me you are well. Tell me the ford holds.

Written in the 7th year of Jilialmagunath, in the burned city.
