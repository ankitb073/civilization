*In the 6th year of [[per:17304|Mashe Simen]], the great king, lord of [[pol:755|Tomözel]], whose arm is long.*

**In the month of sowing.** The sky is brass. We sowed the barley in dust, my husband and I and the boy, and the dust blew it away before the crows could take it. My husband says the rain will come at the new moon. He said it last year. The old women say this dry has sat on the land for twenty years and more. Travellers from the river road say it is [[evt:17220|the Great Drought in Mikhukhizhi]] and it has come down on us like a guest who will not leave. I do not know Mikhukhizhi. I know my seed is gone.

**In the month of the first heat.** A hawk sat on our roof three mornings. On the third morning the well rope came up dry. My mother-in-law poured oil at the door-stone and said the words. I gave the household gods a cake of millet and ate none myself. The priests of [[mov:25|Mietseyeshio]] tell us to be moderate in all things. We are moderate. We eat once a day.

**In the month of the threshing that was not.** There was nothing on the threshing floor but the stones. The tax-man of the king came anyway and looked at the floor and wrote on his tablet. My husband asked him what he wrote. He said: [[evt:17385|Famine in Tamthal]]. He writes it, and we eat it.

At the market a measure of barley is five [[cur:343|Moa]] and a piece over. Five Moa! For five Moa my father bought the ox. I sold my mother's bronze pin for two Moa and the loom-weights for one. We bought half a measure and mixed it with acorn meal and chaff. The children's bellies ache from it. The little one cries at night, and in the morning she does not cry, and that frightens me more.

**In the month of the red moon.** The moon rose red as a cut. My husband's brother came home from the king's army. He went out in the war against Linnat. He says the king himself led them at the [[evt:17364|Battle of Tunavükhini]] and the men of Linnat ran like goats. He says peace was sworn at Tiaiosh and tribute was sent to [[set:3|Eshueethie]], wagons of it. I asked him: where are the wagons? Did they pass our door? He laughed and then he did not laugh. Now he eats from our pot.

The roads are bad. Since the foreign kings fought each other at the [[evt:17347|Battle of Biyitl]] the grain carts do not come as they did. That is what the carters say. Perhaps it is true. Carters say many things. The priest says it is our sins. My husband says nothing. He goes out to the field every morning and stands in it.

**In the month of the long nights.** Three houses at the end of our lane are shut. The family of the potter took the road to the city to beg at the temple doors. The widow next to them went to sleep and did not wake. We carried her on a door. Her son was in the army and has not come home.

I have put aside one jar of barley for seed. Every night I look at the jar. Every night the children look at the jar. I have tied the lid with a knot only I know.

**On the last day of the year.** May the gods who keep this house keep it. May the king remember the farmers of [[pol:755|Tomözel]] as he remembered his soldiers. May the rain come in its season. I write this for my daughter, if she lives to read it, so she will know that her mother kept the seed jar shut.
