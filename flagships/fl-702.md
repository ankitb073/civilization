*In the first year of [[per:39709|Esh Ege]], whom the Wheel has raised over us.*

**At the sowing.** I write this in the back leaves of my father's prayer-book, for there is no other skin in the house that we did not boil. On the holy day the priest read that what ends will begin again, and that the Wheel does not stop for the weeping of one woman. I believe him. So I set down which turns of it we have lived, for my grandchildren will not believe me.

The rains have been failing in this country since before my grandmother was a bride. She used to say the sky over [[reg:11|Tzey]] had forgotten its duty, as a bad lord forgets his. But in the year the old kingdom broke and the lords of [[pol:1693|Nemeth]] took up the rule, the sky forgot us altogether. The next spring the barley came up a hand high and died yellow in the furrow, and then the [[evt:40119|Famine in Tsiey]] was upon us, and it stayed four years.

**What it cost.** In the first winter a sack of grain cost a ewe. In the second it cost two ewes, and by the third the miller would take nothing but silver, and we had none. My husband sold the plough-ox to the steward's man for a sack and a half of rye with the chaff left in. We ate the seed-corn. We ate nettles, and elm bark pounded fine, and once a dog. The children's bellies swelled and their arms went thin as distaffs.

Our youngest, the girl, went in the second winter. The ground was iron and we could not dig, so she lay in the byre under clean straw until the thaw. The priest came and said the words of release, that she was loosed from suffering. I said she had known nothing else. God forgive me, I said it to his face.

My husband's brother took his wife and his two boys down the road, saying there was bread given out at the gates of [[set:76|Nemeth]]. We have heard nothing of them since. The reeve says that one in six of the people of this country are dead or gone away. I think he says it to sound learned. I count our own lane: of nine hearths, three are cold.

**At midsummer.** It has rained twice since the sowing, a soft rain, and the barley stands to my knee. The bells rang in the city for the young queen's crowning, and we heard them across the fields. They say she is younger than I am. I pray she has a kinder sky than we had.

Everyone has someone to blame. The old man at the ford says nothing has grown right since the [[evt:40024|Battle of Nimid]] in my girlhood, when the armies trod the fields to mud below the city. Others blame the old king, others the drought, others our sins. The priests of [[mov:57|Eheshio]] say that blame is only another wheel, and that the wise step off it.

**At the harvest.** The steward came for the lord's due as he always comes. He was not a hard man this year. He took less than his right, and said it was in honour of the new queen, and we kissed his hand. Grain is still dear, but there is grain.

We have reaped. It is not much, but the children ate bread on the evening of the last sheaf, true bread, and the little one laughed and asked what it was. I wept into the dough. If the Wheel must turn again, let it turn slowly past this house.
