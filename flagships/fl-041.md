*A Letter of His Majesty to the Lords of the Council of State at Yethithi, written in the camp, and by His Majesty's command copied fair and read in the houses of prayer.*

[[per:42371|Nilu Mix]], by the grace of the One, King of [[pol:1769|Wemu]], to our trusty and well-beloved Lords of the Council at [[set:104|Yethithi]], greeting.

My Lords,

Since there is no evil in a commonwealth so dangerous as a rumour that runs faster than the truth, we have thought it necessary to set down for you, with our own hand, what passed before [[set:176|Azha]] in these days, that you may correct by this letter whatever the taverns and the hawkers of news have already published.

You know the justice of our cause. The province of [[reg:6|Iith]] has long been held by the kings of [[pol:1772|Tuxi]] against every claim of right and every rule of good neighbourhood; its people have petitioned us; its roads have been closed to our merchants. We did not take up arms lightly. Reason, which our faith teaches is the lamp of every honest man, persuaded us that a peace kept by injustice is not peace but a slow war, and that it is the duty of a prince to end it.

At [[evt:43387|Ash]] our army came up with the enemy. The general of Tuxi, [[per:42600|Aüyuf]], an old soldier who has grown grey in the business of avoiding a fair fight, had taken up a position such as no honourable commander would choose: behind earthworks, marsh on one flank and the river on the other, with his guns sunk in pits so that they could neither be seen nor answered. Our troops, who were superior to his in number, in spirit and in the justice of their quarrel, went forward against these works with a courage that I do not think was ever surpassed by the soldiers of any nation. That they did not carry them is no reproach to them. It is a reproach to an enemy who will not meet men in the open field.

I must tell you, my Lords, with a grief that I do not try to hide, that our general [[per:42903|Yeüji]] fell at the head of the second assault. He was young, and he was brave beyond prudence; he died with the colours in his hand. I commend his family to your care, and I desire that a pension be paid to them out of our privy purse, in [[cur:808|mimi]] of full weight, from the first day of next month.

Our loss is heavy. I will not insult your understanding by pretending otherwise, nor will I insult the dead by allowing the enemy's gazetteers to number them as they please. Theirs is heavier than they confess. I am credibly informed by deserters and by prisoners that the enemy lost some forty thousand men, killed, wounded and run away, and that whole regiments of Tuxi exist now only on their paymasters' rolls. An army that wins such a victory twice will not need to be beaten a third time.

The army is not broken. It has fallen back in good order upon its magazines, and the regiments are being filled up. I have reviewed them myself, and found them sober, obedient, and angry. I ask of the Council three things: that the new levy be raised without delay; that the bills for powder, bread and pay be honoured at sight; and that the preachers of the [[mov:69|Kievi]] be instructed to remind the people that a just cause is not made unjust by one unlucky day, nor a war decided by its first battle. Moderation in victory and constancy in reverse are the two virtues our faith most demands of us, and I do not mean that Wemu shall want the second.

Let it also be known that I will not hear talk of an accommodation with Tuxi while one village of Iith remains in their hands. The [[evt:43383|war]] was begun for reasons; it will be ended for reasons, and not for fear.

Given under our hand in the camp, the 11 Nim 2111 of the Kievi.

**Nilu Mix**
